Baldur's Gate..
An interview about the Expansion pack!
3DGN hooked one of the first exclusive interview with the producer of the expansion pack for the most popular RPG of the year, Baldur's Gate. (Thanks for the tip in Rick!) Here are some highlights for you:

What is the continued story line for Tales of the Sword Coast, and how is it tied in with the original?
Tales of the Sword Coast (TOTSC) is an add-on that can be installed at any time over your installation of Baldur's Gate (BG). It adds new areas and creates new quests for you to adventure in. As a player, you would go to your world map and notice "hey cool, new areas", and off you go. The quests that are added in are separate from the main game's story, so you can do them at any time you wish, though we recommend an average party level of 6-7. Two VERY big subquests and a number of smaller ones add approximately 30-40 hours of game play.

Along the lines of, um, newness (I'll have to see if that word even exists), what can we expect? Yeah, I think we all know there will be more items, quests, sub-plots and such, but is there anything new that really sticks out?
More high level spells and some truly nasty monsters. Because TOTSC is for a higher level character than BG was, we added new higher level challenges. I can't comment right now on the monsters (it would give away some of the plot), but the new spells really help to power up the magic-using classes. New items will similarly empower some of the other classes.

What are some of the graphical enhancements, other than bug fixes, that can we look forward to?
We have refined how we create our art. On the whole, the quality of the art is much higher. In fact, some of our content had to be "toned down" due to the fact it looked too good compared to the rest. BG2 is going to look absolutely amazing

Cybermercs..
A review
Happy Puppy has brought us a review of the Diablo like game known as Cybermercs. He gives it a poor 6 out of 10. Here are some of his comments:

The characterization of the game is a bit limited. You have a choice of four characters, each with varying skills and abilities. These differences are noticeable in the beginning stages of the game, so you may want to play a while in order to choose which one works best with your playing style. Your weapons include handguns, shotguns, lasers, and the like. This is a strictly combat-based world. Magic does not exist in this reality, unlike other cyberpunk scenarios. Instead of magic, characters gain special strengths through cybernetic implants that increase strength, intelligence, and dexterity. All character development is based on money. For me, this became frightfully boring, though it is effective enough. You are paid well for your accomplishments and money was never a real issue in my progression through the game. Avid consumers and vain players alike may get a thrill out of spending thousands of hard-earned dollars on body upgrades, and other personal-ability-enhancing items. Still, I wish that they would have allowed for more character enhancements based on experience.

The biggest, and most obvious flaw with the game is the extremely poor documentation. The player's manual, which is riddled with grammatical errors, is difficult to use. The basics of how to use the interface are skimmed and this proves to be quite frustrating when beginning to learn the game. After a good deal of trial and error, the interface becomes understandable and actually ends up easy to use. However, having to go through so much trouble just to learn to use the controls is inexcusable. Seasoned gamers will quickly adapt, but woe be to the novice who picks this up as his or her gaming introduction. The same thing applies to setting up a multiplayer game. The networking instructions are more comprehensible, but they still seem written toward someone with a previous knowledge base. Novices may wish to contact technical support, which is available through email only.

Even with the pitfalls, the game still provides entertainment. Splattering gruesome aliens against walls, bettering yourself and your lethal abilities, and making gobs of spending money are all activities that the average gamer can certainly enjoy. The difficulty level increases at a steady pace, and the game remains challenging throughout the different missions.

-Dragons

Final Fantasy VII..
An Interview...
Brought to us by the Vault Network, this interview with the VP of development for FF VII is worth reading if you were a fan of VII. Here are some of the highlights:

[Speaking of VII]What areas if any would you have liked to change or improve?
Randall Fujimoto: Gameplay-wise, nothing (it did sell millions of copies!). PC version-wise, I would have liked a slightly longer final QA period to ensure compatibility with all the various and new PC hardware.

What do you consider the game's defining strengths?
Randall Fujimoto: Characters, storyline, graphics, and gameplay (including minigames). Also, people say that the "Aeris scene" is one of the most emotional moments ever in the history of gaming. Eliciting emotion from the player is a trademark of our RPGs.

More Everquest..
A Preview...
Chanodin.net has released its preview of Everquest. Here are some highlights from it:

As I mentioned above, and you probably already know, Everquest takes place in beautiful 3D world set in a medieval fantasy land. Unfortunately along with pretty graphics and special effects comes some heavy hardware requirements. Your going to need some type of 3dfx card, or one that the game supports, but who really doesn’t have one these days?. They’re planning to run the game at 640X480, but there is no word yet on whether or not you’ll be able to change this setting. The engine will also be able to create wind, rain, and fog that will each affect gameplay accordingly. You’ll also have the ability to go underwater in certain zones, bringing a whole new world to the online RPG.

The servers are supposed to hold from 1000 to 1500 people at a time. I’m a bit skeptical as to whether it can hold that many people at one time, but alas, only time will tell. Gamers will be able to create 8 characters on a server, and thankfully will be allowed to move characters from one server to another. I can’t explain what it was like in Ultima Online when you wanted to use a character that was on another server....ugh. This may have a price though, as it’s rumored that you can only bring your character, and not your equipment to different servers.

If the Everquest design team can pull together all that they’ve been talking about, this could be one of the greatest games ever. A few things worry me though. I’ve played Ultima Online for quite some time, and the lag is minimal, but still there. Even after all the patches, server fixes, and the number of people who I’m sure have quit playing, it still lags. I’m wondering how the servers will actually handle all those people, and how much the 3D engine will affect the lag. Besides that, the balancing act alone of a game this caliber is immense. Creating a working economy, and skill ratings that everyone can agree on is tough at best. I’m hoping that Everquest will shape up into something every gamer can enjoy, and with the potential it has right now it just might do that.

-Dragons

Plantscape:Torment..
Takes a BIG step forward...
Last week Torment took a major step towards its completion - although that is still quite a while away. Last week we reached a critical milestone that had been dangling in front of us for quite some time. We finished a three level area of the game and brought it to a point where you can actually sit down with it and play. We had made it a point to have most elements of the game in this milestone, including things like automaps, the journal, inventory handling, spell screen handling and so on. Of course the implementation of the story itself was also on the list of things that needed to be finished for this milestone.

When we finished the milestone we finally had something in our hands that felt like a game. After almost two years of work on the project, we finally came to the point where we could start up the Torment executable and it looked, felt and sounded like our game. All the interface art is there, all the NPCs are there, doing their thing and reacting to you. The first puzzle is in there and, hell, it felt great when the first of the game's NPCs joined the player's party. We felt strong relief, not only because we had achieved our milestone according to schedule, but also because after months of exhausting work, we were finally able to appreciate all the work we had done and see it come to life on the computer screen.

Even Brian Fargo, Interplay's CEO, took a copy of the game home over the weekend to play with it and gave us his feedback first thing Monday morning. It is very satisfying to see the project take shape and see the kind of enthusiastic response it triggers in literally everyone who has set his eyes on the game.
