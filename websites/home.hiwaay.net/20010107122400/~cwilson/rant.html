PAST RANTINGS

July 7, 1998

For a writer, there's nothing as inspiring as the emotional distress of ending a relationship.

Today was the first time in my life that I have ever broken up with somebody. Screw that "oh, it was a mutual thing" garbage, because it's always one person or the other who's unhappy first and starts thinking that maybe it should be over.

It is common knowledge that long-distance relationships don't work. I've given that advice to my friends for years. And then hypocritical me thinks I can be so incredibly cool that *I* can make one work. Now don't get me wrong -- I think I'm a generous and caring person in a relationship. I was very compatible with the girl I was with, and we did/do love each other.

One of my biggest fears is that I'll become one of those controlling assholes who wants to know where his girlfriend is at all times, or can manipulate her into staying in a relationship and doing things when she doesn't want to. I don't feel that it is in my nature to be that way, but the thought of becoming a harmful person is very disturbing to me. Perhaps it's because I've been in relationships with girls who wanted to be controlled, and I didn't know how to handle it.

I am an insecure person when it comes to relationships. I can't just "date" someone without having an emotional attachment (don't even get me started about the physical aspects). I have never dated more than one person at a time, and I've never been with anyone who was dating other people; I don't think I have any desire to.

There is a very fine line between insecurity and obsessiveness. I felt that my girlfriend wasn't putting aside enough time for me, and when we did get to talk, it was more about how much we wanted to be together in person rather than over the phone or the Internet. She has very little free time lately, but I wasn't even getting a fraction of that; she was always out with her friends or playing with her nephew. As terrified as I am of crossing over that line between jealousy and the need for attention, I know that in this case I did the right thing -- I let her know how I felt, and when she didn't seem to be willing to make any effort to change things, I told her I thought we shouldn't be together anymore.

Which leads to my point: Where is that line? When are you a caring boy/girlfriend and when are you a controlling asshole? So far I've been fortunate in that everything has seemed fairly clear; I can tell when I'm not being treated fairly, and I try to think things through before I react to what's going on. But what happens when the sides are a little blurry? I feel selfish for telling my girlfriend that I wanted more time from her, but I also know that it was a necessary thing for our relationship, and that it wasn't an excessive request.

But that conflict of emotions just... sucks.

Woah. First I want to apologize for not updating this page in three days. Between Christmas shopping (yah, I hate commercialism, but what ya gonna do?) and blowing off end-of-semester stress with my friends, I haven't had much time to be around the computer at all.

I wanted to mention that I bought three really incredible CD's this week. Any one of them would have been up for my Album of the Year award, had I waited a week to post my selections. But life is a harsh and cruel master, so I'll at least give them a mention here.

Since R.E.M. did all their best stuff in the 80's (at least until "New Adventures in Hi-Fi"), this is a great revisit to the less mainstream side of the band.

Front Line Assembly - "Reclamation": Yet another collection of B-sides and unrealeased mixes (why do a ton of these always come out around Christmas?). FLA is a great electro-industrial band and the music is awesome to listen to while playing Quake2 -- which is all I do lately. :-)

Anyway, none of these are actual studio releases, but they all have some major kick-ass content. Go get 'em.

December 10, 1997

I got Quake 2 yesterday, which, incidently, is why this page update is a few hours late. If you're not the least bit interested in Quake, you might as well stop reading now because it's all I'm going to be talking about today.

Okay, where to begin?

First of all, I am utterly astounded by what the ID Software team has managed to do in just one year. There are a ton of games that take several years to develop *cough*MechWarrior*cough*, and they don't even come close what ID is pounding out on a yearly basis.

That said, the biggest problem I have is that in their rush to get the game out for Christmas buying season (which is probably Activision's fault more than anyone else's), the game has a bit of an unfinished feel to it. Granted, the single-player mode is AWESOME (and as far as I've seen, almost bug-free), but their multiplayer code is awful. Let's see:

1. Many people are getting a ton of "Connection Refused" messages from servers for no reason. I've been told that this problem pops up when a player is running a 3D-enhanced version of the game while the server is not (or possibly the other way around). I had to try about a dozen times to get on my ISP's Quake 2 server last night -- and no, it wasn't full.

2. Once you do get in, the gameplay is pretty rockin'. Unfortunately, when the level ends, EVERYONE'S COMPUTER CRASHES!! Whoops, ID, did you guys forget to test something? As far as I can tell, 80-90% of the people on our deathmatch server get a system crash at the end of EVERY level.

3. There aren't any customized death messages in multiplayer! This would have taken maybe 5 minutes to program in, and as superficial as it might seem, the death messages add a lot to the game. ("Well, now we know who has the Rocket Launcher, don't we?") The thing that really bothers me is that ID coded in stuff like taunts (if you haven't been flipped the bird yet, just wait), but didn't do something as simple as different death messages. *sigh*

4. I've been getting a lot of "Overflow" messages popping up in the game, though it doesn't seem to affect gameplay. This was on a server with 6-10 players. I'd hate to see what it's like on one of those 64-player monster maps. Alas, I'm on a 28.8 so that's not going to happen anytime soon. :-(

Anyway, despite all this, I played Quake 2 until about five this morning (at which time I realized I had a class in three hours). When they fix the multiplayer code (and GameSpy gets updated), I predict that this will be THE net game for many months to come. I just felt I had to vent a little frustration that what could be a PERFECT game was limited by bugs that shouldn't have made it out the door.

And if you haven't seen Quake 2 yet, go buy it. Now. I'll wait until you get back to write another column. :-)

December 9, 1997

Today, boys and girls, we're going to talk about elitism.

I saw something rather disturbing on USEnet today. I was skimming through the newsgroup devoted to my favorite rock band, and came across a post from a newbie. It went along the lines of:

"Hi, everyone! It's nice to find a newsgroup about [band name]. I really like their album [album], and I want to buy more. Can you recommend some of their better ones?"

This in itself isn't what bothers me. The thing that really had me grinding my teeth were the fifteen or so replies from people totally slamming and trashing this guy for liking "that album".

Okay, I have absolutely no respect for people who do this. You know who they are. They're the ones who go to concerts wearing all of their band paraphanelia, yell out the names of the most obscure songs that the group recorded in their basement 15 years ago, and are just generally annoying and practically scream for a kick in the forehead.

Anyway, the new guy gets back on the newsgroup and says something along the lines of:

"Uh, I'm sorry... I'm sort of new at this and I didn't mean to offend anyone."

Next thing you know, this same bunch of "musical elitists" is blasting the guy for being new.

"Clueless newbie."
"Go back to AOL you loser."
"I've been on this group for THREE YEARS and have never seen anyone as stupid as you are."

It made me pretty ashamed to be associated with these people, as I post in the group on a semi-regular basis. Is this what USEnet has become? A bunch of self-absorbed people who flame people just because they want to feel superior to someone? A collection of people who have ceased to say anything and are content listening to themselves speak? Fuck that.

Let's face it, people. The only thing more pathetic than being elitist about music is being elitist about the net. The Internet has become a global society, and there's always going to be a few total nutsacks surfing around. But trying to act superior to someone because you've *gasp* been around longer just makes you look like you're trying to compensate for... something.

Whether it's "Ha, I was listening to this group when they were a garage band" or "I've been using a modem since the Apple 2", elitism is something that ruins a society for everyone involved. Just say no.
