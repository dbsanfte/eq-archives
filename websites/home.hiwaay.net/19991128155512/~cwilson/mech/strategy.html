MechCommander
General Strategies

This area is for general MechCommander strategies, such as combat techniques, custom mech configurations, and how to deploy mechs in different situations. There is no mission-specific information here, and no "spoilers" will be posted.

If you have a strategy or technique you'd like to see here, E-mail me and I'll look at your tip and possibly post it here...

Mech Configuration

Ammunition vs. Energy Weapons
Energy-based weapons are almost always preferable to ammunition-using weapons. Sure, that Ultra Heavy AC is going to rip apart the first few mechs you face, but halfway into the mission it will be out of ammo. Since the most difficult conflict is at the end of most missions, your mechs will be carrying around a bunch of useless, empty weapons at the most critical time.


Which Weapons are "Better"?
Which weapons are most effective when you consider weight, damage, and recycle time? Almost all of the weapons in MechCommander are very well-balanced. Here are my preferences...

  1. Clan ERPPC
  2. Clan Large Pulse Laser
  3. Inner Sphere PPC
  4. Clan Pulse Laser
  5. Inner Sphere Large Pulse Laser
The reason I prefer the PPC's to the Large Pulse lasers is that they do a larger "chunk" of damage per shot, which means the damage locations won't be as spread out on enemy mechs. Note the lack of ammo-using weapons, as the only ones I ever use are Clan LRM's and the occasional SRM (to fill in that last 3 tons of space.) Also, range usually isn't an issue, which is why I choose the PPC over the ERPPC -- I can use that extra weight for a Clan ER Laser.


Choosing the Right Weapons
Different missions call for different mech configurations. For example, on an escort mission, you may want to take more long range weapons such as ERPPC's and LRM's to handle trouble before it gets to whatever you're escorting. On defensive missions where you'll have a Mech Repair Bay, heavy autocannons are wonderful; they chew up ammo at a rediculous rate, but this isn't a problem if you can reload them after every attack wave.

In short, read your mission briefings carefully and think about what configurations will fit the theme of the mission.
Combat Tactics

How Do I Salvage a Mech?
This is by far the most frequently asked question.

There's no easy answer. You can have your units target a specific location such as the head or legs, but I have never had this work. I've had 4 heavy/assault mechs with Elite Mechwarriors unloading everything on a Commando's leg for almost 10 minutes and they never hit once. The same thing has happened every time I've targeted an armor location.

So if this doesn't work, what's the solution? Concentrate your fire on a mech until its armor is in bad shape; you can see an armor diagram of an enemy mech by holding down [i] and clicking on it. When it starts to get torn up, call all but one or two mechs off from the attack with [backspace]. With less mechs firing, they are less likely to cause enough damage to the engine to make it go critical and explode. I've had this work many times, on several different targets.

Fuel tanks and artillery strikes are a great way to damage a target when you first encounter it, but if a mech is destroyed this way it's very unlikely that you'll get any salvage. It does happen occasionally, but usually the explosion rips the mech apart. Use with caution.


Avoiding Damage
When engaging an enemy mech, keep in mind what weapons it carries. You can avoid a lot of damage by simply changing your distance to the enemy. For example, if you're fighting a Mad Cat, you'll want to get right up in its face so it can't hit you with those PPC's and LRM's. But against a Hunchback-IIC, you'll want to stay out of range of that nasty Ultra Autocannon. Of course, your own weapons must be appropriate to whatever range you've chosen.

Another good way to avoid damage is to have your units run circles around enemy mechs. Simply direct them to attack the target, and then keep ordering them to move behind it at full speed. Not only does this produce some nice back armor hits, but it makes your mechs harder to hit.


Vehicles and Mechs
Enemy vehicles are not harmless. While I will usually target mechs before I go after the supporting vehicles, this is not always the best course of action. In particular, LRM carriers can do a lot of damage from long range, and since they are so easily destroyed it's often better to hit them first. Also, those Von Luckner tanks carry a gauss rifle, which a mech of any size should be very afraid of.

Always look for the opportunity to take out the most firepower with the least amount of effort.


Use the Terrain
The landscape can sometimes take an active part in combat.

Forests and buildings will block almost all weapons except for missiles. This means that if you have LRM's and your target doesn't, you win.

Elevation can also be a huge advantage. Standing on top a hill will let you see great distances, and it seems harder to hit mechs that are at a higher elevation (I haven't confirmed this). If you've got jump-capable mechs, you can often get up on a ridge and keep some distance from enemy units. This strategy works especially well against Hunchbacks, which have to get close to you to fire the Autocannon.


Take Advantage of Your Opponent's Resources
There are automated turrets in most missions, there is almost always a turret control building nearby. Grab these whenever you get a chance -- not only will your mechs take less damage, but the turrets will now fire at enemy units. Also, capturing a mech repair bay is invaluable. There are some missions that are made ludicrously easy once you capture a repair bay. You don't get unlimited repair/reloads from them, but they do last quite a while so use them whenever you get a chance.
