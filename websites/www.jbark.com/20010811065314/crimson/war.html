The Code Of War

Raid Organization
1. Each raid will have a designated leader who is an officer or someone designated by an officer. This leader may change during the course of a raid but any changes will be announced. The leader is responsible for forming groups, designating a puller, and approving of any non-guild mates that might need to be invited to fill out a raid. The leader will also announce instructions and will be the final decision maker as to what strategy will be employed. The leader may designate the puller to announce when and where the raid moves. While anybody may make suggestions as to strategy and execution, any decisions made by the raid leader are final and not open to argumentation.

2. Each raid will have a designated Assistant Raid Leader. The primary purpose of this person is to assist the leader, not lead. If you have suggestions or think the leader has forgotten something important, this is the person to /tell. If the raid leader should go LD or die, this person will assume the leadership of the raid temporarily.

3. Each raid will have 2 officers designated as Raid Control Officers. These two people will primarily be in charge of ensuring that these policies are followed and that the Raid Leader and their assistant can concentrate on the raid itself. Should the RCO’s observe behavior that they feel is unacceptable according to this policy or any other established guild policy; the RCO’s will inform the offender of the infraction. This is a warning and is not open to debate during the raid. The RCO may or may not have time to fully explain why what you are doing is unacceptable. Additional infractions or attempting to argue with the RCO during the raid may result in your removal from the raid.

a) If you feel that they are in error you are free to tell them that you disagree and that you would like to discuss this with them when they have time. The RCO’s will determine when this will take place. It may be during a pause in the raid or it may not be until after the raid depending on when they have time.
b) After a raid you may choose to discuss any action taken by the designated RCO’s with any officer. If you feel your complaint merits further exploration, then you may request that it be brought before all of the officers.
c) Remember, being asked to leave a raid is something you bring on yourself. Even if you disagree with them, for the duration of the raid, you are expected to comply with a directive made by either of the RCO’s. Please deal with any disagreements after the raid, not during.
d) If any member is removed from any two raids during a 90-day period, the Officers and the Guild Leader will review them for dismissal from the guild.



The Code of War
1. The Raid Leader has final authority over all aspects of the raid. When and if the Raid Leader assigns certain responsibilities to other members, they receive the authority to carry out that responsibility.

2. Whenever possible, raids will be announced on the private board. Please help us meet our objective by responding whether or not you will be attending, or if you will be able to attend but will not be able to begin at the scheduled time. We will be pre-assigning groups based on this information. Please be aware that if you are unable to begin at the scheduled time, you may not be able to attend the raid at all, depending on our objective and the current status of the raid.

3. Raids are not mandatory. It is expected that RL commitments will always take precedence. However, we have engineered our numbers and class make up based on what we feel is necessary to consistently succeed. It is expected that you will attend large-scale raids whenever possible. Additionally, we ask that you make reasonable effort to plan certain RL activities, such as making dinner, around the raid schedule. Please make every effort to come early and stay as late as necessary to ensure victory.

4. On any scheduled raid, if you plan to attend, you are expected to be at the designated meeting location a minimum of 30 minutes before the raid is scheduled to begin, preferably an hour. If you will be unable to log in by the time the raid is scheduled to start then you are expected to have camped out in the zone in which the raid is taking place. If at all possible bind close to a raid site so that in the event that we need to coffin summon we only need to gather up melee classes. If you are already online and need a ride to a raid, you are expected to make yourself available at a location that is convenient for a druid or wizard to pick you up at least 1 hour before the raid is scheduled to start. It is always preferable that you log out the day before a raid in the raid zone, even if your intent is to log on hours before the raid. If you log on after the raid is scheduled to begin, and you are not in the raid zone, then, if there is still a spot for you, you are expected to find your own ride to the raid. DO NOT ask for a member on the raid to leave the raid to come get you.

5. At certain times on a raid, the raid leader, or his assistant, may announce “Let’s Get Serious”. From this point on, until the leader is finished with the task at hand, you are expected to comply with the following.
**Guild chat, say, ooc, auction, and shout are to be kept at a minimum. If it doesn’t relate to the raid, use /tell. If it does relate to the raid, still use /tell if possible and /gu or the designated raid channel if not.
a) No exploring, dueling, emoting, or non-essential afks will be tolerated at this time.
b) At all times during the raid it is expected that only the raid leader or their assistant will issue orders using the designated raid channel. If strategy is being openly discussed, feel free to offer suggestions. But if it’s not, any suggestions should be made in /tell to the raid leader or their assistant. If you offered a suggestion and the raid leader did not choose to use it, then please get behind what they did choose to do. In no way does it help for you to continue to spam the raid with what you think we should have done.

6. At no time should anybody but an officer, preferably the Guild Leader, deal with any conflict the raid might be having with another guild or others in the zone. If the other party is attempting to initiate any negative conversation with you, please refer them to an officer present on the raid. If the Guild Leader is unavailable, then the Raid Leader, or their designee will deal with any external conflicts. If the situation does not directly involve the raid, it may be passed off to another officer, leaving the Guild Leader and/or the Raid Leader free to focus on the raid.

7. Success has as much, if not more, to do with attitude as it does ability. Negative comments will not be tolerated, especially if someone makes a mistake or after a failed attempt.

8. Groups in a raid are designed to accomplish a specific purpose. Feel free to express a grouping preference when you post on the board that you will be attending, but understand that the Raid leader may not be able to accommodate your request due to the people attending and the objective at hand. Requests to not group with someone or requests made after groups have been announced will not be entertained.

9. Crimson Twilight sponsored raids are for guild members only. If you have a friend you would like to invite, ask the raid leader ahead of time to consider them for an invite. If there is not a conflict, they may be invited. Also, from time to time we will invite non-members for specific purposes, such as when additional people of a certain class are needed or to familiarize the guild with recruits

10. Upon the completion of a successful raid, an officer shall be the first to determine what items the mob dropped. This should be either the loot officer, one of the RCO’s, or the Raid Leader (if an officer). If none of them survived the raid, either another officer, or someone appointed by an officer in guild chat should be the first to inspect the loot. At no time should anybody else drop down on the mob until the loot has been inspected. Please make sure that any guests are aware of this. Additionally, it is expected that anybody who ever drops down on a mob to inspect loot does not have no-drop confirmation turned off. There is NO excuse for accidentally looting a no-drop item. If you are not included on a lotto drop roll then please refrain from making roll(s).

11. Group Leaders are there for a reason; each group leader is expected to do the following things during the raid.

a) Inform the Raid Leader of the status of your group (i.e. gtg, 50mana, not ready)
b) Master Loot all of the kills your group makes, please keep the area clear of corpses to cut down on lag. Must mention what loot drops in the present raid channel so that everyone knows what has dropped. (You may assign another to do the Master Looting)
c) Group Leaders must make sure that all in their group understand all orders given by the Raid Leader. This includes making sure your group stays with the raid when the command to move is given.
d) Announce all LD's from your group in the current raid channel.


12. All guild dwarfs must customize their helms , if they do not abide by this rule they must forfiet all loot till they do. (Glumner's note: fook you guys)

Any infraction of these rules will be met by a warning. On a second Warning you will be removed from the raid. Remember there are at least 17 other people on that raid that you are disturbing if you decide to do something fun that jeopardizes the raid, not to mention the hours that the raid leaders have spent to make the raid work. Please be considerate of their time
