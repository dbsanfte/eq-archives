The Reasonable Rogue Petition (click here for list of current supporters)
Revised 6/22/1999

The Reasonable Rogue Petition is made up of a few reasonable requests in our (the EverQuest players backing this petition) pursuit of making the rogue class the Masters of Stealth that Verant had originally intended the rogue class to be but fell suddenly short.

We are requesting completion of the advertised skills, and in-game objects to utilize these skills, as well as enhanced versions of two rather weak and inconsistent existing stealth skills. We feel that the issues as described herein inhibit rogue players from being the true Masters of Stealth as intended and advertised by Verant.

As a result of these shortcomings, rogue characters play a greatly diminished role in EverQuest's fundamental group-oriented adventure system. It has been proven time and again that rogues have little to contribute to a group of adventurers. This claim is substantiated by the fact that rogues are very rarely invited to group with other players, other than as an additional combatant, but most groups would prefer to rely on Warriors, Paladins, Rangers, and Monks for combat duties when these classes are available.

Following is an itemized list of the issues we feel warrant this petition. Each item is thoroughly assessed and includes our requested solutions that, in our opinion, would make the rogue class a viable, contributing factor to most any group if implemented. Additionally, with the proposed skill enhancements and completed skills and objects, rogue players may begin to realize the full potential of being a rogue as adverstised and described by Verant:

1. Sneak

Assessment:

A nice skill but not very useful or practical, especially when compared to Camouflage and Invisibility spells available to spellcasters as early as 4th level.

More specifically:

  • Sneak movement rate is too slow to be practical in most scenarios.
  • Sneak is only semi-effective, not allowing us to pass through an NPC's Field of Vision unseen no matter the distance—this is a significant weakness, particularly in confined settings such as dungeons.
  • Sneak should allow us rogues to slip past sentries and go places few others (without the aid of magic) can go. Thus we should be able to scout ahead for our group and search for (and disarm) traps. Essentially, we'd be utilizing several skills that make up the very backbone of the class and be doing so in the name of teamwork. As it stands now, a 4th level Enchanter technically makes a better scout than the highest level rogue in the game!

Solutions:

  • Speed it up at later levels or as the skill increases (like Swimming). Perhaps gradually getting up to normal walking speed and going all the way up to full running speed with absolute mastery of the skill.
  • Have Sneak render the rogue completely invisible (a la Invisibility spell) from behind (like it is now I assume) but also when in an NPC's/player's FoV, when beyond a certain distance. The distance could be calculated based on the rogue's Sneak skill, with higher skill allowing him or her to get closer without being seen. Thus, we're not even asking for total invisiblity all of the time, which isn't quite logical, but we should be able to get closer to PC's and NPC's than non-rogues and not be seen while Sneak is active.

2. Hide

Assessment:

Hide is a decent, but limited, skill. We believe that Hide works well enough for those times when one simply needs to sit and heal, or avoid an oncoming "train", or when one goes /afk. However, Hide becomes utterly useless with regard to various NPC's (that seem to see right through its effect) but, more importantly, as a method of escape from an angry NPC.

In light of the popularity of Rallos Zek and with the advent of a possible Race War server, Hiding should be more effective in PvP as well. Hiding is completely useless in PvP as spells and arrows continue to hit their mark regardless of whether or not the intented target is hidden.

We feel that a successfully hidden character should be removed from all targetting, friend or foe, and remain so until unhidden. Thus, spells and arrows should not automatically hit their mark if the intended target is hidden. The balancing factor here is the ability to see invisible with the aid of magic effects.

Solutions:

When we Hide we should be hidden—period. Whether before a fight, during a fight, or after a fight we should be able to hit our Hide key and vanish (assuming a successful roll) based on the rules presented below, from both PC's and NPC's. With the former we're requesting that we not get hit automatically with spells and arrows when hidden. With regard to the latter case, we're asking that Hide simply remove us from the NPC's Hate List.

As the alleged Masters of Stealth in EverQuest we scratch our heads and wonder why the rogue is one of the few classes without a viable method of escape when, in reality, escape should be second nature to one living the life of a con, assassin, thief, burglar, etc.. We'd naturally be quite good at such a thing.

Perhaps some rules could apply to this Enhanced Hide to keep it balanced and exploit-proof:

  • Cannot be used for a short period of time after taking a hit or failing a pick pocket attempt (works this way now but the point is moot since we can't hide from an NPC we're battling anyway). Perhaps give it a reset delay of 5-10 seconds or somesuch, under the pretense of a "momentary recovery of senses".
  • Enhanced Hide may only be used when/if a certain distance from an aggressive NPC can be reached. The distance should be calculated based on the character's current Hide skill and perhaps modified with regard to terrain/weather (e.g. distance needed would be reduced in heavy forest, rain, darkness, etc. while it would be increased in a barren desert)
  • Enhanced Hide removes us from the NPC's Hate List for a limited amount of time based on our Hide skill. Thus, giving us ample opportunity to high tail it out of the area before the NPC "relocates" us.
  • To curtail possible abuse in PvP, attacking will break Hide and thus make the hidden character visible again. Additionally, the Hide reset time goes into affect so hiding, attacking, and hiding again in quick succession would not be possible.

If not the above solutions then at the very least give us a method of evasion similar in effect to the Monk's Feign Death skill. Who better to be able to vanish into thin air than a master rogue?

Point is, as the Masters of Stealth, we desperately need a viable escape method.

3. Lack of locks and traps

Assessment:

Self-explanatory; simply not enough of either of these objects in the world.

Solutions:

Currently being implemented but the sooner the better. We want to start seeing shouts like "Rogue needed for dungeon expedition!"

4. Poison and related skills and/or trades

Assessment:

Self-explanatory; does not yet work.

Solutions:

Currently being implemented.

Conclusion

In summary, we rogue-class players of this petition feel EverQuest is a great game and, contrary to popular belief, actually enjoy playing our rogue characters. However, we feel that our beloved class, the Master of Stealth, is incomplete and needs some fine tuning to make us a viable and contributing factor in an adventuring group where reliance on teamwork and the applied skills of each and every member of the team is crucial to a successful career for everyone involved.

Unfortunately, we cannot begin to realize this type of group success until we have the skills and tools necessary to give groups the incentive to invite us to join them. And as one of the most group-dependent classes in the game, not being able to find a group is a serious drawback.

We also contend that there's too much emphasis (in the EverQuest community) being put on the rogue's combat effectiveness and this fact has become a basis for arguments against even reasonable petitions such as this. The rogue is not all about combat!

Only when these issues have been resolved will we be able to play a rogue in EverQuest as it (the game) was fundamentally designed to be played—as a multiplayer group-oriented role-playing adventure game.

Thank you for your attention to this matter.

Sincerely,

Hundreds of Reasonable Rogue-Class Players
