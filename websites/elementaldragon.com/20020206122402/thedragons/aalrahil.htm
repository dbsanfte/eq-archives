I have not told this tale to anyone before, but I feel it time to share it. This happened before I sought out Freeport, still on the Isle of Erudin, my home. I had reached the fifth mark, and headstrong, I rushed south, beyond the protecting bridge to the unexplored lands beyond. There I found kobolds who fell to my magic, and clattering undead who killed me, like as not. Once, as I wandered through that dark mist, I began to intone the spell of illusion, to change my shape and let me rest a bit in peace. But my fingers turned oddly, and my voice mis-spoke a word, and my spell failed. I shook my head, and began to cast again. But then something strange happened..the forest looked different, I felt dizzy, and fell to the ground stunned.
When I arose, I knew that I had summoned some different magic, some effect I did not know. And I..could hear. As I never had before. And see..the things behind what my eyes saw. I staggered about, and saw Truth. The kobolds, rough and mis-shapen, muttered to themselves the tales of their fathers, heroes and enemies, sagas that would rival that of the most ancient bard. I travelled on, amidst the trees that seemed older and wiser than any of my masters, and sang to each other of the world of old. My steps took me to a mine, guarded by powerful skeletons, who to my eyes took the shapes of men, good men bound by powers beyond their own, and seeking to end their sorrow. I heard a roar, and sought its source. The troll, Runggupp, who slew so many of my kind! Coming closer, I heard him scream of what had been done to him and his, by those of the Light, and I saw the images in my mind, and fell back, aghast and weeping.
I came upon the sea, and it seemed to speak most of all. My new perception saw the poison brine that had drowned legions of sailors, as well as the laughing spray that kissed the prow of a ship on a lover's errand. I felt that I should step into it, to seek it's embrace, but knew that to truly know the sea was to die there, to share in its mysteries was to share in its madness. So I stepped back, and fell, my mind unable to grasp any more images of such power. When I awoke, I was in a bed in the Palace, returned by another acolyte who had come across my fevered body.
I know not what conclusion to draw from this experience. I do not claim to have drawn much wisdom from it, save for the fact that there is always more, beyond our perceptions. In fact, I dread this happening again, for too much Truth can be fatal, I fear, to all save the mad. But I share my tale with you, my companions, perhaps you can see more in it than I.

Aalrahil, writing from Kelethin, on a high platform above the swaying forest-sea.
