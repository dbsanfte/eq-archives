Bringin' Back The Gods

 








 

 

 

 

History of ye Guild

As decreed by Auriana, Guildmaster of the Elemental Dragons and writer of this text

Translated into common tongue from the excerpts of "A History of the Dragon" by Rodrick

I had been doing some business in the fetid city of Oggok, and was travelling through the barren desert of Northern Ro to my home in Rivervale. The business had gone well, but I was tired. The journey had been long, lonely and dangerous. As my father had always told me, better to avoid the fight than lose it, and I had used his advice wisely (the pen is my chosen companion, not the sword). But as I rested, having just sneaked through a particularly malodorous Orc camp I noticed a curious sight in the distance.

Through the heat haze I spied a lady, or at least, what looked like one. She was kneeling, her hands clasped in front of her, eyes cast to the Gods as she murmured a hushed prayer. At first, I took her to be struck by madness and gathered my belongings to move on, but then I spied the glint of steel at her side. Being fairly knowledgeable in the ways of the desert (I have traveled much, despite my yearnings for home) I found the sight particularly unusual. I approached the lady cautiously.

As I drew near, I realized that the glint of steel was not only a gleaming bastard sword, battle worn but still sharp as a halfling's wit, but also the finest plate mail inscribed delicately by clever hands from hilt to tip. Also, a radiant helmet which lay by her side, half buried in the sand from the endless wind that gusts over the sparse landscape of Ro. It seemed that she had been there some time.

I kicked the sand from my feet, and coughed lightly to make her aware of my presence.

"What is it, halfling?"

"Rodrick Scribblerus, at your service!" I said, and bowed with my most practiced flourish. I then told her of my heading and asked if she would need a travelling companion. She didn’t answer, instead she looked at me disarmingly, till I had to avert my gaze and find something interesting at my feet.

"Very well. Lead on Rudrick."

I decided not to correct her on my name. I was already beginning to second guess my decision to approach this unsavory person. As we walked, I talked of many things. I Told her of my beautiful homeland, and of my love of all things natural. I heard the occasional grunt of acknowledgement, but she said little to encourage me. Eventually we silently agreed to continue the journey without conversation.

We traveled for many hours that way. The endless desert stretching into eternity. Not once did the lady drink, indeed, when I offered her water I detected a smile, as if such things were for those of an inferior nature. With sand rapidly matting my unkempt hair I grew agitated and, as any of you who have befriended a halfling would know - that is when we chatter. Being unable to hold my rather rapid tongue any longer I mentioned the Gods and their unusually long absence. A fairly usual topic of conversation I thought, and I do find it so interesting hearing of other’s beliefs.

The lady stopped. She looked at me in such a way that made the very hair on my feet stand on end. In her eyes, I saw a pain, danger and wisdom. I saw truth, honesty and fear. Not fear of death - I would say that this woman had faced death many times - rather fear of failure. Of what, I could not say at the time.

She began to speak, as though possessed. A deep, vacant sound that chilled me. So fast did she talk, that I barely had time to take out my parchment to jot a few notes down about this extraordinary event. It was obvious to me that she had within her the blood of the ancients, and it was that blood that spoke to me now.

Here, I end my narration, and will allow you to hear this lady's words to judge for yourselves:

The gods have left us. Lured by the trappings of gold, drink and rank, we are no longer even puppets in their eyes; rather, we are the wisps of spider silk, aimlessly floating in the breeze, lifeless, without direction, and hopelessly lost. The gods do not despise us. They do not know we exist. Do you think we are the center of their attention? You are a fool. I will tell you of a legend, passed to me from the fifth acolyte before me, and hundreds of ones before him. I will tell you a legend that has encouraged the hearts of thousands, but a legend that has crushed their spirits a thousand times more.

The legend started with a man known only as the Tailor. When the Tailor was a young shopkeep, a Stranger from a distant land visited him in his humble shop. The stranger’s skin was as black as pitch and his eyes as red as blood. He offered the Tailor a golden needle for a simple bowl of soup. Greedy for the gold, the Tailor bought the needle, gave the Stranger his soup and promptly sent him on his way.

That night, while sewing some patchwork boots, the Tailor accidentally pricked his finger on the needle. His blood ran black. He broke out in boils and fell into a feverish sleep. As he slept, he was visited by 16 violent and terrible dreams and awoke the next day violently ill. His sixteen brothers and sisters fed him broth and Jumjum for 16 nights, but on the dawn of the seventeenth, he died.

When his brothers and sisters dressed his body for burial, they noticed that his boils and scabs had fallen away from his back revealing a text written in a spidery hand. They took the Tailor’s body to a local hermit who, after 16 days and nights, translated the inscription:

The Barbarian shall be his legs

The Human shall be his feet

The Dark Elf shall be his talons

The Erudite shall be his wings

His scales will be Dwarven strong

His eyes Wood Elf keen

His chest shall be Ogre made

His shoulders Troll in strength

The Gnome shall be in his hands

The Halflings form his skill

The High Elf shall grant his mortal soul

And the Half Elf grant his hate

What does this mean, you may ask? For generations our order was confused. The sixteen siblings had formed the sect in order to publicize the miracle, but they were not sure how to interpret the miracle they had found.

Then, a man they called the Zealot came to them and interpreted the scroll (which was none other than the skin of the Tailor, little boy!) The gods were ignoring us, the Zealot said. It is time to get their attention. This scroll holds the key. Listen, he said. The gods first appeared in Norrath when Veeshan tore her talons into the ground. They then created us and left. How do we get them back? We tear the ground again. The Zealot removed some salt from his pocket and spread it on the scroll. The image of a dragon appeared. This is the Elemental Dragon, he said. When the 12 races come together and drink blood from the Traveler’s Cup, they will form into the dragon, combining their souls forever. The Elemental Dragon will then create a rift in the earth. Veeshan will become so enraged when she sees a powerful god-dragon walking the land, she will once again appear in Norrath. The gods, again jealous of Veeshan, will appear once again to restake their claim.

The Elemental Dragon will meet each one in turn. He will slay them all. Our kind will be avenged. The Zealot finished his interpretation. From that point on, our order has been divided into 12 racial factions headed by 12 acolytes. We established other councils within the group, but I will let you learn of that from the bureaucrats. Our main goal, as it has always been, is to learn more about the gods, so that we may learn how to defeat them as the Dragon. Our order is called: "The Elemental Dragon," because when the time comes, all in the order, living and dead, shall make up that terrible beast.

The lady stopped abruptly. Her eyes came back to focus. She looked at me, a curious mix of fear and excitement in her gaze.

"Did he speak to you?" she said.

"Yes. Yes, I think so." I put away my notes, and offered the stranger some water.

This time, she partook of my offer and drank deeply.When she was done, she smiled. A smile of friendship.

"If he spoke to you, he must trust you." she said, and winked.

"Erm…" I fumbled. I found this new attitude even more disturbing than the reticence. "Who, exactly, are we talking about?"

The lady grinned. "In time, friend, in time. For now, I am Auriana. And I am at your service." she bowed gracefully, her flourish brushing me back.

"Yes. Yes. Jolly good. Well…"

"Welcome to the Guild." she said.

I didn’t like the sound of that…
