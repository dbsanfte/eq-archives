Carvajal Brings Acclaimed Dance Troupe to 2002 Dance-Along Nutcracker

The new artistic director of the internationally renowned, Bay Area dance company, Dance Through Time, is none other than veteran Dance-Along Nutcracker choreographer Carolyn Carvajal, and she's bringing her new troupe to the Dance-Along this year. Performed by the San Francisco Lesbian/Gay Freedom Band under the baton of Artistic Director Jadine Louie, the Dance-Along Nutcracker kicks up its heels Saturday, December 7 (2:30 & 7 pm); and Sunday, December 8 (2:30 pm), at Yerba Buena Center for the Arts, 701 Mission Street in San Francisco. Gala Night Show with holiday reception: $40. Afternoon shows: $20 general admission/$12 children & seniors. Box office: 415/978-ARTS. Information: 415/255-1355; www.sflgfb.org.

We're excited for Carolyn in her new creative role with Dance Through Time, and we're thrilled to have them at the Dance-Along," says Louie. "This is a return appearance for Dance Through Time. They were our original feature dancers in 1997. In fact, Carolyn First appeared in the Dance-Along, as a member of the DTT troupe, dancing the beguine and the Lindy Hop."

Dance Through Time, which performs the Broadway-style feature dances this year, is an internationally acclaimed dance company committed to researching, authentically presenting and educating the public about historical social dances from the 15th century to the present. Founded more than 20 years ago by Carol Teten, the company performs year-round in concerts, special events, master classes, lecture/demonstrations and residencies. As a noted dance historian with a contemporary vision, Ms. Teten used her research and personal collection of over 700 volumes of rare dance manuscripts, journals, books and film clips to create the company's repertoire of five, flill-act productions and over twenty-five one-act shows. She also culled Dance Through Time1s 500-plus historical costumes from her private collection. Featured on a Hallmark television special a few years back, Dance Through Time's recent milestones include its 10th national tour and performances at the Palace of Versailles, France.

The 2002 Dance-Along Nutcracker marks Carvajal's sixth consecutive year with the show. Prior to being named artistic director, she toured extensively with Dance Through Time over a 12-year period, expanding her knowledge to include social dancing from 500 years ago to the present. She began her professional dance career in the San Francisco Ballet after a year as an apprentice for the Joffrey Ballet. Later, as a principal dancer with S.F. Dance Spectrum, she performed the works of Carlos Carvajal and associates with the Arthur Fiedler Pops Concerts and in dance showcases at S.F. Palace of Fine Arts, Grace Cathedral, Zellerbach Auditorium and other major Bay Area performing arts venues. Carvajal also danced with the S.F. Opera Ballet, retiring in 1998 after 29 years on the Opera House Stage. Other credits include teaching for the S.F. Girls Chorus, the Young People's Teen Musical Theater Co., the pre-ballet divisions at Shan-Yee Poon Ballet School, and dance workshops at the Embarcadero YMCA.

Pro dancers aside, though, the one singular sensation of every Dance-Along Nutcracker is its audience chorus-line. Described by the 5an Franci£co Chronicle's C.W. Nevius as "inspired foolishness, tomfoolery on a grand and witty scale," the Dance-Along Nutcracker lets the audience concoct its own choreography beneath the colored spotlights. While the Band performs Nutcracker favorites like Dance of the Sugar Plum Fairy and Waltz of the Flowers; sugar plum wannabes leap and pirouette about the dance floor. Tutu rentals and fairy wands are available for those with nothing to wear. Here, wallflowers have as much fun as dancers.
