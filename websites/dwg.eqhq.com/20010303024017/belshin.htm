Knight Commander of the Deathwatch Guard


   It was a rather mild day. The summer rains had passed last evening leaving a clean feeling throughout the forest. The animals were active, and the sun brought the welcomed warmth through the treetops. Belshin sat next to a small brook, looking around the forest. He could hear the hum of some distant wasps that had been plaguing the forest lately, but paid them no mind. There were others to take care of that. He watched the clear, cold water flow by his feet. Little fish darting about, with little visible purpose. Belshin let out a sigh. What was he doing with his life? Where is he to go now? A cry rang out through the forest. It was a voice calling a name. It was his name! He stood up and faced the direction of the voice. He saw a young Wood Elf darting though the trees. Galis. He knew this Elf, he was his mothers new little pupil. Belshin’s mother was a respected Druid in the city of Kelethin, and often had several young students to learn her art. Everyone loved her, and she was determined to teach as much as she could to anyone that would listen. Of course, Belshin never listened.

   "Galis! Over here!" Belshin shouted.  He moved toward the young boy. Galis, seeing him, waved and ran at a full sprint towards him.   "Belshin! Belshin!" He shouted out of breath.  Galis stumbled up to him and tried to talk, but was so out of breath he could just wheeze.  "Calm down little one. Just take a moment and catch your breath. I am sure whatever news you have will keep a moment."   Belshin looked around the forest, somewhat surprised that his mother would send someone after him out here. He has been keeping to himself lately, but then again he was never around his home too much. The little Elf caught his breath and started to blurt out the words, "Belshin! You must … you must get back to the house … your … your father has fallen ill and … your mother is afraid that this is the day …" Belshin did not give him a chance to finish, he darted from his spot and ran as fast as he could home.

   The city of Kelethin usually causes travelers to stop and simply stare. It is an entire city built in the great trees of the forest. It’s platforms are so high up that not even the adventurous squirrel would attempt the climb. If you think about it though, it is an excellent design. The orcs of the area are unable to challenge the city and keep their distance. Even bandits have hard times getting themselves into the city. Belshin ran up to one of the few lifts that would take him up into the city. The lift was a large platform that seemed to rise into the city by magical means. He was not sure who built or designed the lifts, but they were definitely the work of a master in the magical arts. You never know though. It could be some Gnome invention. That is unlikely since they do not frequent here from the Steamfont Mountain range, but you never know what past ruler worked out some deal with them. He stepped on the lift and moved over to the operation mechanism. He pulled the lever down and slowly rose into the city. He was very impatient. Even though these lifts were a masterwork they were still slow.

   The lift came to a halt at the loading platform and Belshin ran into the city. He dodged surprised people and darted across the many bridges that connected the platforms. People hailed him and waved but he paid them no mind. He really did not like the city or cities in general. He made his way to his home, to one of the smaller platforms that had about five homes off of it. These were actually where the druid counsel resided. His mother was an elder member and one of the leaders. He glanced to the meeting hall and saw no one. In fact, he saw no one on the platform at all. Confused he went into his home. His question to where everyone went to was immediately answered. Everyone was here, and he could hardly get in the doorway. He saw his mother near the entrance to their private room, standing like some kind of stone Golem guard. He made his way over.

   "Mother? Mother, what is going on?" He asked as he approached her. The voices in the room were subdued and quiet. "It is you father, I am afraid his illness is finally going to take him." She looked sad, yet was keeping up a strong front. It is probably her attempts to not make others worry. Belshin knew though, that deep down she was crying out to the gods. He stood next to her and said slowly, "The healers can no longer help?" "No", she replied quietly, "I am afraid that the sickness has gone past their means to keep it under control my son." Belshin looked around the room. Most were friends and fellow Druids of his mothers. There were a few of his fathers friends here, but they seemed to be uncomfortable in the present company. Two men came out of his parents private room and glanced at his mother. "Peace of Tunare be with you Cylmendra. We have done all we can." At that the two men made their way for the exit of the house. Belshin watched them go and then glanced at his mother. "I am going to go in and see him." His mother looked at him for a moment with sad eyes and then nodded. Belshin made his way into the room. Sunlight was pouring from the window to brighten up the room. A slight breeze followed the light into the room and a couple of birds could be seen just outside the window. He looked over at his father.

   His father looked terrible. He lay there in his bed with the covers pulled all the way up to his chin. His face ashen, and his breats came in short rasps.  His eyes were closed and he did not seem to notice Belshin entering the room. Belshin slowly walked towards the bed, not knowing weather to speak or not. He never had that close bond father and sons usually enjoy but he was his father. Once he got to the side of the bed he kneeled down and spoke with barely a sound, "Father? Are you awake Father?" No response. He looked around his parents room. Everything was in a neat and ordered manner. He could see his fathers belongings in their usual spot. The longbow he frequently hunted with. His father was a local guide and hunter. He did fairly well, but the family was built on his mothers success as a Druid. A sound came from his father that snapped his attention back, "Son?" His father uttered faintly. "Yes Father?" he whispered. "Son, I am surprised to see you here … on this day. How long has it been since you were last home? A few days? A few weeks? Ever since you have been old enough to take care of yourself you have been away from home. Why come back now? On this day?" "Father, don’t say such things. You know why I have come here." Belshin paused for a moment trying to think of words to say. "Do not worry my Son, I harbor no ill will towards you." His father smiled at him weakly. "Father what is to happen now?" asked Belshin. "What is to happen now? It is simple my son, I will pass into the other world. There is something that I wanted to tell you." He paused and coughed. Belshin looked at him in curiosity. "Have you ever heard of the old Elven king Talaz Dal’mah?" "No, I have never heard of the name." Belshin replied wondering to him self why he would even care of knowing this dead king. "Well, son. It has been", his father paused and took a deep breath, "it has been a few generations now, but our family has history to that King. A history I never bothered telling you. I figured you did not care, and well you probably won’t after I tell you. I know how you would rather live your life out having no responsibility to anything or anyone." Belshin feigned surprise on his face. His father let out a small chuckle. "Belshin, it is no secret how you feel. Don’t think that your parents don’t see this." He coughed and paused for a moment to catch his breath. "Now, where was I? Oh yes, the Elven king Taliz. This man was one of the greatest Elven kings to ever live. His kingdom was huge, and he was extremely wealthy. He got paranoid though. Having all this power and wealth caused him to believe that his life and the life of his closest friends and family were in danger." His father paused again to cough and to take a small drink of water from the skin next to his bed. "Well, having all the wealth and power had advantages. He used his vast resources to create an elite guard. This guard was comprised of the best warriors, magic using types, rangers, you name it there was someone in there that was considered a master of their field." His father looked out the window a moment and then back to Belshin. "This guard became the most feared guard in the land and no one would challenge them. You get the idea. Well, something happened during that time. Some angry God decided to take revenge on the kings people. The lakes and streams dried up. The air turned dry and hot. Fires became a problem and slowly the great forests and farmlands died. This land you might have heard of … the great Desert of Ro. It was there that the once great kingdom existed. Well I don’t have to tell you that the king eventually died and the great Deathwatch Guard died with him. It was the end of a great Legacy." His father paused again. He took a breath, coughed and started again. "Belshin, go over to the little shelf, the one above my bow, and take the ring there. The one with the raven inscribed on it." Belshin rose and walked over next to the bow. On the shelf sat many things. Belshin knew these were the most prized possessions of his father. Sitting there among the items was the ring he described. It was made out of silver. The entire ring was the raven. The head of the raven was the top of the ring, its eyes had small rubies inlaid in them. The beak was made to be the loop of the ring to secure it on your finger. The detail was amazing. Belshin could not think of what kind of master jeweler would make such a thing. The ring was also blackened. The only way he knew that it was silver was because the protruding areas were well worn and the silver showed through. He picked up the ring. It was unusually light. He carried it over to his father and laid it in his outstretched hand. "This, my son, once belonged to the leader of the Deathwatch Guard. Why do I have it you ask? Well, it is simple. That leader is my great grandfather." Belshin sat back a little bit in surprise. His father never mentioned this to him. "You seem surprised, but well I guess I never spoke of it." Belshin thought for a moment about his grandfather…his fathers’ father. He was a powerful warrior and belonged to the Paladin guild. He died though before he was born, and only has this knowledge from what his mother had told him. "Belshin, I have mentioned my father to you before but I never told you why or how he died. He was a warrior and lived the life of a warrior. He studied with the Clerics of Kaladim and gained some insight on how things worked. Thus, with this knowledge, he became a Paladin. He fought in many battles, but the most notable was the last Orc invasion on our city here. That is when he died. He took a fatal wound in battle, and there were no healers around to take care of him. He had spent all his energy in the fight, and could not even use his clerical knowledge on himself. I was old enough to know what was going on, but still too young to understand why he did what he did. This ring was his. His father had given it to him. My mother, your grandmother, had told me about the Guard and what the ring had stood for. I never did find out what was said when it was passed down from his father. My mother did not let me have the ring either. It was after the time she passed into the other world that I got it. I did not know what to do with it and eventually it was forgotten." He paused again, looking very tired and seeming to not breathe as deeply. "I am giving you this ring Belshin. You know what it represents and its history, it is up to you to decide what to do with it." At that his father motioned him away. Belshin stood there for a moment looking at his father, then the ring. He went to the doorway and spoke to his mother. "He is growing weaker mother, I think he is trying to sleep. You might want to go check on him…he does not seem to want me in the room right now." His mother gave him a sad look and went into the room. Much later that night, his father passed away.

   The next morning Belshin found himself sitting next to the small brook again. He was pondering what had happened to him the last couple of days, and what to do next. Sitting there, listening to the birds sing their unknown songs he watched his broken reflection on the water's surface. He tossed a small pebble into the stream and looked up. Directly across the stream from him sat a wolf. He had no idea how long that wolf had been sitting there watching him. It was the most magnificent beast he had ever seen. It was quite larger than a normal wolf, and it eyes had the look of intelligence about it. The fur on the beast was of a silver gray color and it gleamed with an almost holy light. The wolf simply sat there staring at him. "What do you want?" He called out to it a bit nervously. "I have come to speak with you my child", the wolf replied. It was a female voice, and seemed to be all around. The wolf’s mouth never moved. The voice was almost like a melody of sound that even the most talented Bard could not match. "Why would something as magnificent as you want to speak with me?" Belshin simply had a feeling of awe about him, he could not understand why this creature wanted to talk to him. The wolf paused and considered Belshin sitting there. She seemed to be waiting, but then he heard her voice, "You have learned about your destiny my child. You have learned about your heritage from your father. There was more to it though. More to the story he told you." She looked around at the animals. Suprisingly the animals seemed to not be frightened by the presence of such a large predator. They actually seemed to be closer to her than before. "What you mortals call the Deathwatch Guard was actually a request of mine to the one known as Taliz. I had asked him to put together the most noble of the land and take up the fight against the evil that seemed to abound. There was a plague of evil during that time, and the good people needed something to look up to. They needed the comfort of knowing there were people out there willing to challenge and destroy the evil. This was my request to Taliz because I knew he could make it happen." She paused in her talking and looked at the stream flowing by, the little fish darting in and out of the small rocks on the bottom. Belshin simply sat there and listened. He had no idea what this creature wanted, but it was obviously old … and not of this mortal world. The wolfs attention came back to Belshin and her voice was once again heard in his mind. "Belshin, I want you to take up where Taliz left off. I want you to put together the strongest and most noble people of the land and take up the fight against evil. Something big is happening. The evil races are making bold moves against the land. The people living on it are living in fear. I charge you with the responsibility of this task, to return that safety once felt by those people." The wolf looked to the sky, at the sun shining down from between the massive trees of the forest. Belshin looked around and noticed that beasts of all sort had gathered around the wolf in a semi-circle. Silently they watched this meeting. Bears, deer, wolves, birds of all types and colors … all types of creatures from the forest. Belshin could not believe his eyes, to see so many hunters and their prey coexisting in one spot with no fear or desire to pounce. Something else bothered him. There was little sound. The birds were not singing, and nothing seemed to move. Every animal seemed to be focused on the conversation. Could they understand what was going on? Could they hear her voice? He looked back at the silver wolf. She sat there watching him, as if waiting to speak again to not interrupt his thoughts. "Belshin", the wolf spoke with that musical voice again, "Will you accept this task? Will you do as I ask?" Belshin sat back for a moment and looked at the sky. He thought about his life, as short as it has been and what he has accomplished to this point. He thought about his mother, and his father. He looked to the wolf and answered simply, "Yes." The wolf seemed pleased and smiled, if a wolf could smile. "Then my child…consider yourself as an Avatar … an a warrior of Tunare." With that the wolf turned and bounded past all the animals, disappearing before Belshin could utter another word. The animals started to wonder off, still ignoring their primal instincts to hunt and flee. Belshin sat there a little stunned. "Was that Tunare?" He asked himself. Slowly standing Belshin turned towards his home and began slowly walking…
