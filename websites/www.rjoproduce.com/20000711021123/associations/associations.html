Since 1901, Produce Reporter Company has been the leader in providing the domestic and international fresh produce and exempt transportation industries with timely, accurate, and reliable credit and marketing information. Produce suppliers, buyers, brokers,and transporters alike rely on Blue Book information to make safe, informed, and profitable business decisions.

Trading Memebership is an honor service. It represents certification by Produce Reporter Company and is available only to the best firms in the industry. To be a Trading Member, your company must be financially sound and have an exceptional reputation for honest, liberal trading policies and payment practices
The Red Book's BCA (Business Character Award) logo and plaque are awarded annually without charge to members that establish and maintain a reputation of high ethical trading practices, business competence and financial stability. Performance is reviewed annually or more frequently if warranted by trade reports. A firm that fails to meet the standards outlined here is ineligible for the award
PMA, founded in 1949, is a not-for-profit trade association serving 2,500 members who market fresh fruits, vegetables, and floral products worldwide. Its members are involved in the production, distribution, retail, and foodservice sectors of the industry. The association's mission is to create a favorable, responsible environment that advances the marketing of produce and floral products and services for North American buyers and sellers and their international partners
RJO is also a member of the President's MClub. Through a generous contribution, the President's MClub allows PMA to continue its efforts in the areas of food safety and industry research.
RJO is a strong advocate of the national program "5 a Day - for Better Health!" 5 a day is a national program sponsored by the Produce for Better Health Foundation and the National Cancer Institute to improve the health of Americans. 5 a Day aims to increase adult consumption of fruits and vegetables from the current average of 3.5 daily servings to at least five servings a day by the year 2000 in an effort to decrease cancer and chronic disease.
