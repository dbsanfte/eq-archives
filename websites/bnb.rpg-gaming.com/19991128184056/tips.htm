That "green bar" is more a meter of sorts to show how your connection to the game is. The outside numbers relate to a ping. The inside numbers are the percentage of packet loss being experienced. When packet loss starts to climb, the middle numbers will turn yellow. The higher it gets, red follows. If it stays red too long you'll either get "server not responding" or "disconnected". It doesn't HAVE to be on the screen, but was put there by developers so you could keep track of how good you're doing communication wise.

      Tips on Archery:

    9. This is a method to max your archery skill each level. Buy 1 or 2 tiny daggers (a spell component available from most magic shops). These tiny daggers only do 1hp damage but use your archery skill. Now go find groups who are hunting things that you find challenging to hunt. (it is only polite to let them know what you are doing so tell the group you are going to 'help' a bit. Have faith, you cannot steal a kill when you do 1hp damage per hit). When the group attacks a monster jump in and attack it. By doing so, you will get experience with archery without the heavy expense of buying or making a lot of arrows and the daggers don't disappear.

      Tips on Tailoring:

    10. A very valuable item that is difficult to make is the backpack. Unlike the ones you buy from a vendor, the backpacks you make have 10 slots instead of 8. This may not sound like a lot but, look at your bank and think what the extra slots can mean to storing things.

      Tips on Weapon usage:

    11. Many consider dual wield to be a weak skill compared to two handed weapons. This is NOT true. Whereas you do less damage per hit, you strike faster (lower delay) and have a better chance to hit at least 1 time with 2 weapons rather than 1 weapon. With a two handed sword or halberd someone may do more damage than me per hit but I will hit more often and, in long fights, I will tend to do more damage than they will. Also, missing or getting a low damage hit can be very rough on a two handed weapon person vs. someone wielding 2 one handed weapons.

      Newbie Rangers and Armor:

    12. This tip works better for elf Rangers in Greater Faydark. A trick I found starting out was to find a tailor and make a deal with them to exchange 2 or 3 pelts for each piece of armor they would make for me. This can be tough but, some tailors are willing to make this deal, which works well for both parties. You get the experience from hunting and they get the skill increases from the wolf hides (the one I made my deal with was a druid who also cast spells on me so I could hunt better. I was able to equip my Ranger in full patchwork armor before 5th level).

      Tips on starting your Ranger:

    13. A nice tip for new people of ANY class is to ask higher level folks passing by to enhance you with spells. Most high level folks don't mind doing this and the enhancements can really make a difference to a starting player. When I get a new level I will often swing by hunting grounds where low level folk hunt and cast my spells on them to improve my skills with magic before going off to hunt again. Skin Like Rock adds about 60-70hp to a player and increases their armor class plus Feet Like Cat gives a nice 20 agility boost to them, increasing their armor class and letting them run faster. Such boosts to starting characters can allow them to fight longer and pick tougher things to fight and, if you are lucky enough to get a 30th level cleric to enhance you, the bonuses they can give are spectacular.

      Tips on using spells:

    14. One misconception about spells is that spells of higher level are more powerful than those of lower level. This is NOT true. I have used Snare on things that Grasping Roots would not work on. YOUR level and skill means more than the spell level. If you wish to look at spells and effects think of them as your level, minus the level you got the spell equals the effective level of the spell so, a 9th level spell cast by a 20th level Ranger is cast at 11 levels of effect. A 15th level spell cast by the same ranger is cast at 5 levels of effect so more things will resist it than the lower level spell.

      Flame Lick

    15. This spell is misunderstood by many Rangers. They think it is a direct damage spell. It is not. Flame Lick is like poison. When you cast it on a creature it slowly leaches away their hit points. It is an effective spell to use for drawing things to you (pulling).

      Snare

    16. Even at my level I keep Snare memorized. Snare has many advantages over Grasping Roots in many situations. If you have a rooted monster, it will fight to the death. If you snare a creature, when it tries to run, it runs slowly and, when it is almost dead, it will still try to run even when it cannot. With some larger creatures this can save you and your party well over 100hp of damage. Also, due to the lower cost in mana of casting it plus the higher chance of the monster not resisting it, you can slow or stop a fleeing injured monster from getting help and, if it does resist, you can cast it again for less than casting Grasping Roots.

    Hopefully this will help other rangers.

    Tastes Great, Less Filling!

    This might seem silly to some, and outright idiotic to others, but, recently, I have discovered the advantages to becoming a drunk. I was taking the long boat ride from Freeport to Butcher, when I noticed this Halfling wobbling around, and talking in some funny language. I asked him, for the benefit of all, if he could switch his language around to common. His reply was, "Sheesh, I abtalbin covvon," and he proceeded to hand me six short beers. I, being the adventurous type I am, quaffed the brew down. I noticed some very interesting things. First off, everything began to sway back and forth. This, I thought, was not good. I also noticed that my strength and stamina went up, one for each beer I drank. This could be to my advantage, I thought. Me and my new found drinking companion began talking. Being drunk, I understood him as well as any could. He informed me that he his skill in alcohol tolerance was up to 90, which meant he could drink about 4 or 5 beers, with no negative effects, but, he still gained the 4 or 5 points to his strength and endurance. When first drinking, you will lose intel points dramatically. But, on the long boat ride, I managed to sober up before I reached Butcher, and, brought my alcohol tolerance up to 7 in the process. This isn't something I'd suggest to a low level running out into Qeynos hills and trying to kill bats, but, for someone with some time to kill, say, on a boat, this is an ideal way to get a new experience, and, maybe in the long run develop a skill that could be very benificial. These skills that seem almost comical in their initial development, might one day save your hide. How many times have you come from a battle with 4 health left, saying, dang that was close? About as many times as you know you would have lived if you could have gotten one more hit in. We are Rangers, and, no skill is too trivial to be of use. We make the best of what we have, so, go out, get drunk, have a good time, and soon enough, you will enjoy the benefits of it..

    Ranger Tip

    I would like to submit a tip for Rangers that really helped me through my middle to upper teen levels. I always recommend a group for hunting, however there will be many times when you find yourself in the solo mode. When I was soloing I would always use this tactic to help improve my chances of slaying a tough blue or a weaker yellow monster. I would hit them with grasping roots from a distance and once they were rooted begin to shoot them with my bow or cast burst of flame on them in order to wear down their hit points. I would do this from the very edge of my range so that when grasping roots wore off, I had a chance of casting it again on them before they closed for melee range. ALWAYS snare your opponent when going solo. Actually get in the habit of snaring every creature you engage in battle. There have been many times that my life was saved because my opponent was snared and I could outrun them to safety. Also, as a ranger my advice would be to practice your fletching early on so that you can partake in the Trueshot Longbow quest as soon as you are able. This bow is a great weapon for the solo ranger, and when combined with more effective arrows, is a deadly long range weapon. Also, I would advise staying with one handed weapons for the majority of your early to 17th level career. I was a dedicated 2-Handed slashing ranger, until I began to see the benefit of dual wielding as I approached 17th level. When I made 17th level I had to make a switch in weapon style/type that put me in the doghouse for days... I also noticed that when I went to 1-Handed slashing weapons I was more effective against the casters because I was able to hit them quicker and keep them from getting spells off. When I was in Crushbone there were numerous times when a weak blue Orc Oracle would really tear me down before I was able to kill it, because he would be getting all his spells off against me in between the swings of my two handed blade. I am always happy to help out a fellow ranger. If you are on Solusek Ro, send me a tell. Good hunting and may Tunare watch over your paths!

    Keeping Your Weight Down



    As was said earlier weight is a big problem if you are a wood elf. My own strength is only 70 so every stone counts bigtime! If you rightclick on your common backpack you will find out that it weighs 3 stones. Not much, but if you carry 4 or 5 of them (as I do) its 12 or even 15 stones! Quite a lot, but there is a way around that. Most vendors who sell molds that are used for blacksmithing will sell you something called a spit. Its actually a miniature oven that you can use to prepare food in, like in a regular oven. It has 6 slots and weighs only 0.2 stones!!! This is one fifteenth of the backpack weight! Even the largest piece of equpment will fit in this container and you can also prepare food inside!

    More Amor For Your Bucks



    Starting Rangers, as with everyone else, will be lacking in funds and armor. One way to help get rid of this is to run up to CB just North of Kelethen. Yes i know you can't hurt anything there yet.. BUT you can ask for CB Belts and Legionere shoulderpads, if possible pay 2 gold for them. WHY?? because they are a QUEST item that will get you experience, MORE money, and ARMOR; not to mention an occasional rusty item. If i remember right its Padded armor which is quite a step up from the cloth when you start out, and selling it is more profitable than selling the belts and pads. I suggest filling up several backpacks with the belts if you can putting them in the bank and then running over to the warriors guild in the Dwarf city. i'm sure you can figure the rest out. But in a little time you should be able to get all the armor you need till you move to leather and Banded. Also ask around in the Dwarf W. Guild i hear rumors of a Quest for Troll heads that when delivered can get you blackend iron armor, the boots of wich can be used to kick creatures that can only be hit by magic.

    1HS Vs 2HS



    I have read below a number of comments lauding the benifits of 1hs vs 2hs. Even comments including such as " seeing the error of my ways " relating to low skill in 1hs when reaching lvl 17 and dual wield.

    Which is better ? I tell you clearly that niether is better. Right up thru lvl 17 I kept both skills maxed. I fear I must admit that since 17 I have let 2hs slip a bit but will correct that soon.

    In a well balanced group each member has an assigned task. A tank must be able to dole out enough damage to keep the enemy focused on him. For this a good 2h weapon is needed. At 22 I have both dual weild and double attack and if tanking is the job of the day my BIB is the tool for the job. I tell you clear 28 pt dmg per hit x 2 with double attach gets am mobs attention quick .If I am teamed with a warrior or a paladin they get that job, both are more effient tanks than this wood elf ranger and I happily switch back to my shortsword of morin and silvery ax. the right tool for the job is the key.

    As a final comment, fear not a low skill lvl with a given tool. I was able to max out both dual wield and double attack within the first 2 bubs of receiving them. Your skill with alternate weapons will rise equally fast. Again with a good group backing me I have never heard a complaint of the time invested in skill gain.

    Good Hunting and Stay Safe!
