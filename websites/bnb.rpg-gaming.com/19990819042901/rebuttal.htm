A Rebuttal To The Killing Of Wolves And Bears


I wrote an editorial dealing with the killing of wolves and bears by Rangers since I get alot of e-mail asking if it's ok or not ok. This seems to be a popular topic, so I decided to publish different points of view other than mine so that all points can be expressed and commented on. The e-mail's I recieved below by fellow Rangers are just a few. If you want to express your thoughts on the subject, send it in to be published so it can be shared with others.

Greetings Dalvarian!

I thoroughly enjoy your newspaper Blade n' Bow. Of particular interest to me recently was your article about the hunting of wolves and bears outside of Surefalls Glade. While I completely agree with you that to hunt the bears of the Glade is sheer folly and utterly rude, I disagree with your allegation that hunting the wolves and bears in the Qeynos Hills area is alright.

I'm a Ranger of more than the 10th circle of ability. To date, I've killed exactly one - and only one - wolf. I've never slain a bear, or been in a group that slays bears or wolves. It simply goes against my beliefs. I agree that as hunters, we have a need to hunt the animals for the items we need to sustain our lives. But what do we as Half Elfs, Humans, or Wood Elves receive from the slaying of either wolves or bears? Food can be foraged with no difficulty where ever a Ranger roams, so there is no need for the little meat they may provide. The pelts from these creatures produces armor uitable only for the smaller races, and for the Barbarians - respectively. The only suitable pelts for producing medium sized patchwork armor comes rom the ruined cat pelts one can obtain by hunting the lions of the Karana lains. It is because these great cats have the required pelts I need to armor and clothe my person, that I can justify the killing of these beasts in Tunare's eyes. While I agree that all creatures are special under Tunare's will, there are justifiable reasons for the hunting and killing of some of these wonderful creations. I have advanced in my circle of skills quite nicely without ever needing to harm one of Tunare's wolves or bears, thank you kindly.

So I therefore submit that the slaying of wolves and bears by Rangers is wrong, when viewed from the "kill for necessities" standpoint. The only other reason one might hunt and kill one of these creatures is for the experience one gains. This is utterly wrong, and a direct sacrilege of Tunare's wisdom and guidance! While it may not in fact harm your standing within the guild of Rangers, how can a true Ranger ever condone the slaying of one of these animals when there is no genuine necessity to do so? And many's the time I saw a non-Ranger greet Hager Sureshot or Larsk Juton only to be rebuked as a "Despised Killer of wolves and bears!" While I can stand nearby with a clear conscience, knowing that I am indeed following the wisdom of Tunare to the best of my meager abilities. And if, in fact, killing these beasts is not frowned upon by the Guild of Rangers. How do you explain Holly Windstalker's and Cros Treewind's summary executions of anyone they see attacking a wolf or bear in the Qeynos Hills area?

There may be many a Ranger that disagrees with my philosophy. But I wanted to share this viewpoint with you before many new and untried Rangers took to the hills of my birth and began to slay these creatures without remorse or pity, because they hadn't an opportunity to hear an opposing viewpoint.

Tunare guide and protect you,
Kel'anth of Fraterni Lux
Fenin Ro


I read the rebuttal and was somewhat taken aback by the poor unfortunate who wrote it. As a wood elf Ranger, My guild holds no such notion of hunting for clothes (armor) to be prohibited. Only you of the human and half elf stock seem to see these creatures of nature as being sacrosanct and hold prohibitions on their hunting. Why do you think the great cats are fine to hunt and the wolves and bears are not so? All living things are from Tunare (and it is Tunaria not Antonica) and as such, by your logic, no living thing should be harmed by a Ranger. Not the wolves, bears, lions, bats, rats, snakes, crocodiles, etc... Nothing living should ever be hunted by a Ranger.

Wolves are very plentiful in my homelands and hunting them for armor never struck me as any form of a crime against Tunare. I have killed few bears in all my travels (mostly to save others from foolhardy attacks or to put a poor diseased beast out of its misery when it attacked me) but I see nothing wrong with it. I do follow the customs of the lands I visit where applicable (no I do not hunt halflings when in the dark elf forests nor do I hunt the protected beasts of the hills. A preserve is a fine idea and Rangers should honor such areas set aside for this purpose).

Just a few thoughts on the notion of sacred beasts from a Ranger who travels extensively.

Zepheloth Truseeker
27th level Ranger

Dear Dalvarian,

I have read several articles touching upon the moral and ethical issue of what to hunt and what not to hunt. This was recently spurred by the question of whether Rangers should or shouldn't hunt wolves (especially in Surefall Glade where both wolves and bears are off limits). Well, as long as we are on the topic I thought I would give it my copper's worth.

Personally, I don't hunt wolves of any color. I will only kill one if it attacks me first which has not happened yet, and I have literally run into them. I bought my patchwork armor cheap from someone who didn't need it anymore, so hunting for the pelt is a non-issue with me. Hunting wolves for food is out of the question since there is plenty of succulent rabbit, fish, roots, berries, and fruit out there to be foraged. So, I don't have a problem with saying that I don't see a need to hunt wolves, but let us move into the larger question of hunting other beings.

Would anyone really kill a unicorn, or a fay drake? They are sacred animals! I would not bother a fairy of any kind, they certainly don't bother me. And, I refuse to kill any more Pixies either. I have brought Lady Ran Surefire two pouches full of pixie dust (one of a young Ranger's Quests) all for a big "thank you, we'll remember you". I have not seen the Pixies start any forest fires lately, and…I kind of like them…they are spunky. Plus, I have not seen them initiate an attack on anybody except once when I saw them attack a dwarf that was hacking away at a wolf. Uhmm…maybe we should think a little before we start killing everything that moves. For myself, I will reserve my blade for any evil and vile thing that happens to cross my path.

May Tunare guide you and bless you all.

Treena Starleaf, Wood Elf Ranger of Kelethin
