DCoE Raid Guide


 		  1. Raid Policy

     We raid for fun, camaraderie, and for a sense of accomplishment at conquering challenging dungeons and monsters. Do NOT expect to get any experience on a raid. Do not expect to win any rolls for loot on a raid. Expect to get killed many times. Expect to sit around a lot waiting for group assignments, buffs, or rezzes. We raid to get better armor and weapons to to make the guild stronger. With a stronger guild we are able to take on bigger and meaner mobs. Never fear, if we get an item once, we will get it again. The goal is to have the entire guild well equipped.

     Listen carefully to your raid leader and your group leader. Follow all instructions from these individuals immediately. Respond to tells for buffs or assistance immediately. Stick with your raiding party, do NOT run ahead, fall behind, go wandering off, or go /AFK without permission. Do not complain or be disruptive during the raid.

2. Raid Preparation

A. Raiders Responsibilities

     - Guild raiders and guest raiders should sign up for the raid in the raid forum. This allows raid planners to gauge the strength of the raiding party and saves time with group assignments at the beginning of the raid. A forum is set up for this use, so use it. Also please indicate the name, level and class of the character you are bringing to the raid. If the raid will be in an area that magic resists will be extremely important, please list what your unbuffed resists are.

     - All raiders should familiarize themselves with the zone to be raided. People are expected to have either personal knowledge of the route, or maps to and from the raid zone in, as well as a map of the raid zone. You must be prepared to lead a group to the zone in or find it yourself. Don’t expect the Druid or Wizard that ported you there to guide you. Most of the time they are needed elsewhere to port others. Maps can be obtained from EQAtlas.com or EQMaps.com.

     - Casters who require spell components (clerics, chanters, shimmies, etc.) should come with at least one stack of their required components. Other classes are encouraged to bring gems in case any casters forgot to stock up. Gem cost will be reimbursed at the end of the raid.

     - Be sure to take your resist gear from the bank before hand.

     - Have your settings the way you need them before the raid begins. If computer performance is a problem (also known as lag), turn your particle effects off (/showspelleffects off). Go to your options and turn 3d sky off, dynamic lights off, luclin models off, server side filtering off, etc.) See Appendix A for DCoE’s official raid game settings for those that have lag issues. Also the Lag Reduction guide below.

     - Put all or most of your ‘battle spam” options off, if possible, or choose to put them in a different chat window. IE: others hits, your misses, peoples spell casting, etc. You need to see the orders you will have to follow. Noticing that the average hit of gnome warriors is 5 more than trolls, is useless. Why? Because at the moment you were reading that, you missed the orders to “BACK OFF!” and your mob got pushed off a cliff. And now its summoning you with it. Can we say Loading, please wait…..

     - Prepare your hotkeys beforehand. (Please see the reference section below for examples of hotkeys).


B. Forming Groups and Buffing – Raiders responsibility

     - Don’t mess around when gathering for a raid. Come to a druid ring/wizard ring ASAP, and gather in the designated place.

     - One of the most important things you can do is BE ON TIME! You should be at the raid meeting spot 15-30 minutes ahead of time. If you might not have time to run to sight on the day of the raid, then camp your character there the night before. This allows for faster grouping and the sooner we get going the sooner we will get drops.

     - As soon as you arrive at the designated meeting spot, turn on /LFG and take off /Roleplay and /Anon. This is done by typing /LFG ON.

     - The group leader is responsible for buffing, not the raid leader. If you need a key buff that your group does not have, tell your group leader. He will then /tell the appropriate caster. DO NOT ask in the raid channel for the buff. You will be ignored. You must follow the procedure. Be sure the group buffs itself with enough crappy buffs (so that dispelling mobs) will dispel those first. If you are a caster with group buffs, be sure the group is gathered in the same spot. To target another group and buff them, type /TGB on. This turns your target group buffs on. You do not need to be invited into the group you can stand where you are and cast the buff on one of the targets in the group.

     - For casters that are responsible for buffing, it is best to TIME your buffs. This is done by casting the buff on yourself and then casting it on everyone else. Usually your buff will start to blink when it is wearing off and you know that it is time to start recasting. This will also save on others screaming for their key buffs.

     - Don’t ask for unnecessary buffs. Not everyone needs the same buffs, and not everyone needs symbol, rune, etc. If your are doing your job only certain people need these buffs as no one else should be getting hit and the gems cost money. Don’t go playing or running around during buffing, just be patient and wait in your spot.

     - Do not ask questions to your raid leader, unless you are a group leader. Ask them in your group or in /say. The group leaders usually know what’s going on and can help you. Don’t hesitate to ask questions, if you think they are important, or if this is your first raid. You won’t learn unless you ask. Just be sure not to ask the raid leader the question.

     - Listen to every word your raid leader shouts. Keep the raid channel clear. Raid leaders, main debuffers (slowers, mezzers, etc., should be stating what they are doing in the raid channel only. Do as you are told as quick as you can. Do not bug the raid leader with tells. (Can you see a trend forming?)

     - Try and keep your chat channels to a minimum to reduce spamming. You won’t be able to see orders if your too busy chatting in 10 channels. With the new chat channels open, many people even turn off guildchat when they are raiding.

     - Do not ask for specific mobs or loot. Do NOT expect loot, you might get something nice but probably not. Be glad someone got something, people will remember that you helped them out, and will return the favor at a later date.

3. The raid process

     - There will be a minimum of 1 Main Assist (MA) and Secondary Assist (SA) and hopefully Alternate Assist (AA). Pullers will bring mob/mobs to camp and the MA will engage main target and SA and AA will engage adds if non-mezz-able/charm-able and kill them until the MA’s mob is dead. All players should stay on the MA’s target. The only time they should switch is if the MA calls for a switch.

     - The MA gains Agro (and positions mob) and will call for melee’s to assist. All raiders should have a macro with /assist (MA’s name) and hit it after MA calls assist. Only the MA should be using taunt. (See hotkeys below). When the MA calls for melee assist the designated debuffers should cast slow/tash/cripple depending on availability. It is highly recommended that you have a main snarer. This person will be designated. The snarer should not cast snare until the mob is below 80 percent. This allows the MA to gain agro. The MA will give a second assist call for casters to nuke or he will give the caster instructions in his/her first call. IE: All tanks assist me NOW! Casters start nuking in 5 seconds.

     - Mob Positioning – This is very important. The MA will be the only player in front of the mob. All other melees shoud be behind or on the side of the mob at max melee distance to avoid riposte and rampage damage. (Hint: look at where pets are relative to mob, pets almost always melee from max distance to mob.) If you get agro turn off attack and stay in place until the MA regains agro. All pets should be shrunk to ease in targeting. All large races (Ogre, Troll, Barb, Vah Shir) should be shrunk.

     - Information on Pullers – Preferably have FD monks. Do not heal the puller unless the puller says it is okay. The puller may still have agro after FD and by healing the puller you have now gotten agro they retained after FD (because FD did not perform a mem-wipe on the mobs.)

     - Always use assist, and have attack assist off. (See hotkeys to set this up.) If the main tank calls for a switch, do it, as fast as you can. If you don’t have an SA, then someone should root it.

     - Only the main tank (MA) ever breaks a mez. The main assist will announce very clear what the main target is. Everyone else should assist him. Do not attack a mezzed mob until the MA calls assist. If you are the MA and you are attacking a mezzed mob, be sure to taunt him a few times before actually attacking. This will cause the mob to whack you and not the enchanter that mezzed him. You can check if the mob is taunted enough by /assisting the mob and seeing if he targets you.

     - In general do not use DOTs in raids on regular mobs. Sometimes people need to switch targets mid fight. When you use a dot this means the enchanter dies because the mob was unable to be mezzed and since the enchanter was the first one the mobs hate list, he/she dies instead of you. These mobs usually die far too quickly for the spells to be effective.

     - If you are attacking a mob that summons, be sure to have someone stunning him so it will break his spell casting. Clerics can now bash with their shield, don’t be afraid to use it. If the mob gates, be ready to have the entire raid party camp. The RL or MA will call the order for camp. Do this immediately; otherwise he will summon you one by one until you are all dead.

     - If you are not a cleric but can heal, be sure to save enough mana to effectively backup the clerics. If you are a druid you should also save enough mana to succor the party out. Each Cleric on the raid needs to create a hotkey that sends a "LOM - Mana Dump now" tell to a Necromancer on the raid. To complement this, each Necromancer needs to create a hot button that does the following:
     /rt -> This useful comment targets the person that just sent you a tell
     /cast Mana Dump

     - Necromancers should save 40-60% of their mana for MD, and use the rest to DD.

     - Don’t be afraid to use your special abilities. Lay on hands has saved many tanks, clerics, and enchanters in dangerous situations. By saving them you might save the entire raid. At level 30, warriors now get the ability to shield a target and absorb up to 75% of the damage their target would have taken. With this in mind, low level warriors can be used in a dual role as a "Defensive Line" for Clerics and Enchanters (each cleric/enchanter can have a warrior assigned to them for this purpose). Since warriors need to use a shield for this to be most effective, they can also be used to bash / stun mobs that may cast or try and gate out. The lowest level tanks can be used to form this "defensive line" as they are unable to gain agro at the rate a higher level would be able to.

     - Do not sit when people have an incoming target, the mob may agro you. Stand up as soon as soon as you see the incoming message.

     - When you get aggroed, invis drops, or you pull too many, die away from the raid force. You can be dragged or summoned, or rezzed, and new break in because of a total wipe is only fun for the mobs. You will spend a long time waiting for that CR, instead of waiting a few minutes to get that rez.

     - You should not go /AFK for more than 5 minutes UNLESS it is a designated break for all, at which time a time limit will be set. If you go /AFK for more than 5 minutes, then you risk being left behind. In too many places, the tactic is clear and move. If 30 people have to wait 20 minutes for you to come back, they will not be too happy. Besides, your are jeopardizing the whole raid and can cause a wipe.

     - If you make a mistake, admit it, apologize and move on. Be sure to learn from the mistake, and don’t do it again.

Class Specific Issues Recap

     Warriors - ONLY use taunt when you are the main assist. It's that simple. If you find yourself getting hate when you're not the main assist, then stop attacking and back off slowly until the mob has been back on the main for a few seconds. Only the main assist should break mezzes. Make a hotkey that says "/shout Taunting %t". Hit this hotkey, and then taunt the mob 3 times. Have another hotkey that says "/shout Breaking mez on %t". Use this, and then attack the mob. Doing this will save your enchanter's life and your cleric's mana.

     Paladins - See warriors above. Refrain from flashing or stunning the mob unless you have to (example: The mob is healing/gating, etc). Remember that mobs really hate being stunned and blinded and WILL start hitting you, so be careful. Only root for crowd control and to keep a mob from running or force aggro. When you lay hands on someone, expect that mob to come after you with a vengeance.

     Shadow Knights - See warriors above. Don't tap or dot at the beginning of the fight! Wait until the mob has taken some melee damage before you start firing these spells. Don't chain cast either, always wait a few seconds between casts. If your pet is up, make sure you have him on a leash, and that he is only attacking what he is supposed to be attacking. If he's not where he's supposed to be, kill him. Don't ask, don't think, don't use /pet back off, kill him. The enchanter or the necromancer will take care of any adds. Remember that harm touch doesn't start fights - it finishes them. Never open a fight with a harm touch, save it for when the mob is under 30 percent health.

     Rangers - See warriors above. You DO NOT want to be getting hit. Yes, you have pretty chain armor. Guess what, it won't do you much good. Your job is to be beating the snot out of the mob while it beats the snot out of one of those plate mail-wearing fools. Always let the main assist pound on the mob for a moment before you jump in, to make sure he has its attention. If you find yourself getting aggro during the fight, stop attacking and back off or JOLT. We know you like to be in the thick of it, but there are other, safer ways to damage the mob. In open areas, back off and use your bow. You do have a bow, right? Rangers do more damage with bows than any other class, so use the darn thing. In close quarters, like in dungeons, use your spells. Only cast when you are not in direct melee with the mob, because it will decide it doesn't like you otherwise. Make sure you are snaring mobs that try to run.

     Rogues - Like rangers, you don't want to be getting hit. Try and stay behind the mob so you can do the most damage, but keep in mind that when you land that huge backstab the mob is probably going to be quite upset with you. If necessary, stop fighting for a moment and step back to let the tanks build up some hate.

     Monks - You remember that feign key? It's not just for pulling. If you find you have the aggro in a fight, feign. And stay feigned for a moment while the tanks build up some hate. The mob should never be hitting you, no matter how wonderful a twink you are - leave that role to the folks with lots of HP and AC.

     Beastlords - Pet control! Be ready to kill your pet if he gets out of hand. You're in the same boat as the monk, only you don't get to feign, so if you get aggro, stop fighting and back off while the tanks regain it.

     Bards - That shiny plate looking armor you get is just for show. Besides, the mob hitting it might scratch the finish. If you find yourself with aggro, stop fighting and back off. When in doubt, twist songs rather than melee.

     Clerics - Try not to open a fight with a complete heal. If your puller or tank is in trouble early in the fight, hit them with a celestial line heal and then follow up with a standard or complete heal. Remember that chain heals will severely tick off the mob while eating away at your mana, so try to be as efficient as possible in healing.

     Druids - Don't dot mobs unless the main tank is on them. Don't start fights with nukes. Remember to snare mobs that try to run. Only use roots for crowd control and not for actual combat (I don't care how much damage they do). When healing, try not to chain heal (I know it's hard).

     Shamans - See druids above. You have some nasty dots; so know when NOT to use them.

     Wizards - Don't chain nuke. Never, ever, ever chain nuke. You will quickly rise to the top of the hate list and be a very dead wizard. Don't over nuke - conserve mana. A good idea is never to blow more than 30% of your mana pool on one mob, unless it's a boss-type mob. Don't use AE spells when your enchanter is mezzing, or in close quarters where they could aggro something else.

     Magicians - See wizards above. Also, pet control. Keep that pet leashed at all times. If your pet won't listen, kill it. Don't back it off, don't try to pull it back to you, just kill it. A lot of raid leaders don't even allow pets on their raids, and the reason that happens is bad pet control. Don't allow your pet to justify their reasoning. Know the pathing of your zone, and once you have that summon companion spell, never ever have your pet follow you. It doesn't matter how cool your pet is, it is expendable, and be ready to kill it at a moment's notice.

     Enchanters - When in doubt, stun mobs before mezzing them. At the same time, announce (loudly) that you are mezzing that mob so that anyone on it can get off of it. See wizards above for advice on nuking. Remember your primary duty in a fight is crowd control. Don't blow your mana pool on one mob, and remember there are times when it may actually be better not to mez.

     Necromancers - See the sections above for advice on nuking, casting, etc. Remember pet control, just like magicians. Watch your dots like druids and shamans. And follow the advice for wizards on nuking. When fighting big mobs, NEVER use a spell that turns them into a skeleton. This causes most of the tanks to stop hitting the mob, which will cause problems. It doesn't matter how good the spell is don't use it.

     - The key to aggro control is know when to use your abilities and when not to. Remember that you are part of a team, and that not every ability or spell is appropriate in every, or even in most situations.

General Agro Reduction Methods

    Archery:

     - As a general rule archery will not steal aggro from the MA, so it is perfectly acceptable to shoot arrows before the call ONLY if certain conditions are met:

          1) You wait until MA has complete aggro, could be 10 seconds… could be 30. Wait.
          2) You are out of the MoBs Melee range, preferably some distance away and behind.
          3) The MoB is NOT above 60th Level.
          4) If MA is having trouble controlling the MoB, stop firing until MA has regained complete control (ie MoB goes after the cleric).
          5) If MA says stop firing arrows, stop firing arrows. The MA has a better feel to how much control they have over the MoB.

     - Remember you don't have to fire an arrow every time it refreshes... pause and watch the situation, make sure MA still has complete aggro.

     - Situations where you should NOT fire arrows before the call or should stop firing all together:

  • On raids with MoBs that we have never killed before or very powerful MoBs (the stakes are high): Wait until the assist message is called. If you don't and the MA goes down then you are 2nd on the aggro list. The MoB then will run to you, usually at max bow distance, and you will go down faster than the MA.
  • The MoB starts bouncing from player to player until a tank can re-establishes aggro. This will usually lead to a wipe.
  • If the mob stuns or slows the MA then you can gain aggro. All Rangers should have the spell Jolt mem’d, this is what we use for de-aggro. But if the MoB is level 60 or higher it is always resisted.
  • If you do manage to take aggro away from the MA in a raid then try to determine the reason for it. Learn from your mistakes. Then adjust your strategy.

The MA rule is:
- Use Beg/Taunt/Bash/Kick/Slam/Etc every single time they refresh. Stay as close as possible to the mob and in front of it at all times.

The melee rule is:
- Don’t use Beg, don’t sit down, and stay at MAXIMUM melee range not in front of the mob at all times. If you should get aggro, turn off attack immediately and use FD/Evade/Jolt etc to help. The 10 or 15 seconds of damage you don’t do isn’t going to kill the raid but if you continue attacking it just might cause a wipe.

The caster rule is:
- Wait for the mob to lose 25% life. Walk into melee range right behind the main tank and nuke then walk backwards out of casting range. Sit for at least 15 seconds minimum before walking back into melee range and nuking again. This is a hard fast rule. It is likely you will never ever get any aggro using this rule and will do significant damage to the MoB without costing your cleric any mana.

4. Loot Distribution

Essence Point System
=================================================

DCoE uses a point system for loot distribution. This point system has one goal, to reward people who give time and effort to the guild as a whole. All workings of this system have been designed with that one goal. We will call the points people earn Essence Points or EPs for short.

Basically people will earn EPs based on attendance at guild events and helping with organize guild events. Simple as that.

Once people have EPs what do they do with them? Well items on raids will be either awarded (in the case of quest items) based on EPs or the will be auctioned off at end of raid to be paid for with EPs. More details on that later.

When people attend guild events they will earn EPs. If something happens and they can not attend the entire event they will earn partial EPs. Here is the list of events and what EPs are earned for each one.
________________________________________________________

  • Key Guild Event (This would be a major event designed to benefit guild as a whole or a event which requires long time commitment. i.e. planes and weekend raids)
    • Full Attendance 150
    • Partial Attendance 40 EPs
  • Guild Organized Epic Nights
    • Full Attendance 50
    • Partial Attendance 20 EPs
  • Organized Weeknight Event - must be on forums for a week before event date
    • Full Attendance 100
    • Partial Attendance 20 EPs
  • Leading/Organizing any of the above events
    • 50 EPs (see rules below)
  • Mobilizing to Kill a Mob
    • 50 EPs /(no partial)

To qualify for EPs each event will be assigned a start time and stop time. One of the leaders of the event will do a /who at start time and take note of the names. Then do a /who at end time and take note of names. Those who appear on both list get the Full Attendance Eps. Those who appear on one list get Partial Attendance points.

To qualify for EPs reward any event must have a minimum attendance of 6 members.

Leading Event - To qualify and get points for leading event the following must apply:

  • Posted in message boards for over a week
  • min 6 people show up ( this includes leader)
  • The event must be logged from start time to finish time. Which must include a /who at the start of the event and a /who at the conclusion of the event.
  • Pix must be taken for guild news board
  • Also if multiple people lead a event they all split the 50 EPs

Reporting Essence Points Earned
=================================================

Someone who is approved by the guild leadership must report the earning of EPs to a thread that will be created for such a purpose. At the moment only officers are allowed to award EPs.

Also we want to give people points to work with as soon as they get in guild. People will get more points if they sign in on the guild roster at Guildmagic. We will also award points for creating a Magelo profile, this must be linked to Guildmagic account though. Btw Guildmagic and Magelo accounts are free, it just takes a small portion of peoples time to set up and maintain the accounts. We also believe if people decide to devote the time it takes to be a officer then they should be awarded points as well.

One Time Essence Points Awards
=================================================

  • Invited to guild - 50 Eps ( You have to sign up on Guildmagic to guarantee getting these EPs )
  • Have a Magelo Char profile - 50 EPs
  • Have a GuidlMagic Profile - 50 EPs
  • Offered and Accepted Role as officer - 100 EPs

Essence Points Spent
=================================================

Ok, you have been attending events and got some EPs, now what do you do with them?? The main thing you will use EPs is for auctioned items from guild events. At the end of each event, there will be an auction for all tradeable items. All bids will start at 50 EPs and go from there.

Also guild time/raids to help people with quest items will cost EPs. If people want the guild to raid somewhere so they can get quest items it can be arranged, but will cost EPs.

  • Bid for item - All bids starting Cost 50 EPs and are up for auction there after.
  • Assigned no drop/quest item - Cost 50EPs (should they accept item of course)
  • Epic/ Quest Help - Cost 100 EPs ( If multiple people can arrange both of their items on same event then they can share the cost of the EPs )

    Rules for items availability on guild raids
  • Epic items - go to the person who can use for epic that is farthermost along on their epic.
  • No Drop - Officers/Event Leader will decide who gets it based on EPs
  • Auctions - To be able to participate in item auction you must be able to use it. Please use common sense, if the item drops that you can equip but will not use we ask you to refrain from bidding on it.
  • You must have attended a minimum of 5 raids before a BP/Robe can be awarded and will be awarded by the Guild Leader or Officer in charge of the raid. Receiving another item will not eliminate a person from receiving a BP/Robe.

Essence Points Removed
=================================================

Someone who breaks the guild code of conduct can loose essence points determined by officers. This penalty must be approved by the majority of the officers.

This loot policy is subject to change as deemed necessary by the guild and its officers

5. REFERENCES

Lag Reduction

  1. Turn OFF Luclin Models.
  2. Turn ON serverfilter by typing /serverfilter on.
  3. Turn OFF unneeded chat channels.
  4. Turn OFF other’s hits and misses.
  5. Turn OFF damage shields.
  6. Turn OFF other player’s spell casting.
  7. Turn OFF NPC spell casting.
  8. Turn OFF bard songs, unless you are in group and not getting much lag, and just want them on.
  9. Go into your c:/program files/everquest/folder (or wherever you have EQ installed) and open the file named “datarate.txt.”. The value defaults to 5/0. This value controls how fast the server can send you data. If you set it too high for your connection, you can flood your connections and be send LD. Too low, and you will have casting/attack delay. The table below shows how high it should be set for your given connection:

28.8 = 3.1
33.6 = 3.9
56k = 6.0
Single Channel ISDN = 9.0
Double Channel ISDN = 15.0
Fast Cable or DSL = 20.0 (or higher)

This change can help tremendously for people on broadband connections. Please, if you have cable or DSL (good service) there is no excuse to have connection lag, this trick helps a lot. No more casting delay, no more dying to enrage because you haven’t shut off attack.

Making Hotkeys

  1. Limit group macros to needed communication. Saying you are going to damage or attack a MOB or cast a buff is irrelevant. Saying you are going to root or fear it, however, is something everyone needs to know. If you are using DoT’s it may be helpful to announce this, especially if you have an enchanter in the group. Using DoT’s makes a mob unmezzable.
  2. Note that only one of the macros say that you succeeded. This is because failure happens on all the rest. The purpose of the macro is to announce your intent so everyone can react accordingly.
  3. On all attack-based macros, make sure you have an additional macro saying that you succeeded!!! It does not work well to say you are going to root/fear/snare/mezz a mob and then not tell anyone when you have succeeded.
