Biography

A clockwork midwife was the first thing I saw when I was born into this world. That perfectly measured rhythm of work done well was the first thing I heard. I was the only child of Gimble and Helia Tinkerdun, the greatest parents a gnome could ever ask for.

My father was a distinguished member of the Eldrich Collective. Though he dabbled in Wizardry, his greatest talent and his most well-remembered legacy was his skill with a toolbox. It seemed he could build anything for any occasion. As a direct descendant of Gimblestan it was a tradition of his line to do so. Most people knew him as the designer of the famed Guides of Ak'Anon who keep tourists from bumbling around trying to find their way. My mother was a merchant of the great works of art she tailored daily. We weren't the richest family in Ak'Anon, but I never went a day without wearing something truly grand. She was a loving mother and a lovely wife to my father. But I've never had the best of luck in my life and that's why nothing so great could last very long.

My father always wanted me to follow in his footsteps while improving the path along the way. Though he only dabbled in magic he had higher hopes for me. Though all the masters of the Library of Mechanamagica were too busy to apprentice me themselves, my father wouldn't let the magic he knew dwelled in me die.

For my 18th birthday, as tradition deemed in Ak'Anon among magic-using families, I received my very own spellbook. I was overjoyed, until I flipped through it realizing it was empty! But that was also when my father gave me my first spell, Minor Shielding. It was a simple beginner's spell of protection, but I cherished it and immediately wanted to know how to scribe it into my spellbook. My father had worked secretly over many months, but his efforts were heavily praised when he revealed my very own clockwork teacher.

This wonderful work of programming and craftsmanship couldn't actually use the magic it was to teach me, but it was able to tell me how I could. I ran with it into my room where it taught me for hours on the basics of scribing and then the actual usage of the skill of abjuration. My father had let the contraption sit and record many of the teachings of the greatest wizards in Ak'Anon over an extended period of time. So the thing had much to share as the lessons poured out in the coming months.

I learned all I could about every verbal and somatic gesture known to the Eldrich. I even learned about the seemingly foreign use of the divine sorcery known to clerics, druids, and shamans. The only thing that bothered me was the fact that I still only had one spell to practice with. My father claimed that was all I needed and the law of Ak'Anon has always held that you can't purchase spells till you're 21 years of age.

That was when I found the spell. I was walking near the Abbey of Deep Musing looking at the great falls when I slipped and fell into the water. Growing up by Lake Tildunk of course I knew how to swim, so instead of getting upset and running home to dry off, I just kept swimming. I was nearing the Great Wheel when suddenly I noticed something at the bottom. I held my breath and retrieved what I then saw was a small spell scroll.

After I had it I quickly surfaced and swam for the shore. I opened the scroll with delight only to find it was incredibly faded from it's stay in the water. I couldn't make out the name at the top, but I thought I could make out the rest with some work. I ran home and went straight to my room to the bewilderment of my parents.

As soon as I closed the door I started up my teacher and he assisted me in scribing it all down. I knew I couldn't let my father know I had it though. I wasn't sure why, but I just knew I had to hold on to it. I had found it, therefore it was mine now and I didn't want anyone to argue about it. But this meant I couldn't very well use it either, at least not anytime very soon. So I tried to visualize the gestures it entailed and figure out what the result would be.

The only part that seemed vaguely familiar was a healing gesture. But unlike a normal cleric healing spell it had no gesture to beseech any specific deity. It simply drew the healing powers from some other source, which is the part of the spell I couldn't understand. After weeks of trying to figure it out I simply gave up and decided to forget about it till I had a chance to try it out.

By my 20th birthday I had learned a couple things about my father, or at least I thought I had. For one thing, there were numerous rumors about a growing organization in Ak'Anon that followed the dark god Bertoxxulous. No one knew where they were centered, but people were getting scared. People started accusing each other of being members. So of course, the more well known you were, the more chances you had of being accused. Since my father was very well known (while at the same time being a relatively private man) many rumors were abound of his supposed involvement.

Another reason for this is that as any tinkerer has to do at least occasionally, my father frequently had to make trips to the Mines of Malfunction to dispose of defective clockworks. But another rumor held that this is also where the new organization was based. Of course many other rumors told of them being based in places like the Bank, somewhere in the Steamfont, and even below Lake Tildunk. But people believe whatever suits them.

This first really became evident one day when my mother decided to visit the local grocer and he refused to sell to her. He claimed he simply couldn't be known as a supporter of the Dark Reflection. She was outraged when she got home, it was the first real fight I saw my parents have. They were still arguing when I fell asleep.

A week after that incident, my mother found we would have to get the pelts she needed for her tailoring efforts ourselves since the ranger who used to deliver to her suddenly stopped showing up. My father decided we would all go on an expedition ourselves into the Faydark to procure the wolf pelts she needed.

We bought provisions from a dear friend of my father's who would still sell to us in secret and set out on our journey. On our way out, the guards didn't even look at us as we went by. But I didn't even care since I was so awed by the Steamfont Mountains.

My parents were very protective (as are most gnomish parents) and didn't just let me go running out on my own into the wilderness. I had only seen glimpses of the mountains by simply peeking out of the tunnel that led out of the city. But those glimpses had always been wonderful. So I was just as awed upon going out among them.

We followed the well traveled paths and kept wary of the wandering animals, including the local kobolds. But we just weren't prepared for the sudden surge of fire that ignited my mother. She screamed in pain and terror as she collapsed and we turned to see the drake that breathed on her. As I ran to help her my father let loose a short volley of spells at the creature. The beast was slain in a matter of minutes, after which my father joined me at my mother's side.

It was obvious in moments she wouldn't survive without immediate care. But we had no cleric with us, and neither me nor my father knew anything about using a bandage (that is if we had had one with us). That was when I remembered the spell I had been faithfully memorizing for a year now. And if my father asked about it, the fact that I saved my mother's life would make up for any previous secrecy.

I made the gestures and said the words as I oriented myself towards my mother. My father was silent till I made the finally movements when my father shouted and ran towards me, but it was too late. I watched in horror as my mother screamed one last cry and went slack. At the same time, the minor burns I had received from the edges of the fire disappeared from my own body. I finally understood the spell, at the cost of my mother's life. I'll never forget the following lamentation of my father, not only at my mother's death, but at my own hand in it. Even after his voice was gone from screaming her name and wailing across the mountain, he rasped and cried for the rest of the night.

The following morning we journeyed back to Ak'Anon for the funeral preparations. The tradition at the time consisted of a cremation of the body, then the releasing of the remains by the person most close to the deceased into one of the local steam vents to be shot up into the sky for all to see. Not a single person attended the ceremony, my father and I were the only mourners.

That night my father was a wreck, he just kept mumbling something about the plague finally catching up to him. I couldn't understand it as he sure didn't seem sick at the time. That is, until he made an unscheduled visit to the Mines of Malfunction. Upon his return he really did seem sick. He died that night in his sleep.

I was alone. I lost both of my parents in less than 48 hours. I had nowhere to go, nothing to do. I was contemplating my own suicide, walking along the shores of Lake Tildunk, when suddenly I bumped into someone. At the time I assumed I had simply been too distracted and didn't wonder why I hadn't noticed him.

But when I did look up to see who I had run into, I was confused indeed. He was twice my size and clad entirely in black, making his blue, seemingly elven features blend into his robes. But until then I had never heard of such a race. I had seen my share of elves pass by, from the tanned, even wood colored shades of wood elves, to the most pale, white-faced high elf. In fact, I couldn't recall any creatures that were blue except the local crushbone orcs (who only rarely reached Ak'Anon on their raids).

The man looked down and smiled as he handed me a small slip of paper while saying, "I saw you in the Steamfont, boy. You have a great talent within you waiting to be released. I can see it just behind your eyes, it calls to you. Do anything but fight it. Visit the man known as Eonis Mournunder and seek the ways of True Magik. You have nothing to loose, nothing else to live for. Listen to me, boy. You have marvelous potential, I'd hate to see it go to waste." His words were followed by a flash of shadows passing before my eyes and he was gone.

I looked at the piece of paper in my hand. It was a guild invitation. I was to find Eonis Mournunder and join the Dark Reflection in the new art of necromancy. I had nothing better to do, so I journeyed to the most likely place for it to be.

I could hear the sound of murmuring and spell casting immediately upon entering the Basement (the more common name I learned for the Mines of Malfunction). So it didn't take long to follow the sounds and come to the place of the Dark Reflection. Eonis Mournunder was a strange man indeed, but he welcomed me heartily and gave me my apprentice's robe. The robe looked just as common as any of the other magic user's robes in Ak'Anon, but fellow members of the Dark Reflection would be able to recognize me easily.

Over the following years I learned the dark art of necromancy and the awesome powers it unleashed within me. I took regular journeys out into the mountains purely to prove my worth and practice my skills against the creatures that roamed there. Though this also proved to be profitable as the Dark Reflection scare faded in Ak'Anon (both from people disbelieving the rumors and from others simply joining the organization) and merchants would begin to warily sell to me.

But it was around this same time I purchased one of my first truly necromantic spells, Cavorting Bones, my first pet spell. I ran outside and started casting it as soon as I had it scribed. Unfortunately, the spell failed every time I tried it. I couldn't believe that I had copied the spell incorrectly but I went over it anyway. I then read that it required a bone chip to cast, something I simply didn't have yet. But I did remember that plenty of skeletons ran around in the basement.

So I ran down to my guild and found the nearest pile of bones to begin casting on. I had only started the gestures when Eonis interrupted me saying, "Please, do not slay one of your undead brethren. They cause no harm to you and we use them often to practice our skills on. Find the bones of another species to harvest for your new spell usage." I was stunned at this. The dead had already proven themselves unworthy in life (otherwise they'd still be alive), so why show them mercy in the unlife. But I've always trusted my guildmaster, so I followed his advice.

I now had a dilemma though, where in the Steamfont would I find the bones of another species? The answer I realized was nowhere. I wasn't going to find anything but gnomish bones locally. It was then that I remembered the elven bard who kept the mail going just outside the bank. I ran to up to her and hailed her asking, "Do elves die?"

She laughed at me saying, "Well of course we die, doesn't everybody?" So the next step in my journey of life would be to go to the land of elves and find the bones of their dead for my own usage. I journeyed day and then night through the forest of Faydark, looking for the unliving bones of an elf. But before I found one of those, I happened upon a smaller version of something I had only seen rarely enough already, a crushbone orc. The funny blue thing strutted through the forest carrying a small ax in one hand, and a piece of meat in the other. I watched as it passed until it suddenly turned to me and laughed.

"What are you supposed to be?" the young thing asked in halting common.
"What do you mean what? I'm a gnome, what of it?" I replied.
"Well you look funny, that's what. If I didn't already have this meat for dinner, I'd have to kill you and eat you right now."

Now it was quite obvious this dumb creature had no experience in dealing with gnomes, let alone a necromancer. Just from looking at it I knew I could probably take it with only moderate effort. Now I knew from my studies in the basement that these creatures were associated with many groups in various ways. If for instance I were to kill one I might seriously effect different peoples' views of me. So at the time I had no desire to kill the darned thing and I said just that.

"Oh, you think you could take me, huh, little man?" the creature said, dropping his dinner, "Well let's just find out then, have at it!"

I had no choice but to slay it post haste. My only rewards were the ax and the cash it had dropped. Unfortunately, whatever kind of meat it had been holding had been taken by some birds in mid-fight. But from this experience (and many later similar experiences), I was able to come up with a surname for myself (since my old one died with the parents who gave it to me): Foolsbane. I then journeyed on, eventually finding a nice decaying skeleton. I was then able to jump-start my true necromantic career.
