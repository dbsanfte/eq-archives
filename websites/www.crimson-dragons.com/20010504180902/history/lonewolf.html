To The Lone Wolves

  

By VanZant Bravesword
————————
Greetings to Whoever Might Find This Journal,

As I sat in my room alone this morning, I stared at the painting of the wolf that hangs on my wall just above my desk. It is a black wolf, with a thick and well worn coat. His head is hung low with his sharp, yellow eyes looking forward. He plods along through the snow, looking determined, yet somehow seeming lost.

They are funny animals, wolves. The most interesting thing about them is that they hunt in packs. They hunt together, eat together, sleep together and even fight together. When one wolf is injured, members of the pack stay behind to protect it. When a new wolf is born, the entire pack takes part in the rearing of the young pup. A pack, in a way, is much like a family.

Sometimes, for some reason or another, one wolf sees fit to break off from the pack. Maybe that wolf became lost, or just didn't feel like sharing the company of the other wolves anymore. Being the "Lone Wolf" seems appealing to many. The Lone Wolf, completely free from the binding ties of the pack, requires no help from others. The Lone Wolf takes care of its self. It passes through life without having to make those sacrifices to the pack that the other wolves must endure.

The wolves in the pack must share their space with each other to keep warm and safe on the long, winter nights... but the Lone Wolf is free to spend those evenings of blistering wind and gusting snow on its own. The wolves in the pack must hunt as a group, taking down large prey and sharing the food... but the Lone Wolf is free to hunt on his own, stealing chickens from nearby farms and digging through garbage for scraps of meat.

When a pack of wolves is seen running through the forrest, they are respected by all other creatures. The Lone Wolf is content to survive on its own, hiding in the shadows and keeping its distance from those who would do it harm. The pack, strong and ferocious in numbers, is safe from the hunters. The Lone Wolf, must be strong enough to live its life as a target... as a single animal with no others to help keep it safe.

The Lone Wolf may take up residence with wild dogs from time to time, maybe working together to hunt for much needed food. The Lone Wolf has no obligation to these dogs however... not like the wolves who travel in the pack. In turn, these dogs have no obligation to the wolf. Should they desire, killing the Lone Wolf and taking its share of the meat would not be out of the question. Alas... this is the price that the strong, independent "Lone Wolf" must pay to be free from the constraints and conformities of the pack.

I rocked back in my chair, and contemplated this Lone Wolf for a while. I don't identify with this wolf. Crimson Dragons is my pack... and together we have always triumphed over any obstacles in our path. The lone wolves in the world of Norrath never make it very far, always meeting some ill fate that could have been avoided if they had been willing to seek help. Perhaps the Lone Wolf is stronger, but one can never truly be stronger than an entire pack. I pity this wolf, who has forsaken the pack. I mark myself a fortunate man, for I will never have to be alone... not like the "Lone Wolf."

Comforting Thoughts on a Cold Morning,
VanZant BraveSword
