Zinbik's Guide to Necromancy

As you walk down the hallway, you have an odd feeling about yourself. It’s almost a mix between giddiness and shear terror. You pass a large scroll on the wall. In large lettering, in what you immediately recognize as Gnomish, then in Common in smaller lettering, it reads: PLEASE, no druids or paladins beyond this point. Their auras disturb the delicate miracles I perform here for my research. Also, no pets (charmed or otherwise), animals, or light contagions. Large races must be shrunk before going further. Do not disturb any experimentation in progress.

After a short hesitation, you enter the workshop. You shiver slightly at the chill temperature maintained here. The air is filled with a musty, mildewy smell which you aren’t sure if you want to identify. One wall is covered by a huge contraption covered in a light frost, making a continuous hissing noise. The wall to your right has shelves full of various sized glass containers. A sign above them reads (once again in Gnomish first, then in Common): Virulent Diseases and Contagions - Handle with EXTREME care! You WILL be held liable upon breakage. The wall to your left is covered in shelves filled with new and ancient texts. Books, scrolls, and papyruses are organized as neatly as possible to avoid any damage.

It’s the table in the center, though, that truly catches your eye. A dead aviak in an advanced state of decay is chained tightly down. After moving closer to inspect it you realize that it’s not dead at all. It writhes slowly as if in pain for a few seconds, then stops. The moment it does, a frustrated cry from behind lets you know you’re not alone. You quickly turn around to see Zinbik who’s now screaming at you, “Can’t you read Common?! It took me months to bring unlife into this specimen and you run up to it like a curious child and remind it of the natural cycle of life and death. Aviaks don’t cling to life like a normal humanoid. They… oh what would you know anyway?”

He sighs and pulls a lever on the contraption you noticed earlier. After a great deal of noise, part of it begins to open up. A great, freezing mist falls out and reveals a compartment about the size of the body on the table. The frustrated gnome proceeds to lift up one side of the top (removable) portion of the table, then stops and looks at you expectantly. Trying to avoid angering him any more you run to the other side and lift. The two of you then proceed to lift up the body (still chained to the slab you’re carrying it on), carry it to the contraption, and slide it into the compartment. With the pull of a second lever it closes back up and continues it’s hissing.

“So why do you disturb me in the first place?” Zinbik asks you with some annoyance. Your response lets loose a great deal of laughter from the necromancer. After regaining control over himself he amusingly responds, “You want to know of the Dark Arts? You want to enter the fold and learn some necromancy? Well, I suppose you’ve came to the right place. I don’t claim to be an expert but I’ve mastered quite a few of the skills involved. I’ve also amassed plenty of information on the subject.”

He waves you over to two small chairs in a corner of the room. You each take a seat as he continues. “Well, where should I start? Ah, I know: race. There are five races known to practice necromancy: Gnomes, Dark Elves, Erudites, Humans, and the Iksar. Some may have their opinions as to who makes the best necromancers, but the truth is, a master is a master. If you could choose the race you were born with, it’d simply be a matter of taste and the way you plan on living your life. Let me first discuss Humans.

“Humans start out as blind as a bat and not much smarter. Both human cities have local necromancer infestations, but their styles differ greatly. So close to Neriak, the Dismal Rage of Freeport follows Innoruuk. Far from such influences though, the Blood Sabers of Qeynos have learned to follow Bertoxxulous. Either way, human necromancers are generally forced to live out life trying to get people to overcome their initial hatred of them. Otherwise they must stick to the shadows when sneaking through dangerous territory and travel far to sell their collected wares.

OOC: Humans lack any gift of sight and their starting stats are…average. But this is just as viable a race as any other. At level 8 you’ll get the spell Deadeye (infravision and see invisible) which you’ll have to keep active in order to see decently until you get to 24, when you get Shadow Sight (ultravision). Intelligence-increasing items will be what you fill your inventory with to get rid of your initial stupidity. Factions are tricky with humans. They start out with a lot of enemies, but they won’t be as KOS as a Dark Elf, for instance. Humans are supposed to level faster, but Verant won’t validate this. It’s all a matter of choice and play-style. But this is probably best suited for someone who wants to start out in one of these specific cities or for role-playing purposes.

Default Stats: Agi: 75, Cha: 75, Dex: 85, Int: 85, Sta: 75, Str: 75, Wis: 75, 30 points for allocation. Suggested point placement would probably be 25 (the maximum) points in Int and 5 points in Agi.

“Erudites start out just as blind, but at least they’re smart old boys from the start! Also, their native city of Paineel is a necromancer paradise. Founded by necromancers, maintained by necromancers, frequented by necromancers, this town is great. If it weren’t out in the middle of nowhere (Odus) I’d visit more often. Erudites start out with plenty of local enemies. Also, you’re gonna have to wait till you can use the spell Gather Shadows or make a nice friend out of a passing druid or wizard if you want to leave this dinky little island. Luckily there’s creatures enough to entertain you until then. I also must admit that these guys look the best in a Robe of Enshroudment, but that’s just my opinion.

OOC: Erudites also lack any gift of sight but at least they start out pretty smart. They aren’t much in many other stats, but hey, you can take care of that later. Since you start out that smart you won’t need nearly as many intelligence-increasing items. This means you can worry about stuff like strength (to carry more loot with). Faction is tricky with these guys. It’s either a lot of mana spent on invisibility or a lot of time spent fixing factions. I’ve heard that these guys get some sort of experience penalty but that’s just hearsay. This seems best suited for someone who really appreciates Paineel, wants to start with a nice foothold in intelligence, or doesn’t want to care about faction at all. This is because plenty of people aren’t willing to spend much time fixing factions. So once you’re at this point you might as well enjoy the privilege of killing whatever you want. You’ll always be welcome in one city, despite it’s remote location.

Default Stats: Agi: 70, Cha: 70, Dex: 80, Int: 117, Sta: 70, Str: 60, Wis: 83, 30 points for allocation. For point placement I’d suggest 15 points in Int, 10 points in Agi, and 5 points in Str.

“Dark Elves tend to be known as the most common race for necromancers. Native to the grand city of Neriak, they are the children and avid followers of Innoruuk. As members of the most respected profession for their race, they’re more than welcome in their home town. They’re even appreciated by those stupid ogres and trolls. Of course this means that anyone with the slightest idea of goodness will instantly wish for your death. But hey, these guys look GREAT in a blighted robe!”

OOC: This is the one race who doesn’t have to wait till 24 for Ultravision. They start out with pretty nice stats too. Neriak is also a great town with a great location. The only problem (if you want to call it that) is faction. Most use this to their advantage. After all, this means you can kill whatever you want. You’ll be spending a lot of times hidden in the shadows though. And you either have to always have a friend to sell for you or be willing to make some long treks for the nearest friendly vendor. This race is best suited for someone who wants to start out powerful, kill whatever they please, and/or role play a typical arch-villain.

Default Stats: Agi: 90, Cha: 60, Dex: 85, Int: 109, Sta: 65, Str: 60, Wis: 83, 30 points for allocation. Suggested point placement is 25 points in Int and 5 points in Agi.

Zinbik leans back in his chair and grins at you, “If you want to see a real necromancer though, find a gnome. We aren’t so consumed by hate or fear to have a good time. The politics of Ak’Anon are a bit tricky to understand for an outsider, but our brethren don’t hate us of the Dark Reflection. They merely find some distaste in our company. Steamfont is also the best place I can think of to start practicing your powers. The only problem is tricky faction. You have a choice: hanging in the shadows or taking some time to get people to like you. Not many people will ever love you, but it won’t take much work just to get them to let you live. It’ll save you mana, time, and careless deaths in the future.”

OOC: They start with infravision, decent stats, and a nice starting spot (Faydwer is great for the beginning levels, though technically you could stay here forever). Like Erudites they play the faction game. With a couple dead Crushbone Orcs, RunneyEye Goblins, and Highpass Orcs, most of the people that hated you will at least let you pass. The problems are A.) Verant doesn’t seem to care for them. There isn’t a single quest to be had for the Dark Reflection. And B.) Some people don’t respect the smallest race in Norrath (being them or looking down at them). This race is best suited for those who love starting on Faydwer, want a nice average-starting necromancer, or for role playing reasons.

Default Stats: Agi: 85, Cha: 60, Dex: 95, Int: 108, Sta: 70, Str: 60, Wis: 67, 30 points for allocation. Suggested point placement is 25 points in Int and 5 points in Agi.

“Finally, we come to the Iksar. I must say, these guys have some incredible inborn abilities. They’re excellent swimmers from childhood, they regenerate as fast as trolls (a great advantage when it comes to the Dark Pact spell line), they can forage an endless supply of food, and they possess infravision. Other than that though, they start out as dumb as humans do and only their own people will accept their presence. This excludes them from countless quests and makes hunting on three entire continents an extreme challenge. A member of the Brood of Kotiz spends almost their entire life lurking in the shadows.”

OOC: Excellent starting abilities, all your spells in a single zone, and some decent stats (besides intelligence) make these guys at least decent. But that lack of intelligence, inability to so much as enter the Temple of Solusek Ro, and the fact that you can’t easily leave Kunark makes things really difficult. Expect your divination skills to be constantly maxed since you’re only ever safe in Cabalis. And if you want all those nice non-Kunark items, you better start saving your platinum or preparing a deadly trek across the world. This race is best suited to someone who truly wants a challenge. Even dark elves can get around semi-decently in two continents. And finally, Iksar look really weird in robes.

Default Stats: Agi: 90, Cha: 55, Dex: 95, Int: 85, Sta: 70, Str: 70, Wis: 80, 30 points for allocation. Suggested point placement is 25 points in Int and 5 points in Str or Agi.

The necromancer pauses a moment, then continues while picking some lint off his robes, “Starting out is basically the same for all professions. Find your guild and therefore your new guildmaster and submit your note of admittance. Be sure to have him train you in the way of piercing as much as possible before you leave him. Now put on your robes, equip your dagger, memorize your spells, and head for the nearest newbie grounds. For Dark Elves and Humans this is right outside the gates to their home towns. For Erudites this is up the elevator, down the path past the hole, and into the newbie valley area. For Gnomes this is out the gates and to the left. Kill everything of equal skill to you. As soon as you hear that magical ‘ding’ that tells you you’ve advanced in your level of skill, be sure to sell all you found on your kills. Then buy all the new spells you have money to buy.

“Now one mistake most young necromancers make is to grab the Cavorting Bones spell and instantly summon up a pet to help them out. The truth is this will only slow you down until at you’re at least three. You could in fact easily get away with not buying this spell at all. But I did find it useful when it was appropriate. Be sure to start collecting bone chips now, you’ll need them all your life. Something to note about gnomes is the decaying gnome skeletons in the Basement and in the Steamfont are your friends. If you kill them the Dark Reflection won’t be happy. So when it’s bone chip harvesting time, you’ll have to head for Greater Faydark until you’re old enough to take out the older skeletons.”

“Keep this up till four. Be sure to train once in meditate. The spells you get at this level will introduce you to a very useful strategy you’ll use for quite some time: Reverse Kiting. (The name comes from the hunting method druids and shamans call a strategy: Kiting.) The spells involved are Clinging Darkness, Poison Bolt, Fear, and Life Spike. You simply pull the monster with Clinging Darkness, sic your pet on it, fear it, and start Poison Bolt. Life Spike as needed. With this wonderful tactic, you can take out creatures far stronger than you (OOC: yellows, even low reds). Of course this only works in a large open area, but early on that’s all you’ll be fighting in. Something to note is the fact that the entire line of darkness spells is highly resistible. So many times it won’t stick on the first cast. Just back up as your pet tanks and recast. Hopefully it’ll stick after a couple casts.”

A large pile of bones in the corner suddenly rises and forms Zinbik’s pet. It stumbles out of the room and runs down the hallway. Zinbik smiles and waits as he smoothes out his robes. In a few minutes the pet returns with a small tray. After setting the tray on the table it returns to the same corner and collapses once more. “Drink?” Zinbik asks as he pours some Gnomish Spirits from the tray into his stein. He then offers the bottle to you along with a small glass. You politely accept and fill the glass. After a few sips, Zinbik strokes his beard in thought before continuing.

“Pets are wonderful things. For two bone chips you get an instant tank to serve your every need. In the midst of battle though, its death can easily mean your death. So learning to keep it alive is quite important. In the very beginning you always melee along side it, taking most of the blows. A little later on, learn to take a few hits, back off to leach it, then go back into melee. When you get Mend Bones things get a little easier. You can stay out of melee entirely and keep your pet healed. (Remember, these strategies and tips still apply as you get older and get spell updates.) By about 20 or so, you shouldn’t be going into melee at all. The damage you do won’t be enough to risk getting hit. And the spells you’ll be casting will make the monster angry enough that you should be working to keep your distance anyway.

“Technically you don’t even have to use bone chips if there’s some undead already in the area. We get two undead charm spells in our spell progression. You won’t use these often at all though. For one, necromancers are not known for their charisma (which is fairly important for these spells). Second, charms usually don’t last long, especially because of our low charisma. This means at any moment it’ll break the charm and attack you (in a solo situation this is NOT good). This brings us to the biggest and last problem: you can’t charm with a pet. This means if you want to charm something you won’t have the advantage of a pet when it breaks charm. And charm is also easily resistible, instantly aggroing the creature on you without a pet. The only times this is useful are A.) You have to get in the area invisibly and there isn’t a safe place to summon a pet inside. B.) You’re trying to separate a spawn so you charm one of the creatures and keep it alive for a bit. Both of those require a group to back you up of course. The only time to use it in a solo situation is C.) There’s only two creatures and you plan on feigning the moment the non-charmed one is dead (this is quite dangerous in any case).

“This brings me to what I believe to be the best spell you’ll ever get: Feign Death. This spell will save your life NUMEROUS times! As soon as you reach 16, buy this, memorize it, and never forget it. At the first sign of fatal danger, cast this immediately. As an experienced necromancer you’ll know so much about death you can easily fake it. As far as anyone else is concerned you’re dead. A very useful thing about this is you might have noticed you can cast on someone even when they’re dead. The same applies to you when you’re faking it. This is great when you feign in a dungeon and have 5 creatures standing on top of you. If someone can get near enough to cast on you without aggroing the creatures they can cast invisibility on you so you can get up and escape. Or if you’re low on health and are forced to feign to get away from an angry monster, the healer in your group can take the opportunity to restore your health.

“Something to note is that due to some crazy change in the way world works (OOC: Nerfing), after playing dead for two minutes your pet now comes to believe that you’ve abandoned him and he can go back to being dead. So if you feign in the middle of a battle, be sure to get up in that time limit or you’ll be in some trouble.

OOC: I don’t know who the heck was able to let their pet hunt for them for an entire night’s sleep while they stayed feigned on the ground but this is hardly a viable hunting method. Contrary to popular Verant belief, when you feign, your pet will not last long on it’s own even against blue creatures. The only way you could possibly do this is if you set your pet up at some place where a very low blue spawned slowly enough for your pet to recover it’s health in between fights. This is ridiculous, it’d take days to get a whole bar of experience. And in that time someone who IS awake would surely come along and take your camp. This is the kind of problem you worry about when all major problems are already solved, which was not the case here. End Rant.

“Harmshield is another lifesaver. This one is also permanently memorized. It makes you completely invulnerable to magic and melee (including healing magic) for approximately 10 seconds. It only recovers every 7 minutes though (OOC: or when you zone) so you can’t just be indefinitely invulnerable forever. The only problem whatsoever is that you can still be stunned. But this is great if you run into something like a Hill Giant or Griffin (before you’re old enough to take them on) and you’re somewhere near the zone. Just cast and run.

“Now most of you’re life combat will consist of your pet tanking and you DOTing. But there are a couple exceptions to this rule. At level 24, your DOTs will be quite outdated, but you will get Shock of Poison, your first direct damage spell. At this point you start fighting like a mage. Just hold out on the nuking until your pet’s had some time to taunt and damage the creature. This way they won’t instantly aggro on you the moment you cast.

“Let me take this opportunity to make a note about the life tap line of spells (which I generally referred to as leaches until someone actually invented the spell: Leach). This is one of the lines of spells which gives us the ability to be a very solitary class. We get to hurt an enemy while healing ourselves at the same time. But many young necromancers make the mistake of treating this line like normal DD spells. This is very inefficient. Always give yourself an excuse before using these. At the early levels just melee some so you can take some hits (and let your pet avoid some) then back off and cast.

“By level eight though, you’ll get a much better excuse though: Dark Pact (and the rest of this line of spells). This is a wonderful series of spells which trades health for mana. This will allow you to recover your mana a lot faster as well as taking some health away, giving you a reason to life tap. And they only cost 5 mana to activate, so you’ve got nothing to lose. Just make sure you keep track of your health (you’ll feel really stupid if you manage to kill yourself this way). Of course when you get to level 34 this gets even better. This is when you get my favorite spell: Call of Bones. This has the same effect as the previous spells in this series but also turns you into a skeleton. Just be especially careful since most guards that usually let you pass will suddenly hate you. Though surprisingly, some people like you MORE as a skeleton (like the Dark Bargainers). What really gets me though, is the fact that most undead still hate you as much as they always did with this spell on.

“Of course, with the same powers we use to transfer health from our enemies to ourselves, we can transfer health from ourselves to our friends. The Dark Empathy/Shadow Compact line is quite useful in emergency group situations. We can’t heal nearly as well as one of the healing classes would, but if they’re out of mana, or totally absent, we can fill the role. Just be sure to not to over-heal and keep life tapping to make up for the health you’re giving away. “Another interesting line of spells is the Scent line. These spells lower a monster’s fire, poison, and disease resistances. It’s also a poison-based spell. It sounds quite useful until you find out how resistable it is. It pretty much requires you to have an enchanter there to Tashan or it won’t work. But if you’re expecting a long arduous battle you should probably consider it.

“Something you might wonder about when you get to 20, is what to specialize in. Well, there’s only two choices you should really consider: Alteration and Conjuration. Alteration is the choice for most necromancers (like myself). This covers most of our important damage spells as well as our healing spells (enemy to self and self to friend). The other choice, Conjuration, covers the pet spells, the darkness line, and the two DDs we get. Though we might use divination plenty (for invisibility), there’s no reason to specialize in it since you don’t have to worry about it in the heat of battle. Evocation only covers our undead DDs which just aren’t used as often as alteration spells. The only thing you might worry about with Abjuration is Feign Death (I still don’t understand why this uses Abjuration) since it’s important for this not to be interrupted as well as to have the mana you need to cast it. But it’s just not worth it.”

OOC: Be aware that as soon as you get to 20, spend all five points in specialization. Put one point in each specialization skill. For some reason if you only put a point in Specialize: Alteration, it won’t advance properly. Besides, it makes spells cost cheaper sometimes if you use the specialize skill for each skill. Specialization won’t kick in as much for them, but it will sometimes.

You hear a snap from across the room. Zinbik reflexively grabs his harvester (making one of his eyes turn grey and go slack) and scans the room. Relaxing, he sets it back down and pauses, “The good people of the Temple of Solusek Ro are very generous. In exchange for the items they’d need to make another pair, they’ll give you the boots off their feet! I guess now’s a good a time as any to give you my advice on how to deal with their little quests.

“The biggest problem with each one is the shadowed item you’ll need. Each quest requires you to get something off those blasted Shadowed Men. Luckily, Vurgo’s quests (the Harvester and the Words of Darkness) allow you to turn in the shadowed item alone for a note (reminding Vurgo of the fact that you already turned it in). One way to solve a few problems at once though is to head to Nektulos. The Shadowed Man hangout here is generally surrounded by a bunch of large spiders and shadow wolves. This way you can take care of all the shadowed man items and the shadow silk at the same time. It’s also close to the Temple of Solusek Ro so you can turn the stuff in as soon as you get it.

“The easiest quest is the Shadowbound Boots. I did this at level 20. You need Shadow Silk, a Scepter of Sorrow, an Eye of Shadow, and a Skeletal Toe. I’m not master tailor, but I’ve kept my mother’s trade alive. It doesn’t take that much skill to make the Shadow Silk, but it might help to find someone who has a little skill with a sewing kit. For the Scepter of Sorrow, just head to Mistmoore and go to the crypt. Pick up the flower off the coffin, go behind the building and give it to the former shadowed man there for the scepter. The Eye of Shadow has to be gotten from an actual Shadowed Man. The Skeletal Toe is found on Giant Skeletons in the Rathe Mountains. You don’t even have to kill one though since most people will leave these on their corpses. Just take all these back to the Temple and you’ve got some black boots.

“The Shadowbound Gloves are almost as easy to get. They require Inky Shadow Silk (just a little harder to make than the regular kind), a Scepter of Tears, a Hand of Shadow, and a Skeletal Finger. For the Scepter of Tears just get a map and head to Lower Guk; it’ll be lying on the floor so just pick it up and go. The Hand of Shadow is off Shadowed Men. The Skeletal Finger is also on Giant Skeletons in the Rathe Mountains. Take them all back to the Temple for some nice black gloves.

“The Harvester is a little harder to get. You need a Shadowed Scythe, a Fungus Eye, a Shadowed Knife, and a Fire Opal. The Shadowed Scythe is off of Shadowed Men. The Fungus Eye is off the Mortuary Fungus in Unrest, people often leave the eye on these as well since it’s no drop and you can’t sell it. The Shadowed Knife is found on the Goblin Headmaster on Oracle Island in the Ocean of Tears. You might have to kill him a couple times since sometimes he carries the mask mages need for one of their quests. You CAN find Fire Opals on Seafury Cyclopses, but it’s a lot easier to find Genni in the Temple. She charges 55pp for them.

“The Words of Darkness definitely requires some friends. You need a Shadowed Book, a Book of Darkness, and a Book of Frost. The Book of Shadow is off of Shadowed Men, but it seems litteracy isn’t rampant among them since they’re rarely dropped. The Book of Darkness is in Lake Rathe. When you come to the lake from the Karanas, just head to the the island with the tall building. Inside you’ll find two Erudites: a female mage and a male wizard, you want the mage. Two or three people in their 20s should be able to handle them both. The Book of Frost is found on the Icy Goblin in the jail in Permafrost. He’s got lots of friends with him though so I’d suggest a very large or high-leveled group. Turn all these in along with 300gp for your very own cool-looking open book.

Zinbik runs his hand over his robe and frowns, “The Robe of Enshroudment is by far the most annoying quest. You need Large Shadow Silk (the hardest Shadow Silk to make), a Globe of Shadow, a Mask of Shadow, and a Werebat Wing. The Mask of Shadow is off of Shadowed Men. The Werebat Wing is pulled off a Werebat in Unrest. The Globe of Shadow is the most annoying part. It’s found on the Nokta Shaman in Upper Guk. He’s not that hard, but he’s got quite a bit of friends nearby. This isn’t the half of it though. Most of the time he’s got a double (OOC: Placeholder) in his stead. Even when he does show his ugly face though, he rarely drops the Globe. I’m convinced that when he’s about to die he breaks it just so no one else can have it. But after hours of killing Frogluks you might just get it. Just expect a long wait.”

Zinbik stretches, shifting in his seat. He continues, “Groups are something you’ll learn about much later than most. It’s just so much easier and more effective for you to solo at the early levels. But eventually you’ll have to join a group and you’re going to need to know what role you’re to fill. First let’s look at partnerships. This is the simplest group, but still quite effective. Since the number of people in the group is proportional to the ease you can take things on, you can be doubly effective with a single partner. But with only one other person grouped with you, just act the same way you would if you were soloing. Deviations depend on who you’re hunting with. I hunt a lot with a mage friend of mine. This covers a lot of ground. We’ve got the most powerful DOTs, the second best DDs, minor healing power, unlimited food and water, two tanks, unlimited weapons for our pets, and limitless possibilities for strategies. We do just fine in dungeons or outdoors. Another good partner if you’re outside is the rogue. They can backstab away while you keep the monster feared allowing major damage possibility.

“Another possibility for a partner not to overlook is another necromancer. With proper coordination you can divide up the DOTs (they don’t stack with themselves) so you each use only half the mana. Also, you can take on something as much as five levels above each of you by attacking with your pets and life tapping. Don’t waste mana on anything else since they’ll only be resisted. But the life tap line is only resisted by extremely magic resistant creatures. You’ll use up a lot of mana and you might be forced to feign if things get ugly, but you can do quite well like this.

“At the later levels though, partnerships just won’t be enough if you want to take on the big boys. So you’ve got to learn how to act in a dungeon, in a full group. Many classes have specific, defining role in a group. There’s tanks, healers, nukers, crowd controllers, group buffers, death speeders, and travel agents. Necromancers fulfill every one of these roles in at least a minor way. But be sure you understand what’s going on around you so you can fulfill your own role properly.

“First there’s tanks. A tank’s job is to keep the enemy on themselves and to stay alive. There’s plenty of classes who can fill this role: warriors, monks, rogues, paladins, shadow knights, and rangers are the usuals. Bards, druids, clerics, shamans, and pets can fill this role as well. They keep the monsters busy while you cast. Your pet is what helps you fill this role. Just keep your pet healed and on the right monster and you’ll be doing your job in this role.

“Next there’s healers. A healer’s job is to keep everyone alive and well. The best classes to fill this role are the mystics: clerics, druids, and shamans. Others can fill this role in a minor way too: bards, paladins, rangers, and you. Of course any class can train in bind wounds to heal up to half health. Your most common fulfillment of this role is to heal yourself with the life tap line. But when the need is there you can heal others too. You should usually reserve this role for between fights. You can bring a warrior back to full health, but it takes time. So your best chance is to concentrate on killing the enemy before you bother healing the group. Also, before it comes time for battle make sure the group healer (assuming you aren’t trying to do this) knows not to heal you (since you have the life tap line) unless you’re getting low on health and are under attack or if you’re out of mana.

“Next are the nukers. A nuker’s job is to finish a monster off quickly. Some classes are better at this than others: Wizards and mages are exceptional. Druids, enchanters, clerics, shamans, even you can nuke at times. Technically rangers can nuke when needed as well. The only DDs you ever get are Shock of Poison at 24 and Ignite Bones at 44. So you can’t always fill this role very well at all. But when you do, just be careful about it. Wait till the tanks have done more damage than you can do before you nuke or you’ll get yourself in some trouble.

“Next are the crowd controllers. This includes a few classes: enchanters (the masters), wizards, clerics, bards, and necromancers. You fulfill this role in three ways: fearing, mezzing, and undead charming. You’re no enchanter, your use in this role is limited. Fearing is only useful outdoors (unless you have a death wish), but it can be quite useful to reduce the number of enemies on you (it gives you the time it takes for the creature to run, then run back). You get Screaming Terror at 24 though, which mesmerizes a creature for about 30 seconds. Of course this is effected by charisma, which you probably won’t have much of. I’ve already told you about charming.

“Then there’s group buffers. Some classes are pretty darn good at this: shamans (the masters), clerics, enchanters, and druids fill this role well. Wizards, mages, and you can also fill this role at times. (Bards fit this role during battle itself.) You don’t have many targetable buffs, but you do get some. Just make sure they’re up before it comes time for battle.

“Next are death speeders. These classes are enchanters, bards, mages, shamans, druids, and you. This is where necromancers really shine. With the best DOTs in all of Norrath, the scent line (to help the other casters), the Heart Flutter line, and a pet (a physical DOT) we make death come quick. The key in a group though is to cast the fastest DOTs you have (poisons are the best). Only when you don’t have many people in the group or it’s an especially rare and powerful monster should you pull out your slow acting DOTs.

“Finally there’re the travel agents. These classes really shine in a long term group. But these classes also help escape a bad situation. There are some who are definitely better than others at this: druids and wizards are the best; whisking you miles away from danger at the cast of a spell. Others can help too: bards, shamans, rangers, monks, shadow knights, and you. (Clerics, with ressurections, could be considered to fit this category as well.) The way you (as well as monks and shadow knights) help is simple: Feign Death. This is a major role you help with when things go bad.

“Say your group is highly engaged in an epic battle with many enemies. Everyone’s fully occupied, you know you’re barely going to make it. Suddenly a pair roaming monsters come along and decide to join in. You’re the only one who seems to notice, so you’re the one who yells for everyone to run. You’ve been working hard up till now to keep the monsters from all ganging up and beating the crap out of you. But now you want to use their anger to your advantage. As everyone else gates, evacuates, and high tails it out of there, you cast Harmshield and jump into the fray. All the monsters jump onto you, frantically trying to hurt you despite your invulnerability. In a matter of seconds it drops. You madly cast and miraculously aren’t interrupted in trying to feign death. As you drop to the ground they all run after the rest of the group who’s already almost gone. You’ve saved the day!

“Also, always pay attention to who you group with. Followers of the Prime Healer may normally seek to heal and protect. Some of them might include you in that. Others might feel letting you die heals a great stain on Norrath. Tribunal followers might leave you for dead since you didn’t pay your taxes. Erollisi followers might decide you don’t love your pet enough. Don’t always assume good guys will seek your death, but be careful all the same.”

Zinbik yawns as you do the same. He continues though, “One thing more. Whenever you’re practicing in the field, always remember you represent something larger than yourself when you practice necromancy. Always act with honor, style, and dignity. Just because Innoruuk tells you to hate thy neighbor doesn’t mean you act like a common thief. And be consistent. If you refuse to group with high elves, don’t group with high elves. Don’t let the Tunarian cleric into your group simply because you’re desperate for a healer. If you wanted to be a rogue, be a rogue (not to say all rogues are untrustable, common thieves). By acting like a jerk, you might be ruining the chances for a nicer necromancer to get into an important group.”

Zinbik rises from his chair and shakes your hand as he says, “Well, I can’t think of anything more to say. I bid you safe journeys and a good career in necromancy. If you ever have any more questions, just ask.”

When you start to get up you stumble; you’re left foot is asleep. You shake it and massage it until you finally regain feeling. When you look up, Zinbik is gone. You head down the hallway you came from until you see a cryptic message appear in front of you: LOADING, PLEASE WAIT.
