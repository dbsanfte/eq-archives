Nouns
This language marks number with suffixes.
Dual -at, -t
Plural -en, -n
A Whole Hell Of A Lot -oa, -hoa/-a

The first suffix is used for nouns ending in a consonant (e.g., adal, adalat) and the second is for nouns ending in a vowel, except in a case that would call for -hoa to be added to a noun ending in "o", where the suffix is reduced to -a (e.g. kema, kemat, keman, kemahoa; teso, tesot, teson, tesoa).

Case is marked with prefixes.
Case C initial V initial
nominative (null) (null)
genitive ma- mah-, m'-
accusative on- on-
dative te- teh-, t'-

Mah- is reduced to m'- when added to a word that begins with "a". Teh is reduced to t'- when added to a word that begins with "e".

Pronouns
Pronouns take the same number and case markings as nouns.

1st ya
2nd do
3rd- 1 li
3rd- 2 et

Yat means "you and I". Yan is the inclusive 'we'. Yahoa is the exclusive 'we', and can also be used in a collective sense to refer to "all people like us".

The first 3rd person is used when there is only one person or thing being spoken about. When more than one person or thing is being spoken about, it is used to refer to the first or most important thing mentioned, (or the topic); the second 3rd person refers to a second (or third, etc) or less important person being spoken about. For example, if we were talking about John and mentioned that he hit Bob, we might say, "He (li) hit him (onet)."

Demonstratives and Articles
na, that
sa, this
These take number and case markings when they replace a noun. When used with a noun, they are not marked at all.

Lu is the definite article.
