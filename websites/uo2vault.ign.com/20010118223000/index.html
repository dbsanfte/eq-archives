 Official Site Updated-Comments from Starr Long and Contest
Two updates to the official site, the first being in the form of a new Comments from the Team by Starr Long:
Hello Everyone,

Right before the holidays, our latest video trailer was released. If you have not yet downloaded this latest video, I highly recommend it! We put the video together for the UO World Faire to show at the ORIGIN happy hour, and we're glad to finally get the opportunity to share it with the rest of the community, and hear what you think. We think it shows our game well, but it's hard to really express where your game is going through a video alone. As I've mentioned before, we have four main goals that we hope to achieve in the making of this game:

1. Amazing Combat
2. Cool, Unique World
3. Unbelievable Customizability
4. Great Accessibility

The first three goals listed have very strong visual elements, and you can see them well represented in the video -- with the amazing vistas, varieties of characters, and crazy kung fu. It is important to note as well that what you see in the video is pure gameplay! There are no rendered or cinematic sequences at all.

For those curious fans who have asked, The Grim Faeries once again provided the music for the video, and right now we have three different composers creating music for the game itself. We chose to have three so that each of our continents would have a very distinct sound to go along with their distinct looks. For the traditional medieval fantasy Ultima areas we have our very own George Oldziey back in the house. You may remember him as the composer and conductor of the amazingly beautiful orchestrated songs in Ultima Ascension. For the Meer areas we have Cenk Eroglu, a renowned producer and musician from Turkey, to give us a taste of world music. For the technological areas we have Curse Mackey of the Grim Faeries (and My Life with the Thrill Kill Cult) for that hard industrial sound. Each composer not only works within a unique musical style, but also uses different instrumentation, timing, and effects. This really helps the areas have very diverse atmospheres; especially when you add in the different architecture, skies, clothing, flora, fauna, and terrain.

It's pretty exciting work watching all these separate pieces coming together as we wrap up systems, fix bugs, and optimize. None of this is an easy task, though, so I ask that you all remain patient, and understand that we are more interested in delivering a quality product than meeting any specific date.

Finally, although it's a little late, I wanted to say how amazing the World Faire was. It makes me feel good about what we do here to see the passion stirred up by our fans. You are what makes these games such amazing and unique experiences. I only got to meet a few of you, but I hope that next year, when ORIGIN is a part of the Faire, that I will get to interact with more of you. Until next time...

Sincerely,
Starr Long
Producer, ORIGIN

 World Gaming Network Interviews Windfeather
The World Gaming Network has posted an interview with Windfeather; here's a snippet:
*WGNet* - How do you plan on stopping camping in dungeons? I find it very hard to believe that it's going to be possible to have an 'Ancient Wyrm' spawn just anywhere in Despise. What are your plans on stopping people from camping the big loot monsters in dungeons yet keeping it fair enough so that the monster does spawn yet makes the fight worthwhile?

*Windfeather* - Our general spawn philosophy is to spawn things in regions, rather than specific locations: For example, you might have a large area of the map - or a dungeon - that spawns a specific type of creature (say, a tribe of orcs and their shadowtusks). 85% of the orcs and shadowtusks would be in a tight level band, say level 12-15. You'd have about 10% harder creatures, such as orc warriors and orc shamans, 4% really hard creatures such as orc lords and perhaps an occasional evil NPC, and 1% 'rares' - say, for example, a 1 in 10,000 chance of a really nasty, evil NPC or other rare creature that would be rare, rich, and really, really hard to kill.

When we do have a concentrated spawn, it's really just a smaller region, such as a graveyard. There are no specific 'boss monsters' that only spawn in one place, so you can't squat somewhere and wait for the really ph4t l3wt to appear - you have to go find it.

 Official Site Updated-Comments from the Team by Jeremy Gaffney
The official site was updated with a new Comments from the Team by Gaffer; here's what he had to say:
Everybody was Kung-Fu fighting...

So, now that the video is out, there have been some questions about our super-cool flipkicks o' death going around, so I thought I'd spend a little time addressing that. I'm Jeremy Gaffney, aka Gaffer - Tech Lead on Origin, and I've been spending a fair amount of time overseeing combat as it integrates with the game (as initially designed by the Mighty Mobius, and now being worked on by Windfeather).

Getting the flipkicks into the game was pretty challenging from a technical standpoint - it'd be very easy to make them waste a lot of bandwidth (= lag) or not work across server boundaries (= exploits) or generally look or feel bad (= unhappy fans). We've overcome those issues, though, so we are making sure to add in lots of Matrix-style fighting moves as part of making combat exciting. Some people feel that it's just graphical fluff, but it actually enhances gameplay as well (well, ya gotta admit, it does also add a certain graphical flair).

So, what makes combat fun? Well, there are a variety of different roles you can play in combat, depending on your skill tree choices. You can play a brutish tank of a fighter, standing in the front rank, absorbing damage, and dealing out close range blows. You can play a more precise fighter, hitting less often but able to penetrate enemy armor more efficiently. You can go for a flurry of attacks, dealing less damage through armor but making it almost impossible for a mage to get a spell off. You can play an unarmored fighter, doing distance kicks and whirls to fly in at your opponent and then duck out again before they can return the favor to your unarmored hide (you hope)! There are roles for maul-wielding warriors to try to unbalance or knock down their opponents, leaving them vulnerable for their lightly armored roguish companions to duck in, deal massive damage, and scurry away before the foe can retaliate. Combat is designed to be very interesting, both for solo play and especially in groups.

Our combat trees are set up to help out this kind of specialization as well - you can either go down the weapons trees picking skills to your own design, or choose a "profession" tree (such as becoming a knight, archer, etc.) which uses the weapons tree as one of the criterion to advance - for instance, to become a journeyman knight, you might need to achieve a certain level of proficiency with swords, hammers, or axes. The profession trees open up new attacks and skills to you as well, so as a specialized archer you might gain access to attacks in the bow tree not open to generalist plinkers. We're very excited by this system - it allows you to really customize a profession to the degree you'd like, not choose a profession at all to customize your character entirely, or to mix and match professions that you'd like, as long as they aren't in direct conflict (paladin abilities block off rogue skills, for instance). Most of the professions specifically encourage specialization in groups as well - so as a magician, you know that a tank type such as a knight or warrior would be very useful to support the front rank, or that if you have specialized in stunning monsters, having rogues or similar professions to take advantage of the vulnerable critters is in your best interest.

We're still nailing down a lot of details, so expect more information to follow - I have to say, though, I personally am tremendously excited. We have a great system from a technical standpoint to work with, and adding in details and interesting things to the combat system has been a breeze over the last few months. This excites me as well, because not only will we ship with a system like no one's ever seen before, but it will only get better from here.

Jeremy Gaffney
Lead Programmer
UWO: ORIGIN

 Official Site Updated-Team Comments from Cynthe
The official site has been updated with a new Comments from the Team by Cynthe; here's what she had to say:
I introduced myself in my last letter, so now I'd like to talk a little bit about what I do. I have many responsibilities, but a big hunk of it is maintaining good relations with our fansites and providing information to the fans.

In my lofty goal to make everyone happy and to distribute lots of great information, I'd say I have three big hurdles. First, there's the simple issue of priorities. Needless to say, distributing screenshots and information about the game is a lower priority than, oh say... finishing the game. :) A fansite operator suggested recently, "Why don't you just give out two screenshots per week?" Actually, that's not as easy as it sounds. Since we're still in the phase of game creation rather than straight testing, we don't actually spend all that much time in the game, with clothed characters, arranging to meet up with other characters, and taking screenshots. So most of the shots are taken by me, or taken by our artists when we put together videos or special showings. This means I have only a set selection of shots at any given time, and I try to do my best not to run out! Interviews are the same way, since every interview takes at least a little time from a dev team member, and I have to balance those time requirements, along with the amount of time spent by them answering my incessant game-related questions for the boards and the FAQ.

Another difficulty is the desire to never give out inaccurate information. Sounds easy, doesn't it? When I first started, my thought on that was, "Well, duh." What I didn't fully understand at that time was that with a game (and a game industry) that changes as much as this one does, it's almost impossible to know from one minute to the next whether the feature you're looking at right now will still be there in 6 months, or if something better will be discovered later. To truly be sure to give out no inaccurate information, we'd be resigned to, "Well, we're making a game... and you'll be able to play with other people... and you can use weapons and wear clothes... and it'll be really fun." This approach, of course, is about as exciting as mud, so for the most part, we just say as much as we feel is safe, and try our best to keep our information as current as possible.

My third hurdle is the fact that there are simply so many sites! The amount of support we receive from the community is truly amazing, but that also makes it almost impossible to give everyone the attention they deserve. Currently, we post a Question of the Week that alternates between two sites, a screenshot rotation that gives a screenshot each week to one of five sites, a FAQ board where I get suggestions for new FAQ additions, IRC chats once a month that alternate between three sites, and a weekly (or sometimes bi-weekly) Q&A with Ahriman. In addition, we do interviews about every 2 weeks with sites all over the community (and I'm not counting other promotions and interviews done by our PR and marketing department). Of course, there's also our own boards here, and the UWOO eGroup discussion forum, both of which enable us to touch even more people.

I'm always on the lookout for the magical way to make everyone happy... who knows, maybe I'll find it! Until then, I would just like to take a quick moment to say thank you to all of our fansite operators and the fans who visit those sites and ours. This week, we have something great to offer you, which is the trailer we showed at the UO World Faire. I wish we could release something like this every week! Of course, the game would never be finished if we tried that…

Happy Holidays to everyone, and enjoy the video!

Cynthe
UWO:ORIGIN Community & Web Coordinator
Thanks!

 Diary of Designer Quotes
From the Official Boards:
Even More on IP Sharing
I'm just hedging - I know of no current reason to block, there's notechnical thing currently forcing us to block. I don't expect us to blockmultiple clients on the same IP. I'm just reserving the right to havesomething I don't know about come up (which, sadly, does sometimes happen).

Shouldn't be an issue tho.

More on IP Sharing
So, to expand on Gaffer's comment (since I'm the one with his fingers in thenetworking code):

We don't currently have any restrictions on playing from the same IPaddress, with the exception of the amount of bandwidth available over yourconnection. I wouldn't recommend having two people share a connection thatruns over a 28.8 or 56K modem.

Other good reasons for us not to restrict this include the fact that lotsof people like to play from behind firewalls that hide their real host'saddress (i.e. NAT/IP Masquerading) or that redirect and/or restrict theports used.

I'm guessing that since we haven't seen a need to do this for UO, thereshouldn't be a reason to restrict it for Origin.

The only thing I can really see us restricting that runs along these samelines would be preventing people from running multiple instances of the gameclient on a single computer.

That said, we may still come up with some reason to restrict multiplelogins from the same IP. If that happens, the limitation will be a matter ofpolicy rather than technical - and you probably won't see me playing thegame, at least from home, where I share a masqueraded connection.

Multiple Clients On Same IP
I'm actually not sure if you'll be able to play multiple times from thesame IP or not.

At the moment, we're not blocking it (sometimes folks play multipleclients from their PC simultaneously, actually) but I'll need to talk withTyrant to find out if there is some exploit or tracking reason to disallowit.

So, no real word yet, sorry.

Limitations to Spellcasting
Other things that keep you from just constantly unloading spells:

* Certain spells "block" you from doing other spell-or-combat type actionsfor a period of time (well, all spells do this for the duration of the"cast", but some are longer than others)

* Reagents

* Stamina. This is actually a biggee, because most of the coolio combatmoves take a lot of stamina too. It's one of the reasons why being afighter-mage isn't uberpowerful; it's the same resource being drawn on byboth professions.

UO2 Test Center on UO Trace
Nah, for internal use only - we're testing our login system. Someday, though, that's probably the server set we'll be using for externaltesting too.

Adranath
I've said that Adranath will have a presence in Origin. This istrue. And according to the history you've read, you know that his spectralform was finally banished to the Abyss after the Bloodstone was destroyed.What would that tell you?

I asked the question myself, "Will Sosaria ever be threatened by this fouldemon again?" As to how the Lord of Necromancers might return, that you'llhave to wait and see. ;)

Jukan Names
For Juka names, I was inspired by the sounds of Turkish and Persian. Iwanted something that was both "hard" and "melodious" which seemed to fitperfectly somewhere in between those two. But you are correct in that I alsoused some Indian names for Jukan heroes.

Can you tell I wanted something different and fresh from the typicalEuropean medieval fantasy setting? ;)

Shiny Stuff and Reflections
We will have lots of shiny stuff in Origin. Right now we have sphericalenvironment mapping in. It looks very cool but isn't terribly accurate asfar as reflections go. We are hoping to swing back and replace our sphericalmapping with true cube mapping rendered from the world. We have to evaluatehow costly that is and iron out some technical issues regarding howdifferent hardware handles environment mapping.

For armor we were planning originally on not doing the very shiny in favorof more muted specular sheens. The env mapping, however, has turned out verywell, so we are using it more and more.

Other places that you will see heavy use of env mapping is in the crystalcity of Ishpur. Don't ask for screens of that one yet, though, I still havesome bugs I need to iron out before it is done :)

Multiple Weapons Use
Multi-weapon use is based on the ownership of certain skill boxes in thetree - unless you train at it, the extra weapon doesn't do you a heckuva lotof good.

Unarmed and Armed Attacks
There's actually a rather nice synergy to the unarmed/armedattacks - many of the attacks that are in our system are called "combos" -these aren't combos in the twitch game sense, but rather a chained set ofmoves that go off when you do an attack. So, for instance, one of thesecombos might be slash with your weapon, stab with your weapon, do afootsweep, bash with the pommel of your weapon, and do a kick.

Because of the way the skills are set up, as you approach the masterylevels of your preferred weapon tree, the "slash, stab, bash" portions ofyour weapon combo might do more and more damage (or be faster, or protectyou more, hit more often, or other stuff based on the skill choices youmade). However, even at the higher levels the sweep and kick portions of theattack don't tend to do much more damage (though there may be someskills/bonuses that affect -all- melee type values regardless of weapon)

However, if you going up both the unarmed and armed trees simultaneously,you'll find both portions of the combo doing more damage as appropriate.Ditto of course for offhanded attacks.

Dodging and Unarmored Combat
Unarmored fighters will benefit greatly from having some big ol'tincan wearing hunk of hitpoints nearby to take the brunt of monsters'attacks.

You can get quite good at dodgy-type skills so that you rarely get hit,but if the bad guys DO land a hit, watch out - that's where armor is reallyuseful, in reducing the damage when you get whacked. A couple of lucky shotsin a row, and you'll be learning firsthand about our xp debt system.

Water Current
There are currently no plans for water current.

Mounts and Guilds
Mounts and boats are two things that we would love to see implemented, butat this time, we can't promise them by launch. It's something our Live teamwill be looking at, however, and could be added in the future.

Hairdressers

Experience Debt
You're correct in that these two options are the same inasmuchas you must pay X experience back to your debt before you are 'debt free'.What we'll probably go with is to send the ratio of debt you have vs thetotal experience you need to make the next level to pay off your debt, witha minimum of 10% earned experience going to the debt. In other words, if youhave a 2,000 XP debt and need 10,000 XP to level, 2,000/10,000, or 20%, ofany experience you earn will go towards your debt, and 80% towards yournormal experience.

This creates an interesting situation when you level while holding a debt.If you went from level 5 (needs 10K experience to hit level 6) to level 6(needs 20K experience to hit level 7), and had a 2,000 point debt, thepercentage of experience going towards your debt would be 20% (2,000/10,000)at level 5, and instantly change to 10% (2,000/20,000) at level 6.

I think the other option, which sends 100% of your earned experience toyour debt until your debt is paid off, would feel too much like takingexperience away, and would also make the over-time erosion of the debt lessuseful. Time is your friend when you have a debt.

Projectile Weaponry
If you fire an arrow at a target, you turn to face the target, an arrowshoots out, and hits or misses. Silent Bob Thats my point. I've spoken to many, many 2 hour players who feelwronged because they cannot advance at a reasonable rate compared topowergamers. There will be ten schools of magic at launch.

The base four schools are the four elements (Earth, Air, Fire, and Water). The next four schools are hybrid schools: Lava, Vapor, Nature and Crystal. In order to use those schools, you'll have to get to a certain point of advancement in the corresponding base schools (for example, Lava is Earth plus Fire). The amount of interest and anticipation surrounding the game has been overwhelming, and I can assure you that the team is as eager as any of you to actually play the game!

If someone had told me a few years ago that I would soon be working on the largest, most anticipated MMRPG of all time, I would have said they were crazy! It’s been quite a ride, and shows no signs of letting up soon. In fact, to tell what seems to be a common story in this industry, I never planned to go into game programming at all. I went to the University of Miami to study marine robotics and ocean engineering. After graduating, one thing led to another and I ended up with a job porting a Windows flight sim to the Macintosh. Within the first month, that project was cancelled and I moved onto the team working on the next version of Air Warrior for Kesmai. After releasing a couple versions of Air Warrior, my work caught the attention of a new team, which turned out to be the UWO:Origin team. Two years later, here we are.

First of all, let me say I’m not the only one working on the client graphics by any stretch of the imagination. On a team of 50 or more people, including artists, designers, programmers, and everyone else it takes to complete a project like this, we all take our little share of the design and development. There are several people directly responsible for the client engine, like Hyperion, Melior, myself, and a few others. We each work on a small part of the whole. That said, I am responsible for terrain, water and lava, a good portion of the client-side physics, 3D graphics engine core, and general optimizations, among other things.

I’ve just recently completed the conversion to DirectX 8.0, which gives us quite a performance boost, as well as a list of cool new graphics features to find uses for. Speaking of which, a cool side-effect of my job is that I get to fly all around the country to meet with all the 3D accelerator hardware companies and learn all about the latest and soon-to-be greatest 3D hardware. They want to make sure we take advantage of all the new features, and we want to make sure we get the best performance possible on each piece of hardware. But the best part is that I get to play with all the new toys long before I could buy 'em in the store!

While I have the spotlight, I thought I’d tell you a little bit about the terrain and water data in the game. Origin is huge! It looks like we’ll have at least 2 CDs worth of art, music, data, and game code. Included in that is a huge amount of terrain data. Heavily compressed, this data takes up about 50k per 256 square meters for the height data alone, and there are hundreds of square kilometers of world to explore. When you add in the textures, that’s a lot of data to push around. To top that off, you can walk continuously from one end of each continent to the other without stopping (other than to rest, that is). Streaming hours and hours of terrain data while maintaining high frame rates and handling creatures, magic, and every other function of the client simultaneously is not a trivial task. It's taken over a year to design and implement, but the hard work is paying off. We now have a working system that looks great and works very well.

In addition to the static terrain, we have a dynamic wave system that allows us to put real waves on the entire ocean and every bit of water in the game. This same system is used for lava, swamp, and any other liquid in the game. We set parameters like viscosity, wave height, wave direction, number of waves, etc. and it goes about its business from there. Another great feature is that it's completely scalable and doesn’t take that much CPU power to run. That means that it will likely be available even on the lowest end machines, albeit at a lower mesh resolution.

That’s about all the space I get for now. I look forward to writing again, when I can tell you about more of the amazing features of this amazing engine.
