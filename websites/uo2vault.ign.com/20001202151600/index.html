 New Questions Added to FAQ and New Comments from Usul
The Official Site was updated with six new questions added to the Official FAQ and then a new Comments from the Team, this time by Usul. First though, here's what was added to the Official FAQ:
Will I be limited to specific starting cities depending on what race I choose?
No. There will be five cities available as a starting point, and any new character can choose any of those five cities regardless of race.

Will I be able to group with any player, regardless of race?
Yes, any race can group with whomever they choose.

Cool! Will I be able to group with other players, regardless of level?
Yes! The experience given will vary according to level to keep the advancement rates fair, but lower level characters can still gain experience when grouped with high level characters.

Will there be any strength or intelligence bonus given to characters based on gender?
No, there is no statistical difference between female and male characters.

Will the game include a built-in mp3 player?
No. Mp3's are not small files, for one thing. Our music will use DirectMusic technology from Microsoft's DirectX, which allows the music to be much more dynamic, use custom digital instruments, and maintain a much smaller file size.

Is there a soundtrack?
Definitely! The music has received a lot of attention throughout our development process, and should add tremendously to the mood of the game. We have even employed three different composers to create the music - one for each continent, to add to the unique feel of each race's homeland.

And now, the Comments from Usul:
Hello,

It’s been a few weeks since my last letter, and things are starting to come together quite nicely. We now have Test Center up and running, which is where the Pre-Alpha phase of the External Test will occur. Currently, a date for this has not been set, but our internal testers are moving around the world and testing systems as we speak. In the meantime, I’d like to give you a little more detail as to how we will conduct the test.

It will be divided into several phases, each phase consisting of specific entrance and exit criteria. This includes the Pre-Alpha phase, meaning we have certain entrance requirements that must be met before that phase can even begin. Some examples of these criteria are servers staying up for specified periods of time, no client crashes, etc. We absolutely will not begin the External Test or move into the next phase until the specified criteria for each phase have been met. In addition, with each phase the criteria become more and more stringent, ensuring stability and quality when we ship.

We will also be taking into account the overall tester mood when moving from phase to phase. This factor is not as easy to measure, but we feel it is equally important. The development team has committed itself to incorporating community feedback into the game’s design, and the test will be no different. If our criteria have been met at a certain phase, but the majority of the testers feel that there is an outstanding issue that must be addressed, then we will certainly listen.

Since my last letter some time ago, I know some of you have begun speculating that Pre-Alpha testing has already begun. This information is incorrect. For the record, Pre-Alpha testing has not yet begun and testers have not yet been notified. I would like to provide you with a date; however, we’re just going to have to wait until everything falls into place and the entrance requirements have been met. I assure you that we will make an announcement once the Pre-Alpha phase of the External Test is underway.

I hope this gives you a bit of insight as to how we are approaching the External Test. We are dedicated to shipping a quality, stable game, and we are confident that this is the best way to go about doing it, no matter how long it takes. More coming soon…

Craig "Usul" McDonald
Online Testing Coordinator

 UO2 Vault Feature: UWO: Origin Interview
Although the rapid rate of progress within the industry sometimes makes it feel like it happened at least a few years earlier, Ultima Online was actually released commercially by Origin Systems in 1997. Other games can lay claim to being the first persistent online world, but there can be no real doubt that UO was the landmark title that very dramatically ushered in a new era for the entire computer gaming industry. It demonstrated not only that “massively multiplayer” gaming was feasible with unprecedented numbers of players, but also that the subscription-based revenue model was practicable, and thus that it could be hugely successful, with unprecedented numbers of subscribers.

Last autumn, the company announced an exciting follow-up title, thereby becoming the first developer to reveal plans for a second generation online world. Initially referred to as Ultima Online 2, Ultima Worlds Online: Origin also promises to set new standards for the genre with a plethora of innovations and features. Among these is a strong backstory that incorporates a new Ultima world on a divergent timeline and two new races, the technologically-oriented Juka and the magically-inclined Meer, along with humans. Recently, we had the chance to learn more about this aspect of the game in an interesting and informative Ultima Worlds Online: Origin Interview with its Minister of Fiction, Aaron de Orive, also known as Ahriman.

 Dev Infobits on Stratics DevBoard
The Dev's over Thanksgiving have posted a few things over on the Stratics DevBoard; here's a rundown:
Pre-Placed Housing
How about the Design Team puts the houses in the game rather than letting us place them. We just have to buy one that is already placed and recieve the master key. Once we own it, it acts just like a house from UO except that if upkeep is not maintained, then the house is not destroyed but rather placed back on the market.

This is what I did in GemStone III and DragonRealms (a couple of good text MUDS, try 'em on Microsoft's Game Zone) when I put houses in those games.

It actually was a lot of fun for the players; we had the empty houses on display for a while before we let people take them, and people got really attached to specific homes in specific locations. If you didn't get the one you wanted right away, you always knew that someday (if you were lucky) it would be empty again.

And yeah, it did limit the clutter, which is pretty important in a text game :)

Windfeather
Designer o' Stuff
That Ultima Game

Real life is the ultimate roleplaying game.

Intelligent Creatures and Spawn
I am all for unique NPCs and monsters, but not if it means using static spawns and camping. All spawns should be pretty much random. I wouldn't mind "Nefir Nefarious the Infamous Pirate" spawning anywhere in the world by the shore, but I would mind if he spawned in the same exact place every time and there was a group of people lined up to take turns killing him.

For the most part, intelligent monsters (Orcs, Ettins, Snipes, Foozles, etc) have their own regions on the map where they are the prevalent thing to hunt. Thus, when walking about you'd know you were in 'Goblin Territory', 'Orc Territory', etc.

Within those regions you will find special sites - graveyards, forts, gigantic shoes with old women in them - where you can hang out and kill more concentrated, and tougher, groups of monsters.

Fauna (critters) will spawn according to the geography. Rats in swamps, artists in coffee shops, executives on golf courses. That kind of thing.

There won't be any static spawns of 'named creature X' showing up to be killed repeatedly; however, you may find unique areas that contain the only spawn for really hard monsters such as Undead Orc Assassin Ninjas.

Windfeather
Designer o' Stuff
That Ultima Game

Real life is the ultimate roleplaying game.

Starting Cities
Will you be able to start ANYWHERE in one of origin's city's OR will you have to pick a city within your selected races' Province ?¿?

There are currently five starting cities, all of them in safe zones (since there's not much to do in the Wild Lands as a newbie except cause trouble). Three of the cities are Britannian in flavor, one Meer and one Jukan. You may start in any of them, regardless of your race.

Windfeather
Designer o' Stuff
That Ultima Game

Real life is the ultimate roleplaying game.

Death Animations
You can die in lots of different ways, actually - there are a number of death/knockdown animations. We didn't want to show em off just now in the video (they haven't all been fully beautified)

-jg

Grief Players
You misunderstand.

The commonly accepted definition of a grief player is a player who plays to ruin the good time of other people for no good reason. Grief players are generally considered bad, and the game design needs to try to account for them. Player Killers (PKers) are a subset of grief players, but you don't need to be able to kill someone to cause grief - you can cyberrape them, train monsters on them, steal their loot, etc, etc, etc, etc.

Players who take part in consentual PvP because they are at war with another guild are not considered Grief Players. They are considered PvPers. Game designers like PvP. We wish more people would take part in consentual PvP. PvP can be the very lifeblood of the game.

In short, the term 'grief player' is a shorthand way to refer to troublesome elements in the game, and does not include everyone who engages in player-killing. I would go so far to venture that a fraction of UO's player killers are actually grief players (of course, where that line is is hard to define since it varies from person to person).

Ubiq

 RPG Planet Visits Origin
RPGPlanet had the opportunity to visit Origin while at the UO World Faire and wrote up an article about their visit; here's a bit from it:
In Britannia, we visited the towns of Yew and Vesper. Instead of a bunch of trees connected by bridges, the tree in Yew was the size of a mountain with platforms winding around it. The Juka that was running around the area was dwarfed by tree and could not see the ground from the walkways. Vesper is on the water with a central lighthouse. At night, they plan to have the lighthouse's beam of light rotating and piercing the darkness. The character hopped into the water and swam on the surface using a breaststroke. The water in the game is deformable, and thus waves looked realistic. Initially, characters will only be able to swim on the surface, although underwater areas will be built later.

Instead of the blue sky of Britannia, the Juka sky had a yellowish haze, caused by the primitive industries, that partially obscured the sun. Starr Long described this world as "a land from the future, which is sort of this Victorian, factory technology gone mad, metal-clad…polluted sky. This is the city that actually sits underneath, and those are the pipes that go up to the city that is floating in the sky above it. You take elevators up."

In contrast, the world of the Meer looked somewhat like the lands in Interplay's Sacrifice. Starr went on, "this is the land they live in, which is kind of a Jurassic Park, crystal, shamanistic, magic kind of place with these big dinosaur-like creatures, our interpretation of what dinosaurs should have looked like." Looking at the three worlds, it was clear that each has a very different atmosphere that would appeal to players looking for more than the typical medieval world.

 GameSpot Looks At Origin
In part of GameSpot's Online RPG Week, they have put up a section dedicated to Origin including a Q&A with Ubiq; here's an excerpt from the interview with Ubiq:
GS: Origin won't be using the same system of magic spells as Ultima Online, but rather, different schools of magic. Could you please explain how Origin's schools of magic will work?

DS: Certainly. We will have ten schools of magic at launch, with the ability to add more after launch. Each school will have their own specialties and strengths. For example, fire will have the best damage spells and water will have the best healing spells. Water may have some damage spells, but they'll tend to be lower in damage and take longer to recharge.

The base four schools are the four elements. The next four schools are hybrid schools: lava, vapor, nature and crystal. In order to get those schools, you have to get to a certain point of advancement in the base four schools. For example, lava is earth plus fire. These schools have some very powerful spells, and we've tried to put these schools within reach of a casual gamer that plays very hard.

Beyond that we'll have two master schools. We'll talk more about those master schools later, but these schools are reserved primarily for the Origin diehards. Achieving success in these schools will take a serious time investment, but the rewards should be ample. We've consciously tried to make multiple paths like this in most of the skill trees, so that casual gamers can experience success and feel like they can compete but the hardcore gamers still feel like they have something to gain for going the extra mile. How successful will we be? I think we'll be very successful, although it will probably take some tweaking in beta to get the values just right.

Mini-FAQ On Beta
It's been pointed out that many people come to this board and post questions regarding the beta. Given the traffic on this board, it's understandable that some may have missed previous information we've posted. Although this information can also be found in the main FAQ, located at the link to your left, we think it would help to put this information here as well.

Q. When does the beta start?

We have not yet set a time for the begining of external beta testing. The timeline is dependant upon reaching certaint milestones in earlier testing phases. We will let you know when it begins.

Q. How can I get into the beta test?

That has yet to be determined. So far, the only people who are currently signed up for beta are those people who attended the World Faire. We will have tens of thousands of beta slots we need to fill, so we will definatly be letting everyone know once the sign-up begins.

Q. Isn't it unfair to let people from the World Faire into beta?

Not really. Ultima is built on the loyalty of it's fan base. Some of our fans have been playing Ultima games since the very dawn of the personal computer age. We wanted to say "thank you" to those loyal fans who made the pilgramige to Austin to attend the World Faire. An important thing to keep in mind is that there are still tens of thousands of slots open even after you count the people who went to the Faire. World Faire attendees barely scratch the surface of the number of people we need to do a proper test.

Q. But what about people from Europe or Asia who couldn't afford to go?

Well, there were people from Europe and Asia who attended. Despite that fact, we understand your concern. As we stated, there are tens of thousands of open slots and we will do our best to be fair to as many people as possible.

Q. How long until the game is released?

As stated in the answer about when beta testing begins, we really don't know. We will not release the game until we feel that it is ready. Let's say that a bug appears in the game during beta. We would have to put off release until it is fixed. Conversly, if fewer bugs are found than expected the date of release may come sooner than anticipated. Given that, it's really not possible to give an accurate release date.

Concerning Balance
Hey there.

We will try to balance out all of the weapons and magic as best we can, but one thing to keep in mind is that we have a 'rock-paper-scissors' mentality when balancing this game. What this means is that certain weapons and armor will have strong advantages over other weapons and armor.

Balance does not mean equality, as in both do an equal amount of damage in any given fight. Balance means that both paths are feasible, and that both paths have their advantages as well as disadvantages.

Concerning Powerful Creatures
This is actually a common problem in MMORPGs.

Developers create a creature that they assume could *never* be defeated by a single player (Ubiq, recently, brought up the example of the Yeti in Merdian 59). As time goes on though, players find a way to solo the creature in question. As the game goes on, creatures that once brought a feeling of fear amongst the players now become a simple kill.

We think Dragons are prety darn mean. We know, however, that players are smart. Really, really smart. Too darn smart for their own good. :)

Concerning Stars
Stars are not managed as individual objects. For efficiency, they are handled and rendered in groups of around 500. Because of this, what you describe is not practical.

Hyperion

Concerning RAM
Depends a lot on the game. In a game like ours, where we have little control over the environment (thanks to the players and their creativity), more RAM is always appreciated. Although we probably don't expect every player to have 256. =)

On the other hand, I've noticed that several games that used to be slow on my home machine sped up considerably when I boosted to 256. Some of these games only recommend 64 MB of RAM! If you do a lot of graphically intensive gaming, I suspect you'll find it worth your while.

To reiterate: More is always better, but we're hoping to keep the minimum spec within reason.

Ubiq On Origin
We haven't released our specs yet, but the obvious answer to your question of 'why design a game that can run on more people's machines' is quite simple. We'll sell more copies, meaning that we can pay our rent and EA will let us make more kewl games. =)

To be less flip about it, the beautiful graphics of the game are not the most important part of the game. Underneath the remarkable character renderer and the amazing skies is a very deep, intricate and addictive game, and we don't think ya need to see every bell and whistle in the world to see it.

However, people who plunk down extra cash for high end systems will be rewarded with a better-looking game. The things that dial themselves down, we try to ensure that they are more about visual appearance than about anything that could possibly affect game balance.

Ubiq

Faster Connection and Faster Machine
This depends a lot on the situation - and this is true of virtually all online spaces.

If you are in an online game where you have a great number of people taking part in an activity at the same time (say, 30 people in a guild war), a 56K modem may see degraded performance. The worst is when that many people are moving around at the same time - movement packets are by far the thing most likely to lag ya out. On my previous project, we found that 30 people performing high activity could flood a 56k modem.

Now, arguably, this is a fairly extreme case, and we have designed our network protocol to be fairly resistant to this issue (with a nifty priority scheme that Vaxhacker and Gaffer came up with), but truth be told, you got the better connection, you'll probably see less wierd stuff when the world gets crowded.

On the other hand, we're pretty sure that we've eliminated most of the gameplay advantage from having a faster machine. Hard to say until we get the sucker into testing, but a lot of good thinking has gone into making sure that players with better hardware have a better looking and feeling game without having an undue advantage.

Ubiq

You guys sure are reading a lot into d00dism. I think the truth may be a lot simpler than this.

People who talk in d00dspeak do it because that is part of the culture that they are in. Just as those who roleplay surround themselves with others who roleplay and they learn and feed off of that, those who talk and learn 1337sp34k do it by hanging out with other people they feel comfortable with. These are often (but not always!) young kids, often those who spend lots of time in the internet, and who frequently have habits in their 'speech' and attitudes towards other people shaped by their time in IRC chat rooms.

They don't roleplay because (a) they feel it's unimportant, (b) they think it's kind of wierd, and (c) they think it's unnecessary. It's easy for us to jump all over people like this, but think back to when you were young, playing paper games back in school. I certainly didn't roleplay back in junior high - me and my friends did monty haul hackmaster type campaigns. We sure thought we were roleplaying, but we weren't.

As for grief players (which I suspect what this article was about, deep down), I've found that there are two kinds of grief players. The first is the kind that does not grok that there is another person on the other end of the telephone wire, and/or does not know what is acceptable behavior in this little community that we've created. These people need to be socialized and educated as far as what the standards of the community are - or they will forever do things that tick you off.

The other kind of grief player is the kind that knows full well that there is someone on the other end of the telephone wire, and enjoys tormenting them in order to feel power over them. Unfortunately, there is little good response to these people other than to ban them. Fortunately, there are fewer bad people than you would suppose, but like many things, the bad ones tend to be memories bad enough to make it difficult to remember the pleasant encounters in a MMPRPG.

 Character Weight/Height Customization
From Ubiq on the Stratics DevBoard:
We haven't commented on height and weight yet, because we're not sure we can get it in, quite simply. The problem, perversely, is that of our clothing. If you just paint all of the clothing on over a single mesh, doing height and weight deformation is a simple task. However, once you do what we are doing (i.e. ripping apart your mesh and rebuilding it at run-time, so that clothes actually change the shape of your avatar), height and weight deformation becomes computationally expensive and technically challenging.

This is complicated by clothes that hang. Check out this screenshot: http://origin.stratics.com/sections/sod/screens/origin2.jpg. The coat that he has on hangs on him. When the character animates, it is very difficult to do things like keep the legs from passing through the coat when he runs or fights. Allowing players to grow or shrink their avatar makes this even harder on the art staff.

We still have a couple of avenues that we can explore, and Melior hasn't given up on it yet, but it is a challenge, so I wouldn't count on it until you hear otherwise.

 Grief Players
From Ubiq over on the Origin DevBoard:
You misunderstand.

The commonly accepted definition of a grief player is a player who plays to ruin the good time of other people for no good reason. Grief players are generally considered bad, and the game design needs to try to account for them. Player Killers (PKers) are a subset of grief players, but you don't need to be able to kill someone to cause grief - you can cyberrape them, train monsters on them, steal their loot, etc, etc, etc, etc.

Players who take part in consentual PvP because they are at war with another guild are not considered Grief Players. They are considered PvPers. Game designers like PvP. We wish more people would take part in consentual PvP. PvP can be the very lifeblood of the game.

In short, the term 'grief player' is a shorthand way to refer to troublesome elements in the game, and does not include everyone who engages in player-killing. I would go so far to venture that a fraction of UO's player killers are actually grief players (of course, where that line is is hard to define since it varies from person to person).

 The Success of the UO World Faire
From Ubiq on the Stratics DevBoard:
Depends on how you define success.


* Did we get good feedback from the fans about our products? We got awesome feedback, some of which will undoubtedly be incorporated into both products.
* Did we have a successful unveiling of UO3D? I think so, the buzz about it has been very good.
* Did the people who came feel like they got their money's worth? Judging from the feedback we got, it seems that most of them did.
* Did we make money? We did okay. Not a lot, but that was never really as important as the other goals anyway.
* Did the Origin team need an excuse to stop working and go and drink together? Most assuredly yes.

So by that measure, I guess that it was a success!

Probably our greatest feedback was that people wished that the faire could have been held elsewhere (most frequently in their own hometown =). Granted,
we may have gotten a greater turnout if we had held it in a big city like LA or NY, but it would have had other negatives. For one thing, convention halls are pricier there. For another, the fact that the event was in Austin meant that the MAJORITY of both the UO and the ORIGIN teams were able to attend. If it had been elsewhere, that would likely have been unfeasible.
