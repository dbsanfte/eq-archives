Skills and Skill Trees Explained
The following will hopefully give you a good idea about how the skill trees work in Origin and the benefits they hold. Be sure to also take a look at our example skill trees and our skills list of the skills and trees we know will be in Origin.

What's A Skill Tree
Technically, Origin is level based where upon attaining a level, a player receives skill points to buy skill boxes. Realistically though, you are the sum of your skills and levels are only there to provide a measure of progress for the new player. The crux of Origin's advancement system is the skill tree, each consisting of individual skills called skill boxes arranged into a branching structure. These skill trees closely resemble Civilization or Command and Conquer tech trees. Each skill set, such as combat, magic, blacksmithing, tinkering, etc., will have its own skill trees and these trees vary widely in their size with the smallest having only two skills and the larger ones such as combat having over 150 skills.

What Is A Skill Box?
A skill box is a binary representation of the skill meaning you either have it or you don't. There is no 99.1 in a skill box and you can't go for multiple levels of a skill box like you can in Diablo 2. Most skills cost between 2 and 5 ankhs with the exact price being based on the value of the skill box and the player's race. You may not put more ankhs in a box than it costs. Skill trees start from a few number of these boxes (about a dozen) and expand and unfold quickly as you advance.

What Are The Various Effects Of Skill Boxes
In a single skill tree, there may be a multitude of branches. While some of these will allow you to make new items or cast new spells, others allow you to use pre-existing skills faster, better and easier. For instance, a skill box may affect your combat role, increase your stats or modify the effectiveness of a skill you have.

About How Many Skills Will Be Original Ones And Not Just Improving Ones You Have?
About 50% of the skills are ones that will give you new abilities and not just improve ones you already have.

How Many Skills Are There?
There are "way more than 1000" skill boxes.

How Do You Learn Skills?
When your level increases, you are given skill points called "ankhs" to buy skill boxes from teachers. These will usually cost between 2 and 5 ankhs. Once you get these ankhs, you will never lose them and they may not be given away. If you are, for instance, level 10 and have 35 ankhs, then every level 10 player has 35 ankhs to spend on skills. The skill trees are prerequisite based and high level skills may not be learned without first learning a lot of low level skills.

To learn a skill, you must first find a teacher who teaches it. These teachers are spread throughout the landscape in logical places and the ones who teach you the best skills will be in the most dangerous areas of the game. Learning a skill will be easy at low levels, but it will sometimes be challenging in higher ones. Some of the teachers will also require additional favors.

So I Have To Go To A Teacher?
Yes, you will need to go to a teacher anytime you learn or relearn a skill. Teachers are the interface for gaining skills.

What Happens As Your Level Increase?
Skills will still cost between 2 and 5 ankhs per skill but the number of ankhs you gain decreases the higher the level you are. Thus, the difference between level 1 and 25 is much greater than the difference between level 25 and 50. Thus, getting the last levels are less important that the first ones and shorten the gap between the hardcore players and the casual ones while still rewarding the hardcore players for their dedication.

Are There Level Requirements?
No. There aren't level restrictions on skills like there are in Diablo2.

Will There Be A Level Limit?
At first, yes, there will be a level limit just to be sure a bug didn't get through that lets one advance without bounds. At launch, there will be a level limit of 50.

What Primary Stats Are There? How Do They Improve?
There will be 3 primary stats true to the Ultima heritage (intelligence, dexterity and strength). These will improve via skill trees thus mages will have higher int naturally due to the skill tree they are using and warriors will have higher strength, etc. This removes the macroing problem to set stats in UO and makes sure you won't find 100 str mages running around.

What About Secondary Stats? (i.e. Mana, Stamina, etc.)
Yes, there will be secondary stats but as to exactly what they are, we don't know yet. However, most of them will be largely invisible to the players.

Does Origin Use A Use-Based System? How Do I Get Better?
No, Origin does not use a use-based system. If you want to get better in something, you need to buy more skill boxes in that tree that benefit it. So to get better in sword smithing, you buy more boxes in that branch allowing you to make swords better, faster, etc. For example, let's say we take "Sword Smithing." This skill allows you to craft a few different swords at a beginning level. They won't be very good and you may fail too. Now if you want to get better at making the shortswords, you can pick up additional skills that enable you to craft them faster with less chance of failure. Further into the branch you can make them more durable or do more damage or be more accurate. If you want to make more than just shortswords, you can take up longsword smithing and then vikingswords and then greatswords. Therefore with the same number of skill points you can make really nice shortswords or make beginner level greatswords.

Can I Drop Skills And Reuse The Ankhs? Do Skills Atrophy?
You can absolutely drop skills and reuse the ankhs and skill atrophy is completely voluntary. You will never lose a skill unless you want to lose it. Every two days, a player is allowed to go on a Quest of Enlightenment which allows one to atrophy a skill box and reclaim the ankhs used to get it. The only restriction is that you cannot choose a skill box that is a prerequisite for another skill you have. These ankhs go back into your general pool and then may be used as normal. To sum things up, you have total and complete control over the skills you have and nothing happens to your skills unless you consciously decide to do something, either taking a new one or atrophying one you already have. You will never lose a skill for disuse and you will never gain a skill you don't want.

To stress another point, using this system one cannot turn from a mage into a smith overnight. It would take over a month to do this and the teachers granting the skills may ask tasks of you which would complicate the matter.

Can I Have Ankhs Left Over?
Sure. If you have five ankhs and want a skill costing four and there are no one ankh skills you want, you can just save that last ankh until the next time you level.

Will Skills Affect What I Can Use?
Yes. The most powerful items in the game will require one to have high-level skills in the skill tree to use them. This is a key way to, for example, avoid letting newbies and mages wear plate armor.

Is There A Benefit For Staying In A Single Skill Tree?
Yes, there is. The benefit of staying within a skill tree is not that there is a reduction in skill cost or anything to that effect but that the different branches of a skill tree share many prerequisites. For example, if it takes 90 skill points to master sword smithing and 90 skill points to master axe smithing, one would assume it would take 180 skill points to master them both. However, a number of prerequisite weapon smithing skills augment both swords and axes. As a result, it may take only 60 skill points to master axes once the 90 skill points have been used to master swords. Along the same lines, a sword smith would enjoy a reduction in cost to master chain armor smithing but it would not be as dramatic since the two disciplines share fewer prerequisites. A sword smith would have little to no reduction in cost to master a magic school though since they are completely different skill trees.

Can I Become A Grandmaster?
Yes. When you reach the culmination of a particular branch of a skill tree, you can become a grandmaster. Many of the skill trees have multiple grandmasters. For example, there are 4 or 5 potential grandmaster warriors you can be, each with different specializations and foci. How many ankhs it takes to do this varies. For the most powerful schools of magic and the most gratifying warriors, it will take nearly every ankh to become a single GM in one of these schools. Players with less time can become GMs at slightly lesser schools of magic or combat. Some skills also assume you will need some combat to act as support and thus are cheaper (e.g. thievery).

How Much Of The Skill Trees Will Be Visible?
In order to keep confusion down, inside the game only the skills you can learn or can almost learn will be displayed. However, most of the skill trees will be documented so that you can plan your choices for the entire career of your character. There will still be a few hidden mystery skills though that we will have to discover.

Will Skills Give Titles?
Yes. As you learn milestone skills, you will be granted a title associated with that skill box. In the words of Windfeather, there are "lots and lots of earnable titles." You are allowed to display one of your choice on your paperdoll but there will be a way for people to see the other titles you have learned.

For example, the skill "front kick" will give you the "Kick Boxer" title. As you move further into the tree, your title will be improved ("Journeyman Kick Boxer", "Advanced Kick Boxer", etc.)

What Effect Does Race Play In Skills?
All three races will have basically the same skill trees but some will enjoy a reduced cost. For example, Jukans will find warrior skills require fewer ankhs while Meerlings will find magic skills cheaper. Also, some skills will be unique/specific to a race although there will not be too many of these. For example, "Iolo's Deadly Crossbow Aiming" gives an extra damage bonus to crossbows that only humans may learn. Each race has a set of skill such as this that only they may learn. However, they are making sure there isn't a "best race" for a specific skill tree. In terms of combat, humans get the aforementioned crossbow bonus, Jukan's get a polearm bonus and Meerlings have access to living weaponry.

What Kind Of Trade Skills Will There Be?
There are a lot of trade skills. To name a few, blacksmithing lets you make metal weapons, armor, shields and a number of simple tools. Cooking gives you access to a hundred or so dishes as well as brewing. Tinkering contains most of the technological items such as tech weapons, tech armor and tech tools. There's also tailoring, alchemy, fishing, mining, lumberjacking and bowcrafting to name a few.

How Many Skills Are Devoted To Crafting?
About half of the entire skill system involves harvesting resources and using those resources to create and/or modify items.

If I'm A Smith, Can I Use Combat To Get Skill Points To Put Toward Smithing?
Yes but if you are an accomplished smith and you attempt to advance by killing things, you will find it inefficient. You'd be far better off doing what you know. However, the advancement of a pure craftsman is slower than that of a pure adventurer, the price for being able to advance in relative security.

How Do You Craft Items? Does It Take Time?
A number of items can be made very quickly like in UO but the high end stuff will take a good amount of time to make (as in days). When you craft an item, it begins as a crafting project and as you work on it, it moves closer and closer to completion. This allows you to stop and start the project over time so you don't need to complete it all at once.

Furthermore, crafting high level items generally is a multistep process. Once the basic item is crafted, other crafting actions must be performed on it in order to complete it. For example, a tinker may want to make a really good clockwork longsword. First, he needs a blacksmith to craft him a really good longsword and then the tinker will want to use high end components such as springs and gears and such which require their own specialist to get the best results. Then the tinker needs to take all the pieces and craft the complete clockwork longsword after which he may pass it off to a mage for further enhancement. For some crafting skills, items will be made in this way, using items from other crafters to make your item. In other branches, this occurs much less frequently.

What About Combat vs. Spells?
There will certainly be plenty of skills such that you can devote your entire gaming life to that one skill set. At higher levels of combat, your character will have a number of special abilities such as powerful attack combinations which should be in the same realm of power in combat as spells. The most significant of these abilities are the combos and high level attack combos will enable warriors to unleash a large string of attacks in very rapid succession with other effects as well such as knocking down and stunning your opponent.

Will There Be A Tank Mage Problem?
Absolutely not. In order to be a great warrior or wizard, you will have to devote the majority of your skill points to that discipline. If you try to mix and match something that requires an immense amount of learning, you will find that your abilities pale in comparison to those who have devoted their entire lives to the study. Just in case, some skill boxes are mutually exclusive from each other to make sure this isn't a problem. So if you have a high-level mage skill you may be unable to use a high-level armor skill.

How Do The Skills In The Magic Schools Compare?
Each spell school has its own strengths, weakness and identities. There are some similarities such as all of them have some way to deal damage but each has different recharge times, different reagent costs and differing flexibility.

Will The Archery Skills Require Aiming?
No. All of combat will involve having a current target set. You will autofire at a selected target.

What Kind Of Thief Skills Are There?
There will be a wide range of thieving abilities such as stealing, hiding and stealth. Hiding and stealth should prove to be exciting as the system will be more than simple hide if you succeed and don't hide if you fail. Whether or not you succeed is based on an individual's perception which is another thieving skill allowing you to see hidden enemies. There's also poisoning, backstabbing, lockpicking and trap disarm to name a few.

Will There Be Skills That Benefit A Group?
Yes. You will be able to form up parties and the party leader has access to commands such as enabling party searching, sharing gold/treasure, etc. These parties also allow you to take advantage of party skills that give bonuses to the entire party. One such example is Warrior's Leadership which gives everyone a combat bonus if the leader has this skill.

What About Fun Skills?
There will be fun skills such as juggling and dancing.
