'TWAS THE NIGHT BEFORE CHRISTMAS,
He lived with the feel, of hard and cold steel.
I had come down the stack with presents to give,
and to see just who in this place did live.
I looked all about, a strange sight I did see,
no tinsel, no presents, not even a tree.
No stocking by mantle, just boots filled with sand,
on his rack hung pictures of loved ones and far of lands.
With medals and badges, awards of all kinds,
a sobering thought did come to my mind.
For this place was so different, so dark and so dreary,
the home of a sailor, now I could see clearly.
The sailor lay sleeping, silent, alone,
curled up in his rack in this forty bed home.
Was this the hero of whom I just read?
curled up in a ripped blanket, this rack for a bed?
I realized the families that I saw this night,
owed their lives to these sailors who were willing to fight. 
Soon round the world the children would play, 
and grownups would celebrate a bright Christmas day. 
They all enjoyed freedom each month of the year, 
because of the sailor, like the one lying here. 
I couldn't help wonder how many lay alone, 
on a cold Christmas eave in a land far from home. 
The very thought brought a tear to my eye, 
I dropped to my knees and started to cry. 
The sailor awakened and I heard a rough voice, 
"Santa don't cry, this life is my choice; 
I fight for freedom, to keep my country free, 
my life is my god, my shipmates and me." 
The sailor rolled over and soon drifted to sleep, 
I couldn't control it, I continued to weep. 
I kept watch for hours, so silent and still, 
and we both shivered from the evenings cold chill. 
I didn't want to leave on that cold, dark night, 
this guardian of honor so willing to fight. 
Then the sailor rolled over, with a voice soft and pure, 
whispered, "Carry on Santa, Its Christmas day, all is secure." 
One look at my watch, and I knew he was right. 
"MERRY CHRISTMAS MY FRIEND, AND TO ALL A GOOD NIGHT."
