Professional sales people are an interesting bunch. With the bulk of their time spent out of the office, sales people are largely unsupervised. They are held accountable for their efforts by weekly sales plans and by meeting monthly selling goals or quotas. As long as their numbers are in order, they are free to manage their time as they wish. That is where our two main characters come into play…

The strip revolves around two bumbling, reluctant salesmen, Skip Walker and Hank Slim. Skip and Hank are warm outgoing fellas, but underachievers with little to no ambition. In fact, they would rather do anything but sell! Since they are usually out of the office or “in the field”, they are free to waste their workday pursuing whatever they would rather do. As a result, they develop relationships with, and are harassed by movie theater managers, golf caddies, and video arcade patrons. There is almost no limit to the trouble they can get themselves into while playing hooky from work.

Skip Walker

Skip Walker is the more cautious of the two. He is friendly and agreeable, but like Hank, he would rather waste the day than seize it. Skip means well, and does attempt to make an occasional sale, but always seems to get sidetracked. Still, as long as he has Hank's lead to follow, Skip will continue to zig where he should zag as he bumbles away his selling career.

Hank Slim

Hank Slim is a man's man and the consummate selling professional. At least he has sold himself on that concept. The more outgoing and bumbling of the two, Hank shamelessly justifies his lack of work, or selling achievement as the result of his never ending pursuit of perfection. "Hey, even God needed six days" is a mentality that Hank would eagerly accept. Although his boss may suggest otherwise, Hank would tell you that he does work. He works at fishing, works on his golf game, works on his sun tan…
