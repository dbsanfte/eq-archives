Force Sensitive vs. Jedi
 StarWarsGalaxies - 14:14 EST - jcsmith

Haden Blackman cleared up any questions we may have had about the differences between being Force Sensitive or a Jedi. Here's the real deal Hollyfield...

A Force sensitive in Star Wars continuity is anyone with a connection to the Force. A Jedi is someone who is trained to use the Force for specific ends, within the parameters of the Jedi code.

Star Wars Galaxies will have both Force sensitives and Jedi. Up until this point, we've been using the terms interchangeably, but we would like to call out the fact that both types of characters will exist. In my post yesterday, I made mention of "becoming a Jedi will be blah blah blah," when, in fact, it would probably be more correct to state "becoming Force sensitive will be...."

The general progression for a character will involve first becoming Force sensitive, then becoming a Jedi. Not all Force sensitives will become Jedi... Therefore, a Force sensitive is not necessarily a Jedi, but a Jedi is always Force sensitive.

Becoming Force sensitive will be rare and mysterious. Capitalizing on that Force sensitivity to become a basic Jedi will be difficult and also a bit mysterious. Once a player attains the status of "Jedi," progression within the Jedi ranks will be pretty straightforward.

Long before the current battle took shape, the two companies fought to win over independent game developers, who are critical to any platform's success.     Dark Age of Camelot Preview
         DarkAgeofCamelot - 1:24 EST - jcsmith

    Dark Age of Camelot appears to be picking up a lot of steam recently. PC.IGN became the latest site to serve up a preview of the MMORPG earlier today. Here's a sneak peek...

    The kicker for this particular MMORPG is the use of realms as enemies. They even go so far as to make it so that nobody from different realms can converse with each other. There will be no treaties and no mercy. Anytime you see a player or NPC from another realm, you can feel free to kill it. Of course, raids aren't the only way that you can steal those relics. You could also leave that up to the professionals. This is the first game that I've really ever seen that truly encourages players to sink themselves deep into the solitary role of a master thief. If you have taken the right path and practiced all of the right skills, it is possible for you to slip in and steal a relic without anyone being the wiser.

    Romtec's Trios, Internal Review
         Hardware - 14:46 EST - jcsmith

    Gaming in 3D has another hardware review for you guys and gals today. This time it is of the Romtec Trios. Here's a clip...

    Since the Trios is merely a supped-up switch, it has no software or drivers. What it DOES come with is a manual, 4 IDE cables (1 for Trios to Mobo, 3 for Trios to Drives), and 2 power connectors (one for Power Supply to Trios, and another for Trios to Drives). The manual was well laid out and thorough, although some of the pictures of the cable installation were cluttered, so it was hard to understand what the manual was telling you to do by pictures alone. Then again, if you can install a harddrive, you can install the Trios. Installation was a snap. Just find a free 5.25" drive slot, slide it in, screw it in, and then just plug in the IDE and power cables. Done. Now, whenever you want to switch between drives, power down, press the appropriate switch, and then power back on. Bam!

    Age of Mythology Preview
         RealTimeStrategy - 14:38 EST - jcsmith

    There is a preview of Ensemble Studios' Age of Mythology on Gamespot. Peep this...

    In many ways, Ensemble's new game looks similar to Age of Kings. But it is different enough to not be a sequel. According to Codesigner Ian Fischer, they want to keep the gameplay that made Age fun, but also do things differently. "We wanted to give people a new gameplay experience. And mythology lets us do that," he says. By adding the presence of gods and mythology, they are doing just that. Functionally, Age of Mythology will play much like Age of Kings and other RTS games. You start from a small town center and build up to an empire, exploring the world and dominating your rivals. But in Age of Mythology, through it all, you'll be calling on the gods for divine inspiration and intervention, whether it's to bless your crops, send you mythological troops, or smite your enemies with fire from the heavens. It's this latter aspect, this fantastic element of divine intervention, that really makes Age of Mythology stand out, and it should make it a much more exciting game than its already great predecessors.
