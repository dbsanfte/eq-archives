Step 2: Select a Domain Name

Once you've decided on a good Web host, you should choose a domain name. A domain name is a customized online business address; it's a unique name owned by you that identifies your Web site. For example, Norwest Associates owns the domain name nwassociates.com; Snap owns the domain name snap.com. Compare the following URLs:

1. http://www.hostingcompany.com/members/yourbusiness
2. http://www.yourbusiness.com

In the first URL, the domain name "hostingcompany.com" is owned by someone else: the hosting company. With this URL, your business name wouldn't be in your email address. Instead, your email address would be you@hostingcompany.com. The second URL contains an example of a custom domain name that you own: "yourbusiness.com." With a custom domain name, you can have domain email addresses such as sales@yourbusiness.com and support@yourbusiness.com.

A domain name helps you create and promote a professional, prestigious Internet presence. It establishes your company name with a worldwide audience. It's also a point of contact where prospective customers can learn about your products, and current customers can find support after the sale.

Step 3: Develop a Web Site

Once you've registered your domain name, decide the goals you want your site to meet and create an organized outline of everything you want to include in your site. Maybe you want to post business hours, contact information, directions to your location, and a short biography of your company. Perhaps you'd like to include pictures of your products, details of your services, and a searchable inventory database. You may want to sell products or provide online customer service to your existing customers. Think of questions your customers ask most often; use this information to help decide what to put on your site, such as a frequently asked questions area. Your outline will help you determine the focus of your site and provide a good starting point for the building process.

Once you've created your outline, there are a number of ways to build your site:

Step 4: Add E-Commerce

Adding electronic commerce to your Web site lets your customers order and buy products online 24 hours a day, 7 days a week. It can even allow customers to pay online using their credit cards.

Studies show that shoppers are more willing to provide credit card information online if they have a secure transmission. Secure Socket Layer (SSL) technology allows your customers to send sensitive payment information to you online in an encrypted format. The information is scrambled so no one between your customer's computer and your computer can read it. Norwest Associates' E-Commerce Solutions use SSL technology so your customers can buy products online and protect their personal information.

E-Commerce sites use special software to create online "shopping carts." Shopping carts track items ordered by customers, allowing them to add or remove items. When a customer decides to "check out" and purchase the items in a shopping cart, the E-Commerce software sends the order information to you, the merchant. The software can also send a receipt back to your customer.

If you'd like to add these features to your Web site, check out our E-Commerce Solutions. One is sure to meet your needs.

Step 5: Market Your Site

Once you've built a well-designed site, you'll need to promote it to your audience. Making sure your site is listed in the top search engines is very important.  However, you will find that to get to the top of these search engines requires many hours of work.

One of our favorite search engines, is Google.  Recently new to the Internet, this search engine has been making a big name for itself.

Start by registering your site with the major search engines: Yahoo, WebCrawler, Excite, HotBot, Lycos, and AltaVista. Look for an "add URL" or "add your site" option at each of these sites, as well as links to other search engines. You should also read and follow each search engine's rules and tips for submitting your site. Some search engine sites charge for a expedite fee.  Consider paying this fee to insure that your site is listed. Registering. Many search engines offer additional advertising packages.

You can also visit places like Submit-it! to simultaneously submit your site to several search engines. Some of these services will even optimize your pages to maximize the number of times your site gets brought up in a search result.

There are other effective ways to market your site as well.

  • If you have company letterhead, business cards, yellow page ads or other advertising material, add your URL and email address to them. Consider an additional listing in the white pages to include your Web site.
  • Notify customers of your new site by mail, or use direct email to get the word out. Be sure to use a list provider that offers "opt-in" email lists whose recipients specifically opted to receive marketing materials via email.
  • You can set up an affiliate program at your Web site. An affiliate program can increase your site traffic, create qualified leads, or generate sales. Visit ClickTrade for more information.
  • Use banner ads to advertise your site. Microsoft bCentral offers a banner ad program, plus other promotional tools.
