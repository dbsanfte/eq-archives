Ogre

Mykil's View
Trolls are stupid. It is an undeniable fact of nature. They are big, dumb, green, ugly, and smelly creatures who pride themselves on being able to smash things nearly flat with their clubs. Their towns are composed of ramshackle buildings and smelly garbage heaps and refuse piles within a badly constructed yet very effective wall, in which body parts and bones are strewn about the streets, and the flies are bigger than the humies (before they been smashed), but not by much. But given all this, a troll cannot stand up to the raw glory of the near total ineptitude that is inherent to the race known as "ogres." If it requires any sort of knowledge whatsoever, nothing is incapable of being able to do it infinitely better than an ogre, and the ogres pride themselves for this trait (even thought they are too stupid to realize it).

Just about as smart as a pebble (or less) and many (many many...) times as large and heavy, an ogre is as dumb as they get. Nothing currently known to exist surpasses the ogre in raw stupidity. Thinking hurts them, smashing and stomping elates them. Peeling the carcasses of their prey from under their ponderous feet and from their clubs and then eating them is also a favorite passtime of the ogres. The only different between ogres and trolls here is trolls bother to bring what they eat back to town. Most ogres know not of such a concept. Some would think that, for this reason, the ogre towns would be somewhat cleaner than a troll town, but this is false. Ogres are so sticky with the grime that they pick up during their travels that what they miss during feeding literally sticks to them. Then, when they return to town, some of these assorted goodies peel off of their own accord as they rest and regain their strength. A bath is another concept most ogres have never heard of, the extent of their hygiene being scratching off dead animal refuse using sticks and eating whatever gets caught between their fingers and toes.

Ogres are best known for their physical strength. They are a simple race and know very little in the ways of magic, but a few who are smarter than the rest do know a little about tribal magics and some have even picked up some necromancy, although nothing so complex that they would hurt themselves thinking about. This is the maximum extent of their knowledge, and ogres can be powerful semi-mage types, but ogres are not renowned for their exploits in these fields. Ogres are renowned for their club swings. You want something flattened flatter than a sheet of paper? Find an ogre. But beware. Oftentimes they will forget what you want them to do unless they know who you are exceptionally well, and being confused by your civilized ways, will become angered at the mental strain that even your simplest comments put on them, and will smash you instead. When handling a conversation with an ogre, take care to always talk in single syllables. They actually might stand a chance of understanding you then.

Bottom Line
If you have fun playing stupid, an ogre is a good choice. The least civilized of the races and by far the most detestable, ogres can be fun to play, but only if you enjoy playing a stupid character. They make the best warriors and good simple spell casters for stamina and strength, but lack regeneration which the trolls, in exchange for some those statistics, have. Ogre spell casters also have the best stamina of any other spell casting class in the game (although only two such classes are available to the ogres). Ogres and trolls can both be hard to roleplay, but once the technique to being "stupid" is mastered, it can be quite gratifying. More often than not, I expect to find lots of ogres around with unusually high communicative skills. It can be hard to remember that an ogre speaks like this - "Ug ug, me hungry, me smash humie, swallow humie. Humie taste good but look ugly. Me eat ugly humie!" - as opposed to this - "Well, I have to be off now. Im famished and a couple of friends and I are going hunting for dinner, which will be accompanied by dwarven wine while we eat." I am not saying that this is necessarily so. This is only what I expect, but I am fervently hoping that I will be proved wrong in this, but that is beside the point. To roleplay an ogre one must keep his/her mind on the fact that they are playing a ogre, and a smart ogre does not exist. I mean if you wanted to roleplay an ogre that was smart, by all means do so, but for the advance in intelligence to be believable at all (which is a very small possibility even if you roleplay this for years) would have to be really....really........slow. Im not talking a few months either. An ogre can be a very fun race to play if you remain in character. If you don't remember that simple fact, you had better think up a really good excuse for why you, as an ogre, are talking like a human. That is the trick to playing an ogre: think stupid. And the best way to do that with an ogre is to pick up your club and "Ug ug. Smash thingies. Eat for food."
