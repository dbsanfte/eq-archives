Shaman

Mykil's View
The clerics are often associated with the large scale healing and defense spells which are their signatures. These souls, having devoted their lives to the healing of Norrath of pain, wounds, and undead, are recognized for their valiance. The clerical order is known throughout the land, and no one who has lived has never heard of one at one time or another. But once we get to the great frontier lands of Norrath, where "civilization" has yet to expand to, clerics are few and far between. Men and women live by their wits, having to fight to survive. Through all the strife that their people face, there are a few men and women who urge them on, both back at their ramshackle towns and on their monster infested fields. These wise ones - shamans as they prefer to be called - are the spellcasters of the uncivilized. Through the use of ancient lore handed down from their forefathers, they can weave magics that other magic users, for all their knowledge, can only wish to understand.

The shaman is a tribal witch doctor and/or tribe chief of sorts. Found among barbarians, ogres, and trolls, this class tends to think much more than other classes in these races. Still, in the cases of ogres and trolls, whether they burn off your skin with a spell before or after they bash you is usually the extent of their decision making. Barbarians are the smartest of the lot, and are the most accepted type of shamans in Norrath. One will often find a good few barbarian shamans in any town at any given time. They are wanderers by nature, as barbarians are nomads. The other races are also far spread, but not in the towns.

The powers of shamans are extremely varied. Not bound to one discipline like clerics and wizards, shamans share a little magic from all callings. They have spells that enchant, attack, defend, heal, conjure, and perform other functions normally limited to other magical classes of a single doctrine. Clerics often have to hang behind the main group in a fight. Having low stamina and only a few attack spells, they are better suited to supporting the fighters who take the brunt of the attack. Shamans have no such limitation. Having been raised in savage lands, shamans have high stamina. Although limited to blunt weapons, they can make good use of those blunt weapons. They make relatively good fighters, so they can survive on their own. They will never equal the raw melee power of other fighting based classes, but they are solid warriors as well as magic users. Magically, their power lies in simple tribal supersitions. The spirits of nature, such as wolves, snakes, monkeys, and other animals, give the shamans their power, and despite their simplicity, a shaman's power is not to be underestimated.

Bottom Line
Simplicity is beauty. The shaman is the perfect character for people who enjoy simplicity, for the shaman is a simple character. From this same simplicity is where the charm of a shaman springs from. Being of simple minds and simple ideas, shamans lack the complexity yet retain all the depth that all the other classes exhibit, and therefore maintain the same level of roleplayability. You could claim to hail from "the lands where ice is ground" and that you are on a journey for a treasure to help your tribe (beware rogues). Or, you could claim a mystical spirit came to you in a vision, and sent you to a specific place to find something, yet you don't (or do, your preference here) know what that something is. Shamans can also be evil characters, leading groups of savages in raids on farms and towns, causing all sorts of mischief like slaughtering all the cows and stealing the meat. But to be a good shaman you must use your mind and muscle as well as the spirits of the world. They are all around you and aid you. They give you the strength you need to survive in a harsh world. Forsake them, or anger them to the point to forsake you, and the spirit of the wolf might not come in time to save you from your enemies.
