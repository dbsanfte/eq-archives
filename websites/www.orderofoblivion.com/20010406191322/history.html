The History of The Order
- -As recorded by Father Charoth BloodOath, Inquisitor of Shadows, Patriarch of Oblivion
In the beginning we were the Brotherhood of Pentagram, formed by the Master a century ago to consolidate and explore the power of Necromancy. And in spirit, we are still of the Pentagram, tho in latter days much has changed, through the Masters Will and the will of others. Of the high history of Pentagram much is told in our archives, but the account of the lost TombLord Frostmarrow is considered the most accurate, as he was present from the start.

This is the account of the beginning, as told to me in my apprenticeship, by the TombLord Frostmarrow himself:

In my youth, more then a century ago, I chose to follow the dark path of necromancy. My reasons are a tale for another time, but the Art in those days was shunned even more then now, as much out of ignorance as fear. Necromancers were a scattered, fractioned group, rarely assembling, choosing instead to pursue their studies in solitude. Each kept to his own, perhaps taking an apprentice to help with his research in exchange for knowledge.
A day came when I, and apparently many others, received a summons by a strange messenger: a large raven, with wings which I can only describe as the color of darkness. Not black, but the opposite if light. The handwritten note born by this bird called us to an assembly on faraway Odus, in Toxxulia forest.
I was tempted to dismiss the message out of hand, but something in the words compelled me. The style of the writing, even the hand, seemed familiar, as if I had seen it before in some dusty tome. Whatever the reason, I undertook the long passage to Erudin, and to the Forest beyond.
Arriving I founda great host of Necromancers assembled, all in confusion as to the summons. Some clamored against the meeting; others spoke in twos or threes about the mysterious note, and the handwriting they felt they had seen before. At the last, all settled in to meditation or sleep or quiet study beneath the guard of their undead servants, awaiting the dawn and, it was hoped, some answers.
As day approached, the apprentices set to watch the camp spied a figure approaching from the hills far off. An old man, ancient even, clad in ragged robes and leaning upon a staff. Ravens circled above him, a black cloud of night denying the sun even as it rose.
As the Old One entered the circle of those assembled, he was beset upon all sides with questions. “Who are you to summon us here, and by what power?” they cried. The Old man lifted his head to meet their words; tho his voice was low, it rung clear through the throng. “I am Pentagram. I have come to show you the true Power of the Art.”
Then began the Great Debate, as the Old Man (who we now call the Master) related his plan for us. To unite the necromancers of Norrath in our studies; to bring our power together as one force, that the strength of each could strengthen all. The talk lasted much of the morning, and tho many, especially among the young, found the words intriguing, the majority were opposed to the plan. It seemed the conclave was at an end.
But alas, for as the talk increased, and calm words became angry threats, the guard upon the camp turned its interest away from the forest and to the debate within. And seeing the weakness, and the rich equipage of the host (for many ofthe necromancers were wealthy, and vain), the brigands of the forest struck the camp, in force full four score. I believe that was I first to realize the attack, and using my power I sent a chill through the necromancers there, to warn them of the danger.
It may be that my action saved many, for the bandits were upon us, slashing with steel as skeletal warriors leapt to intercede for their masters. Whatever my part, the brigands had made a grave error, for the power of the assembled necromancers unleashed was far more then they expected, and they were utterly destroyed.
But the battle may not have ended there, for many of the elder mages sensed conspiracy on the part of the Old One, and they turned to confront him, alone as he now appeared.
Smiling, the old man lifted his arms, and began an incantation. First whispered, then with full voice, and then like thunder in the mountains, his dark litany rolled over the crowd as the stood in awe. These words of power had never been heard, or even hinted at in all their studies! One by one, the fallen forms of the bandits began to rise and walk to the bidding of the Old One. And as the ranks of undead, the full four score, settled in around him, he smiled and said, “I am Pentagram. I have come to show you the true Power of the Art.”
So was the Brotherhood of Pentagram born, in the vision of the Master.

-- From THE BEGINNING by Frostmarrow, TombLord of Pentagram


While this eyewitness account is considered a factual record of events, as the word of a TombLord should not be doubted, there are left some questions in need of explanation. The identity of the Master is shrouded; some might infer him to be the great Archmage Miragul. This explanation has been discounted, however, as all reports of Miragul indicate he destroyed himself in a failed attempt to achieve Lichdom. Further, the level of power demonstrated in the ability to control multiple undead is unheard of; certainly the feat has never been repeated. Regardless, the existence of the Master is a certainty, as is his Will in our works, as will be revealed later.
