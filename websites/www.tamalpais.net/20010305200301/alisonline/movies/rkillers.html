I want my MTV: The Replacement Killers isn't worth the $7.50

"I like action movies," my fiance said as we exited the theater after seeing The Replacement Killers. "Nothing happened in that movie."

Produced by John Woo, renowned Hong Kong director of classic action films like The Killer and the recent blockbuster Face/Off, and directed by Antoine Fuqua, The Replacement Killers glides along on a well-greased track to nowhere. "Slick" might be the best way to describe it; director Fuqua's claim to fame is a Coolio music video, and like an MTV clip The Replacement Killers unfolds scene after scene of smooth, uninterrupted gunfire with a thumping techno score. Problem is, most MTV videos have more plot than this film.

Hong Kong action star Chow Yun-Fat is the righteous assassin John Lee, a stony-faced, debonair type with excellent sunglasses and a really good tailor. In debt to Wei, a sinister underground lord of some sort, Lee promises to complete one more favor to even the score. The favor: Target the seven-year-old son of police detective Stan Zedkov, the man who killed Wei's own son during a drug bust. Oh, and make sure he dies in his father's arms.

Of course, honorable family man Lee can't pull the trigger, and as he scrambles to leave Los Angeles to return to his own family in China, he's hounded by scores of Wei's machine-gun-toting hit men. So Lee gets to shoot a lot of guns. Along the way he picks up Mira Sorvino as tough broad Meg Coburn, a passport-forging street chick who also gets to shoot a lot of guns. She also gets to wear a lot of black lingerie-looking outfits and some pretty atrocious blood-red lipstick.

So there are some chase scenes with guns, and some hide-and-seek gun ambush scenes, and a few slow-motion pastiches of people yelling, running, and shooting guns. All of this takes place in your generic action-movie world: the trendy nightclub, the docks at night, the slummy apartment building, the parking garage. And there's the appropriately lavish palace of archvillain Wei, done up as a large Asian-decorated cliché that looks like a revamped set from Mortal Kombat.

There's not much in the way of character development, which doesn't seem to disappoint Chow or the Oscar-winning Sorvino. Chow's main purpose is to shoot guns and look cool. Sorvino's character seems to like hanging out in her underwear forging passports; she gets lines like, "What you need is gonna cost you. At least a grand." You talkin' to me?

If the movie's characters were one-dimensional at the expense of really great action sequences, this might be forgivable. But The Replacement Killers just slides right by without any thrilling Hollywood destruct-o-matic wonders. In Face/Off, John Woo gave us a spectacular chase scene with jumbo jets and a slow-motion shoot-em-up to "Somewhere Over the Rainbow;" the best Fuqua can manage is a limp waste of ammunition in a car wash.

There are some interesting camera angles in The Replacement Killers, and if you're extremely passionate about smoke, broken glass, and gunfire, the movie may prove worthwhile. But action film aficionados looking for the next Indiana Jones gunfight on a moving truck won't find it here.
