Basic Farm Facts

   Peter, Dedie, Carl and Peggy decided to buy the farm over lunch at Friendly's in Ware in April, 1972. We had fallen in love with the location at the end of a dirt road, on a knoll at the head of a beautiful valley, but didn't know how to make a living in such an isolated spot. Jimmy and Pat White, the Lithuanian couple who worked the farm as a dairy operation were sad about having to auction their cows so the four of us decided to make an offer for cows, equipment and all. We paid their asking price which was $72,000 for 100 acres, 45 milking cows and about 20 young stock, all the farm machinery and most of the furniture in the house. It even included Jack, the collie dog.

   Since none of us had ever lived on a farm or knew anything about milking cows or haying, Jimmy and Pat decided to live with us for the summer and teach us the basics. Luckily Peter and Carl were very mechanical and handy and Peggy and Dedie were good with the cows. Also we were lucky to have wonderful neighboring farmers like Bob Klem and his father Eddie who helped us with haying and vet problems.

   Peter's brother (and Carl's cousin) Steve moved in that first summer. Over the first several years five children were added-Simon, Rose, Sophie, Galen and Eli. Steve also married Debbie and she moved in too. As space was getting pinched, Dedie and Peter fixed up and moved into the attached L of the house with Simon and Sophie.

   Many people came to help out with the farm, staying anywhere from a weekend to two to four years. These included Frank, Ian, and Rid as long- termers and many others for shorter periods of time. Each person added his/her particular flavor and energy to the farm and we still look forward to their visits. We were and are also fortunate to have friends from all over the U.S., from Japan, Nepal, and Italy come to visist.

The physical farm is always changing. Cows, horses, pigs, chickens, dogs and cats have come and gone. There are always works in progress. The people composition fluctuates too, especially now that the five children have grown up and moved on. But everyone still visits when they can and the farm lives in everyone's hearts.
