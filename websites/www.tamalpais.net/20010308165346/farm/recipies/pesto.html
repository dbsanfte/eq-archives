The Basil is the thing...

Fresh

  • Fresh basil right from the garden is best. If you have fresh basil, pick the tops of the plants where there are 4 leaves attached together…especially the young, tender ones. I don't like to use the little seed clusters but some people do. For four people, fill a quart bowl kind of packed down a little. When you get inside, pick the leaves off the stems which are sort of bitter.
Store-bought
  • If you buy basil in the grocery story, use two clusters for four people and pick the leave off as with garden basil.
Home Frozen
  • We freeze a lot of basil and use it to make pesto through the winter. You pick the basil in late summer when there's too much of it to eat fresh. Pick off the leaves and grind them to a paste with some olive oil in a food processor. Don't add anything to the basil except the olive oil. Push the paste into ice cube trays and freeze it. Then eject the frozen cubes and store them in the freezer in a plastic bag so you can use the ice trays for other things. If you are using frozen basil like this, use four cubes for four people.

Ingredients

  • Basil - per above
  • Garlic - 3 cloves
  • Walnuts - 1 cup
  • E.V.O.O.* - as needed (see directions below)
  • Salt - a level teaspoon
  • Parsley - ½ cup (optional but nice)

Directions:

Put a four quart pot full of water on the stove to boil for the pasta. I like linguini.

Put the basil in a food processor (that's the short, fat machine with the big blade, not the tall one that you make frappes in). If it's frozen basil you need to thaw it first. You can do this by putting it in a plastic bag tightly tied and leaving it in the water that's heating for the pasta. Throw the garlic cloves and nuts into the machine and turn it on. Add the parsley if you want to. Dribble the EVOO into the mixture as it's grinding up. The olive oil will make the mixture just loose enough to not stick to the sides of the machine but fall into the center and really get all mixed up. Pour the salt in as the last mixing is going on. The mixing only takes a minute or less.

Scoop the pesto out of the machine and put it into a small mixing bowl. Put the pasta into the boiling water and cook it not too long. When it's about done, take a ladle of the hot water from the boiling pasta and put it into the pesto mix and stir it around with a fork.

When you serve it, it's nice to put some pine nuts on top with some fresh-grated parmesan cheese. The pine nuts are too expensive to actually put into the pesto.

serves four people

*Extra Virgin Olive Oil…it really is better than the other kinds.
