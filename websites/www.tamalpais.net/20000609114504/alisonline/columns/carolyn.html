Mad About Carolyn
Newsweek sets the style for the next generation of American women

Finally, I have a role model.

I think I speak for many women of my generation, undefined, apathetic Xers that we are, when I say to the media: thank you. The cynical twentysomethings have their own Jackie O: JFK Jr.’s new wife, Carolyn Bessette-Kennedy, has been elevated to icon status ever since she said “I do” in a white satin slip this September.

What a relief! Now I know what I’m wearing to my wedding; the Dress was on the front page of every paper, and copies of it will be parading down the runway this fall. And as far as I can tell, the media blitz is only beginning. Tabloids report that my Icon is being pursued by every major women’s magazine for a cover spot; she’s even made it onto the cover of Newsweek.

It is heartening to know that in an age where women are celebrated for orbiting the planet for six months, marrying well and looking pretty can still get you on Newsweek’s cover. Touting the newly married Mrs.JFK Jr. as the “new icon of American style in the ‘90s,” Newsweek dedicated a recent issue to the research of “The Carolyn Style.”

Some of the timeless lessons we can learn from the newly proclaimed First Lady of Fashion:

  • It doesn’t matter what you do in life, it matters where you shop. “Wearing a long, clingy black dress, black shawl and pearls, her most notable accessory was a shopping bag from Barney’s,” says the first paragraph of Newsweek’s cover story. “Details on Carolyn are still sketchy, but this much is certain: she knows how to shop.” Forget college, employment, community service, scientific research. Just carry that department store bag and you’ll never have to write a resume again.
  • Stop knocking yourself out trying to write that novel! Art need go no further than the closet. “I’ve seen her wear an inexpensive T-shirt and an expensive jacket. That shows personal expression,” designer Michael Kors told Newsweek.
  • The meaning of life: Be Thin. Women have been subconsciously programmed through media images to want to look like models, and it’s a great relief to be told outright that being thin is actually critical. “Don’t be overweight,” Newsweek cautions in a Carolyn-style “How to Snag Your Man” sidebar. “Slimmed down since her more voluptuous college days, Carolyn is a svelte size 6.” The cover story tells us on the same page about Carolyn’s “remarkably fat-free physique -- six feet tall and a svelte size 6.” Did I mention that Carolyn is a size 6? Oh, I’m sorry. A svelte size 6.
  • Spend countless hours to look like you just threw yourself together. “You know it took work to get it to look that unstudied,” Allure magazine’s Linda Wells said of Carolyn’s famous Style. Women don’t have anything better to do with their time, right?
  • Those popularity-contest social skills you thought were exclusive to high school really are important in the adult world. Newsweek refers to Carolyn’s “formidable social skills,” and quotes a Boston Globe source who describes her as a “good schmoozer, a good networker.”
  • A caption underneath a picture of the newlyweds says it all: “She knows how to keep her man happy.” Well, I am truly relieved. I was lost without a role model, envious of my mother’s generation when pillbox hats and a strand of pearls were a clear and attainable goal. Newsweek’s article has defined for me the “very ‘90s nexus” that is Carolyn, a merger of “fashion, sex and publicity.”

Now that my values are straight, I can get to work on imitating the latest apple of the media’s eye. Thank you, American media, for showing me the way. I’m bagging that paper I have to write, working out, and heading to Bloomingdale’s.

Published in the San Francisco Chronicle, 11/15/96
