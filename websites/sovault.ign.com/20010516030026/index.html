Monday, May 7, 2001

Computer Games Magazine article update

Well it appears that Sovereign has changed quite a bit since we last had any significant information release on the game. I should also note that the screenshots in the article look very nice. Here's a small snippet of the article mentioned last night in the June issue of Computer Gaming Magazine.
A player in Sovereign will have a homeworld where he keeps his units when he's not in a battle and where research is conducted in real time whether he's logged on or not. It can take real world days, or even weeks, to complete a particular technology. When a player is in the game, he'll usually be fighting on one of Sovereign's contested planets. These planets are reached by transports, which have a limited amount of room. The choice of what you bring from your homeworld is a crucial part of the strategy.

I suggest picking up a copy of CGM if you wish to read more of this article.
- posted by LAR @ 7:05 PM PDT

Sunday, May 6, 2001

Computer Games Magazine article

Dachannien posted the following message on the official forums today about an article he saw in the June issue of Computer Games Magazine. There are some intesting things mentioned in Dachannien's post. I'll see if I can find a copy of the magazine and post more detailed information.
Dunno if it's been mentioned on the boards here anywhere, because I haven't been reading up a lot recently, but I thought I'd mention this to you guys.

There's an article in the June issue of Computer Games Magazine that talks about both Planetside and Sovereign. The screenshots for Planetside are pretty incredible, and it looks like it has a whole bunch of potential (too bad the whole FPS thing just tends to stress me out!).

The second half of the article talks about Sovereign, and while (to be brutally honest) the screenshots don't come close to competing with Planetside's visuals - and yes, I'm sure at this stage there's a whole lot of improvement planned - there was also a whole lot of talk about the redesigned game concept, which was something I guess I missed after I stopped reading the boards here.

The article includes items like GZ saying that choosing your starting resources for entering a game was a lot like building a M:TG deck, as you will take different things with you in your limited transport space depending on your goals for the game. It also intimates (but doesn't outright say) that the city concept is a lot less important than originally planned. And the theme has moved drastically from the original of current-day warfare to relatively far-future combat (especially considering that you source your starting resource from your home planet).

Now, maybe this is just me being the last to know, but that's a whole lot different from the Sovereign we'd been arguing about here for many months I guess time will tell whether it proves more popular, as well as more implementable....

- Dachannien

Thursday, April 26, 2001

Ah, capitalism

Froech made these comments today on Pay to play vs Ads in play, on the official forums:
You know, to be honest, I don't know the answer to that question...

But I'll bet if you call the folks here at the station, they'd be quick to give you an answer. I'd be quite suprised if you couldn't pay ahead as much as you wanted to.

In the past, I'd considered the possibility of selling ads like hatechaucer suggested, as a way of defraying the cost to the consumer, but like many other people seem to feel, the idea rankled me from the player perspective.

The fact seems to be, these days on the internet, that you have to pay something to get good content. Free (or cheap) seems to be going the way of the dodo.

Some of this is bad, some is good. I don't mind weeding out the chaff to get to the grain, as long as the grain is good.

On the other hand, I understand your dilemma. I too want to try as many games as possible, and I don't have a salary that is out of this world to allow me to do that. So, I pick and I choose carefully, much like you are. It isn't a perfect solution, but I'm not sure I have a better one yet. I haven't given up looking, though.

Still, putting ads inside a game has some comedic possibilities. Imagine playing in EQ, and going to your favourite merchant, and finding he is selling Adidas and Nike shoes for your ranger, so you have to run over to the Wells Fargo bank to get your silvers, and then take the Toyota lift to get up into Kelethin,

Frankly, I'm not wild about sponserships getting the name in the title of the event (it's the Indy 500, not the Coca Cola Indianapolis 500 brought to you by Firestone or somesuch), but in the world we live in, we have to deal with some of this.

I recently read an article on another website complaining about how the computer games companies had sold out (he wanted to see nudity in the games is what I got out of his discussion). I don't see us as having sold out. However, when you see stuff like in game sponsorship, you can say we have

Froech

Wednesday, April 18, 2001

More on balancing

Today, on the official forums, Froech made these comments on the balancing Sovereign.
Some interesting points here.

We're certainly well aware of some of the issues, and continue to wrestle with the issues, and will likely do so for quite a long time. But these are indeed issues that need to be addressed when dealing with the persistance issue, and the newbie/veteran concern.

It is hard to make any points of similarity to EQ or other RPGs, because this is a real time strategy game, and doesn't have the same type of play elements. But the issues are certainly germane in both cases (and in fact in every subscription based game in the world). You want to have people enjoy themselves as much the first time out as the hundreth. These are the issues that we're working on, and continue to discuss on a daily basis. And we don't think we're beating a dead horse, either.

In the grand scheme of things, this is likely to be one of the most difficult issues any game will deal with, and we want to be as successful as possible. We want this game to be as popular 20 years from now as it will be when we release it. We're pouring our hearts into it... we want it to be enjoyed forever.

And I'm against monetary discounts myself, since I want to continue eating at least once a week.

Froech

Tuesday, April 17, 2001

Money makes the world go round

Froech made this post on the official forum today talking about where money should come from in Sovereign and asking for your opinion on the subject.
Carefully points the team at this thread.
Most interesting discussion, folks. Hard for me to agree or disagree, although I don't think we'll be having slaves in our game.

A question for you all is where does money come from? Does your population produce it? Do you have to sell stuff to get money?

The reason I ask is that if it is just generated by population, what is to keep me from sitting in a game for huge amounts of time, amassing wealth, and then jumping into a game where someone doesn't have the same assets as I do?

Granted, I'm extending this to the ridiculous, but I want you to think about it. It does tie into the discussion we've had before about how to allow newbies to play and enjoy as much as the veterans can play and enjoy.

Frankly, I don't have all the answers. But I want you all to lose as much sleep as I am over the issues

And let's keep this discussion going. I'd certainly like to hear additional points of view.

Froech

No Beta in '01

Clint has posted a new producer letter on Sovereign's official site. In it he revealed what's sure to be topic of descussion for a while, "the beta test process is not planned for this year". Here's a snippet of the letter:
The development of Sovereign has been a challenge in many areas and I am sure that many of you have been waiting to hear some news surrounding the Beta test. We are currently not discussing information regarding the Beta testing of the game and, to quell some rumors, the beta test process is not planned for this year. We have a duty to get this game out as the first truly Massively Multiplayer RTS game; and we do not want to sacrifice quality or depth to meet an early release date.

Monday, April 9, 2001

More bang for your buck.

Froech made comments today on the official forums on multi-game subscriptions.
Absolutely, the "higher ups" both see this stuff (when I summarize it for them) and read it (which they do on their own time, too!).

The concept of a multi-game subscription is not new in the industry, either. I can't discuss anything about it here, but certainly at my last company we were working on something like that for the last several years.

It was oddly hard to implement, at least in that system (I'm not involved in our billing system here, so I don't know). But as this market matures, as you all have eloquently pointed out, there is greater competition for the consumer dollar, and the company that can offer the best "bang for the buck" will do the best.

You can interpret that both as the best game, and the best number of games for a single price.

I am not making any promises, or foreshadowing anything, but I personally think you'll see this kind of model everywhere that there are multiple games, in the next few years.

Froech
