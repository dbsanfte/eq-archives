Saturday, March 24, 2001

Are you a loser or just AWOL?

Froech posted this hypothetical situation yesterday, on the official forums, and asked for some discussion on the topic.
Wonderful question... to which I have an associated question.
What should happen to someone who abandons a game when they are losing?

Let us assume there is some penalty (or perhaps just no reward?) for not winning a game. What do you do to keep people from bailing out early to keep from being tarred with the "loser" label?

Lets say LAR and I are fighting together as allies against Iron_Fist's empire, and we're getting our clock cleaned, because Froech's just a wimp, and Iron_Fist has more resources available to build units. Suddenly, Froech packs up and leaves, before we reach either victory or defeat. Certainly LAR will think again before setting up an alliance with that chicken Froech, but should there be an additional penalty to him?

And if there is an additional penalty, are there loopholes? What if Froech's hard drive crashed, and he disconnects, leaving his units under less decisive control than normal? Should Froech get hit with a penalty for not "finshing" out, if he's losing?

I'd be interested in a discussion on this.

Froech

Monday, March 12, 2001

Classic News

In this article, posted on Sovereign's main site on December 18th 2000, Geoff Zatkin and Clint Worley discuss what exactly they're planning to have that will make Sovereign "persistent". Here's a snippet:
What sort of rewards can you want in a persistent RTS, beyond the ultimate satisfaction of winning the game? "The main advantage of persistence, in Sovereign in particular," says Clint, "is that it offers something new to the player, in the form of empires that remain in the game, units that gain rank, experience that carries over from game to game, and many other aspects that give players a reason to log back into the game. Being able to show other players what you have accomplished, goals you've met, and awards that you have received are all possible in a persistent game."

Classic News

In this article, originally posted on Sovereign's web site on November 30th, Clint Worley and Geoff Zatkin talk about some of the things they're planning for Sovereign's servers. Here's a snippet:

"The real key is tying the servers together so that those people in Japan aren't just playing people in Japan. We're really trying to get into real time translation, from language to language, so that players in other countries will be able to type in their native language but it will translate to wherever you are. In online games with an international player base, you see people typing in another language, and that creates a barrier, like there's them and us. And I figure if we're doing a game online we should try and break that down."

- Reported by LAR
