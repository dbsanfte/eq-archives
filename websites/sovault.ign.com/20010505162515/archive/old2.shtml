AI control while logged out
In this recent post to the SOVault genboard, Detrytis describes the problems inherent in allowing the computer to control the actions of your empire while you are logged out, and lists three possibilities:

1) All units will be controlled by the AI. You tell them when to fight, where to fight and what to fight with, but unlike an RTS, you cannot personally control your units. You can only give them general orders. Thus, when attacking someone who has logged out, you are not given any serious advantages over them, the basic battle tactics are AI controlled. The AI may not be terribly sophisticated, but since everyone's troops are controlled by the same AI routines, battles are always fought on equal ground in this regard.

2) You can control your units with as much detail as a RTS game. You don't just tell them where to attack, you tell them how to attack, unit by unit, target by target. Consequently, if you're not online, your forces are going to be devastated, since the human player is going to be a heck of a lot better than the computer player.

3) Not only is this a revolutionary game in scope, but it also has revolutionary computer AI. The computer is so good at fighting, you'll sometimes let it control the battle even when you ARE online, because it knows how to concentrate firepower, it knows how to group up for an attack, it knows when to retreat, it's hell on silicon.
You will have the ability to hand over control of your empire to an ally, but how do you think Verant's computers should control your units? Add your thoughts to Detrytis's post.

Balancing initial resources
Here's a response from Verant's Bart Rothwell to a question posted on the SOVault devboard:

I agree that how "fairly" the resources and empire starting locations are placed on the globe is very important. We certainly don't want anyone to win a game just because they got lucky and were plopped down on top of a huge supply of oil. So to answer your worry, the map isn't truly "random", and we will be making sure that the resources and terrain are spread out fairly between the empires. But this doesn't mean that everyone will start with the same thing -- your suggestion that "one player could be in an easily defensible canyon valley and another could be in open desert" may very well happen, but then the player in the desert would start with some other advantage that the canyon player did not have. This will make the game tuning stage more difficult, but hopefully the added variety will keep the game interesting to players for a long time.

Bart Rothwell
Sovereign Lead Programmer

RTS clashes with RPG?
Brad had this response to charges that adding too many role-playing elements to an RTS like Sovereign will make people hoppin' mad:

I think you are confusing gameplay elements that are traditionally used in RPGs (especially online RPGs) with elements that are truly part of the RPG genre. Sovereign because of its online nature uses elements of persistence and because of its cooperative nature uses elements of interdependence. As for newbies, they can play against other newbies, but they will also be valuable in larger games. Don't worry -- we've really thought this through :)

Scale and map curvature
Here's Sovereign lead coder Bart Rothwell answering a map-size related query:

Good questions. The size of the globe does depend directly on the number of players in the game, and the one you've seen in the screenshots so far has been the globe for a 32-player game. The 512-player globe is bigger by a factor of 16 (in terms of surface area) so that you have about the same amount of area per empire. Since it's bigger, you don't see as much curvature when you're down low. But the 8-player globe is going to have even more curvature. The units will look large in comparison to the smaller globes if you think of them as being the size of Earth, but I think that when you think of them as being the size of, say, Thebe (what?), now the scale will look better.

Bart Rothwell
Sovereign Lead Programmer

Bart on lag issues
Here's another name we hope to see often on the SOVault boards -- Bart Rothwell, lead programmer of Sovereign. In this debut post he addresses l...a...g, the prominent issue in all massively multiplayer games:

Actually, we don't expect lag to be much of a problem for Sovereign. The pace of the game will not be as fast as a shooter like Halflife or even a faster RTS like C&C, so there isn't as much of a need to broadcast one player's actions to all the others in the fastest possible time, which is what creates the lag artifacts. I'm not saying that the game won't be a steady adrenaline rush -- you'll stil have to think fast, just not faster than your connection.

As far as having problems due to 500 people being on one server, that's just an issue of bandwidth and processing power (read: $$$) on the server side. We've designed everything to be scalable, so if we start to get clogged on our end, we just add more hardware and everyone is happy.

Bart Rothwell
Sovereign Lead Programmer

Verant announces Sovereign
Sony Online Entertainment, parent company of Verant Interactive, issued this Standard Fair press release on July 28th, announcing their new on-line strategy title: Sovereign.

Sony Online Entertainment Inc. together with Verant Interactive, Inc. a premier developer of online games, announce the development of Sovereign, the first ever massively multiplayer real time strategy game scheduled to launch exclusively on The Station@sony.com in the 2nd half of 2000. Components of the game will debut to the public at the GenCon Game Fair, in the Midwest Express Center, Milwaukee, Wisconsin from August 5th through the 9th. Further information can be obtained at The Station at http://www.station.sony.com/sovereign. Sovereign will join 989 Studios’ EverQuest® as the second Verant game created by the Verant Interactive development team on The Station@sony.com’s game roster.

Sovereign is a real time strategy game played on a grand scale, allowing up to 500 people to gather online simultaneously in a single game. Built to take advantage of cutting edge Internet technology, Sovereign, a game of empire conquest, is similar in structure to classics such as Diplomacy, Command & Conquer, and Civilization and features diplomacy and persistence as characteristics crucial to success. In a stunningly rendered game environment, players can roam an entire planet constructing cities, airbases and seaports, building and deploying massive armies, launching air and land attacks with nuclear or chemical weapons and spying on enemy empires via orbital satellites.

"We pushed the envelope of massively multiplayer gaming with 989 Studios’ EverQuest and created an entirely new set of expectations for the fantasy role player. Building on what we’ve learned and applying it to a strategy game will result in an incredible new product. Sovereign is this product, " stated John Smedley, President and CEO of Verant Interactive.

The persistent nature of the Sovereign world allows strategy gamers to spend every waking moment online playing out their dreams of conquest. But for those who must occasionally sleep, Sovereign offers options for allowing empires to remain operational while the player is logged off, to be run by allies, the computer, or simply be shielded from attack.

The game also delivers on a unique roleplaying element to offer the ability to develop and roleplay custom characters (or personas). Each persona, ranging from soldier to spy, from scientist to theologian, continues from one game to the next, thereby allowing players to advance in rank over an extended period of time by gradually building technology and experience points.

Like EverQuest, Sovereign will be sold at retail and played exclusively online at The Station@sony.com (www.station.sony.com). The first month will be free, and thereafter, unlimited play is available for a flat monthly subscription fee. Subscriptions can be purchased by the month, or for multiple months at a discounted rate.

"Sony Online Entertainment is thrilled to present this groundbreaking title as part of The Station’s commitment to the serious online gamer," said Robert Gehorsam, SOE’s Senior Vice President, Programming and Platform Development. "It’s our goal to revolutionize the strategy gamer space as successfully as we’ve done with our mainstream titles such as JEOPARDY! Online and Wheel of Fortune Online."

"With Sovereign, Verant continues in the tradition of creating outstanding games that support massive online communities," stated Brad McQuaid, Vice President of Verant Interactive. "The development of Sovereign promises to further define Verant as one of the leading developers of online games and propel a new and revolutionary era in strategy gaming. The incredible success of 989 Studio’s EverQuest on The Station has excited us to partner with Sony Online Entertainment again to offer the serious gamer the best online game experience possible."

The Station distributes online games and gameshows from Sony Pictures Entertainment, 989 Studios, and third-party developers.
