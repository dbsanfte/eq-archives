Sovereign Gameplay At E3
For the first time in a long while, Clint made a post to the devboard detailing what has been happening lately. He also made mention of what to expect at E3 in May:

I do have to apologize for the lack of information in the past couple of weeks. We have been hard at work on the trade between the players and between the empires. City simulation as well as unit production and unit repair. As I am sure most of you would be happy to hear that we are moving to battle next. The artists are studying explosions and turning out conceptual samples. The goal of the combat in Sovereign is to mimic real war as closely as possible. I do believe that there will be a happy medium that the players will enjoy. If it is too real then the gameplay is to fast and not fun, but then on the other hand we don't want to make it an arcade game. BTW, are any of you going to E3? Saturday, January 22, 2000

Letter From Clint Worley
In his first "Producer's Letter" to the community, Clint shares a bit of his strategy-gaming background and his ambitions for creating Sovereign:

I want to welcome all of the RTS gamers out there to a new world, a new concept and a new experience. Verant Interactive, known for its huge success with EverQuest, is bringing a new game to the Massively Multiplayer genre, Sovereign.

I have been a strategy gamer for many years now. Starting with pen and paper games and then moving into the graphical PC style of RTS games I have had dreams and ambitions to make a RTS game with elements that borrow some of the best aspects from other games that we have grown to love. The goal with Sovereign is to bring a Massively Multiplayer RTS game to the people, offering immediate interest and long term playability. As many of you could imagine, there are some obvious hurdles to clear when designing an online RTS that offers persistent games. The hurdles seemed trivial when the drive and ambition was to create a game that had never been played before. The design of the game was guided by the interest in RTS games and the tried and true formulas that can be found in turn based strategy games. I truly believe that RTS fans have been waiting for a more complex game that offers persistent gameplay. I believe that Sovereign will please not only the diehard RTS fans but it will allows new players the opportunity to play a truly unique game that will break standards that we have all become accustomed to.

– Clint Worley aka "CobraJet"
Producer/Designer Sovereign

The letter is posted at Sony's official Sovereign site.
-Luxor @ 9:47 PM PT

GamezNet Preview
GamezNet has published the net's latest online preview of Sovereign. This lengthy 6 page preview documents many of Sovereign's upcoming features and even features several screenshots. Here's an excerpt:

    For the nasty player, attacking civilians can be a valid option. Each city has civilian and military sections. The area of the city that is attacked by an enemy will determine the type of damage that is dealt to the users city. Losses in the civilian sector will hit the production, morale, and the tax flow. An attack in the military sector might cripple a key airport, slow the production of a barracks, or even cause a breach in the security of the city against espionage related units. They're not totally defenseless though, as civilians can garrison themselves in for a bit of protection.
