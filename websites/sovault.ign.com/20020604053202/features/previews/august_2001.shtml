Sovereign: An in depth overview

About three months ago the gaming community was notified at the 2001 E3 that Verant’s Massively Multiplayer Real Time Strategy game had been given a complete overhaul. While some applauded the change and others scored Verant for abandoning the original vision, the Sovereign community eventually learned to accept the changes. That was just about three months ago. Since then not a lot more has been released on the newly redesigned Sovereign. However, some trickles of information can be seen making their way out from Verant’s San Diego facility. This article will take you through all of the developments so far in Verant’s first Massively Multiplayer Real Time Strategy game, Sovereign. Cue the music.

I AM your Sovereign.
The player’s role within Sovereign is that of the ruler of an empire. This empire is one of many to have come out of some kind of catastrophe, where the ability to make most modern technologies has been lost. Fortunately for your empire, the ability to travel between planets has managed to survive. Why, you may ask, is that so good? Well, your empire hasn’t managed the natural resources of your Homeworld too well and, to be frank, you’re almost out of them. So it's off the rent-a-spaceship with you. Well not literally, but lets not forget that the ability to create a lot of technology has been lost. Therefore, a “central interstellar travel” type organization now controls all of the interplanetary transports that you'll need to rent in order to transport your troops and resources between other planets, and they charge by the mile.

The most important of these other planets you'll be traveling to are called Excursion Worlds. Excursion Worlds have no native civilizations, contain lots of natural resources and can be found all throughout the universe, with more being discovered all the time. All one needs to do is mine the resources off the planet and transport them home. Oh, wait… Everyone else is doing that also. Hmm, now we have a problem. Chances are that if you’re going to a planet to mine the resources off of it, someone else has had the same idea. That brings us to the combat portion of Sovereign.

How many clowns can you fit in that car?
Excursion Worlds are where all combat in Sovereign takes place, which is lucky for you because that means that your nice clean Homeworld isn’t going to get all shot up. Which brings us to one “minor” problem. How are younger players going to be able to compete on an Excursion World without instantly having their forces stomped into a bloody pulp by people who have played the game longer? Good question. The first thing that Verant has done is to make it so that players come to Excursion Worlds on equal footing. When a player decides that they want to travel to an Excursion World they will first need to fill a transport with both units and resources that will help them survive on the planet they’re traveling to. However there are stipulations. Not only are there limits on how much material a player can bring based on the quantity of what he or she is loading, but players are also limited to how much they can bring based on the quality of what they wish to transport. This means that even though a player may be able to transport 100 units with them when they first start playing Sovereign, that number may be down to 50 further into the game because those units are so much better then what a beginner would be using. Why not bring more than one transport, or go back to your homeworld to pick up more materials, you might ask. Well… you can’t. Doesn’t that suck? Players are only allowed to bring one transport to an Excursion world and they’re only able to travel to one Excursion world at a time. To add another wrench into the mix, players can only control their forces on an Excursion World while the transport they’re using is still in orbit. As you can see, unless someone keeps their game connected 24/7, which I’m sure someone will do, player’s will need to start over every time they land on an Excursion World. But don’t fret, because before leaving an Excursion World players are able to take whatever they can fit into their transport back to their Homeworld with them.

My dad can beat up your dad.
Now you may be saying to yourself, “Well that’s cool, but once someone who’s played a while is established on an Excursion World they’re going to walk all over the newer players.” And you would be right in that assumption, if Sovereign was meant to be played as a grand RTS melee. Thankfully, for anyone who’s ever played “Mankind”, Sovereign is designed so that players can group up and work together with relative ease. “We're looking into ways to make it very, very painless to end up in a group or a team”, explains Geoffrey Zatkin, lead designer for Sovereign. “Literally so much that you don't have to put any effort into it. Honestly, if you're playing a massively multiplayer game you're playing it with other people.” With this goal in mind, Verant will place players into two groups. The first, and probably the more important of the two, is that upon creating their empire players will join a guild, with each guild being made up of between fifty to one hundred players. If someone’s picking on you then there’s a chance that one of your guild mates might come running to the rescue. In addition to each player being a member of a guild, each guild is also part of an all-encompassing house. Cool, huh? So that players don’t become too comfortable with always having their house behind them, all of the guilds in a particular house will also be battling it out for control of the entire house. Just think of it, ONE group of players will be determining the fate of hundreds of others. With some Excursion Worlds capable of supporting up to 500 players, guild wars, I’m sure, will become a fierce, and very cool, part of Sovereign life. As if the politics of Sovereign weren’t already complex enough, players may also break away from their guild and create their own Mercenary group. Just don’t be expecting to have the might of a house backing you up if you decide to do so.
