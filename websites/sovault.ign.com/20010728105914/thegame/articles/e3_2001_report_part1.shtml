Sovereign E3 2001 Report - Part One

Over the last two years the Sovereign that we’ve seen has been of a massively multiplayer game in which players start a game on a planet, with anywhere from a dozen to five hundred other players on it, and battle it out for weeks on end. After seeing Sovereign’s current iteration at the E3 I can only say is “All bets are off.” In the year since the last E3, Sovereign’s core design has changed from a real time strategy game(RTS) with massively multiplayer(MMP) elements in it to a new design of a massively multiplayer game with RTS elements. Initially that may not seem like a big deal, but it’s a critical change in both how Sony Online Entertainment views the game and is designing it.


Click to enlarge

The biggest thing to have change in Sovereign is that the persistent aspect of the game is no longer directly tied to the RTS part of the game. It's this that has really changed how Sovereign will be played. Gone are the RTS battles that would take place for weeks on end. In their place now are smaller “excursion” type battles that will last anywhere from a few hours to two or three days. “What we’re doing is we’re look for ways to keep the persistent aspect of the game while still letting people not feel like their getting chimped for not playing all the time” says Geoffrey Zatkin, lead designer for Sovereign. “One of the things we didn’t want is to make someone have to play eight or twelve hours a day to be competitive. We want to be able to have somebody be able to play two hours a day and have a really good experience.”

The persistent part of Sovereign now comes in the form of your homeworld, which is essentially the center of a player’s empire. A player's homeworld will be where they do all of their technology research, where they'll trade units and resources from. It’s going to be where resources will be stored, where the players cities will be located and their empire’s population will live. Unfortunately your homeworld also has one big problem, it doesn’t have many natural resources on it. So players will need to go out, find these resources, and transport them back to their homeworld.

In order to find the resources that are need to run an empire, players will need to go out into the universe and land on what are being referred to as excursion worlds. It’s these excursion worlds that will consist of the RTS part of Sovereign. Once on the planet a player will unload their transport and establishes a base, but what happens after that is really up to the player. You won’t need to always fight other players if you don’t want to. If the player wishes they can land on a planet, establish a small base, mine as many resources as their transport can hold, then leave. However, before players make the trip to an excursion world they need to load their transport up with enough units and materials so that they can establish a base on this new planet.


Click for details

The transport is the great equalizer in Sovereign. It limits players to not only being able to bring the same quantity of items with them to excursion worlds but also takes into account the quality of what the player brings with them. That is to say that if, for instance, a player wishes to fill their transport with a lot of low tech units they would be able to bring a lot more than a player that brings mid or high tech level units. When the player's ready to leave, since this is the same transport, it will have the exact same amount of space in it as it did when he or she came to the excursion world. If a player is planning on just mining resources off the planet it might not be a good idea for them to bring a fully loaded transport to the planet. If they do they might find it hard to make a profit on the excursion. In addition, if a player wishes they can send units and resources up to their transport before they’re ready to leave the planet. This gives the player a little bit of security just in case someone decides that they don’t like having that person on the planet with them.

Also having been added to the game is a very intricate system for players to interact with one another. “We’re looking into ways to make it very, very painless to end up in a group or a team. Literally so much that you don’t have to put any effort into it” explains Geoffrey. Upon entering the game you become a member of a “great house”. The game will contain a, yet to be determined, number of great houses that will each comprise of hundreds of players. It’s the conflict between these houses that will be the main source of conflict within Sovereign. In addition to being a member of a great house, players will also be members of a “guild” within their house. Now this is where it gets interesting. All of these guilds, each composing of fifty to one hundred players, will also be battling each other for supremacy of the their house, with the leading guild having special "perks". Also, if a player wishes to they will have the ability to change their allegiances from time to time. But be warned, a log will be kept of your betrayals. It should make for some interesting conflicts.

- Larry "LAR" Peters
