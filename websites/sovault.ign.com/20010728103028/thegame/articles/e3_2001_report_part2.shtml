Sovereign E3 2001 Report - Part Two

The first thing that we were treated to when shown Sovereign was the game's beautiful soundtrack, composed by Jeremy Soule and played by a 96-piece symphony. While it was difficult to hear the detail of the music, due to the other noises at the E3, it still sounded quite good.


After logging into a server the first thing that needs to be done in Sovereign is to create an empire. There is actually a fair amount that needs to be choosen when creating an empire. The first of which, other than the empire’s name, is to choose a flag for your empire to use. An empire’s flag is important because it’s really the graphical representation of your empire. A little flag hangs above all of an empire’s units, and it’s attached to all in-game messages that are sent to other empires. It’s really the most important thing that's used to identity an empire, other than it’s name. So that only one empire uses each flag, when an empire is created Sovereign’s servers will check to make sure someone else isn’t already using that flag, or one similar to it. If someone is then the person creating their empire needs to choose something else for their flag. In addition to being able to use a stock flag, players will also have the ability to create their own flags through a design interface which will be provided within Sovereign. After creation, custom flags will be checked to make sure that inappropriate images don’t make their way into the game. The next thing that needs to be done when creating an empire is to choose what traditions you want your empire to follow. Traditions are like a player’s class in an RPG. What traditions you choose will determine what “skill levels” your empire will have and what areas of the technology tree your empire will have access to research. At the time of the E3 the listed skills were Research, Innovation, Statecraft, Corporate Theory, Public Credibility, City Planning, Economic Theory, and Industry. I should note, however, that these skills will probably change before the game is finalized. Instead of giving players just one type of tradition to play Verant has decided to give players both a military and a civilian tradition to choose from. The combination of these two traditions will ultimately determine what type of empire a player will have. Players will also not be limited to traditions that are closely related to each other. If, for instance, a player wishes he or she will be able to have a military tradition of Assault and a civilian tradition of Espionage.


After your empire has been created you’re ready to enter the game. Unfortunately, in the version of Sovereign that was shown at the E3 the homeworld wasn’t included in the game. So, upon entering the game we were brought directly to the transport loadout screen. From here our transport was loaded with various resources and units and we were off to an excursion world. The first thing that we noticed was that the game’s graphics don’t really look like any of the screenshots of Sovereign we’ve seen in the past, not even the ones from last month. The reason for this is because that Verant has decided to move from a free moving camera to one that can only be in put in either an angled, Command and Conquer style, isometric view or a straight top down view of the planet. From there the camera can also be rotated 90° in either direction and has three different altitude levels from which to see the planet: surface level, a middle level, and an orbital level. The graphics engine being used now is actually the same one that we’ve seen all along, however players are just more limited in how they can use it now. Only about half a dozen “test” units could be seen in the this demo version of Sovereign, and they won't necessarily be in the final release of the game. Pictures taken of the game can be see in Sovereign Vault’s Image Gallery. Also, in addition to the terrain shown in these screenshots Verant is also planning on having other tile sets with different type of terrains for different types of planets.

All in all Sovereign is definitely coming along nicely, but it still has a long ways to go before it’ll be completed. Verant’s current time table is to have the game go into beta sometime late 2001 or early 2002 with a release sometime in 2002, but that is in no way set in stone and can easily slip back further. All we can do now is patiently wait for this very cool looking game to be completed.

- Larry "LAR" Peters
