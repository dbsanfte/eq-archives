Discuss Sovereign on the Boards
SOVault Exclusive Interview: Clint Worley

Since being announced last August, Sovereign has generated big buzz from the press. It landed on PC Gamer's 10 Ten most anticipated games of 2000 and promises to change the very face of the RTS genre. We recently had a chance to interview Sovereign Producer Clint Worley, the man responsible for the development of the game, to find out how Sovereign has been progressing since last August.

Aurum: First off, how has Sovereign progressed these past few months? Is everything on schedule?

Clint Worley: We were primarily focused on the trade between players, so players can offer resources and accept and decline trade agreements. This whole process included the units that are to be used for trade, since trade can be done with land, air or naval units each unit had to be made to suit its purpose. Part of the trade completion was tying health and payload to each trade unit. This will allow the users to damage or capture a trade unit without destroying it, a worthwhile strategy in Sovereign.

The interface was also crucial to the trade between players, since Sovereign has a much more robust trade system then a traditional RTS game the interface had to be improved not only in the additional information but also in the presentation. We want to offer more abilities but keep the interface straightforward, a challenge in and of its own. We have now moved to combat.

We are primarily focused on land combat first. This includes all of the animation required to accurately represent combat between units. Particle systems to display the effects that our artists will create. Different models to show the destruction from the battles, and last but not least the sounds. The goal with the sound in Sovereign is to deliver sounds that make everyone turn the speakers all the way up. From the explosive firing of the tanks to the alarms on the cruisers to warn of incoming missiles, we have it all!

Aurum: Now that it's been several months since Sovereign was first announced, what new information is there about game that you'd like to share with us?

Clint Worley: We have started stress testing the servers already and preliminary tests have returned great results. We are currently working on optimizing network and server code to squeeze the high demands on Sovereign into normal connections. Trade has been quite a task and the team has fun playing around with backstabbing and trading resources. The artists have been working on animation of the units. This is part of a larger task that includes the programmer support of the animations and the actual purpose of the animations. As I said above we are currently working on the combat portion of Sovereign. So the team is quite busy for E3. Believe me the game will look incredible at E3 and we will be revealing some new features that have yet to be announced. Here is a hint * think of gaming on the go *.

Aurum: Could you tell us a little about the interface Sovereign will use?

Clint Worley: It is very similar at first glance to a traditional RTS games interface. The interface resides at the bottom of the screen taking up approximately a quarter of the lower portion of the screen. We are offering new techniques to the user such as configuring the interface to suit each users style of play. The configuration is done in real time also so you can move important information to areas that you use more to make it more readily available. The real trick has been making the interface fast and intuitive and it is the goal of the design.

Aurum: How complex will Sovereign be to your 'average' player? Will there it be big on micro-management?

Clint Worley: We are designing it to cater to both types of players. We are currently working on various deals with peripheral companies that will take the hardcore experience to new levels. The more casual gamers will have their own games to play in and they will not have to worry about being attacked and crushed without plenty of notice to react. The beautiful aspect of the design allows players to play more casual games to get accustomed to the game and then when they feel ready they can jump into a more immersive experience at their own pace. This will allow the players to decide what level they feel they are ready to play at which is one of the main focuses of the design, give the players options.

We have removed much of the micro-management that is standard fare in traditional RTS games to let the user focus on the overall picture. Between the empire upkeep, trade, and battle, the user will have more then enough to monitor.

Aurum: What kind of classes will Sovereign have? Could you tell us a bit about them?

Clint Worley: There are 6 classes in Sovereign. They are the Soldier, Diplomat, Spy, Scientist, Economist, and the Theologian. Each has its own specialties that are key to the overall technology tree and will require the user to ally with other classes to advance through the technologies.

    Soldier: Specializes in military skills and units. High offense and defense with limited government and civil abilities.
    Diplomat: Specializes in government skills. Deals with the civil and social aspects of the empire.
    Spy: Espionage and stealth tactics are his high points. Obvious reduced offensive power is tied to offensive advantages gained through stealth tactics.
    Scientist: Exploring the advancements in science allows for new upgrades and skills. The ability to upgrade existing units and weapons make this class a real threat. Main downsides are slow production time and slightly lower offensive power.
    Economist: The ability to access the global market and establish loans with the other players offers a new way to play the banker. An average military and social standing helps this class.
    Theologian: Religious conversions, blessing and other abilities make this class the most dramatic class. Military side is strong rivaling the soldiers, and the civil side is usually dedicated but can sway.
Aurum: According to the information, Sovereign will have several rpg-elements. Could you tell us what they are, and explain how they affect the game?

Clint Worley: We decided that to have a persistent RTS we would have to research what made the persistent RPG games so appealing in a long-term way. We realized that the player’s creation and development of a character was one of the key elements that players loved. We allow the players to create up to 6 different characters each with their own class and unique look. We also allow the players to distribute skill points across the general skills in the game. This lets the players create a unique character every time, and changes the gameplay experience with each character creation. This character is persistent and will develop from game to game gaining and losing skills and abilities depending on the way that the players play the game. This is very similar to an RPG but the difference in Sovereign is that we allow you to take your character from game to game carrying over previously gained experience.

Aurum: Is August still the target date to start public beta testing?

Clint Worley: We are not currently stating a date for Beta. When we are ready there will be a full push-marketing blitz that announces the beta sign-up for the most anticipated RTS ever!

Aurum: How will you conduct the beta test? Will it be similar to how EverQuest's beta tests were conducted?

Clint Worley: Yes we will follow the same format. There will be an initial closed beta that we will invite limited people into. This does include people that win beta accounts from various promotional webpages. Following the closed beta we will open the beta to allow access to anyone and everyone. This will help us stress test the servers and determine the actual server needs for launch.

luxor: What technologies will you take advantage of, such as 3D sound and hardware T&L?

Clint Worley: We are planning on having complete 3D sound that will take advantage of surround sound speaker setups. We are also following the Verant theme of support for as many video cards as possible at launch. We have found that the more cards that we support the better the reception, no one wants to buy a game and then wait for the video card patch.

Aurum: Will you allow organizations of players, such as guilds, squads and clans, to exist in the game? If so, how will you manage them?

ClintWorley: We are allowing groups to form we prefer the term Alliances. The answer to your questions is yes. They can be formed outside the game in the chat rooms and they can be formed in the game also. There is a limit to the number of people in your alliance in the game so selection of people in your alliance is crucial to the success of your empire. The GM’s are responsible for the creation and the tracking of the alliances and will have the required tools available at launch to allow people to begin allying as soon as they buy the game.

Aurum: Massively Multiplayer Games, such as Verant's EverQuest, often have major events conducted by the GMs. Any plan for something like this in Sovereign? (something like an alien invasion)

I can assure you that I am a huge fan of player driven events as well as GM events. I believe that there should be major shifts in the world as well as the storyline that these events change or reinforce. It has a proven track record that makes the game better for not only the players but also the future. By creating a persistent universe that Sovereign exists in we can look forward to past events changing the outcome of battles and introducing new skills and technologies that are the result of these events.
