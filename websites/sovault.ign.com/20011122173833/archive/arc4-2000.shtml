Saturday, May 13, 2000

More Sov At E3

I just got back from the Verant booth where quality assurance manager Rob Hill spent a few minutes showing me Sovereign in action. The interface is highly dynamic. Rob had a chat screen opened on the right side of the display. As he grew the chat display to the left, the main window dynamically shrunk in real time. It's impressive to see the display resized very quickly and repeatedly. Many windows such as the email box can float, and be moved and resized at will. Rob initiated a private chat request and yelled (E3 is noisy :) for the Verant guy next to us to accept it, which he did. A small chat request indicator text appeared at the top left of the screen, and a new chat box appeared.

More on cities. Engineers are the units that can build new cities on resources (like Settlers in Civ). You can build several types of buildings including airport, research, and repair facility. I hope to get a few screenshots of some the things I am describing here, there is a lot of on-screen information to deal with.

I got a look at the soldiers. You can zoom in far enough to see details on their faces. They were in full battle gear, armored and helmed. They stood and shoulder-fired their machine guns at a group of tanks, which in turned aimed and fired, shells visibly flying past the group of soldiers. Rob created several ships out in the water, an aircraft carrier, submarine, a few warships and cargo ships. They undulated slowly in the rolling waves of the ocean.

Well this needs to be brief because the show is over! I was very impressed with what I saw. Thanks to Clint and the Verant guys for giving me the tour!

Report From E3

Hey everyone, it's luxor reporting live from the IGN booth at E3. I just spent some time chatting with Clint Worley, lead designer and producer of Sovereign. He gave a demonstration of the game and it looks fantastic. All the screenshots we have seen so far don't really capture the movement and feel of the game. Once you have seen a zoom from the ground up through the clouds and into space, you'll know what I mean.

Clint began by explaining a few facts about Sovereign, such as the 6 character classes and the forms of government. There will be several government types you can pick at the start of a game, from communist to democracy, that will have varying effects on your population, such as willingness to protest acts of war.

Your starting city, the capitol of your empire, will be randomly selected. They have decided to use random maps to prevent people from having initial advantages like a defensive mountain range. Subsequent cities will be built on top of resources (gold for example) placed throughout the map. The city will then begin to produce that resource at varying levels of productivity, depending on morale. Cities have two types of buildings: industrial, which produce quantities of the resource, and residential, which contribute money and morale. You can manage these buildings by constructing, demolishing, raising, and lowering each individually.

Resources. As Clint said earlier, there will be a global marketplace for resource trading which will operate on a supply and demand basis. Players may also trade directly, and ship via air, sea, or ground. The amount being shipped will be represented graphically so for example, a ship may only have one level of cargo boxes instead of three. This way, enemy marauders may pick and choose the heavily laden transports to pirate.

The game will ship with 60-75 combat units, Clint estimates. Jose Araiza, Sovereign Lead Tester, was running the demo most of the time. He had several artillery tank battles going on simultaneously. As the tanks entered range of their targets, the rear launchers would raise from the back of the tank and send off a volley of shells. Collision detection was not present in this demo, but it looked very cool nonetheless. I heard one of the units say "For the Empire!" as they engaged. Reliance on fuel will be emphasized. Depots and other fuel lines will be necessary to maintain units.

The grpahics, as I said, look much better in motion. Clint was a little reluctant to share all his secrets about graphics and terrain, but I did get a close up view of a swamp. Each tree is individually modeled. Only smaller units may enter terrain of this type, which can be used as a tactical advantage. The day/night cycle happened rather suddenly every now and then, I believe as the sun moved behind the globe. The light went away and the stars came out. The interface is very impressive and highly dynamic. There are many status and chat panels which can be rearanged, opened, and moved on the fly.

Clint was also excited about out-of-game interaction with Sovereign. An instant-messaging type client will be available, in which you can exchange email and trade while not logged into the game. Another probable feature is a webpage which keeps track of your empire. Pager and PDA support are still maybes.

The big news is that Sovereign will launch with servers in North America, Japan, and Europe. Additionally, the game will have real time text translation between Japanese, English, and other languages. Wow! Sovereign will ship for the Windows platform, but X-Box and PS2 ports are a possibility. Peripherals and internet support will factor into the decision on supporting consoles, Clint said.

I did ask when the beta will commence. "We'll let you know on the official site" is the answer. Not surprising, but I had to ask because it's a common question. :) At the end of our meeting, we talked a bit about the future of Sovereign. Adding new units and resources will be possible. Clint also let on about something big involving a Storyline, which will factor into the evolution of the game. We expect to know more about this in coming months. Sovereign looks fantastic in it's unpolished alpha state. I have to say I was impressed with all I saw. Clint and his team have done a great job. Once everything is in place, I can imagine Sovereign will be as fun and addicting as Civilization was.

More to come!

Vault Network Brings Order To Anarchy Online

By the year 29,475 AD, mankind has reached far into the stars, populating many worlds in the Milky Way. Among these is Rubi-Ka, a barren desert planet plagued by sandstorms and heavy winds. Located on the distant edge of the galaxy, it is made important by the presence of Notum, the substance that powers nanotechnology. For many years, the planet and the extraction of Notum have been controlled by the powerful galaxy-wide Omni-Tek corporation, but not without constant conflict against rebel clans opposing its regime.

Based on this long-standing conflict, Anarchy Online is developer Funcom’s massively multiplayer persistent online world that will receive its first major public exposure at E3 next week. With beta testing slated to begin shortly and a projected release date of late this year, Anarchy Online promises to become the first major online world with a far-future science-fiction setting.

With publicity and activity set to ramp up sharply, we are very happy to announce the launch of Anarchy Online Vault to provide complete news and information coverage of this exciting title to the online gaming community. As our launch feature, we are pleased to present the first Anarchy Online Developer Journal in which Producer Tommy Strand provides a very personal look at his game. Here is a snippet to whet your appetite:
I’m often asked: “What will make Anarchy Online different from the other games out there or the games in the near future?” Let me share with you the answer I usually give: First of all, we are 'it' this year. There won’t be any other big online games coming out in 2000.

To top that, we offer the first persistent science-fiction world. We focus on the social setting, but give the player the option to choose a single-player experience in a multiplayer setting. We have created a system where the player can enter at any time, and still have stuff to do. Our first focus was to abolish the “camping” feature; the second was to enable play during a lunch break without having to run around searching for monsters - or someone to team with in order to conquer the monsters on your level.
Other features at Anarchy Online Vault will include game information, news, message boards, concept art, screenshots, humor, reader and community participation features, humor and much more. New content will begin to appear in the very near future.

In advance of the activity that is sure to begin with Funcom’s showing of Anarchy Online at E3, we invite the online gaming community stop in at our brand new Anarchy Online Vault where the excitement will be centered. Enjoy!
