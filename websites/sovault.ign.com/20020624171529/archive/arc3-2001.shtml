Thursday, April 26, 2001

Ah, capitalism

Froech made these comments today on Pay to play vs Ads in play, on the official forums:
You know, to be honest, I don't know the answer to that question...

But I'll bet if you call the folks here at the station, they'd be quick to give you an answer. I'd be quite suprised if you couldn't pay ahead as much as you wanted to.

In the past, I'd considered the possibility of selling ads like hatechaucer suggested, as a way of defraying the cost to the consumer, but like many other people seem to feel, the idea rankled me from the player perspective.

The fact seems to be, these days on the internet, that you have to pay something to get good content. Free (or cheap) seems to be going the way of the dodo.

Some of this is bad, some is good. I don't mind weeding out the chaff to get to the grain, as long as the grain is good.

On the other hand, I understand your dilemma. I too want to try as many games as possible, and I don't have a salary that is out of this world to allow me to do that. So, I pick and I choose carefully, much like you are. It isn't a perfect solution, but I'm not sure I have a better one yet. I haven't given up looking, though.

Still, putting ads inside a game has some comedic possibilities. Imagine playing in EQ, and going to your favourite merchant, and finding he is selling Adidas and Nike shoes for your ranger, so you have to run over to the Wells Fargo bank to get your silvers, and then take the Toyota lift to get up into Kelethin,

Frankly, I'm not wild about sponserships getting the name in the title of the event (it's the Indy 500, not the Coca Cola Indianapolis 500 brought to you by Firestone or somesuch), but in the world we live in, we have to deal with some of this.

I recently read an article on another website complaining about how the computer games companies had sold out (he wanted to see nudity in the games is what I got out of his discussion). I don't see us as having sold out. However, when you see stuff like in game sponsorship, you can say we have

More on balancing

Today, on the official forums, Froech made these comments on the balancing Sovereign.
Some interesting points here.

We're certainly well aware of some of the issues, and continue to wrestle with the issues, and will likely do so for quite a long time. But these are indeed issues that need to be addressed when dealing with the persistance issue, and the newbie/veteran concern.

It is hard to make any points of similarity to EQ or other RPGs, because this is a real time strategy game, and doesn't have the same type of play elements. But the issues are certainly germane in both cases (and in fact in every subscription based game in the world). You want to have people enjoy themselves as much the first time out as the hundreth. These are the issues that we're working on, and continue to discuss on a daily basis. And we don't think we're beating a dead horse, either.

In the grand scheme of things, this is likely to be one of the most difficult issues any game will deal with, and we want to be as successful as possible. We want this game to be as popular 20 years from now as it will be when we release it. We're pouring our hearts into it... we want it to be enjoyed forever.

And I'm against monetary discounts myself, since I want to continue eating at least once a week.

So how would you do it?

Froech posted a new topic today on the official forums discussing the finer points of balancing a MMP game.
On my trip last week, I was telling a friend about this game I was working on, and since he isn't part of the team, there were things I couldn't tell him (just like I can't tell you all).

But, we did get into an interesting discussion about balancing a massively multiplayer strategy game, and what impact the "massively multiplayer" aspect has on the genre.

He works on another MMP game, so he's familiar with some of the needs to keep someone interested and playing, so talking with him was interesting. I wish he'd come visit these boards occasionally, but he's too busy on his own stuff.

But, I wanted to raise the same balance issues with you all, and get your input, and start a discussion. We've been talking in various topics about the benefits of each class type. So-and-so class would be better at this, while another class would be better at that.

It was my friend’s contention that it had to go deeper than that. Not that one class was better than another, but that each class would have to, at some point, rely on another.

He actually used his game as a model, but I'll translate it to EQ here, since many people in this board are familiar with it.

Everyone can play EQ by themselves for the first several levels without too much difficulty (it's always easier in groups, that's a given). However, the older you get, the more you tend to need to have a group of friends to flesh out your hunting party. You need that tank, a healer, and a magic user, just to make sure that you can hunt effectively.

The way I see it, that's good game design. You're encouraging "community" amongst the players. They need to group together, and help each other out, deepening the reward for playing the game, and reinforcing each other. There is no class in the game that can “do it all, and do it all well”.

His contention was (and I tend to agree) that you need to do the same kind of thing in any massively multiplayer game. Start them off fairly well rounded, but as they go up the tech tree (in our RTS model), build in reliance on other classes as well.

He likened it to the current military forces we have. One player becomes an “Air Force” guy. He has basic ground units, but his specialty is air power. He gets all the cool airplanes, helicopters, and stuff like that. Another guy is specializing in the Navy. He gets the same basic ground units, but really works out on his naval units, with destroyers, subs, cutters, stuff like that. The last person is the “Army”. He starts off with the same rudimentary ground units, but then is able to develop all sorts of additional tanks, infantry, artillery, etc. While all of these people would be fine in the early stages with the rudimentary units, as they specialize up the tech tree, they end up needing to rely on each other to build a fully implemented strategic force. Please note that this is just an example… it is not how Sovereign is planning on doing things

The benefit from a gameplay perspective is that you are encouraging people to band together in some fashion. It doesn’t matter whether you call it a house, a club, a guild, an alliance, or a faction, but you are assisting people in grouping together.

Of course, since everyone starts with the rudimentary units, if they choose to be a loner, they certainly can, although they won’t be ultimately as effective as people who join together and cooperate.

I’d be interested in your comments about this view.

Monday, April 2, 2001

Do you have what it takes?

Froech posted more info on testing games in general today on the official forums.
Absolutely, we're going to do our own "alpha" testing to get as many bugs as possible out before we kick it up to beta.

Yes, we do have a paid QA department... and as I've mentioned before, we are currently playing twice a week, for an hour or so. The purpose of these games isn't to be fun, but to help us identify bugs, both major and minor, before we even head to an "alpha" state. As we put in some new system, or new art, we test it, we work it, we massage it. We really want this to be a game that you'll never stop playing, and our efforts are geared in that direction.

That being said, several of you have hit on the truth of beta testing. What we don't want in beta testing is someone who is playing to be able to say "I played in beta testing before everyone else did".... we're looking for those people who can play, identify areas of concern, write intelligently about what they found, why it was wrong, even suggest a possible solution (although the last part is not necessary). We are looking for people who will intelligently test the game. Sure, at some point we'll need server load testing (although we can do a lot of that ourselves) which will only require great numbers of people in the game building, fighting, trading, etc at the same time...

But the key to a beta test is the ability of the user to communicate the problems they find to the development team, so they can be identified, duplicated, and fixed before the game goes "live".

I think that we've got a pretty good community of these kind of people already here in this bulletin board, so I'm not too worried about it. In other places, I've had difficulty, but I don't think I will with you all.

So, rest easy. When it gets to be time to start talking about a beta test period, I'll start talking about it here in the boards. Long before I set up a sign up web page. You folks who read here regularly will be the first to know.

But it isn't yet.

Froech
