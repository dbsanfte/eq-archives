SOVault Interview: Clint Worley - 04-05-00

We once again continue our series of ongoing interviews with Sovereign Producer Clint Worley. Sovereign is the much anticipated massively multiplayer real-time strategy game that has the entire gaming industry talking. In our latest interview with Clint we discuss the role of morale in Sovereign and the effects it has.

How does moral affect your units?
Each unit in the game has morale that is tied to it. This morale dictates the general reaction to orders and also is a modifier to the offensive and defensive capabilities of each unit. The better a units morale the better it defends and attacks and the complete opposite is true for low morale units. There are ways to rally units morale so that you are not stuck with scare units that run from the sight of a mouse * smile *.

In what ways can morale be changed?
Combat, Character development, player actions, trade, espionage, diplomacy, etc… The units are not brainless cannon fodder that you can throw at an attacking force without careful planning and strategy.

What advantages are there going to be in keeping morale up?
Increased offensive attacks, improved defensive stats, they will obey orders without hesitation, They will repair faster and be all around more cheerful.

What are the consequences of having low morale?
The answer is the exact opposite of everything in the previous question. Just think of what you would do if your commander didn’t give you food or fuel, and taxed your family into the poor house. How good of a soldier would you be? I believe that the reactions of the units will be one of the most interesting sides of Sovereign. The fine line between overpaid and overfed, or poor and hungry soldiers is thin, a juggling act that will occupy most of the users time.

What happens to morale when you annex an enemy city?
Depending on the character class and the government that the “enemy” is determines the result. Also take into account how well you have been treating your population. The people in a player’s empire are the real determining factor in the players empire.

What sort of moods can your population have, and how does this affect your empire?
The people can hold parades celebrating the great empire that they live in, they can also protest the attacking on another player empire. The range of emotions that the population displays is broad and covers all the reactions that the people will have to the players actions.

In what ways can you affect the mood of another player's population?
Attacking them is the most direct way to change their mood. There are less direct ways that are tied to the character classes, skills and technologies. It all depends on the way that you want to play the game.

Once again we would thank Clint for taking time out of his schedule to answer these questions. Stay tuned for next week's feature!
