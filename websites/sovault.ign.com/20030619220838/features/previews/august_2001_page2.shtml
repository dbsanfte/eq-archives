Sovereign: An in depth overview

Set the lightning gun to full!
Now if you remember, I said that there was some sort of catastrophe and that the ability to make most modern technologies in the universe had been lost. Luckily, everyone also didn’t become stupid after the catastrophe and your empire, like everyone else’s, is now slowly rediscovering all of that lost knowledge. However, we can’t have all of these empires running around with the exact same technology tree. What fun would that be? Therefore, when an empire is created the player will need to choose one military and one civilian tradition for their empire to follow. A player’s military tradition - assault, defense, raider, siege, and stealth – will determine, you guessed it, what type military technology their empire will have access to, which will mainly be used on Excursion Worlds. Civilian traditions - economic, espionage, industrial, political, and research – on the other hand will determine how your empire operates as a whole. It’s the combination of these two traditions that will form the overall technology tree that a player's empire will have access to in Sovereign. As you may be suspecting by the possible technology combinations, it’s possible for a player to play Sovereign without really needing to focus very much on RTS combat. “If there's someone who really enjoys economics and diplomacy we're going to give you the avenue to play that way”, Geoffrey comments. “If you don't want to spend your time that way you can probably spend as little as five or ten percent on it. So it's really going to depend on what you focus on.”

What traditions a player chooses will also play into their guild life. Since each tradition has its strengths and weaknesses, a variety of players, using all different combinations of traditions, will be needed in order to maintain a fully functional, smoothly running guild. On an Excursion World someone with the defense military tradition will obviously have a strong defense around their base, and possibly the bases of other people, but they won’t be able to do squat when it comes to attacking another player. Also needing to be added into the mix is the fact that players will only be able to control a maximum of 100 units per Excursion World. This, combined with the fact that the largest of the Excursion Worlds can host up to 500 players on them, means that guilds/houses will certainly need a well rounded group of players, with multiple people in each technological specialization, in order to keep from getting wiped out.

But wait… there’s more.
Not only is Verant promising a strong RTS element to Sovereign but they’re also planning on giving players plenty to do when they’re not in direct combat with one another. The most of ambitious of which could possibly be their Galactic Trade Commission. While no specifics have been release on the GTC yet, Verant is planning on having an extensive economy within Sovereign including, according to Geoffrey, “stocks, bonds, resource trading, limit orders, and commodities.” From the sound of it, the GTC could probably be an entire game in itself.

Another part of the game that players could potentially spend a lot of time with is espionage. As with the GTC we don’t have a lot of details on what can be done in the espionage side of things. One thing that we do know is that while players aren’t able to initiate open combat on another person’s Homeworld, they are able to conduct more clandestine actions against them. In other words, a player’s empire could just be going along happy as can be and BOOM!!! One of the power plants on their Homeworld blows up. There’s such a wide range of actions that someone in the espionage tradition could conduct, ranging from eavesdropping to resource high jacking, that it’ll be interesting to see just how much players will be able to do.

Players will also have the ability to create a flag to represent their empire. After all, how many shades of red can you use to distinguish units? A player’s flag will essentially be the graphical representation of their empire. It will be on everything from communications to other empires, to having little flags next to player’s units. In order to keep “naughty” images from making their way onto someone’s flag, all images will need to be created via Sovereign’s own in game flag creator. Since its still possible to make questionable imagery Verant has said that the game will also “screen” each flag for both inappropriate images, and to make sure that it doesn’t look similar to someone else’s flag, before accepting it into the game. If all else fails there will always be GM’s. Since we’re not all artistically adept, Verant will also be providing some images for players to use.

With all of this only being a small portion of what Sovereign will be when finish, maybe someone should tell Verant that the “Massive” in Massively Multiplayer is referring to the number of people who can play the game at once, not the number of features the game offers. But then again, who’s going to complain? Verant’s current time frame for Sovereign is that the game will be released sometime in 2002, with the beta starting possibly as early as this winter. But in this industry anything’s possible. Let’s just hope that when Sovereign is finally ready, that it’ll have a better launch then some of the other Massively Multiplayer games that have recently been released. I look forward to seeing you all in the game when that time comes.

- Larry "LAR" Peters
