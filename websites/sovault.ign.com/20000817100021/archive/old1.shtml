Friday, October 15, 1999

Clint on Cities
Clint recently paid Sovereign Universe's Message Boards a visit. Here's the info:

Bullseye, it was a mistake. The previous information that was released in regards to the citizens reacting to your attack without the declaration of war was correct. To clear the record. The citizens of your empire will react to the actions that you take in the game. Depending on your overall strategy and goal, the user must deal with the management of his empire population.

I've seen the cities, they look cool but when I attack one does damage get done to one building or is it to the entire city.

There are civilian and military sections that can be developed and built in a city. The area of the city that is attacked by an enemy will determine the type of damage that is dealt to the users city. Losses in the civilian sector will hit the production, morale, and the tax flow. An attack in the military sector might cripple a key airport, slow the production of a barracks, or even cause a breach in the security of the city against espionage related units.

Will cities be randomly placed, or can I choose how close I would like to be to a friend's city? And can one person's city overlap and intertwine with an ally's city?

There are games where cities are randomly placed and there are cities where there are no cities placed at all. The decision is placed on the player that starts the game. We want to allow the users the option to start a game with the parameters that they would like to set. The GM's will take an active role in the monitoring of these settings and make sure that the games that are setup by the players are a fair and level playing field for all the users.

Thanks Bullseye!

Wednesday, September 15, 1999

Q&A with Clint Worley
As mentioned in the previous post, the folks over at Sovereign Universe recently sat down and asked Sovereign Producer Clint Worley a few questions. This is most defnitely an excellent read and something to check out. Here's an excerpt:

1. Is it true that you cannot ally with another player unless you are either the Diplomat class, or, a Diplomat class of player assists in talks?

This is not true. You can ally with any other player at any time. Your class does not determine whom you can ally with. It is true that you must send a diplomat unit to another players Capital City to begin the alliance and/or treaty.

2. It was said in a recent online chat that to stop established empires from attacking newbie empires, resources would be lost if that newbie empire was destroyed. Won't that take away a huge element of the game, where wars will be fought over resources? Wouldn't it be better if say, the resources were only 50% of their original value if you attacked and destroyed the newbie empire?

Resources will not be lost entirely, each city has a resources that is tied to its location on the globe. When a city is conquered either through force or persuasion the resource will remain. Depending on the type of takeover will determine the amount of resources left for the player to collect.

New Preview
The latest Sovereign preview to grace the internet comes to us from the blokes over at GamePen. In fact, this preview is only part one of a two part series. Unfortunately, there are no new screenshots attached with this article. Here's a blurb:

If you’re a peevish sort and insist on calling it an RTS, then ask yourself how many RTS games have you ever played where successfully capturing a city might take more than one evening of gameplay? How many RTS games will not let you build a second city if you don’t have enough population to settle the new city? How many RTS games might have your populace take a morale hit if you attack an opponent without first formally declaring war? How many RTS games have a supply-and-demand based world market where you can buy and sell goods, and where, interestingly, you might suddenly find the prices you have to pay jacked up if you drop a nuke on another player. There’s tons more to this game than harvesting, building, and attacking.


I guess the confusion is understandable. Worley relates how he himself was confused originally. “John Smedley (Verant’s CEO) came to me and said, ‘I have an idea for a game’ and proceeded to describe Sovereign. He asked me to come up with a design document for the game," Worley said. He initially thought that the game concept might be unworkable, but after working on the design document for several months, things came together. When he went back to Smedley to present his ideas, he did have one chief worry that has since proven to be true. “John," he said, “You can move tanks and troops around in this game in real-time. Everyone is going to think this is a real-time strategy game." Smedley’s response? “Let them think that." Verant would have time later to get the word out. “Later" is here now, even though the game isn’t scheduled to be complete until Q4 2000. A large beta test is scheduled for August of next year.

It is an excellent read and I recommend it to any Sovereign enthusiast.

Balanced server architecture
Bart answers a question regarding the capability of servers to distribute the load evenly as new players join games:

The server system in Sovereign will be transparent to the player, so you won't know (and it won't matter to you) whether the server hosting your game is running ten other games, or is one out of ten servers just hosting your game. Our expert server architect has designed the system with clustering and dynamic load balancing so that you'll get good performance regardless of what else is going on in the Sovereign "universe".

Bart Rothwell
Sovereign Lead Programmer

Tuesday, August 24, 1999

GameCenter Sovereign Feature
GameCenter has published a rather lengthy news article titled, Game Spin: Verant's Sovereign Solution. Only a small part of this multiple-page article actually talks about Sovereign, but the part that does was quite interesting. Here's what they had to say about Sovereign:

Last week I promised to talk a bit more about Verant's upcoming real-time strategy game, Sovereign. What's different about this RTS title is that it's a massively multiplayer, persistent world game--of sorts. Games will begin and end with anywhere from 4 to 500 players. A 500-player game might take 4 to 6 weeks to play, while a 4-player game might be over in a few hours.

Players will assume the role of a specific leader type, each with its own area of specialization. A player might take on the role of a diplomat or a clergy type, for example. Verant will encourage alliances by making some game technologies available only when two different types of players team up. For example, only the scientist can research nuclear weapons and only the soldier can build them. Players go up in rank and gain access to new technologies as the game progresses.

The game engine I saw at GenCon '99 was fully 3D with a rotatable camera that can zoom in and out. Starting from space, the camera can zoom in to a level of detail, such as the hatch on a tank. Mountains, forests, lakes, and other terrain features are all modeled. The "persistent" element comes into play when a player logs off and his or her burgeoning empire remains. The player can give control to an ally or have the empire go into defensive mode, which automatically doubles the defenses while the player is logged off. An interesting new twist that is sure to enrage wives and girlfriends (or husbands and boyfriends) is that the game is capable of emailing or even paging the player if the empire is under attack. "Sorry, Pat, I have to break off our evening early. My cities are under siege!"

The big question is whether players with no lives who are willing to be online 20 hours a day have a big advantage. Verant is working to counter that. Options may include limiting players to x number of hours per day in which to wage war and so on. It's too early to do much more than speculate, but at least Sovereign is a fresh concept for the somewhat-tired RTS genre. Besides, I've always wanted a reason to get a pager.

Thanks once again goes out to Jonric of the Vault Network for passing this info along.

Nukes: yes
Clint just made these revealing comments on the SOVault devboard:

I have been watching this board go from one side to the other for the last couple of days. Let me step in to clarify some troubled areas.
Nuclear weapons will be in the game, the type of nuclear weapons that will make the final release are yet to be determined. Due to the sensitive material on those weapons the only real way to gain such details would be a direct assault on Area 51. Let me know if any of you are successful.
When I made the decision to include nuclear weapons in Sovereign it was with the goal to have them be more of a threat then an actual attack option. Don't get me wrong, you will gain the ability to use the nukes as a devastating attack, but there are some obvious and some less obvious consequences when using nuclear weapons.
The people of the players empire are the actual driving force on the collection of resources and the stability of the empire. Using nuclear weapons may in the long term benefit you by destroying a "problem" city, but in the short term your people and your allies will react in a harsh and violent manner.
Nuclear weapons are only available when the player either ally with other classes that are required to gain the technology required. Nukes can also be obtained by trading.
To clear up some confusion, nuclear weapons are in Sovereign, are they used as often as other RTS games? Simply, NO. Using a nuclear weapon is a decision made after balancing all of the consequences and deciding that the negative repercussions are worth it.

P.S. the area that I am surprised that people aren't talking about is chemical weapons *hint,hint*

-Clint Worley

Producer/ Designer Sovereign

The Gist List
Sovereign was mentioned in Geoffrey Keighley's latest edition of the Gist List, GameSlice's behind the scenes of the game industry column. Here's what they had to say about Sovereign:

Although San Diego-based developer Verant Interactive only has one game under its belt (EverQuest), the company is aggressively moving into new genres with its recently announced RTS Sovereign. Billed as the first persistent world RTS that will support up to 500 players on one map, you have to admit the concept of having a massive battle that lasts for weeks on end is appealing. Although there have been some attempts at massive online RTS titles (Activision's NetStorm comes to mind), no one has quite been able to figure out how to keep a persistent world running in real-time 24/7 regardless of whether the player is in front of his or her monitor. Verant plans to combat this problem by allowing players to forge alliances and also by offering an option where players will be paged or e-mail when their base is under attack. Can you imagine being interrupted at dinner because your kingdom is under siege?

Sovereign is obviously a very ambitious product and no doubt the first large-scale attempt to meld RTS gameplay with a persistent world environment. There's no question companies like Westwood and Blizzard will also be making maneuvers into this genre over the next few years, but so far, you've got to give Verant points for such an ambitious concept – I can't wait to see if they pull it off!

Thanks goes out to Shawn, of Soveriegn Nation, for passing this info along.
