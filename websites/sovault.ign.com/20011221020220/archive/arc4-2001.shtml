Wednesday, May 23, 2001

SOE announces international plans

The following press release was issued by Sony Online Entertainment yesterday. This release talks about, among other things, the "real-time language translation" that we've heard so much about in Sovereign.
Sony Online Entertainment to Begin Localizing Online Games to Four Languages
SOE Set to Expand Lines of Communication Internationally For Best-Selling Massively Multiplayer Online Games
SAN DIEGO--(BUSINESS WIRE)--May 22, 2001-- Sony Online Entertainment (SOE) Tuesday announced that they have begun localization for several games at The Station, (www.station.com) allowing them to support a global community.

The initial phase of localization involves both human and automated translating agents, beginning with French, German, Japanese and Korean. The localization will allow players to fully interact textually within the game in their own language. SOE is committed to making its incredible array of online games just as accessible to gamers around the world as they currently are to English speaking players.

``The vision of a global gaming community has always been the core of our business model,'' said Kelly Flock, president, Sony Online Entertainment. ``The localization allows us to surpass language barriers and give gamers worldwide the best online gaming experience.''

Leading off the localized products is ``EverQuest®: The Shadows of Luclin(TM),'' the third expansion pack to ``EverQuest,'' the most successful online role-playing game of all time boasting more than 375,000 active subscribers comprised of players from all over the world. Other games planned for localization include: ``PlanetSide(TM),'' the world's first massively multiplayer first-person action game set in a persistent world and ``Star Wars® Galaxies(TM),'' an online game set within the Star Wars universe.

SOE has already begun the multi-language duplication process for several games. The identical version preserves the look and feel of the game while making it accessible to a foreign language audience. SOE's plans include the use of real-time machine translation of in-game player chat. This will allow players to chat even if they do not share a common language. The combination of localization and advanced translation software will facilitate SOE's position as a worldwide leader in massively multiplayer online games.

Sony's localization coincides with current increases in global interest in the Internet. According to the International Development Conference, more than 50% of current Web users reside outside of the United States; this percentage is expected to increase to 60% by 2003. By 2003, nearly half of worldwide e-commerce will take place outside the United States.

Sony Online Entertainment Inc. (SOE), the online gaming division of Sony Pictures Digital Entertainment, is a worldwide leader in massively multiplayer online gaming.

With headquarters in San Diego, SOE develops, publishes and distributes entertainment for the PC and online markets. With more than 10 million registered users, SOE's award-winning Web site, The Station® (www.station.com) hosts a variety of entertaining games and player communities spanning all genres. In addition to blockbuster hits ``EverQuest®'' and ``Jeopardy! Online,'' SOE has an array of cutting-edge online games in development such as ``PlanetSide(TM),'' ``Star Wars® Galaxies(TM)'' and ``Sovereign(TM).''

Friday, May 18, 2001

Sovereign Interview Preview

Yesterday I had the opportunity to conduct an interview with Geoffrey Zatkin, the lead designer for Sovereign. Here's a preview of what's to come.
Sovereign Vault: What types of technologies can we expect to see in Sovereign?
Geoffrey: Something we really, really want to do is start on the familiar and work our way up from there. We want players to be able to look at something and go "Oh, I know what that is and what it should do" purely based off of appearance. What we're going to do is we're using a storyline style called future regressive. Which is there was a more advanced society, they regressed somewhere, and it's pushing it's way back forward. That way we can have cool technologies, but we're starting at a much more familiar point for all the players.

- posted by LAR @ 10:51 AM PDT

Monday, May 7, 2001

Computer Games Magazine article update

Well it appears that Sovereign has changed quite a bit since we last had any significant information release on the game. I should also note that the screenshots in the article look very nice. Here's a small snippet of the article mentioned last night in the June issue of Computer Gaming Magazine.
A player in Sovereign will have a homeworld where he keeps his units when he's not in a battle and where research is conducted in real time whether he's logged on or not. It can take real world days, or even weeks, to complete a particular technology. When a player is in the game, he'll usually be fighting on one of Sovereign's contested planets. These planets are reached by transports, which have a limited amount of room. The choice of what you bring from your homeworld is a crucial part of the strategy.

I suggest picking up a copy of CGM if you wish to read more of this article.
- posted by LAR @ 7:05 PM PDT

Sunday, May 6, 2001

Computer Games Magazine article

Dachannien posted the following message on the official forums today about an article he saw in the June issue of Computer Games Magazine. There are some intesting things mentioned in Dachannien's post. I'll see if I can find a copy of the magazine and post more detailed information.
Dunno if it's been mentioned on the boards here anywhere, because I haven't been reading up a lot recently, but I thought I'd mention this to you guys.

There's an article in the June issue of Computer Games Magazine that talks about both Planetside and Sovereign. The screenshots for Planetside are pretty incredible, and it looks like it has a whole bunch of potential (too bad the whole FPS thing just tends to stress me out!).

The second half of the article talks about Sovereign, and while (to be brutally honest) the screenshots don't come close to competing with Planetside's visuals - and yes, I'm sure at this stage there's a whole lot of improvement planned - there was also a whole lot of talk about the redesigned game concept, which was something I guess I missed after I stopped reading the boards here.

The article includes items like GZ saying that choosing your starting resources for entering a game was a lot like building a M:TG deck, as you will take different things with you in your limited transport space depending on your goals for the game. It also intimates (but doesn't outright say) that the city concept is a lot less important than originally planned. And the theme has moved drastically from the original of current-day warfare to relatively far-future combat (especially considering that you source your starting resource from your home planet).

Now, maybe this is just me being the last to know, but that's a whole lot different from the Sovereign we'd been arguing about here for many months I guess time will tell whether it proves more popular, as well as more implementable....

- Dachannien
