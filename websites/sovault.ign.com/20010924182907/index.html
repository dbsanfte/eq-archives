Monday, August 13, 2001

Multi-tradition technology

Froech posted the follow reply to a "realization" that was posted on the Official Forums over the weekend.
Origional post:
I was just thinking about how their use to be multi-class technologies in Sovereign and how that would change in the dual tradition model we have now. Then I came to the realization of how versatile the tech tree is able to be with two traditions. Lets take the scientist civil tradition as an example. Let just say for this example that the scientist tradition is the only one capable of discovering nuclear power. Now lets say only the siege military tradition can create missiles. If that were the case then only a Scientist-Siege combination would be able to create nuclear missiles. The same thing would be true with the other traditions. Let say the defense tradition is the only one that can make energy shields. Combining that with nuclear power could create something like a stronger shield or a radiation shield. This makes an empire much more that two tech tree’s combined together, this makes each tradition combination a unique class in itself. Even though Player X may be Economic-Siege and Player Y may be Science-Stealth, if my traditions are Science-Siege there will technologies that neither of those players will ever have access to, and vice versa.

Froech:
But I do think that we've hit on the beauty of the two tradition concept...

The ability to have a fairly deep tech tree, with branches available to one type and not another. We're putting together a fairly extensive list of techs, which I think will please you.

But we are making an effort to balance it as much as possible, so that we don't end up with everyone playing the X-Y traditions, because they get nuclear missiles, and that's the only way to crush your enemies.

I envision that as a A-B tradition, I get cheaper costs for building units, although I don't get nukes... so I can put together a larger strike force faster than you can build nukes, and take you out before you get yourself set up to lob a few my way.

Sauce for the goose

Froech

Ryder's Interstellar Transport Division

Froech layed down, what I consider an important bit of information, about Sovereign today on the official forums.
Let's not fall into the trap of confusing the difference between a dropship, and a transport.

But no, you don't own the transport. That is the thing you're renting from the people who control interstellar travel. You don't have the technology to do that. (and watch out for the milage charges... it ain't the simple rental that kills you).

And yes, there may be a possibility of expanding your dropship, or building additional ones once you've gotten to the planet.

But that won't increase the size of the transport, just the speed at which you can move stuff from the ground to the orbiting ship.

Froech

Does the most time win?

Froech posted the following reply today on a thread talking about balancing Sovereign.
Indeed, that is another one of those "hard" design issues we're still dealing with. Some of the answers as as Larry and Kish have posted, and there are others.

But there must be a delicate balance between the "time invested" and the "have a job" folks... I don't think anyone wants to play a game in which you don't advance in some manner as you play. Short term goals, long term goals, getting more powerful... all of this is good, and part of almost (if not) all games I can think of.

The other side of the coin is how much fun would it be if you log on after work each day, to try to play a couple of hours for enjoyment, and each time you get crushed by the person who has spent the last 6 weeks online 24 hours a day? My vote would be on "not much fun".

But balancing between those two extremes is a real challenge.

Wednesday, July 25, 2001

You want me to figure out what?

The following, rather lengthy, reply was posted by Froech to a thread on "unit cap's" today.
But I think you're misunderstanding the need for the unit cap. How much information am I allowed to send down to your client? How fast does your modem run?
Lets look at it from a completely false and overblown example. Each unit takes X amount of video memory. So obviously, we could allow each person who has better video cards larger numbers of units, right? Nope... not unless you want to win by your opponent crashing everytime he looks at the battle your in with him. (Of course, that could be your strategy).

But wait, there's more. Not only does it take video memory, but it takes CPU. Each unit is an entity, which has to follow (limited) commands and AI. You told it to move from here to there. It has to figure out what is the best way to get from here to there, without running through that building... or that other unit, or any other obstruction. That takes CPU.

Then it has to fire. Can it fire anytime? Nope... has to be in range. Which means we're calculating range between every unit on the planet. Put 50 billion units on a planet, I assure you it will add up

Wait a minute... didn't we say something about stealth? Oh, I'll have to calculate this, too. More CPU. And what about defensive buildings? Sure, they don't move, so that removes that problem, but they need to know how far things are from themselves, and whether they should fire.

Wait a minute.. whether they should fire? Of course (slaps head), I'll need to calculate if you are a friend or enemy, based on your Great House/Guild/ local alliance flags, too.

OK... that is what, 6 or 8 variables (I haven't been keeping track, and besides, this is just an example). Each one changes every second. Or more often, depending on how often we set our time slicing. So, every moving object needs 8 variables checked every second, calculated, updated. Almost exponentially, since each object needs to keep track of every other object (only half exponentially... if X knows Y, then Y knows X)

Now, that's a lot of calculations going on each second. But wait... that's just on the server. Now I have to force some percentage of that information down to your client on your local machine. How much can I do? How much do I want to do (I want the calculations to occur on the server side, so it can't be hacked by some 10 year old). So, I've got 5000 units on the screen. Every second, I have to send you a minimum of 8 variables for each one of them (and it's way more than eight... don't forget command states, movement states, etc). How big is that variable? It sure isn't 0 and 1 for each state. Which means when you collect all that stuff together, I've got to send a boatload of information down that little 28.8 pipe that we're shooting for. Every second. And not only that, but the client needs to be able to recover from missed information.

Sure, we could make life easier on ourselves, and say "only people who have cable modems can play, so we can cram a whole bunch of stuff down to them at one time".... and alienate 80% of the potential purchasers of this game.

So, instead, we look at it from the perspective of "how much information can we fit in X, and still expect to have reasonable game play". Then we come up with a cap for number of units.

And then we pass that off to the game designers to figure out an "in-character" way to limti that kind of stuff.

It is all a balancing act. We'd love to have infinite units battling each other at the same time... but it just isn't going to happen soon.

Of course, that's not saying that in the future it won't be possible.

Froech

Monday, July 23, 2001

Lines of Communication

Froech posted the following reply to a discussion on different messaging options for Sovereign:
Ummm, no. It isn't really about privacy, at least in the way you are thinking.
If we provide any form of communication in our worlds, not only can we monitor it, but we actually have a responsibility to do so. That's one of the reasons we have that link down at the bottom of this page to the Terms of Service. Effectively, if we provide the means of communication, we're liable for it, at least in many people's eyes. So yes, we'll monitor what happens in the game.

On the other hand, if we provide AIM / ICQ interfaces, that will favor those people who have signed up (another entire hurdle to cover). If we do something similar ourselves, than we know that everyone is on an equal footing, and it makes game balancing a bit easier.

I'm always willing to bet that someone will hack our front ends, but we're trying to make things fair for the 99% of the people out there. No matter how much work we do, we'll never completely get that extra 1%.

And also, as larrypeters pointed out, getting AOL and ICQ to license us to directly interface with their systems would be expensive at best. Look at the dust up between Microsoft and AOL in the instant message wars.

We certainly don't need to create an AIM competitor for outside out games, after all, that isn't our market or expertise. But inside the games and the station, then sure, we're likely to do something quite similar. After all, we're all about creating communities here.

Froech

But that was two years ago. When Sovereign was shown at the 2000 E3 in Los Angeles, the game looked overly simplistic, and its graphics technology was already showing signs of age. Tuesday, July 3, 2001

Balancing

Froech made the following post on the official Sovereign forums today:
I'd have to say we've touched on a real issue to think about here....Unit balance.

Whether we make 5, 25, or 2500000000 (add your own number of zeroes here) units, the game simply won't be fun if it isn't balanced.

If everyone decides that class X gets unit X, and that is the ultimate unit, then everyone who has played longer than a couple of weeks will want to play class X, and rush through until they get that X unit.

Suddenly, diversity, and it's sibling fun get tossed out with the bathwater.

So, let's look at the problem here. You have Y number of classes, each class has Z number of units, and then AA number of technologies to research on the tech tree. How do you implement the techs so that they don't unbalance the units? Should you have all the techs have no effect on units at all? (seems a bit silly, but it is one extreme), should they all effect every single unit, or a mix of these two?

So, now, come up with the appropriate number of techs, that don't unbalance the units, and then make it work for each of the classes.

Sound like a lot of work? It is. And it isn't just number juggling, either. We want people to enjoy playing this game. We need to make it engaging, fun, exciting. Simply saying each unit has 15 attributes, totalling 100, and shifting the numbers between them won't hack it. We need to come up with interesting and valid reasons for ways that things work, why they won't work for some people, and then make it all meld with multiple styles of play.

Multiple styles? What does he mean by that, I hear you say. Well, I'll let you in on a little secret. I'm not your typical RTS type... nor do we just want to appeal to only RTS type players. I happen to enjoy other aspects of the game.

I was talking the other day to John Smedley, and we were discussing what appeals to people. He still is amazed that in EQ, there are people who spend all day fishing. He can't understand why, or how people can be fascinated with it... but he recognizes that there is a fairly substantial number that do. What does that prove? That in the initial design of the game, you have to think a little bit deeper than the initial gameplay, and figure out what other things are people going to do... and how will they impact the "core" gameplay, and what you need to build into the techtree, and what you don't.

Sound like fun? Apply for a job at a game company. You'll start going bald just like I am

Froech
