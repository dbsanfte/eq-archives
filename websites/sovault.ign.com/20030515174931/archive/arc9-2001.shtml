Friday, October 12, 2001

Brad McQuaid Leaves SOE

Sony Online Entertainment announced today in a press release that Brad McQuaid, one of the masterminds behind the wildly popular Everquest, has decided to leave SOE to explore other opportunities in the massively multiplayer market. It's sad to see Mr. McQuaid go but I'm sure this is not the last we've heard from him.
SAN DIEGO, CA - October 12, 2001 - Sony Online Entertainment Inc. (SOE), announced today that Brad McQuaid, one of the original creators of EverQuest, will be leaving the company after five years of service. Mr. McQuaid was instrumental in the launch and success of the world's largest subscription-based online game and was a key spokesman in the online community that nurtured EverQuest's growth.

"After five years of building the EverQuest franchise for Sony Online Entertainment, it's time for me to explore other opportunities in the creative development of massively multiplayer games," said Brad McQuaid. "I wish SOE the greatest success with all future releases and I have the greatest confidence in the ability of the development staff that we've assembled over the past few years to continue expanding the EverQuest experience."

"Brad's contribution to gaming philosophy that evolved into EverQuest has been recognized throughout the gaming world as one of the premier accomplishments of the past decade in the business," states John Smedley, Chief Operating Officer, Sony Online Entertainment. "It's been a pleasure working with Brad for the past few years as a colleague and a friend and I'm certain that he will continue to contribute innovative and exciting game technology in the future."

"Brad's passion for excellence in all things EverQuest has made a material impact in the success of this groundbreaking title," said Kelly Flock, President, Sony Online Entertainment. "I know he will continue to be a force in this emerging market and all of us at Sony Online Entertainment look forward to the possibility of working with him again in the future."

John Smedley, Chief Operating Officer and a partner in the conception and production of EverQuest, will assume Brad McQuaid's responsibilities.

Sony Online Entertainment Inc. (SOE), the online gaming division of Sony Pictures Digital Entertainment, is a worldwide leader in massively multiplayer online gaming. Headquartered in San Diego, California, SOE develops, publishes and distributes entertainment for the PC and online markets. With more than 10 million registered users, SOE's award-winning website, The Station® (www.station.com) hosts a variety of entertaining games and player communities spanning all genres. In addition to blockbuster hits EverQuest® and Jeopardy! Online, SOE has an array of cutting-edge online games in development such as PlanetSide™, Star Wars® Galaxies™ and Sovereign™.

Wednesday, October 3, 2001

It'll be ready when it's ready

Froech had the following to say today about releasing Sovereign. The quote in question is the first paragraph in this Adrenaline Vault interview with Anarchy Online's creator, Funcom.
Interesting quote, certainly.

There's no doubt that this company doesn't want to "rush" a game to release just to "cash in on a good thing".

The opinion around here is that you'll make much more money on a game done right than a game done quickly (and inherently bad). Sure, we'd love to have already released Sovereign.

But the fact is, it isn't ready yet. And we don't want to release it to you in a state that isn't playable (or even fun). So we'll keep quiet, and get this done right, and then release. When it's ready, not when we think we can get a quick buck.

Fact is, we want you to have fun with this product. The more fun you have, the better we feel.

Froech

Tuesday, October 2, 2001

Exactly when does a little become a lot?

Froech had the following to say today on the 100 unit limit currently planned for Sovereign.
I'm not sure I understand all of your post, but in reply to the first question, no, the 100 unit limit isn't set in stone. But it likely is in a lot of pebbles (ok, bad joke).

The fact that we're dealing with, as I thought I'd explained before, is that when you stick several hundred people on a planet, each with several hundred units, the amount of data being sent to each client so it can represent what is going on on the battlefield starts getting enormous, rather quickly.

One of the ways we're dealing with that is limiting numbers of units (there are other ways, too). On the other hand, if you're willing to change this from a realtime strategy game to a turn based game, with each turn taking 10 minutes to happen, we could probably lift that limit

Just think about 50 people in a battle... each with 100 units. That's 5000 units on your screen, potentially, with data about position, movement, fire rate, effects, damage assesment, and pathing. That is a lot of data to be passing through a relatively small pipe, for every frame of what you're going to see on your screen (and yes, we know to send diffs!).

It's a lot of info. We're trying to do our best to figure out some more inventive ways of handling the data so that we can raise the limit on the number of units.

Froech

Monday, October 1, 2001

Do the Developers listen?

Froech posted the following message on the official forums today. This post was made because of a comment that was reported by the website Lum the Mad as being made by Sony Online Entertainment CEO Kelly Flock. I would also note that, in an unrelated event, the website Lum the Mad appears to have been shutdown permanently as of today.
Several times over the last couple of months, I’ve seen the question being raised on whether we listen to your input or not.
I can, without a doubt, indicate that yes, we listen to you. We read these boards daily, we look at ideas raised, we come up with our own ideas based on comments made here.

We're amused that sometimes you come up with the exact same thing we've been talking about for months. We're amused that you come up with stuff that we hadn't considered. We use your arguments when discussing issues we're dealing with in certain areas of design.

Does that mean that you are designing a game for us? I'd have to say no. We have a specific vision. We have a design document that we're following. We know pretty much where we're heading. Do we stray off course? Sure, occasionally. The fact is, at least in any design document I've ever seen, you don't cover every conceivable notion (although every designer I've known tries their utmost to).

And we value your input. The lead designer often comes in to talk to me about ideas that are being bounced around here in the boards. He will be posting here as soon as possible, to help stimulate your thoughts with the concepts he and his staff have come up with.

We're quite confident we've got a great game here. But we recognize that we're not gods, we don't know everything, and we value the information that you all keep poking at us.

So don't feel like we don't care. We certainly do. We appreciate your input. We are listening, and taking what you are saying and evaluating our design to see that we're meeting both your expectations, and our original vision.

Thanks for sharing your thoughts with us. Please, keep doing so.

Froech
