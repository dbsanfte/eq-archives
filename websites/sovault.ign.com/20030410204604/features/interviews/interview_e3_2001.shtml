E3 2001 Interview

I recently had the opportunity to talk with Geoffrey Zatkin, lead designer for Sovereign, at this year's E3.  Discussed were the major changes in how Sovereign is being designed and how players are now going to interact with one another within Sovereign.

Sovereign Vault: First and foremost, what's changed since you've redesigned Sovereign?
Geoffrey: The last time the public really saw Sovereign was at the last E3. Since then what we've really done is reexamined some of the core components of the game. Looking for what was truly going to be the most fun and the most engaging experience. Everything that didn't look like it fit was ripped out or got redesigned. We still have a lot of the technology that we've been working on, but the way that we use the technology has changed. All aspects have been reexamined. Not all aspects have been changed.

SOV: Have there been any aspects of the game's design that have made you take a step back and do some creative thinking?
Geoffrey: Almost everything. We're trying to do two things that haven't been done in an RTS before. Which is be persistent and have a truly massive number of players, hundreds at a time, playing the same game against each other. And those two have been our biggest problems. Not as problems per say but as things to design in and design around. Coming down from on high in management those are the goals that we're going after, making those two aspects of it fine. From which all other design decisions are made.

SOV: What lessons have been taken from EverQuest and Tanarus that have affected design decisions that you've made for Sovereign?
Geoffrey: What we've taken is a lot of just really what we've learned and what we really want to do. In EverQuest how communities work, how player communities evolve over time, i.e. power gamers vs. casual gamers vs. role players. I come from a psychology background, so watching that [EverQuest] was fitting really interesting patterns of social psychology and seeing these things actually work online and follow patterns that happen in the real world we've been able to use a lot of research and a lot of reading and just a lot of personal experience to design in better community functions, better ways of rewarding players for when they do things, and in whole just making it a better experience. Having been a tester on Tanarus, while I was building EverQuest, because everyone in [the company] wanted to play Tanarus. It was just a fun game, so everyone in the company became a tester. Seeing that now developed and having worked on EverQuest we're taking all of the good community things we learned and a lot of our lessons on how players actually operate in a world and are transferring that now to a different genre.

SOV: In the past it's been said that the gameplay in Sovereign was focused on three area's: Diplomacy, Espionage, and Combat. Does this still hold true?
Geoffrey: Actually, yes we're expanding on that a little bit. We're definitely going to have good RTS combat. The diplomacy we're looking at has really interesting player grouping, player structures, ways to let little groups of players form into bigger groups of players form into huge groups of players and have fun gameplay on all three levels. That's going to involve a lot of diplomacy in the actual formation of that and in addition we're doing an entire diplomacy section to allow people to start making treaties and alliances between each of their factions and groups.
SOV: Is that just going to be on each planet or throughout the whole game?
Geoffrey: Throughout the whole game, part of our persistence is building up really cool groups. I can't talk about it that much yet, but when we talk about it to upper management that gets them excited, our designs for that. Even more than our RTS gameplay.
Espionage is going large part [of Sovereign], but it is not going to be as key a part as diplomacy. We're also going to bring in some parts of propaganda, and we're bringing in a lot of economics. We're going to have some really cool galactic trade market stuff that you haven't seen in any other kind game for the people who like putting in stocks, bonds, resource trading, limit orders, going back and forth on commodities that you actually pick up. We're going to have some pretty cool commodities. Getting and having them in an arena to do something with is going to be kind of fun.

SOV: How much of a player's time can they expect to spend on non-combat related activities?
Geoffrey: That's going to be up to the player a lot of the time. If there's someone who really enjoys economics and diplomacy they can probably spend eighty to ninety percent of their time on that because we're going to give you the avenue to play that way. If you don't want to spend your time that way you can probably spend as little as five or ten percent on it. So it's really going to depend on what you focus on.

SOV: What types of technologies can we expect to see in Sovereign?
Geoffrey: Something we really, really want to do is start on the familiar and work our way up from there. We want players to be able to look at something and go "Oh, I know what that is and what it should do" purely based off of appearance. What we're going to do is we're using a storyline style called future regressive. Which is there was a more advanced society, they regressed somewhere, and it's pushing it's way back forward. That way we can have cool technologies, but we're starting at a much more familiar point for all the players.

SOV: Recently Kelly Flock, President and CEO of Sony Online Entertainment, was quoted as saying "There's a team-based aspect in both our FPS and RTS [games] that goes beyond anything available", referring to Sovereign in that statement. Could you elaborate a little bit more on that comment?
Geoffrey: Team play is going to be critical to Sovereign. Something we realized very early on is that say we run a game with 400 people. It would really sucks if you could only have one winner, per say, in a 400 player game, because no one wants to lose 399 out of every 400 games. So very quickly what we moved on to was saying that it won't be just you going to planets, you'll be going with a team or an alliance. It doesn't matter whether you go in with a team or form one on the planet, but we are really, really going to be strongly encouraging teaming up. To do that we're encouraging interdependence between players in some fairly key ways that is going to make it similar to EverQuest, i.e. when you go in and form a party you want a warrior and a cleric. We are going to have people with roles that they pick through their technology and they're going to be really good at certain things and not as good at others. So the player group that teams up together, who allies and plans out a strategy is going to do much better. We're bringing in true strategic aspects to what normally is a very tactical genre.

SOV: One of the things that some people disliked in EverQuest was that you needed to group with other people in order to play the game. That sounds like what you're leaning towards in Sovereign also?
Geoffrey: You won't need to, but it will be highly advantageous to you. We're also looking into ways to make it very, very painless to end up in a group or a team. Literally so much that you don't have to put any effort into it, because we recognize that it was one of the really troublesome parts of EverQuest. Not grouping per say, but getting a group. Getting a group always took time and we're very specifically addressing that issue. But honestly, if you're playing a massively multiplayer game you're playing it with other people. Otherwise you'd be going out and buying Warcraft III or Emperor: Battle for Dune. We're building a larger community based game.

SOV: In the June issue of Computer Games Magazine they wrote "[Previously Sovereign was] an ongoing RTS session in which you built a base that could have been attacked when you weren't online." "But now the homeworld concept offers downtime when players aren't online." Could you elaborate a little on that?
Geoffrey: Yea, one of the things I realized when I came on as a designer and what we looked at a lot was that we want this game to be fun. A persistent game where you're always online and can be attacked at any time just doesn't have the fun aspect, for a couple of reasons. Mostly because getting attacked at any time when you're not online, especially in a game that would last for weeks, is really hard to have fun on. You come back and your whole empire is destroyed, I'm mean that's just not a lot of fun for you. So what we're doing is we're look for ways to keep the persistent aspect of the game while still letting people not feel like their getting chimped for not playing all the time. One of the things we didn't want is to make someone have to play eight or twelve hours a day to be competitive. We want to be able to have somebody be able to play two hours a day and have a really good experience. If we said that you could be attacked anytime you weren't online that's going to naturally gravitate towards the guy who has the twelve hours to play a day and the guy who wants to play casual isn't going to have fun with that. We really didn't want to setup a game where that could happen. So we made more decisions leaning towards a way to keep the game persistent but at the same time lets you to go in and out and not feel like you're getting destroyed because you can't play for one day.

SOV: How long do you think the typical game will last now?
Geoffrey: I would say a typically game would last indefinitely, once you log in you're always playing. However, you will be playing RTS style excursion games that will last anywhere from half an hour to two or three real days.
SOV: Down from a month.
Geoffrey: Down from a month. Though because there are victory conditions, if all sides are pretty evenly matched it could continue indefinitely. We are allowing for that to happen, but we're also allowing for much shorter games to happen. There will be ways to win excursions.

SOV: How are buildings going to be built now. Are you staying with the city model or moving away to military only bases?
Geoffrey: On excursion worlds you're going to construct military bases, because you aren't settling there for long amounts of time. It's primarily a military, propaganda, espionage kind of thing. On your homeworld you'll be building and improving huge cities. So your huge building structures are going to be taking place on the homeworld. Your small ones are going to take place where you spend smaller amounts of time.

SOV: Are players going to be able to attack other people's homeworlds?
Geoffrey: No. There will be things you can do against other people's homeworlds but military combat is not one of them.

SOV: Are these bases still going to be tied to the resource centers on the planet?
Geoffrey: Right now we are looking at tying them within the proximity of certain resources. One of our big problems is on a world the size that we need to have four or five hundred players simultaneously there's just certain ways we need to compute things to make it run faster. As much for gameplay as for technology that is one of the things we are working off of. That is not to say we'll be there at the time of launch, we may change our direction, but currently we feel that is the optimal way to give players the most fun and have our technology work fast enough so that they can have fun.

SOV: In the past it has been mentioned that there will be population moral and different government types in Sovereign. Is that still something that's being planned?
Geoffrey: I don't know if we're going with government types per say. Pretty much we're setting you up as a dictator. There will be lots and lots of actions that you can choose on how to run your homeworld, how to run your cites, how to run everything that will influence it back and forth. But we're not calling them government styles per say. You're not going to say "I'm a democracy" and "I'm a theocracy", but there will be other cool things to do with that.

SOV: Also along those lines is the technology tree in Sovereign. How has that been affected and are players still going to have predefined classes and some technologies that will only be available by combining other technologies?
Geoffrey: Our technology tree is going to be really huge. It is going to be something that will take years of real play to fully get through. We're designing a very good tech tree. We are still looking… we're not calling them classes, we're calling them traditions. Because in some ways it is the tradition that you follow which is the way that you do things. What we've done is looked at the styles of play and we're molding traditions after styles of play from players. So your going to be able to go in there and say "How do I like to play an RTS game? Oh there we go, this one suits me."

SOV: In the past the development team has said that you're looking into putting real time language translation in to Sovereign. Is that still something that you're looking into.
Geoffrey: Absolutely. We want to be able to go fully multinational and we feel that we don't want to keep our game America or Euro centric. We want to be able to have people play in the language their familiar with because that's going to lead to much better online communities. If we're going after big online communities we want to support them in every possible way that we can. Actually, our server programmer was one of the pioneers on some of the language translation software years ago and he's helping us out with that. It's pretty rad.

SOV: What's currently being looked at to keep players connected to Sovereign while they're not playing. In the past things like being alerted via e-mail and handheld devices have been mentioned.
Geoffrey: We're going to see what makes sense and we're going to do that. If e-mailing you or calling you on your cellphone or sending you a PDA announcement makes it a better game experience we'll have that in there. If it ends up making it not as fun, we'll take it out. It's all about user experience, everything else is secondary.

SOV: Finally, how many simultaneous planets will a character be able to be on at any one time?
Geoffrey: We're looking at keeping you on one planet at a time. Meaning you go somewhere, that's where you are. We're not going to restrict how many you can travel to, but we are going to say where you are is where you are. Mostly because, honestly, juggling multiple planets isn't going to be as much fun as engaging in a full RTS style experience on one of them.

Sovereign Vault: Thank you Geoff, it's been a pleasure talking to you today.

- Larry "LAR" Peters
