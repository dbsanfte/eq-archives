- 10/16/01 - I'm a Newb!

Well here I am playing a new game and I'm officially a newb once more. It won't be for long though and from what I've seen thus far, Camelot is amazing, albeit basic at this point. 

One of the hardest things to do, from my perspective, is to shake off old habits and old stereotypes from past games.  There are alot of EQish things in this game so for an AC player, I have to put it in my mind that this is not EQ and look past those things. There are also alot of EQ players so there is alot of EQ lingo that I don't particularly understand, like why the hell are new critters attacking called an add? Got me, seems lame.

This is a relatively zoneless game. You will incur zoning when going to the major cities or dungeons but nowhere else. Still you have EQ people running around asking what zone this or that is in and I equate it more to what Obsidian Plains was in AC. It's an area, not a zone just in this game it'll tell you that you are entering and leaving it, making it easier for you to tell where you are.

I chose to be on the Palomides server before the game started because I was hoping that it'd be a less popular choice due to the non-flashy name like Lancelot or Galahad, yet it still sounded kind of cool. Unfortunately, it seems more people have had the same thought. It's constistantly in the top 3 servers in popularity, carrying 2250 to 2500 people in prime hours. I just hope it dies down a bit once the game has been out for awhile.

I wanted to be a follower of Thor since I first heard you could, for personal reasons, so I took a norseman because I felt it's more balanced stats would be better for a more balanced class that fought and cast.

You get 30 points to spread around 6 stats on creation. You can dump all those points on one stat, but you'll be gimped if you do. after the first 10 every point added to a stat costs you 2, so it's better to split it up. I put 10 in strength and constitution, 5 in quickness, 3 in dexterity and 2 in piety. Looking back I think I should have put the 2 on Intelligence but I'm still not 100% on that.

The first 5 levels in this game goes extremely fast. Each critter gives you about 10% of the xp you need so you probably will only need about 60 critters to get to 5 where you choose your specialized class.

Every level you get a certain amount of points that you can put in your specializations, or skills. I think before 5 it is 1 times yoru level so if you went to level 2, you got 2 points. I put a point or two in parry, but decided to hold off the rest till I hit 5 so I could dump them all on hammer and stormcalling.

Stormcalling is the Thane's specialization for casting spells. I am generally splitting my points between hammer and stormcalling pretty equally because I think stormcalling adds that extra punch that the other classes don't get. It allows me to cast 2 direct damage spells before I even get into combat and then gives me a stat buff and a damage buff to help me do even more damage while I am in combat. It's nice.

Trying to get money for this game is nuts. It's an uphill battle. I didn't buy any equipment until level 5 and even then had a hard time equipping myself with a full suit of armor. (Something I don't think I actually did until I was around level 8.) And then you need to upgrade your equipment every 3 or 4 levels which makes things more difficult. Luckily it seems to be leveling off now at level 14. Equipment seems to be lasting a little longer and I am not buying new equipment basically every day. Still I am very poor, I have almost 4 gold but my next tunic will cost 7 unless I can find a player to make it for me.

I have found that the real way to make money in this game is to do tasks. Various NPCs in the game give out tasks if you whisper task to them that consist of delivering an item to another NPC or finding and killing some critter. You get a pretty good amount of money and experience for these tasks and I've even heard of people getting items on occasion but I would not be one of them. The task system is basic, but it offers a nice bit of variety and purpose to your hunting. 

Statage

I probably won't be displaying my stats too often for this game. The only thing you get consistantly is power, endurance, and hit points. You do get a stat gain every level but I'm not sure what or how much, I doubt it's anything major that I'll need to update.
