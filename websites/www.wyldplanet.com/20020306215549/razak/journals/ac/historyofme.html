- 05/10/01 - The History of Me. - printable version

Why do you need to know about me you ask? Isn't this a Role-playing game? Well let me tell you, I don't subscribe to the theory that MMORPGs are true role-playing games. I don't believe you truly can role-play for such an elongated time, that's just me. If I role-played a character for 8 hours a day, I would become that character and would no longer be role-playing anyway.

I come online, in part, to meet other people from different places around the world. I like to hear different views from different places. For instance, I used to hang out on an Australian IRC server and don't know a single Australian who likes Foster's Beer, which begs the question.. "What is Australian for Beer?" (My answer would be beer.) 

If I wanted to meet characters that other people make, I would read a book, thus I expect to meet people in Asheron's Call, not their characters. It's all well in good if you act differently than you would RW, it's a different setting, it's bound to happen, it works that way for me too.

I discovered the glories of the internet and online play on a game network called Imagination Network, I probably joined a few months after it got renamed from The Sierra Network, this was about 8 years ago. This must have been the standard made for networks like The Zone, you could play various card games, RPGs, Action Games, Trivia games, Casino Games, don't know if there were strategy but it wouldn't surprise me. In fact I would personally prefer to play an updated version of this with new games than anything out there, I still love the design.

It is here that I found The Shadows of Yserbius. This was mainly a text-based online RPG. I played mainly as Norrit in Fear Forest and was a Count (and later Duke) in the guild of NOR. I have no idea why I stuck around in the guild as long as I did, but am very much glad to be out, the politics there were way too much for me.

After the demise of INN, due to hourly pricing, I moved on. I tried The Realm, actually sort of liked it, but there never was much to it so I went in search for another. 

I found Dark Sun Online, which I felt was the first true MMORPG, The Realm sort of was but was never really big enough or popular enough I thought. DSO was part of The Entertainment Network (TEN), and I believe the price was $9.95 a month for the entire network, the only problem with it was that it didn't offer very many games that weren't already free elsewhere, so again it died.

After my stint in DSO I turned to UO. Now this was a great game I felt. It had a ton of potential, it trully was like a real world. There was alot of area to look through, there were plenty of things to do and you could customize what you could where, what a concept! The problem was the bugs, they shipped WAY before it was ready and I refused to pay for such a piece of crap so I turned to Emperiana.

Emperiana was sort of like a more graphical Yserbius, but still very text based. It was a decent enough game, but it didn't really have much for characters to make themselves nor was there anyplace to sit around and just talk like there was in YS. And let me tell you, when a game is that monotonous you really do need a place to just sit back and chill.

At this time I was pretty much left without a game to play, I spent my time in the aforementioned Australian IRC channel and boded my time for either AC or EQ to come out. I don't remember if I signed up for AC's beta, but I didn't participate. I did get into EQ's beta but they sent me a broken CD and could not install it, I did later try to play the game but it was lame and that is that.

I now enjoy AC thoroughly and look forward to games such as Star Wars Galaxies, AC2, Shadowbane, and a couple other games that I forget the name of. AC has gotten a tad boring recently, but I am content all the same and the last few months have been very good patch-wise so i have no complaint about paying the 10 bucks.
