 - 11/13/01 - The Anatomy of a Bad Healer

In my experience there are 4 main reasons why a group has a tough time staying alive: Lag, unexpected spawns, fighting above your level, and bad healers. Now the first two can't be controlled, but the second two can.

Recently I've had a few run-ins with some bad healers. This compels me to write about what I think these healers are doing wrong. Now I feel I should put a disclaimer at the start of this journal, to siphon out any hate mail that I may get for this journal. 

<disclaimer start>I have never been a healer, and probably never will, it's not my style. I also have the utmost respect for healers and this journal is not a knock on healers in general but some very bad ones that ARE out there on every server, just like there are many bad tanks, and many bad runeys. <disclaimer end>

Now this being said that every class will have it's bad eggs, it's alot more devastating to a group if the healer is bad than it would be for say a shadowblade to be bad. Reason being, they are the ones keeping everyone else alive and if they are bad at doing that, then your whole group is basically screwed. I've actually had a healer tell me recently that he should be pulling because he had more hits and higher af than me,  fool.

The first, and probably most common problem is healers who want to pull. Yes healers can pull, i know many healers who are good at it, but if you have a Thane, Skald, or Hunter (or the realm relevant class)... those are your best choices to pull. There are a couple reasons why a healer (and mages) shouldn't pull. 

First, the time spent gallivanting lookin for something to pull, is much better spend sitting your butt down and getting your power back so you don't have as much downtime. I personally am a very fast puller, I will pull as soon as I am able. I won't stop until the healers are nearly on empty and all healers, not just one. Unfortunately healers generally don't always like this style, but we usually live fine enough and always get more xp out of doing it this way. But the result is healer pullers will usually wait until they get closer to full to pull the next, resulting in lower xp totals.

The second reason it isn't the best idea for a caster to be pullin is that it is sometimes really hard to get the aggro off someone who cast a spell to pull. And the critter's attacks will interrupt a healer's casting, not a good combo at all. This problem is a little less if it's on a runey or something because then you can still have a healer casting but you don't get this when the healer is being attacked. To make matters worse if the healer gets really low on health he will use his insta heals which only aggros the critters more. Then we as a tank get yelled at because we couldn't get yelled at because we couldn't get the aggro off, wonder why.

Now there is one main time when you want the healer to pull. When you are pulling groups. To start out by mezzing a creature is the preferable way to start a group battle, not mezzing it while they are in our faces. So there IS a time and place for everything. But no matter what, if there already IS a designated puller in the group, don't pull, no matter how antsy you are getting. If you have a problem with the current puller, say something like "could we pull a little faster please?" You help no one by just pulling your own stuff.

The second problem that happens to make a bad healer is that he just doesn't understand the part. He wants to be a healer, he just doesn't get it. Sometimes they know they are having problems figuring it out, sometimes they don't. If you are past 20 or 25 and still struggling to figure out how exactly to play in a group so you and your fellow members aren't dieing alot, you should probably restart because you never will. This problem I think actually happens very little in reality. Group members may think it happens, but often it is not the case at all. Which brings me to...

The third problem. Bad chemistry. And this isn't anyone's fault at all. I think for a healer and group to get along they have to have some inkling on what the other is doing and for some reason on some level, that doesn't always happen. Sometimes it just doesn't work. 

You can either try to continue on hoping that the chemistry will grow as you spend more time together, or you could choose to separate. This all depends on the relationships involved, if it's just a random grouping I suggest a split, otherwise it might be worth the growing pains if it's with friends.

The last problem you could encounter, and probably the rarest, are those who don't know how to manage their concentration. This sometimes happens when a healer has friends in group, he'll give those friends more buffs than everyone else whether they need it or not, sometimes this is fine. I often also find myself with purity of mind, and for me at least I don't find a need for it even though I am using alot of spells to pull. I don't go through mana that fast where I need a faster regen rate.

This problem is easily solved but probably won't if it happens to too great a degree because I think the majority of this problem occurs with the aforementioned friends in group, and healers will always wanna protect their friends more than strangers, that's just human nature. However, if you need a spell or don't need one, it's easy enough to mention it and a lot of the time you'll get what you need somehow.

As a tank, or other group member, if you aren't comfortable with the current healer, either /sending other members and getting a consensus on what to do with the guy or just finding a way to leave the group altogether are probably your best bets. If it's a friend, I would suggest talking to him about your worries or maybe even what you heard others say about him. Your not helping a friend if he's becoming known around the realm for being a bad healer, and believe me people keep track of who are and are not bad healers.
