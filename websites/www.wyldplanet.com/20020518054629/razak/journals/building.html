- 10/23/01 - Building the Night Away
Well my second week of play is gone and I have gotten up to level 22. Anyone hoping that you start to get more money at this point, think again. You are just as stretched out for money now as you are when you are level 10. My next hammer will probably cost me more than 20 gold and that's from a weapons tradesperson.

With the rather large cost of playing in this game I thought it might be a good idea to start on a trade. Now you have to train in one of four trades: weaponsmith, armorcraft, tailoring, and fletching (not 100% on fletching.) The only hitch is you can't un-train a trade as of yet so I decided to start a whole new char because they are talking of adding new tradeskills in the future and I wanna make sure I get the right one on my permanent char.

Now there are a lot more trade skills than just these four, but those are the main four and decide how far you can get into the minor skills. The rest of the skills include Metalworking, Woodworking, Siegecrafting, Leatherworking and Clothworking. Most of these are used primarily for creating the parts for the main skills. The exception to this of course is siegecrafting, which makes siege weapons, and woodoworking, which I believe you need to make wooden shields, though not sure.

I decided upon making a skald because I don't know if stats affect the trade skills in any way and I wanted the fast running speed. So I put 10 points in Strength, 10 in Coordination, and 10 in Charisma (the stat that affects skald spellcasting). I quickly brought him up to 5 and followed the skald way and put all my points into the Battlesongs skill.

I then went into Jordheim and found out the master armorsmith near the forge and took up that skill. Now at first I thought the way to do everything was to just start making stuff to raise my skills, it seemed to work but I soon found myself out near Mularn trying to get more cash.

What I didn't know was that I could go to the armorsmith trainer, Gest, to get tasks to make various pieces of armor for various merchants and trainers in Jordheim. This is a really great way to get cash because items that only sell normally for 20 copper will all of a sudden go for 40 in a task, and with my skald speed the time it takes to deliver these is minimal.

There is one downside to these craft tasks though, it takes a lot longer to gain skill in your crafts than if you just made a whole lot without the tasks. The reason is because you are spending a lot of your time running around town and talkin to the various NPCs involved. Alas, this is probably the only way that you can really do the crafting unless you got a big guild supplying you money during the early days.

The interface is good, but not inspiring. You don't have to hunt down recipes on websites or in the game to get the next best piece of armor, it's all easily accessed in the interface by right clicking on the icon. I really hope they can get some special crafting quests in there kind of like the epics that will have some new items to craft, or just items to help crafting, to make crafting a little more interesting every now and then.

Now for armorcrafting, and I'm assuming any metal-related skill, you need to be by a forge to make your wares. Needless to say this makes the jordheim forge extremely crowded at night time when there are more people on. There are more forges around the world, I think I've seen one in Haggerfell, but the problem is that there aren't all the merchants you need near them so they are often unused. 

I hope that Mythic gets the second city up with another good place to do your tradeskills because I believe this is one of the main reasons that cities are so laggy, especially in the evenings. It's kind of tough on the servers for 20+ players to be huddled around a little forge. Oh and give us a horse route from the current cities to the new cities so trades people can get there easily.

Overall I think Mythic has done a good job with the trades, but still have a ways to go. It isn't nearly as good as UO's system and I wonder how much it will have longevity. After all, there is no skill loss like UO, once people get to the max skill and can build everything, there's not a whole lot of reason to keep on being a trade person. Maybe they'll come up with a little quest to get a secondary trade when you get that high...
