The Cleric

Clerics are the healers of the realm, at higher levels they are able to bring players back from the dead (with much of the experience lost in the death).  Subsequently they are one of the most sought after classes in the realm.  It is common to hear the cries “group looking for a healer” and “looking for a res, will pay well”.  There is certainly little need for a Cleric to be alone, it’s not a good idea for a Cleric to try to fight alone.

 

Remember you are first and foremost a healer.  You also have “buffs” to add protection and resolve to yourself and those you join with.  Your actual “fighting” days will likely fade away soon after you get your first few levels.  Your comrades will want to keep you alive so that you can keep them alive and well (yes, you have the ability to cure many ailments also).  So be prepared to work with others pretty much all the time.

 

You may want to spend a bit of time developing some “tactical skills".  Since most of the time you’ll be a bit removed from the center of the battle you will often be in a good spot to make “judgment calls” for a group.  For instance, the fighters who are trading blows face-to-face with the enemy may not know that two more enemies are about to join the fray.  You may have the best “vantage point” to let the group know when it’s time to run or evacuate.  At times the group will expect you to have the presence of mind to camp out entirely, knowing they are facing certain death and will need to be resurrected after “the coast is clear”.  Letting your friends die in this case is not cowardice, it’s good tactics. 

It will be of no value for you to run into the midst of the battle with your blunt weapon getting yourself killed.  In all honesty, some Clerics don’t carry weapons after reaching certain levels.  Another handy tactic will be for you to “king loot” for the group.  That means collect all the loot so the melee types don’t get all weighed down and slow.  You then split it up when your group calls it a day.

 

          So, teamwork is essential for you, tactical (and leadership) skills are strongly recommended, and soloing is pretty much discouraged.  If you want to be successful you’ll really need to be a “people person”, not a “loner”.  Here are some tidbits of information about characteristics you may find worth consideration.

 

          Wisdom (WIS):  This is the most important characteristic for you.  You are a student of the “priestly lore” of old, seeking to benefit those around you.  There will be many spells to learn, and failures on your spells may often leave one or more corpses of your comrades on the ground.  Learn your spells well, and have the wisdom to know when they need to be used.

 

          Magic (sv_Magic):  It may seem a bit odd that this is listed second but a brief explanation may help.  You will get into situations where your enemies have a healer available.  Both you and your group mates know that, if you jump on someone other than the healer, he’ll keep healing the guy you’re fighting and it will seem like you’re fighting an invulnerable enemy.  So the group says “get the healer first, we’ll deal with the rest after”.  Guess what?  All you have to do is heal one of your group mates in battle and your enemies are going to say that same thing about you.  They’ll try to hit you with everything they can, especially if you’re on a PvP (Player versus Player) server.  But don’t get overconfident if you’re not on a PvP server.  Ask somebody how Vox or Nagafen feel about clerics healing people attacking them.  Some magic resistance can help a lot.

 

          Armor Class (AC):  This kind of goes with the situation described above.  All your group’s enemies will want the healer (you) dead.  Somebody’s bound to land a few blows or spells on you.  Some decent protection to keep you alive while the group gets the enemy under control is a very good idea.  Remember, if you’re taking a lot of damage you won’t even be able to concentrate well enough to heal yourself.

 

          Stamina (STA):  This has a couple of points of pay-off.  Probably most important is you have to have some stamina to run, especially if you’re all heavy from “king looting”.  It also slightly increases the amount of damage you can take and affects your swimming so it’s worth giving a little attention to.

 

          Mana:  Wisdom directly affects your mana reserve but any extra reserves are good news.  You are, after all, going to be primarily depending upon your ability to cast spells (which directly drains the old mana pool) and running out of mana can mean real trouble.

 

          Strength (STR):  No, you’re probably not going to be bashing the “baddies” much but you may be carrying a lot of loot.  That’s especially true if you’ve volunteered to “king loot”.  Plus, that nice armor you’re wearing may be pretty heavy all by itself.  Strength affects how much you can carry and, like most healers, I’m sure you plan to be pretty wealthy.  Actually, you’ll have to be pretty wealthy to afford some of your spell components (which also need to be carried).

 

          Disease (sv_Disease):  You’re able to heal diseases but sometimes it’s a bother, some resistance is handy.

 

          Cold (sv_Cold):  You have “buffs” to help handle cold but some casters throw some real nasty cold “blasts”.  There are reports that there’s a Kly in the Crypt of Dalnir with a cold spell that hits for more than 1,000 points.  An ounce of prevention may be worth a pound of cure.

 

          Fire (sv_Fire):  Same type of thing as the cold (above).  Once again, an ounce of prevention is worth a pound of cure.

 

          Poison (sv_Poison):  Another thing to be prepared for (like the stuff above).

 

          Hit Points (HP):  Unless you’re mega-twinked by the time you get armor with very good hit points you’ll be fighting things that hit really, really hard.  Some added hit points are nice if part of the piece but don’t go relying heavily on them.  After all, you are a healer.  Restoring hit points is what you do.

 

         Dexterity (DEX):  This is pretty much of the most value in regards to accurately hitting with a weapon and how often a weapon “procs”.  After you get very much past the “newbie” level you’re not going to be doing a whole lot of weapon fighting.  Don’t worry too much about this.

 

          Charisma (CHA):  About the only thing of value this represents to you is a slight price reduction form various merchants throughout the realm.  Chances are you’re really not going to be so tight on money that you’ll need to worry about it.

 

          Agility (AGI):  It seems a little odd to have this so far down the list but it’s really mostly involved with the ability to avoid blows in close combat and while running.  You’re not going to be in close combat very much and hopefully your running is going to be pretty minimal (you’ll generally be getting “evac’d” or camping out if it gets too nasty).  It certainly won’t hurt but you don’t need to be throwing a lot of time and effort into getting a lot of agility enhancing armor.

 

          Intelligence (INT):  Your “smarts” come from your Wisdom rather than your Intelligence.  Having some isn’t going to hurt you (unless perhaps you have more than your wisdom) but you don’t need to go shopping for it by any means.     ( linkto: cler_sm.htm)
