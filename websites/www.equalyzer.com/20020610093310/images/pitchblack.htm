 How are these formulas calculated?

To explain how just ONE of the Class/Race specific formulas are calculated could take a few hours. We can however give you a general idea. 

We have researched all of the "player guides" published to date and checked the base statistics for every Class/Race combination (yes that's correct, all seventy-three). 

We have investigated & surveyed fellow players for their input and beta testing skills. We did our own player tests, we checked player comments on 100's web sites. 

We researched items designed for specific classes and/or races. We then considered everything that could affect the "performance" of said items based upon our years of compounded research and testing. 

Since it would take too long to list all that we include, we'll tell you what we did not include as "performance" factors.

  • Lore: whether or not you can carry more than one doesn't affect the performance of the item.

  • No-Drop: whether or not you can sell it doesn't affect the performance either.

  • Price, Rarity, or Difficulty Obtaining: While these may affect how hard it is to get, they don't affect the performance once you have it.

  • Appearance: Whether or not an item "looks cool" or matches other items isn't our focus at all. We wanted good battle performance. One of our test warriors looks like a hodge-podge of colors but kicks butt in battle.

  • Bonus points (when setting up the character): We have no way of knowing how each person may have applied their Bonus Points so we couldn't include them.

Everything else is considered in some way. Magic items can hit Undead (ever try to kick a scarecrow or ghoul with Medium Bronze Boots?) so they get a little boost. Even statistics that may not seem to matter are given a really tiny value. 

For instance, Charisma may not mean much to a Rogue or Warrior but for newer players in lower levels of the game, something with only 3 Charisma should still rate above something with only 1 Charisma. You'll at least get a tiny price break with some merchants. We could say, "a copper saved is a copper earned" when you're a "newbie".
