The Code of Karana

as told by

Khaavren Stormcaller, fallen Knight of Thunder

Dedicated to Knight Garad, First Knight of Thunder, Brell Serellis Server

"Followers of Karana believe in the absolute power of storms. They worship the life giving power of the rain and respect the destructive force of a sandstorm or hurricane. Typical followers of Karana are rural humaniods, farmers, ranchers, hunters, and the like. They will often offer shelter from the elements to strangers. Many Karana followers live a nomadic lifestyle, travelling where the winds take them. They are humble, generous people who value strength and honesty and brook no disrespect of Karana and his work for they know it is only through his wisdom and kindness that all of Norrath is not consumed in an eternal tempest."


Well met, aspiring Knight of Thunder. Pardon my uncounted sins against the blessed Rainkeeper for but a brief period of time and I shall present to you all that I have learned in my otherwise wasted life. When I am satisfied that the words of the Lord of Storms has reached enough ears, I shall dedicate what remains of my pitiful life in an attempt to truly serve Karana and mankind both as a farmer on our Lord's fertile plains. Perhaps with years of service, some of my sins may be forgiven.

I am one of the noble Frogloks most hated enemies, having murdered more frogloks than there are stars in the sky, solely in the name of greed. I have slain the clockworks created by the peaceful gnomes time and time again. I have murdered innocent Fairies over and over, despite the fact that at one time, their lovely Princess offered me shelter within their lands.

I have hunted and slain Mammoth, most loved of Tunare's bears who dwells in the Jaggedpine which is home to many of our brothers and sisters which follow Karana's ways. I have been in possession of countless items which although I may not have murdered the original owner to get, I paid in platinum for, which condones the murder of the original owner, such as the Sisters of Erollisi Marr. I am killed on sight by the Wolves of the North, and rightly so, for having hunted down and slain Frostbite, a dog with a more noble soul than I for having sniffed out known murderers. And I did so in cowardice, away from the great beast's master.

Even as a young knight did I slay countless peaceful residents of Kerra Ridge who had never done me any harm. I have walked hand in hand with Teir'Dal, Trolls, and Ogres and even called them friend, as we in our greed and zealousy slew any and everything in our path. I have slain the innocent Aviaks, peaceful inhabitants of Lord Karana's plains. And that was not enough for my bloodlust, also the centaurs, another sentient, peaceful race; I have murdered them time and again though they have never sought revenge against me. I have infinite blood on my hands and countless black holes within my soul. Karana has yet to strip me of calling upon his aid; yet my faith in myself has shattered, and were it not for faith in my Lord I would take my own life in one last depserate act of cowardice.

So heed my words if you will, and lead the noble life that I was too weak for.

(Out of Character) I play Khaavren Stormcaller, level 34 Half Elf Paladin of Karana on the Brell Serellis server. I have no armour of Ro but I do own a piece of melatite and about 20 fairy dusts. In my career I have worn a Minotaur Ribcage, a Charred Guardian Breastplate, a Purity Belt, Gatorscale Leggings, a Collar of Undead Protection, and have wielded a Gigantic Zwiehander. I currently use a Ghoulbane, a Charred Guardian Shield, and two Silver Plated Bracers. I am in no way bragging about my equipment. The commonality of all the items I have listed is that no true role playing Knight of Thunder would use any of the above items; and there are many more that would not be considered.

This page exists for two reasons. One, to point out that in my opinion, Everquest not only fails to encourage role play; it actively discourages it.

The second reason is to give rise to a challenge: I hereby challenge those who feel the call to either create new Paladins and follow the Code of Karana, or develop other Paladin Codes for other Gods and then follow that code with a new character. The basis for any code must be strictly role playing.

So then, here is the Code of Karana. I have not yet decided if I will remake my character just to follow this code, as this would mean no longer grouping with my numerous friends I have made in my 40+ days played in Norrath.

If there is actually enough people interested in trying to uphold the Code of Karana, I will actually start making complete lists of creatures that can be killed, those that can not; acceptable items and quests; and forbidden items and quests. In fact I could see the Code of Karana appealing even to a rare breed of power gamer, those that wish the additional challenge that the Code of Karana presents (a Knight of Thunder who upholds the Code of Karana would be an order of magnitude more difficult character to advance than one who does not uphold the Code). It would be somewhat similar to the Diablo subclass phenonemon, where people placed self-imposed restrictions on items and spells useable by the three different classes available within Diablo. Barbarians, for example, were a subclass of Warrior which were not allowed to use shields, and had other restrictions. Naked mages where mages who did not use equipment at all and relied solely upon their magical abilities. And so forth.

I have my doubts though as to whether the Code of Karana will actually be embraced, the game is difficult enough as it is. But were it to become popular, the single most important thing I can imagine would be some way of letting people know you upheld the Code of Karana, from day one. I would suggest creating a guild on each server named "Code of Karana" to which each Knight of Thunder would belong to. The character would be in roleplay mode, but people would still be able to tell that you upheld the Code of Karana from your guild name, which you could have at level one.

Text in bold type is to be taken as being spoken by Khaavren, the Paladin of Karana that I play who has begun to realize how truly evil he has become. Text in normal type is out of character text spoken by me, the person who plays Khaavren.

Note that I often use the male pronouns in this document. I in no way mean disrespect to the female Everquest players, I use it merely as a convenience.

Rain

Karana, Rainbringer, Rainkeeper, the Lord of Storms. A Paladin of Karana, upon feeling the first raindrop upon his body, will boldly proclaim his gratefulness for being so blessed by the presence of his God.

Acceptable proclomations include:
"Hail, Rainbringer!"
"Hail, Rainkeeper!"

Furthermore, any Paladin of Karana who is engaged in combat while in the direct presence of his Lord Karna (i.e., it is raining), will NOT flee from combat, no matter what the odds. He will fight whatever enemy he is engaged with until the bitter end. To flee in the presence of the Lord of Storms during a Blessing of Rain is sacrilege.

(Out of Character) A follower of the Code of Karana should salute the gods (clear target, and /salute) and then say one of the above. I used to shout Hail, Rainbringer! whenever it rained in game. I have since decided that using say is the better option, as on some servers with a large Karana following, many people shouting at once can create a lot of unwanted spam.

If you are engaged in battle when it starts raining, you may NOT flee or take any otherwise cowardly action. It does not matter if you are level 10 and get pounced upon by a griffawn, you MUST stand and fight.

Non Aggression

Karana is a peaceful god. He does not seek to kill where there is no need. No Knight of Thunder will attack any creature or person which would not attack him first, for any reason.

(Out of Character) What this means is you can not hunt Frogloks, for example. Frogloks are a peaceful race and do no harm to Karana or his followers, and no Knight of Thunder will slay them. Undead Frogloks are another matter entirely.

Wild animals can not be attacked, however, they can be slain if they initiate the combat. For example if you are medding and a grizzly bear attacks you, you may kill it in self defense. By wild animals I mean natural animals (those that would exist on Earth). You may actively seek out and hunt "fantastical" animals, so long as they are aggressive: griffawns, for example.

In general, besides animals, the Knight of Thunder is limited to hunting only those creatures which con threateningly or scowls, ready to attack. This means you may not kill Freeport Militia, unless they attack you first (which may happen after you do several acceptable quests which lower faction with the Freeport Militia). Kerrans are peaceful and a Knight of Thunder would not hunt them. Willowisps are peaceful and thus may not be attacked. Same for aviaks, and centaurs, and treants, fairies, and other creatures. Note that a Knight of Thunder may still hunt creatures which are aggressive but do not attack because of level limits (a level 50 Knight of Thunder could hunt Blackburrow Gnolls, for example).

So what can you kill? All undead, gnolls, goblins, orcs, ogres, trolls, dark elves, aggressive minotaurs, and any other agressive or evil creature.

Sacrifice

A Knight of Thunder follows the virtue of Sacrifice. When battling side by side with companions, and defeat looks imminent, he will be the last to flee, after others have made it to safety. He will call upon Karana's greatest healing abilities on another before himself.

(Out of Character) A Knight of Thunder will always be the last to zone, and the first to die if at all possible. This does not mean he is suicidal, this means that if someone HAS to die, he will do all in his power to make sure it is him before a friend (group member). Lay Hands will be used on whoever needs it, with himself being the last choice (if everyone else is healthy, the Knight of Thunder may Lay Hands upon himself - if the Knight and another person are about to die, the other person gets the Lay hands).

Humility

A Knight of Thunder is a Knight of Karana, and the vast majority of Karana's followers are hard working farmers, providing the most essential necessity of life: food. Karana's followers are hard working, humble people. A Knight of Thudner does not pursue glory, he wards and protects the plains and people of his god.

(Out of Character) No twinking allowed, at all.

Association

A Knight of Thunder is also known by the company he keeps. First and foremost, a Knight of Thunder will always judge by actions first and appearances last. Travel only with those who have proven themselves by word and, more importantly, deed.

(Out of Character) A Knight of Thudner will not be grouping with evil characters. This includes all Shadow Knights and Necromancers, and people who follow evil gods (Cazic Thule, Innoruuk, Bertoxxulous, and some followers of Rallos Zek). Some normally evil races have rare inviduals who are Agnostic and if they prove themselves good in deed, you may group with them. Likewise beware of people that are of normally good races and yet perform evil deeds (there are many evil Humans, for example) - and there are even people who follow good Gods and yet their actions say otherwise. Befriend them not.

Items

(Out of Character) The Knight of Thunder will only use items that can be bought from a store vendor, or obtained from an aggressive or evil race. This means leather, chain, and plate bought from stores is Ok. Patchwork, banded and bronze bought from other players is acceptable (as long as the players are not evil, see association above). A Purity Belt is forbidden, as in order to wear a Purity Belt, a Sister of Erollisi Marr had to perish. Ghoulbane is thus also forbidden. I can not list all the items that are forbidden but the general rule: store bought, or items that come from evil creatures only. A Knight can not purchase Ghoulbane, nor can he accept it as a gift. This rule applies to all such items; since the Shin Lord (a fellow Paladin) was slain in order to obtain the blade. This rule applies to all items.

I can not list all items that are forbidden, as I can not recall them all, but here is a short list of example Paladin items that are acceptable and forbidden. It is the spirit of the law that is to be obeyed, not the letter. That is, this list is not the be all end all list; the concept that any item you equip must have come from slaying an aggressive or evil creature or be store bought or player made is the concept to be obeyed.

Forbidden Paladin Quests

Armour of Ro: There are those who call themselves Paladins; even those were once Knights of Thunder, who have fallen under the sway of Solusek Ro. Solusek Ro is known as the Burning Prince. It was he who blasted the once fertile grounds south of Freeport and turned them into blazing deserts. Before they were burned, these lands were peacefully inhabited by the Elves. Solusek Ro, in his jealousy and greed, did use his elemental powers to carve the lands after his own image, without regard to the life that was already present. Solusek Ro is diametrically opposed to Karana - and no Knight of Thunder will ever wear any armour which carries Ro's name or emblem.

Indeed, within the Mountains of Rathe there is a gathering of once proud Paladins who have fallen under the sway of Solusek Ro. They are not to be trusted. And especially beware of one Lord Searfire within the Temple of Solusek Ro - he requires fairy dusts and ingots of melatite, which can only be obtained from peaceful beings through thievery or murder. This alone is enough to indicate the foul intentions of Solusek Ro and those who follow him. Though the material items that Ro grants his followers may be superior; your faith in the Lord of Storms shall prove greater than any material possession.

Bonethunder Staff: As a young Knight of Thunder you may be called upon to investigate the whereabouts of Drosco Finlayer; a Knight of Thunder sent to infiltrate the Bloodsabers. This is an acceptable quest, and one all young Knights should pursue.

However, you may be called upon by Runethar (the guild leader of the Knights of Thunder) to retrieve the Bayle List. What Runethar does not know is that the list lies in the belly of Mammoth, the most sacred of Tunare's bears within the Jaggedpine. Though Mammoth is aggressive, no Knight of Thunder would take the bear's life simply to further serve the wishes of Antonius Bayle - especially considering that although Antonius may have once well been an honorable man, his expansionism begins to border on the dark side.

Although it is true that Mammoth took a noble Ranger's son; the blame lies not on the bear, which was merely protecting its home. Mammoth can not be expected to be as wise as the sentient creatures. And slaying Mammoth will not bring back the son; and indeed the Ranger who wishes you to do it has already condemned his soul. He took a vow never to harm the bears and yet asks you to do it for him; it is the same thing; he is breaking the spirit of his oath if not the letter.

Deepwater Harpoon: The Deepwater Knights of Prexus fear Antonius Bayle's expansionism and have heard of his plans to construct a massive bridge connecting Antonica and Odus. They wish those loyal to Erudin to find the engineers of the yet to be constructed bridge and slay them. This is an unacceptable solution to the problem - a diplomatic effort should first be attempted; and the engineers are not the problem, Bayle's expansionist ideals are.

Soulfire: The very beginning of this quest requires the Paladin to slay someone who *appears* to be a member of the Freeport Militia. Furthermore, this person begs for mercy and promises to mend their ways. In order to complete this portion of the quest, however, this person must be slain. A Knight of Thunder is not hasty in his decisions; remember - judge by deed first and appearance last. A Knight of Thunder would not fall to zealousy and immediately slay this person, he would feel compassion and the respect for another sentient being's life who has so far done the Knight no harm.

Fiery Avenger: This quest requires that the Paladin hand in a Ghoulbane and a Soulfire. Both of these items are forbidden for a Knight of Thunder to possess; therefore a Knight of Thunder will never own a Fiery Avenger.

Suggestions

Lastly, here I present my suggestions to Verant, if they care to listen, as to how to make the game VASTLY more enjoyable with a MINIMAL of work, in my admittedly uneducated opinion.

First, god specific powers. For example, a Paladin of Karana might be able to call down lightning during a rainstorm. If you follow Karana, it is raining, and certain faction requirements are met, the Paladin would proc a lightning bolt spell in combat in addition to any other weapon procs. The amount of damage done could be scaled based on the level of the Paladin. And a better text than "A skeletal monk is struck by lightning" would definitely be required. Something more like, "Khaavren calls forth the power of Karana and lightning strikes a skeletal monk!"

Paladins of Brell could receive a combat bonus when in indoor zones; a great thing I think would be to give dwarven paladins critical hits when fighting indoors, similar to warriors. "Khardan strikes with the fury of Brell (62)!"

High Elven Paladins, followers of Tunare. Not only would animals con amiable to them (like Druids and Rangers); but Paladins of Tunare are devoted to Mother Nature herself. Nothing is more unnatural than the living dead. High Elven Paladins 30+ and above could proc an anti-undead randomly in battle. Perhaps ward undead at 30 and moving up the list at 40th and 50th level. "A shin ghoul knight staggers as Kaelin's eyes shine forth with holy light!"

Paladins of the Prime Healer would have a reduced time on lay hands. Like 50 minutes instead of 72. "Syrelle feels the blessing of Rodcet Nife!"

Paladins of Quellios are the Peacekeepers. As such, the should have the ability once a day to create a mesmerize like effect on a creature with a long duration; to bestow the peace of the child upon any creature. "A gnoll reaver's eyes glaze over as Ibamn glows briefly with a white aura."

And so on. God-specific abilities would need to be balanced like any other (perhaps dwarven paladins have a penalty in combat when outdoors; for example) - but it would make who you choose to follow matter more than simply your starting factions.

The quests stink. There are so many fouled up Paladin quests that I seriously doubt any of them will be over hauled. Especially Ro Armour. In short, a true role playing Paladin will always have much inferior equipment than a non role playing Paladin. Quests COULD do a lot about that, requiring MANY good factions to accomplish (for example, giving the Fairy Princess an item ... I am already KoS with fairies as Khaavren therefore Khaavren could not do such a quest - as I know of no way to recover Fairy faction).

Wouldn't it be great if you turned in an item from the Ghoul Cavalier as part of a quest to get a Ghoulbane like weapon? And perhaps two silver plated bracers would be part of a quest for a Troll Shadow Knight weapon ... seeing as the Trolls and Frogloks are sworn enemies, and defeating the Shin Lord would be a glorious victory for a Troll Shadow Knight.

This moral dilemna crap from Verant is just that ... crap. I've yet to see more than about ten words said on the subject of Paladin quests which require downright EVIL actions to complete. I'd like to see more comments. But maybe not. I don't want justifications as to why. I want BETTER DESIGNED quests that require GOOD actions to complete.

If Verant would pay me a measly 30k a year, I would add *so* much text content to the game for them. Wouldn't it be nice if each NPC in Qeynos had a few pages of dialogue? And get rid of the brackets ... make people actually have to think on their own to get history, background, and possible quest leads from NPC's. Paineel is a good step in this direction. But I'd be happier with more text content than "more zones! more items! more levels! more spells!" which is what the expansion is all about. Everquest is a power gamer's game, and that's all there is to it. Verant does VERY LITTLE to encourage role playing, and in my opinion, it would be easier to add pages of text content than it would be to design new modles, textures, and other time consuming tasks. But surely they have people who do this? Yet patch after patch, I hail NPC after NPC, and they say absolutely nothing ... if I were a Shadow Knight, I'd kill them for ignoring me.
