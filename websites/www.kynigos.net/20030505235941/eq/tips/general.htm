• You can grab one item off of a stack by control-clicking on it.
  • You select the nearest NPC using the F8 key.
  • You select yourself using the F1 key.
  • You select group members by clicking on their names in the group list.
  • Shift-uparrow recalls the last command you typed in the chat window.
  • /autosplit enables or diables group loot splitting. We usually keep this on out of common courtesy. Selfish folks usually get kicked out of groups...
  • Always consider your targets (with the "c" key) before attacking. The color of the message tells you how likely you are to win the fight, and whether they are hostile to you. You can also con NPCs, which you should do before entering foreign towns, to make sure the guards won't kill you on sight. Also, vendors will offer better prices if they like you. Finally, you can con players to determine their approximate level. See Allakhazam's web site for details.
  • If you die in a dangerous place (like a monster's camp!),it may be difficult for you to retrieve it in your naked state. You can let someone clear the camp for you while you get the corpse; you can try to drag it to safety (see the hotkey section); or you can consent to let an ally get it for you. You give consent by typing /consent name. The consented person can either loot the corpse, or drag it for you. Only consent to people that you trust, because there is absolutely nothing you can do if they decide to keep your loot.
  • If you die and can't find your corpse, ask a bard or necromancer to use their special abilities to locate it. Of course, you SHOULD learn to use the /loc command before dieing...
  • When running from a monster, you can get a speed boost by hitting the jump key (spacebar). However, you cannot jump once your stamina runs out. Also, once your health goes below one bubble, you will not be able to run or jump; you need to make the decision to fight of flee when you still have two bubbles left.

Communication

The following modes of communication filter who sees your messages:

  • /say (the default): people nearby can hear you; useful if they are not in your group.
  • /g: group members can hear you, as well as people nearby.
  • /ftell: currently not implemented; people on your friend list can hear you (see lists section).
  • /tell: the named person can hear you
  • /r: replies to the last peron to /tell you
  • /shout: everyone in zone hears you; typically only used for warning of dangerous monsters in the area. Highly abused by newbie players, so you may want to filter this one out (see section below).
  • /auction: everyone in zone hears you; used to advertise an item and seek to buy an item. Usually folks respond by /tell.
  • /ooc: everyone in zone hears you; used for out-of-character messages. As with shout, you may want to filter this one out.
  • /guildsay: (or /gu for short) your guild members can hear you, as well as people nearby.

Chat Filters

Hit the Options button, then Chat button, and you will see a list of chat filters. These can enable or disable things like shout, ooc, combat misses, and spellcasting messages. Some of these filters allow you to specify group only, which is a nice compromise.

Bugs

Sometimes non-alphanumeric characters get dropped from the chat window. You will frequently see folks spell out plus and neg instead of + and -.

Lists

  • You can ignore messages from annoying people with the /ignore command followed by their name. To see the list of ignored people, just type /ignore without a name. To reactivate a person, /ignore them again.
  • There is also a friend list. To add someone, just type /friend name. To see the list, type /friend. To remove a name (you can only have 20 on the list), type /friend name again.
  • To list the people in the zone, type /who. You can filter this list by typing in the first few letters of a name or class. You can further filter by level numbers. Example: /who monk 9-12.
  • To get a list across all zones, add the "all" word. Example: /who all monk 9-12.
  • Finally, the word "friend" filters using your friend list. Example: /who all friend.

Hot Keys

Just about any button can be turned into a hot key. Just press and hold on it until a copy attaches to your cursor. Then click on one of the six slots on the lower left of your screen. Each slot corresponds to the 1 though 6 key on your keyboard. You have six banks of 6 keys; you can use the arrow to switch banks, or use shift-1 through shift-6 to switch.

Custom Buttons

Your social buttons can be replaced with custom ones that you prefer. They can then become hot keys. To customize, right click on a social button. On the screen that pops up, enter a short name for the command, and below you can enter up to five actions to execute. Here are some popular actions to make:

  • /assist: if you target a friend, then assist, it will attack the same target as him.
  • /corpse: this will pull your corpse if it is close by. You can keep dragging until your are in a safe location to loot it.
    • /loc: this gives your current zone coordinates, which are two numbers between -5000 and 5000. This is handy if you are about to die, so you can find your corpse. Also handy if you get lost and need to find your group.
  • Language practice: you can type in some /g group messages, then teach the language to your group by repeatedly hitting this hotkey. To switch languages, right click on the chat box; then left click on the language until you see the one you want; then right click the chat box again. Language skill can improve as high as the teacher's ability, and you will eventually be able to understand what they say. A popular thing to do when you're taking a boat trip.
I have a /loc attached to a couple of buttons. My "yell for help," "assist," and "incoming" buttons have a line that runs an LOC. This means throughout a fight I get 10-12 LOC's.

Options

  • To avoid accidentally attackiing an NPC vendor, you should remap your attack key (by default it is "a"). I use backspace. This can be found in the Options button, under the Keyboard section.
  • To see farther in the game, you can extend the clipping pane. You need a good video card and fast CPU to handle this. The slider for the clipping distance (how far you can see) is under the Options button in the Display section. Note that fog and rain will obscure your vision anyway.
  • If you have trouble seeing in the dark due to EverQuest's buggy lighting code, you can adjust the Gamma slider in the Otions Display section. This only works for Voodoo 3DFX-based cards; other folks can adjust the brightness knob on their monitor, or find the gamma control in their Displays control panel.

Abilities

Strength improves your chance of doing maximum damage with a weapon, which is (base weapon damage) x 2 + 1. This improved chance is factored into the ATK stat. Each point of strength is one additional point of weight you can carry. Brad also said this speeds up how fast you learn some offensive combat skills.

Stamina improves the number of hit points you have (every extra 11 stamina adds 1 hit point). It also increases your yellow fatigue bar, which is consumed when jumping or swimming.

Dexterity improves how hard you hit with bows; improves the chance to score a critical hit; increases the chance of a weapon's special "proc" effect going off; and reduces the chance for a bard song to fizzle. It has no effect on spell interruptions. Brad also mentioned that it speeds up learning weapon skills and rogue skills.

Agility determines how fast you learn some defensive combat skills; makes you slightly harder to hit; and reduces the damage you take when hit. Every extra 4.5 agility adds 1 AC point (but for monks I believe every 3 agility adds 1 AC).

Intelligence increases mana for INT-based casters. It also determines how quickly you can learn most skills.

Wisdom increases mana for WIS-based casters. If it is higher than your INT, it determines how fast you learn skills instead.

Charisma affects how well NPCs like you. A higher CHA makes it easier to raise faction with them, and improves the prices you get from NPC merchants. (A 104 CHA gives you the best possible prices.) For charm spells, a higher CHA improves the chance of success and duration.

Finally, note that none of these abilities improve your chance to hit. That depends solely on your level, offense skill, and weapon skill.

Melee

A recent patch has made these formulas obsolete. The damage bonus for weapons now scales with the delay, so low delay weapons are not as powerful as they used to be. I got my numbers from this site, so maybe they will update their formulas:

The mode damage a weapon can do in one hit is generally: 

        mode = 2 x damage + min

where the minimum damage is: 

        primary min  = (level - 25)/3     (always at least 1)
        off hand min = 1
        (for two-handed weapons, add another +1 to minimum damage)

The max damage a weapon can do in one hit is: 

        max = (strength + weapon skill + level)/100 x damage

The primary hand damage bonus is applied per hit, so it would seem best to place low delay weapons in the primary hand rather than the off hand.

Your damage done will vary between the min and max. An estimate of how much damage you might do on average (capped by your max damage) that has been suggested: 

        avg = 0.4 x min + 0.5 x mode + 0.1 x max

However, the damage done to a creature depends on the mob's AC and level, so that number would have to be tweaked down for higher level mobs. It also depends partly on the character's strength score.

Given the numbers for average damage, you can then divide by delay to get a damage ratio for the primary hand. For the off hand, you also need to account for how often dual wield succeeds, as stated by Verant: 

        dw ratio = (level + dual wield skill)/600

Finally, Verant also stated the chance for double attack to succeed. You won't need this number to compare weapons, but it is useful if you want to compare your damage output to someone else: 

        da ratio = (level + double attack skill)/500
Here's an example: a 34th level character with dual wield, dual attack, and weapon skills at 170. He has a dragoon dirk (6 dmg 23 delay) and crystalline sword (11 dmg 38 delay). His strength is 100. His min damage will be: 
        primary min = (34 - 25)/3 = 3
        offhand min = 1
Mode damage for these weapons will be: 
        CS in prime = 2 x 11 + 3 = 25
        CS in off   = 2 x 11 + 1 = 22
        DD in prime = 2 x  6 + 3 = 15
        DD in off   = 2 x  6 + 1 = 13
Max damage is: 
        CS max = (100 + 170 + 34)/100 x 11 = 33
        DD max = (100 + 170 + 34)/100 x  6 = 18
Average damage per hit is then estimated: 
        CS in prime = 0.4 x 3 + 0.5 x 25 + 0.1 x 33 = 17
        CS in off   = 0.4 x 1 + 0.5 x 22 + 0.1 x 33 = 15
        DD in prime = 0.4 x 3 + 0.5 x 15 + 0.1 x 18 = 11
        DD in off   = 0.4 x 1 + 0.5 x 13 + 0.1 x 18 =  9
Now we figure his chance of successfully dual wielding, and compute his damage relative to delay for these weapons: 
        dw ratio = (34 + 170) / 600 = 0.34
      
        CS in prime, DD in off = 17/38 + 0.34x 9/23 = 0.580 (5.80 damage per second)
        DD in prime, CS in off = 11/23 + 0.34x15/38 = 0.612 (6.12 damage per second)
Surprise! That's right, although the crystalline blade has a better apparent ratio, the bonus damage for the primary hand makes the low delay dragoon dirk a better choice to place in the primary hand.
