We are humans and we seek to find meaning for our behavior blah, blah, blah. What do we do when something happens to us that we perceive as bad?

It seems that a lot of people dwell on it for a little while, feel a little sniffly maybe, and then they flip it around and make it a smiley rainbow. How so?

Some people like to ask "Why did God do this to me?" and attribute the occurrence of the event to good old God up there in the clouds sending down a positive lesson to learn. "That old coot had me run over by a semi and laid up in intensive care to teach me the virtue of persistence. Looking back on it now, I am glad it happened because I remember some times when I could have been more persistent in the past if only I'd known how important my persistence was to God. Thanks for intervening in my life God! You're the greatest!" (Pun intended.)

I'll admit it's possible that God pulled your strings to teach you a lesson. It's also possible that you were just an idiot that forgot to look both ways before stepping out in front of a semi. It is important to remember that many injuries can be explained by the fact that our bodies have to abide by natural laws of misuse and over-extension.

You know what another possible alternative is? Maybe God hates your guts! Sure. Maybe that's why bad things are happening to you. God wants you to suffer and die and get off of God's planet because you keep blaming God for all the bad things that happen to you, when in fact the devil is in charge of the bad things. Or maybe the devil is in charge of the bad things that happen to you until you can find a life lesson to learn from the evil experience. At that point it becomes something that was attributable to God's handy hands.

An entertaining coffeehouse singer named Matt West was on campus claiming to have seen a sunset so beautiful that it made him say out loud "He [She?/It?] spent a little extra time on this one tonight." Well it's good to know that God exists within our reality of time, and that God has a penis, and that God spends a few extra minutes or hours once in awhile painting breathtaking sunsets instead of sending some terminal illness Hitler's way.

Once again, some people will say that God is responsible for the good stuff and the devil is responsible for causing all those bad things to happen. Obviously, God was put in charge of the flashy, new Edith Mortensen Center, while the ventilation system in Humanities must have been contracted out to Satan.

Different people can interpret the same instances in different ways. I suppose some people would say that Hell maintains the constant qualities of a few rivers of lava, ground laden with broken glass, showers that send out streams of flame instead of water, and plenty of mustaches. But if God sends a Satan worshipper to Hell, is it really Hell or is it Heaven for the Satan worshipper to spend an eternity in a "place" that is held in the highest esteem by that person? I guess those people slip through the cracks of the eternal damnation system.

And I want to know if God or Satan is in charge of the foolish rumors that somebody is sending around campus that says I smoke pot on the way to class. Being as I'm not everyone's favorite person on campus, I can see how people would like to construct my behavior as deviant, but smoking marijuana on the way to class? Gimme a break!

So is God sending an anti-Greg Ryan Argo message through this moron that is trying to tarnish my reputation? From his standpoint I would say yes, because anything I say should be further discredited than it already is because someone "saw me" smoke a doobie in between classes. It's also likely that I am the person that robbed Humanities. I probably carry a bong in my backpack instead of books too. What a crock! If I was one who attributed things that happen to God and the devil, I say the devil is sending a message through this person, because it is personally and socially malicious to spread those sorts of rumors.

It seems more like if God and Satan actually do exist and if they intervene in our lives, they work as a team. What is evil for one is a Godsend for another. And now that this article is over, I can look back on it and be fairly sure that God made me write it so that he could teach a lesson to the Mirror readership. Speaking through me, I am pretty sure God is saying, "You believe that I created you, but in actuality you are the ones who create me." Or maybe I heard God wrong and what was really said to me was, "Boobie grease thatch fly R-rated glue, mutton statue-malady chew hard the nuns procreate tree."

-Argo
