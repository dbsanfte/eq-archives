Don't believe those who say it all is somehow our fault - By TOM TEEPEN

WE are a good nation.

I want you to remember that, for it is true.

Yet, even in the very throes of Tuesday's horrors, with flames still raging and debris still groaning unsettled, some among us would have had you think that this undeclared terrorist war was somehow our own fault.

From every political and social compass point, the predisposed were creeping toward the worklights' glare in hopes of snatching stunned attention, sawing up the moment, parsing the outrage, tendentiously to fit their own agendas.

If only we had fine-tuned our Middle East policy a little this way, or a little that. If only conservatives were not so disdainful of our multilateral responsibilities. If only liberals had given the CIA and FBI whatever they wanted. If only we would withdraw from all international treaties. If only American businesses were everywhere beneficent. If only we gave up global trade.

And because of these declared lapses, the implication went, we brought this on ourself; we sort of had it coming.

This is the time not to ignore but to utterly, openly reject such sly importunings.

No American misstep, mistake, misadventure, misunderstanding justifies the damage visited upon our nation and certainly not the personal tragedies -- death and hurt -- committed against the innocent with such ideological and doctrinal enthusiasm.

We are overwhelmingly a force for good in the world.

Even as the hijacked airliners, made over into human missiles, were powering toward their targets, Secretary of State Colin Powell, and the representatives of 33 other nations, were recommitting the hemisphere to democratic institutions.

Our enemies want none of that.

The nation, prodded by its president, had begun a discussion over ways to integrate even illegal immigrants into our economic and social life.

Our enemies are the lords of exclusion.

We glory in our religious diversity, in a social compact that acknowledges all faiths, even faithlessness, as the absolute right of it adherents.

Our enemies kill the errant.

Think about this: We are the most powerful nation in history -- and we have designs on not one square inch of soil that is not ours. We spend treasure, and venture our lives, not in conquest but against others' aggression and to steady shaky peace. (And do so, remarkably, without prejudice. Repeatedly hit by Muslim terrorism, we moved to rescue, and still stand picket to protect, Muslims in the Balkans.) We send aid and helping hands to the neediest peoples of the Earth.

Do we do all we can, and thus all that we should, to help? Are we always right? No, and no.

We are not perfect. At home a nasty streak of racism still runs through us. We indifferently indulge a degree of endemic poverty that ought to shame us. Abroad we blunder, muscle bound, into fragile cultural ecologies; we are lugs sometimes.

Not a perfect nation, just a very good one.

Largely a religious people, we are nonetheless a secular nation -- modernist, rational and humane. Our enemies are none of those. They hate us for our best qualities, not for our worst.
