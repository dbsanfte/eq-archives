Name: Temdaen Truesilver
Rank: Clerk
Race: Half-Elf
Class: Paladin
Level: 34
Marital Status: Engaged (Artana)
Family: Orphan
 
Biography:
 

I was born on the streets of Freeport, a half-breed with no friends and no family. I don't even remember my mother; she is just a blur from the early years of my life. I spent my days begging for handouts and often getting beaten for it by the Freeport Militia. It wasn’t an easy life, but it was mine...

I was fourteen when my whole existence changed in the span of fifteen minutes.

It was a night like any other for me. I was cold, hungry and in a poor mood. I remember the sky was especially clear that might, the moon up high and the streets light by its pale radiance. It was by this light that I caught glimpse of silver disappearing into an alley.

Curious as only a street urchin could be, I padded silently to the mouth of the alleyway.

Peering inside, I saw a sight that will forever be branded on my memories of that night... several militia guards holding down a single human female in shining silver mail who struggled feebly in their grip.

They laughed as she reached out for her longsword, her blade just out of reach of her grasping fingers. Her already pale face, paled further as he noticed me and mouthed the word ‘RUN’. She never saw the knife that took her in the throat because of her worry for me...

My lower lip trembled as I watched the human woman’s blood spill to the ground, as I tried not to cry out or weep a feeling washed through me... a whispered voice, almost like a divine wind, brushed past my cheek.

“Pick up the sword, child... be brave. Be true.”

To this day, I don’t know why I listened to that voice. I was terrified. These were; after all, members of the Militia... trained warriors. What would a child do against them?

But, I did it. I walked over to the sword and pulled it up, my arms straining from the task. As the men turned from their night’s bloody work; I thought my resolve would slip away and I would run as they blinked in unison and laughed.

“Wha’ hav’ we ‘ere, mates? A urchin with a sword?” said one as he stepped forward from the rest, “You hav’ a death wish, boy?”

It was all a could do to meet his gaze and croak out, “You did wrong...” I never saw what was to come next either. The world spun and I hit the ground, my head ringing.

I heard a voice next to my ear for the second time that night, only this one was harsh when it said, “And who are you goin’ to tell about it, boy?” and I felt a sharp pain in my side as a knife slipped in and out of me.

I did cry out now. The pain made the world swim around me and again I heard a voice, “Be brave. Be strong, child. Help is on its way.” The voice sounded so sure...

I heard the Militia leader laugh again as he started past, then scream as a bright flash of light filled the alley “My eyes, I’m blind!” he cried and his fellows gasped.

I rolled over as my world faded around me. I have only heard stories of what happened as I soon lost consciousness.

As the tale is told, three Paladins awoke in the night to the calling of their God, Mithaniel Marr. With the dying prayer of one of their own in their hearts, they rushed to my aid, pulling me from the grasp of the Militia and from death.

It was explained to me later in the paladins’ hall that the Lightbearer sometimes grants the dying wishes of his followers and that the last wish of the human paladin was that I would be safe.

I learnt the next morning that her name was Asaya MorningFyre.

The paladins set me on my way that very afternoon, their dealings with me finished and their mission completed. But, often over the years they caught me watching from afar, staring wistfully at their stronghold. It was the days they noticed me that I found dinner and a warm bed to sleep, so I let myself be noticed more often.

I never understood my reasons for hanging around until my twentieth year when the Hall Leader approached me as I ate dinner with a few paladins at a great oak table. I remember well how quickly the others left as he sat down and have me a grave look. It was under that look that I felt ashamed as though I hadn’t finished some task given to me.

He simply sat there, staring until finally I blurted out. “My lord, please... do not send me away. I have no where else to go and the streets are not a kind place for one of my station...” My heart was full of panic as I tried to think of what I might have done.

The man just smiled slightly, “Then, no longer are you staying here by good will alone. Our Asaya chose you, she saw something in you, lad and it is our duty to give you the chance to see it.”

I simply nodded dumbly at the man, unsure how to respond as he stood up and left me to the dinner I no longer wanted.

My world blurs together soon after that, but it was that day, those fifteen minutes that my life changed and I chose to honor Asaya’s memory and her faith in me.

I have left the Hall of Truth behind me as I travel Norrath, bringing truth to those without any in their hearts. I am a holy warrior of Mithaniel Marr and I never forgot it was Asaya’s courage, her faith that showed me my destiny.
