The Call to EverQuest Friends


 



If you could have a dreamguild, what would you
have? (You don't have to think of everything,
just what are the first things that come to mind?)

I have been looking over guilds for months now,
reading their message boards and their charters,
looking for clues about their character. There are
some very nice guilds out there but...

...but?

Yes, I hate to say it but none of them is my dreamguild.

There are things I would like to find in a guild,
and it seems that no one is focused that way.
Things like being kind to others as a way of
having fun in EQ (and I guess, in life).

Most of the guilds stress the stuff they kill together,
or get together, and yes, these are ways of helping
other people. But...

... but I just spent a half an hour giving out firebeetle
eyes to young druids in Kelethin, and it was
miraculous! Because I felt so happy doing it, and
it was so happily received! I said ooc, "Attn young
druids, giving away a fire beetle eye to any druid
who hugs me outside the bank." One level 5 druid
was incredulous that I would give them away! He
practically swore an oath of fealty to me!

But I don't want an oath of fealty to me. I would
rather see people give an oath of fealty to an idea.

For quite a while now, while I have been making
notes about my "dreamguild", I came up with the
Random Acts of Kindness and Senseless
Beauty idea.

The concept, as many of you know, is based on
doing good deeds for others without expectation
of reward. Not a new idea, is it? And I have seen
many EverQuesters practicing this idea on their
own and being an inspiration to me, as well as
to others.

I would love it if that were the reason for a guild.
Not so we can get a big group to hunt in dungeons.
Not so we can get all our class-specific armor. But
so we can give away our hearts without reserve
for the pure joy of it!

I know there are other like-minded people who
play EQ who would like to belong to something
that Worthy. Many of these "spiritual" people
don't think of themselves that way--they just
know they like to help other people. So I guess
it just comes down to people to want to feel the
joy of giving.

I think when practicing random acts of kindness,
it's better not to give away big expensive items.
Giving something to a person that is in their level
range is probably the easiest and often the best
choice…

…firebeetle eyes to Druids for their level one
spell,

…spiderling silk to Bards for their first drum,

…a cracked staff to a caster,

…a tarnished two-handed sword to a young
melee-er,

…fifteen minutes every day or two buffing
people,

…A regular event teaching fighting and techniques
for specific classes, game playing skills for beginners,
language festivals, newbie contests, Drunken Naked
Races! (Now where did that one come from, LOL!)

Will it be hard to get a big guild group to go to
dungeon? Yes, it might be, but so what?

Whenever I wanted to find a hunting group in Norrath,
I did. There are always people ready to fight. And I
enjoy that part of it too. But it wouldn't be any fun if I
couldn't help other people on the way. I would be so
bored. With the game. With others and myself.

With my life. In or out of EverQuest.

Another thing to think about here is the choice of
server. For those of us who have been on EQ for
awhile and are on servers where you regularly
hear class-specific quested armor being auctioned,
where when you auction to give away newbie items
you get back questions about its "stats", I think its
time to move to a new young server and start over.

That is why I chose Morell-Thule. It's young and there
are both seasoned veterans there as well as newbies.
It's still young enough for anyone interested in joining
this guild to start a new character for that purpose.

I have been practicing Random Acts of Kindness
and Senseless Beauty alone in EQ or with another
friend for quite awhile now. It has been what has
kept EQ interesting to me, long after the game is
has lost it's newness.

And if you are reading this, then you probably
have too. But I think with a few other people like
us, we could start a guild with a new kind of focus.
One without Demi-God Leaders and elite members
that make the regular members feel unimportant.

All members would be equal. (That way you and I
wouldn't have to keep on doing all the work once
it's in place. LOL!)

Finally, I would like this guild to be in contact
actively with the other guilds on our server,
inviting them to participate in Newbie Helping
Events, and to practice Random Acts of Kindness
and Senseless Beauty themselves.

Well Friends, what do you think?

Let me know. I will be waiting for your advice.

Hamadryad, Druid of Morell-Thule
 
 

The Charter of Random Acts

The guild known as Random Acts (RA) takes its
concept from the phrase "Practice random acts
of kindness and senseless beauty": the
foundation of that is to practice kindness.

Now we as a fellowship are taking it to a new level
in an online game like EverQuest (EQ) because in
order to progress in the game you have to kill a
lot of monsters. Some people think that killing
monsters and practicing kindness go together
like a charbroiled steak topped with a scoop of
peppermint ice cream. (Personally, I love ice cream
on my steak.  LOL )

But if you have gotten this far, I don't really think
you care too much what most people think.

Let us build the house of Random Acts on the
foundation of practicing kindness. As the primary
guild creator,I have a good idea of what I think
will work in the world of Norrath based on my
experience in leading groups in the real world
and in being a former member of an EQ guild,
but I'm not in this alone. I welcome all comments
and suggestions. Please post them on the
message board "Guild Business" or send me an email
at eqrandomacts@hotmail.com. If you want to keep your
comments private, please note that in your letter.

Our Practice and Policies come from these ideas:

One: Practicing kindness begins at home.

Two: Kindness is infectious.

Three: The individual is important.

Four: We all work together.

One: Practicing kindness begins at home:

Sponsorship assures that everyone in the guild
is contacted whenever online. There is nothing
lonlier than belonging to a guild and having no
one say "hello".

Everyone (except the guildleader) will have at
least one Sponsor: the person(s) who recommended
them or someone else in the guild. However,
not everyone must be a Sponsor.

The guild recommends a relationship between
Sponsor and Sponsee: From that time on, the
Sponsor and Sponsee are have a special friendship
within the guild. When they come online into EQ,
they would have someone to greet and to see
how the other is doing.

The guild recommends that each sponsor would
have ten or fewer sponsees. Ten people are a lot
to keep track of.

Two: Kindness is Infectious

Helping other players is what keeps most of us
from losing interest in the game of life and of
EverQuest. When you help one person, you help
the entire world.

Practicing kindness comes in many forms. Often
it is not what you do, but how and why you do it.
Trying to do what is best for you and the other
person is the best policy.

Members may not be playing a PVP character.
They may play it with another character of theirs,
but not with a guild character. Also, the guild will
not sponsor any PVP activities.

Our recommendation on practicing kindness is to:

1) First choice: through our actions and words.
Examples are greeting people, asking them if
they need help or answering calls for help,
buffing and healing them, helping them find a
person, place, or thing.

2) Second choice: through giving something:
our guild recommendation is that the best choice
of things given are items that are kept within the
receiver's level. This is not a hard and fast rule.
There are exceptions to this rule. But problems
will arise if players start to think that they don't
have to earn an item, they can just come to our
guild and beg for it.

* A note on beggars: Beggars are probably
children in real life. Encourage them to learn the
game and try not to hold their begging against
them. If you have time, maybe you can help them
learn to hunt or find someone else to help. If they
sincerely want help, they will be glad for assistance.
If they only want things for free, they will leave you
and look for another player to beg from.
 
 
 
 

Three: The individual is important:

Every member is important. We are all equals
in RA. The board member and officer positions
are rotating positions: every person who
wants the responsibility of being an officer
or board member will have that opportunity.

Recommending Membership: When you
recommend a person for membership, please
advise them that we are a guild of givers not
takers. Coming into the guild means you want
to help others, not that you want to get free stuff
from the guild and then move on.

Fledgling Members: When a person first joins RA,
they are a Fledgling Member. Before they can
become a Fledgling Member, they must read The Call
and The Charter and see our message board. When they
apply, they must give their email address.

A guild officer will find them a sponsor. The Fledgling
Member is required to attend guild events and group
with guild members, when possible. The Fledgling
Member will complete the Help a Newbie Quest, and be
recommended to be a Regular Member by their sponsor
and two Regular Members. The Fledgling Member is
encouraged to post messages on the Message Board.
A Fledgling Member thus becomes a Regular Member
through demonstrating the willingness to practice
random acts to kindness while playing EverQuest.

Regular Members: Regular Members may vote if and
when an issue arises. They may become sponsors, officers,
volunteers, and council members. These are positions
of responsibility for those people who have the time
and energy to contribute to their fellow guildmates
through doing service. They do not donate an elite
position in which one has more rights than others.
Regular Members may assist with the planning and
accomplishment of events, the message board, the
web page, the sponsorship program, and are
encouraged to practice random acts of kindness and
senseless beauty whenever possible. Every regular
member has a voice and is encouraged to bring
issues and comments to our web page forums. A
Regular Member who wants to introduce a guild
issue may do so at any time on the Guild Matters
message board.

Repealing or Refusing Membership: It would not be
kindness to accept someone into the guild when that
person would be a disrupting force within the guild.
Also, when an existing member has shown that they
are a disrupting force, it is the job of the Council to
decide to remove the member from the guild: the
guildleader must actually remove them according
to EverQuest Rules.

Four: We All Work Together

Even while we are all equal, different jobs must be
done to keep a guild running.

All positions in Random Acts are volunteer positions.
The person may resign their position without resigning
the guild. Anyone who demonstrates responsibility
within the guild will be allowed to hold a volunteer
position.

The Council: A regular member may become a
Council Member if he/she has displayed exceptional
dedication to Random Acts through their actions.
It takes someone who is willing to work behind the
scenes on the administrative work, the web page,
the message board, the sponsorship program, the
membership roster, the guild group hunting events
and acts of kindness events, contact outside guilds,
and decide policy. The reward is knowing you are
creating and maintaining something very valuable
to many people: the guild of Random Acts.

The guildleader is an equal member of the Council
and has one Council vote on issues, and is the
Chairperson of the Council. The Council will decide
if an issue warrants discussion and voting.
Discussion and voting will be handled by email.
The Council will vote to decide if something should
be brought to the Regular members as an issue to
vote on. Once brought to the Regular Members, all
Regular Members have one vote on the issue, and
majority wins.

The Council may bestow an award of honor on any
member who has been an exemplary guild member
through a voting process. These awards will appear
as medals next to the person's name on a web page
and will be announced on the message board.

A Council member is automatically an officer.

Volunteers: Volunteers are those who have a little
time to give to running the guild. They may assist
the Council on any aspect of running the guild
they would be enjoy upon approval of a Council
Member.

Officers: The officers will elect one officer to be
chairperson for a two-month term. Officers will
invite fledgling members into the group when they
are asked to do so by a Council member or on
his/her own judgement. Officers will find sponsors
for the fledgling members. Officers, along with the
Council, will approve new members and settle
disputes between members.

When a guild member wants to invite a new member
into the guild, the guild officer should be contacted f
or this. The guild officer will ask the guild member
if they are ready to be the proposed member's
sponsor. If they are not, the guild officer must find a
sponsor for the fledgling member before the fledgling
member can become a regular member.

When inviting a new member, the officer will ask the
fledgling member if he/she has read The Call and
The Charter and agrees with it, and must make sure
they have given us their email address.

It is a volunteer position and may be resigned at any
time without resigning the guild. The Council may
remove a person from being a sponsor, officer or a
volunteer if they are not doing their job. or if they have
become missing or On Sabbatical.
