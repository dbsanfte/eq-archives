From the Journals of Derufin Stubbletoe, Gnomish Warrior of Rallos Zek

It was a cold day in November, when my party finally made it to the foul Estate of Unrest; a deep, cold, almost unbearable place to be.  Legend says that a Dwarf once lived true and noble within the gates, but a curse fell upon his house by the bidding of the evil gods and now the undead walked free within the estate.  Being just past supper time I mentioned we stop for a bite to eat.  My human counterpart didn’t seem to like wasting the time we could be vanquishing the undead; figures coming from a Paladin of Marr.  As we ate outside the gates of the estate, a cold wind blew over of what the Dwarves call Dagnor’s Cauldron.  Warriors of Zek do not sense good and evil, only the enemy, so I was oblivious to what news the wind brought.  It was then, my equally short friend Muad Dib, our Gnome Rogue warned us of getting indoors as quickly as possible.  She told us that when the wind brings a chill like that, nothing good could be riding behind it (even for a Rogue).  So we packed up our belongings, much to my half empty stomachs ache, and headed for the cryptic gates of Unrest.

As we approached the main gate, we found it to be wide open, as if someone was wanting us to enter.  Strange.  We made our way in very slowly, following the brick lined hall to where the courtyard was supposed to be (according to the blueprints we paid a pretty copper for from a shady Dwarf).  Just as we were rounding the last supposed corner, being as deathly quite as we could, I began to hear a beautiful voice singing off in the distance.  Thinking my head was playing tricks on me, I continued on without a word to my companions, who followed close.  There up ahead of me was a pale green light  shrouded in a thick mist which seemed to come from the end of the hall.  With a heavy heart and a mind full of doubts, I pushed on.  It was then that my human friend Boden, of the Brokenblade family from Freeport, spoke as if there was no danger.  “Why are we traveling so slowly?”, he said in quite an alarming voice.  Muad and I nearly soiled ourselves at the sound of such a audible voice.  Even the Gemcutters of Ak’Anon are not that loud and brash.  After waiting for what seemed the inevitable onslaught, we hushed Boden up and pressed on; behind me I thought I heard Muad mutter something to the extent of Backstab working on loud Humans.  As we approached the pale light the singing in my head began to get louder; it was just now pasted the point of a whisper.  It was then we began to smell that old familiar smell, the smell of death.  Upon stepping into the light, the smell became apparent.  Dead bodies.  Many, many corpses lay stricken with rusty swords and blunt blows to the head and body, in the courtyard of the estate.  It seems many had come to lay the Dwarven ghost’s  soul to rest, only to fail.  I did not feel like becoming one of those many.  The mist grew mysteriously thicker.

I turned around to consult my invisible party within the mist on if we maybe had gotten ourselves into something much to big for our own good.  Keeping as quiet as possible, I asked if maybe it was a good time to leave this accursed place to another day.  Just then a entity of great weight and impeccable force slammed into the back of me, and I fell with a thud on to my face.  Dead.  That’s all I could think of; I was dead.  Muad jumped back at the sound, alarmed by what had caused the noise.  Boden drew his blade and began to look around anxiously, although no one could now see me due to the mist.  As the being lifted itself from me, all the while using me to push off of, I felt the cold, broad hands of death approaching.  I laid still; for if death was going to come to me from behind, I did not want to see it (although a warrior of Rallos Zek should never be afraid, but my Gnomish intelligence makes me think otherwise sometimes).  It then turned me over with it’s icy grip; a sunken feeling in my stomach made me queasy and sicken at it’s touch.  I landed on my back with a bit of clanking from my ring-mail armor; I then prayed to Rallos for a quick painless death.  Suddenly the entity laughed a great deep belly laugh that seemed to echo throughout the adjacent hallway of brick.  Boden and Maud nearly jumped out of their skin at the sound.  The laughter continued until I opened my eyes and stared at the figure standing above me.  All I could make out of the shape was a short body and a flat head; it bent down towards me.  The mist seemed to give way.  A Dwarf.  He bore a great smile and laughed again as he saw the look my eyes gave off through my helm.  I was not happy.  Just then another taller figure came shooting through the mist.

“Coming through!”, a females voice called out, “clear a path people!”  The Dwarf turned just in time to dodge the oncoming momentum of a female Wood Elf hustling for the gate; I was not as lucky.  I sat up again just as an armored foot came crashing down on my torso.  The wind was knocked out me and I fell to my back again; very harshly I might add.  The Wood Elf didn’t seem like she was going to stop when the Dwarf called out to her.  “Caisha!  You seemed to have stepped in something!”, again he let out a deep laugh.  I just lay there, wondering what in all of Faydwer was happening.  The Wood Elf (I assumed at this point was named Caisha) stopped and came running back to my position.  I cringed as the thought passed through my head that she might step on me again.

“What do we have here?”, she asked.

“Seems to be one of our Gnomish neighbors from the mountains, and some people from across the sea”, the dwarf replied.

“Dafa’s holding them off as long as possible, we better get going.”

“We can’t just leave ‘em here, they’ll tear ‘em to pieces.”  I still just laid there, staring at the night sky.  Muad and Boden were just as much help, staring at the entire situation in awe.  Now if they stared because they couldn’t believe I wasn’t dead or because some unknown force held them in place is still a mystery.  “It seems as though he’s passed out or just stunned by some sort of magic.  I can’t seem to get a response from him”, the Dwarf said.  Caisha then kneeled down and propped me up on my rear to have me facing the direction from where they came from.  Just then yet another figure came shooting through the mist.

From the size and weight distribution I figured it to be another Dwarf.  I was right.  “Dafa, my wife!  Nice to see you alive still, did you manage to hold them off without taking too much of a beating?”,  the Dwarf asked.

“Yes.  But it seems we’ve ran into some unexpected company here” Dafa replied.

“Precise as usual.”

“I think we overwhelmed them” Caisha added.

Dafa looked distressed, “We better get going, they’re not too far behind.”

“Right!  Let’s grab these folks and get them the heck out of here!”, Caisha said with a big grin (almost laughing).  I was then lifted into the air by the queer Wood Elf and slung over her shoulder, as you would do with a bag of flour.  “Crank, you grab the Human and Halfling!.” 

“Right!” the male Dwarf now known as Crank said.  He then began pushing on Boden and Muad as if he were a farmer moving his herd.  And as if woken from a dream, my two companions then turned and bolted for the gate with Crank following as only a good herd driver did.

Caisha began running for the gate with me over her shoulder and Dafa close behind.  As we ran, or I should say they ran, for the hallway, I could see various shapes out of the corner of my eye making their way for our position very quickly, as if in pursuit.  I squirmed to get down as if I could out run them myself, of course this Wood Elf knew better.  We rounded several corners until at last we reached the gate.  Dafa just made it through the gate as it closed behind her and a horde of angry ghouls and other various undead slammed into it with a force to wake the gods.

The undead behind the gate shook it with furious anger, apparently to get out.  The male Dwarf then coughed up a huge slack of flem and spit it towards the gate.  “Go back to your hole, foul creature!  You know you cannot pass the gates of this estate!” he said as he spat again.  I finally let out a cry to be put down, and Caisha set me to the ground.  Boden and Muad gathered themselves behind me.

“Ok”, I said.  “Three things.  Who are you, what in all of Ak’Anon were those, and hurry with the answers before I pass out.”

Another round of laughter (somehow seemingly at my expense) from these curious folks.  “My name is Crank, and this is my wife Dafa, Clerics of Kaladim at your service.”  They both bowed graciously in front of me.

“Hiyas!  Caisha Leaftuner, Bard of the city in the trees, Kelethin”, Caisha said bowing.  “Those were the carrion ghouls of the Estate of Unrest; nasty, vile creatures.  They’ll tear your soul from your body if you get caught by their magic.  You are ok, aren’t you?”

My stomach churned at the description.  “I don’t feel so good…”, I bellowed.

“Lay back slowly”, Dafa let out.

“Thanks, but I’ll just pass out now, thank you.”  And with that my vision blurred and I fell back.

So was the first meeting of the new Faydwerian Army.
