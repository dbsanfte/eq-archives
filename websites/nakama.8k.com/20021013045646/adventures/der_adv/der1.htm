From the Journals of Derufin Stubbletoe, Gnomish Warrior of Rallos Zek

Kyrial and I have finally made it to the dreaded mines of Steamfont of which the Minotaurs have conveniently taken over with the help of that most evil of Gnomes, the Necromancer Meldrath.  We have set up a base of operations near the entrance of the mines from which Kyrial will do some scouting and we could stay out of the line of sight of the roaming Minos that frequently check the entrance for intruders.  It was a beautiful day in August and the sun was peeking through the brown leafed trees on the slops of the mountains.  After a good hour or so of scouting, Kyrial returned to inform me that three Minos were stationed just inside the mines, and that we could probably lure them out without startling any others further in.  It was then decided that we would start our raids early next morning as to catch them by surprise.  That night we slept with one eye open and one hand on our weapons.

With the disappearing of the stars, brought the morning, and we readied ourselves.  I unsheathed my two-handed sword, dropped to one knee, and gave a small prayer to Rallos Zek.  Kyrial said some words to Tuanre, the mother of all, and readied her scimitar; we were ready as ever.  I looked to the south and saw the windmills of Ak’anon, and remembered the vow about avenging my brother Duilin’s death.  Duilin had been practicing his Wizardry skills near the windmills when Medrath’s lackey the Minotuar Hero came charging over the hills.  The watchman on duty at the windmills, Mylz, was tossed to the side like a rag doll, as he rushed to defend the helpless, young library students who had come out to the mills to practice their new skills.  My brother, being the noble sort, tried to Root the filthy beast and that is when the Mino turned his aggression towards Duilin.  He never even had a chance.  Duilin was picked up by the Hero and slammed against the nearest mill with such force that the several stones that made up the building actually fell out of the structure.  My brother died instantly.  No agony.  No second chance.  No pain.  I was in the Gemchoppers guild at the time practicing my Parrying skills when the Dean of the Library came to see me.  It was then I vowed to exact revenge on the Mino Hero that killed my brother. 

So here I was, after a year of training ready to take out the life that had taken my brother’s.  Was I ready?  Definitely.  I told Kyrial that I would pull the Minos out to this point where we would then ambush them.  She agreed, and I was off to the entrance of the mines.  I slowly crept up the hole in the side of the mountain, and upon arriving at the side of the entrance, I poked my head around the corner.  I was lucky that my dull green helm didn’t reflect any light from the sun, or I would’ve been found out as soon as I stuck my head around the corner.  Just as she said, two smelly, rancid Minos lay sound asleep in the corner, and another sat quietly opposite them with it’s back to me.  ‘Now, how am I going to get there attention without making a huge scene?’, I thought to myself.  Ding!  A light went on over my head.  Without second guessing my thought, I raised my bow (from which I acquired from an old Wood Elf, Ranger friend of mine named Evision) and shot directly for one of the sleeping Mino’s eye sockets.  My arrow hit it’s mark, and the sleeping Mino let out a deathly scream.  The Mino opposite of him couldn’t figure out what to make of the situation; that was, until I shot him square in the back.  By the time I let go of my second arrow, the other sleeping beast was awake and starting to make sense of the ordeal.  They all turned to see me at exactly the same time.  Drooling for the taste of fresh young Gnome, they began to run towards me.  I quickly made my way back to the ambush spot where Kyrial lay in waiting.  No sooner did they make the turn from their mine entrance and they were getting blasted with all sorts of Druid magic’s; none the less, they continued forward.  I then rushed them with blinding fury and slashed one deep within it’s calf .  Blood rushed from it’s wound and it let out a terrible scream.  Kyrial then proceeded to ignite the Mino into flames.  It continued to scream as it crackled and burned.  It then fell to the ground with a thud; dead from Kyrial’s powerful magic.  One Mino then leaped into the air and hit Kyrial with it’s two hoofed feet squarely in the cheast; knocking her out.  “No! Kyrial!”, I screamed.  I was then picked up by my neck from behind, coincidentally, by the one I had shot in the eye, and thrown into the nearest tree trunk. 

I must have been out for thirty seconds or so, because when I woke up Kyrial was being dragged into the mines by the two remaining Minotuars.  I jumped up and rushed the one dragging her and slashed it’s left hand off, forcing him to drop Kyrial.  Stunned that I had the power to do that, I hesitated for a moment.  That’s all they needed.  Screaming in pain, the one handed beast then hit me hard with his handless forearm and I flew back ten to fifteen yards.  The other then raised a horn to his lips and blew a horrific sound; I only guess to call other Minos.  Before I could reach them again two more Minos and a huge beast, to which only I guess was the Hero, stood between them and the entrance to the mines.  I was hopelessly outnumbered.  But I couldn’t just leave Kyrial!  I had to think of something fast.  I figured if I could get them to drop Kyrial she might wake up and be able to get away.  So I did the only thing I could do, taunt them.

“Hey you big smelly dust-balls!”, I yelled.  They stopped and turned towards me snarling.  “Yeah you, with the nose ring!  That’s so last age!  Where’d you get that thing off a dead Troll?!  No wonder you stink so bad!”  Was it going to work?  I didn’t know.  Was it crazy?  Definitely, but I had to try something.  “Hey big guy!  Don’t you take orders from a little man called Meldrath?!  I heard he only using you for his own evil intentions!  But don’t believe me!  Ask your comrades if they know what he is doing!  I’ll bet none of you know what he’s actually doing in there!”  The Hero looked around as the other Minos looked at him.  He looked frustrated and angered. 

He then let out a loud snort.  “Attack Gnome man!!”, he shouted.

They dropped Kyrial and stampeded towards me.  Was this the end?  I sure hoped not.  I braced my feet in the dirt and readied myself for the initial impact of the five charging Minos.  Now I don’t know if it was the power of Rallos Zek or just my adrenaline pumping, but as the first Mino lowered his head to attack me, I leapt high in the air and slashed at his huge hairy neck, and took his head clean off.  The other beasts stopped charging in shock.  Standing around dumbfounded, only their leader the Hero, remained charging.  And this time, the power granted to me from Rallos Zek was gone; I was on my own.  He hit me with his horns and ripped a hole in my ringmail tunic the size of a giant rat, not to mention sent me whirling through the air.  When I landed he was already on top of me.  I was first struck in the chest with his massive fists.  Again and again he hit me.  A cracked sternum let out a small pop as he continued to hit me, and a breaking rib snapped loudly as he pummeled my sides as well.  I began to cough up blood with every hit.  More and more blood spewed from my mouth and my body.  I could feel myself starting to fade.  Sleep was coming down on me hard; my body was shutting down.  This was the end.

Suddenly a sphere of blades appeared over the Hero, and before he could even comprehend what was happening they flew through the air at his body.  ‘Kyrial doesn’t have magic like that, who was it?’, I asked myself.  The beast jumped from my body and blood began to pour from each place a blade had struck.  He seemed hurt to an extent, but more annoyed if anything.  I rolled my to the side to see who was my savior.  My father, Duinhir, the magician of the family was standing just twenty yards away with his trusted water pet Jann.

“Get up son, I can’t hold him forever”, my father said.

“Ky.. rial.. I can’t.. leave her”, I muttered.

“You won’t”, he answered.

Just then I saw a burst of flame hit the Hero square in the back and singe his hair.  The Hero and I turned to see a Wood Elf, Kyrial to be exact, standing proud and as strong as ever.  She began casting, and again he was hit by her flames.  My father then told Jann to begin attacking the beast, while my father cast more offensive spells.  I was of no help to them, but I did manage to get to my feet and look somewhat formidable.  The other Minos realizing that their leader was surrounded, fled back into the mines.  We let them go.

“You small peoples can’t defeat me’s might!”, the Hero yelled.

“We don’t plan on defeating you, stupid beast!”, Kyrial let out.  She then rooted the Hero in place, and then got behind him and cast her fire spell on a earth elemental that was wondering nearby in front of him about thirty yards or so.  The elemental was struck by the fire and then looked around angrily for it’s attacker.  It then looked in the direction of Kyrial, but only saw the Hero.  The elemental then grabbed some of it’s kind and charged the Mino.

“Foolish small peoples, Meldrath will kill you alls!” the Mino Hero shouted.  The elementals had now began swarming him.

Kyrial ran over, picked me up and put me over her shoulder so we could get away; my father and Jann soon followed.  As we ran I shouted back, “I’m sure he will you smelly oaf!”, but then retreated back to silence due to the pain inflicted on my body.  When we got to the windmills Kyrial set me down and administered her best healing spells to cure my wounds.

“That was foolish Derufin.  You could’ve been killed”, my father said.

I answered, “I’m sorry father, but I was enacting revenge for Duilin.  I guess I just wasn’t thinking hard enough.”

“Revenge for Duilin?  Who?  Revenge for what?”, Kyrial asked.  I then explained the story about my brother to her, and I could see her heart sink as I concluded the tale.  “I’m so sorry Der; I never knew.”

“It’s ok, not may people know”, I replyed.  “I try to keep it a secret.”

Kyrial sat idle for a minute, the spoke, “I will help you in your quest.  I vow to do anything I can to help, you only need ask.  If you can’t find me, call my name to the wind and I will come running, no matter where I am.  I believe, as well as Tunare believes, that this is a righteous and justifiable journey to undertake.  Let us form a bond Derufin.  If one of us falls, the other will continue in on in the fallen’s glory.”

Tears came to my eyes.  I couldn’t believe that someone wanted to help me with this surely deadly mission.  “Agreed.”, I finally said.  And with that I put out my hand out in a fist and stared into her eyes; she did the same and raised her fist to mine

My father then spoke up, “From now on you will be known as the Faydwerian Army; protectors of all those that are just and true, and defenders of Faydwer.”

As my father said these words, Kyrial’s and my glance never swayed form each other.  As he finished his speech I could see a spark in Kyrial’s eyes and a bright light filled the room as if a new beginning was at hand, and soon all troubles would be laid to rest
