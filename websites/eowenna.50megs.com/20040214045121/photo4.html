Bard in Training
Quite recently, in the ancient city of Shar Vahl, a young white tigress came of age according to her kind. She'd always had a good ear for music, and an excellent memory. So Pouncibelle went to the top of the castle to see if she could become a Jharin Recruit.

To her surprise and delight, Pouncibelle was welcomed by the most ancient bard in the land - in person!

She ran about town a little, then wandered to the castle basement. When she arrived at the Rogue guild, she found her litter-mate Scre being welcomed as a Taruun Recruit. She had suspected this would be his choice, he had too great a love of surprising people. Scre would, henceforth, be known as a Rogue. But Pouncibelle didn't mind - Scre was still her beloved brother.

They immediately set out to prove their right to citizenship. She had explored the shops a little while Scre was deciding his profession, so she led him to the Registrar. Then they went to the tax collector - who seemed delighted to see them - and finally they found the Official who provided each one with their own history slate.

Neither of the young Vah Shir appreciated being paw-printed, as if already guilty or suspected of a crime. But they soon forgot their grievance as they returned to their respective guilds to show their guildmasters that they had successfully completed their first quest!

Each was given a cloak, and sent to learn a trade. Pouncibelle would learn pottery, Scre would become a chef. About this time both litter-mates discovered that they had advanced to their second season of skills. Hurrah! So each of the young Vah Shir sought and received instruction from those more skilled in their chosen profession.

After training in their primary profession, each presented himself or herself to the trademaster designated by their guild leader. Each was commanded to go hunting for various supplies.

Though each was eager to set forth, they had just been through a very long and very busy day. Both were still very young, and needed to rest before they could hunt effectively.

So they sat down and curled up together to rest ...

... and they will surely be seen hunting together as they grow in strength and skill. One day, they may even depart from their native moon to visit the strange land of Norrath...

Interlude
Adventurers seldom get to rest, Pouncibelle reminded herself, so I should be glad of the chance. Yet she was growing restless, and she knew it.

Pouncibelle had been invited to join an Alliance of friends, and she had done so readily. She was saddened when her dear litter-mate Scre chose a different guild of adventurers. But she had sat on her disappointment and continued to hunt with him. They remained together, that was the important thing.

Lately, however, it seemed they seldom did anything - together or apart. He'd be off practicing cooking, or one of his Roguish skills, and she'd be working her pottery in a different section of town. The Alliance of friends had joined with a larger alliance, and she needed to stay near their old meeting place with a message of welcome to the new one.

Adventurers were known as roaming sorts, and indeed she seldom visited her hometown of Shar Vahl anymore. She was most often camping out somewhere far from home, wherever the need drove her at the time. But there was a meeting place for the Allies, and each one would check in there from time to time. Pouncibelle had been appointed to inform the stray wandering adventurers of a change.

This was a need ideally suited to a bard - she was, aside from being an entertainer, responsible for history and information. Pouncibelle stayed at or near the meeting-place so she could inform every adventurer where their friends could now be found. This was an important task, yet she found no fulfillment.

She knew that most had visited, and received the news. The hall grew increasingly empty, till it seemed she was the only one left. She knew there were at least a Druid, and two wandering Wizards, a mage and a Paladin who had not checked in. There may be another one or two she couldn't remember off the top of her head ... and the leader of the Alliance remained missing.

Oh, there had been letters for a while that said the leader was ok, and had approved the change. But some resources remained in the meeting hall, and the leader had yet to come collect them.

Pouncibelle could not, in good conscience, leave the meeting place till the leader came and retrieved those last few items that belonged to the smaller Alliance. But the waiting was difficult. Few visited anymore, and she was growing lonely.

She journeyed briefly, leaving a letter behind and posted on the wall.

Pouncibelle, and her brother Scre, journeyed carefully into the ruins of an old frog city in a swamp on Norrath. Down in the depths, it was said that an undead assassin lurked. Rumors claimed he held a mask that would cause the wearer to appear in the form of a dark elf. But only if the wearer was a Bard or Rogue by profession.

As a Bard, Pounce felt obligated to learn the truth of these rumors. She did, and was able to arrange that both she and her brother would have their own mask. Now, at need, she and her brother could appear in the guise of dark elves. That was a useful trip, and she was well satisfied with the outcome.

She'd returned, then rumors led her to leave the meeting hall again. Again, she left a letter posted on the wall by the door.

This time, Pouncibelle visited the Jaggedpine Forest. There had been too many rumors, she felt it only right and proper that she learn the truth and spread that in their place. Scre was too busy cooking to accompany her on this trip, much to her regret.

Pouncibelle visited the hidden forest, alone. She received another illusion mask there, but she was too young to use it. Those who helped her get the second mask informed her that when she was skilled enough, it would cause her to appear as a half elf. She'd inquired about this, since she wasn't half a cat. After they finished laughing, they told her they'd meant a half-elf, half-human crossbreed.

She'd been warned that crossbreeds were not welcome in all places, and this saddened her. People were, after all, people. It should be their hearts, instead of their skins, that others used to determine their merits or flaws.

After all, it would be silly to judge a cat by the color of his or her fur! Hmmmph.

Pouncibelle had to admit that the dark fur helped her brother when he needed to be unobtrusive. And she had to admit that there were some parts of the Roguish profession that neither she nor he liked. But her brother Scre still had a good heart, and any who came to know him generally grew to love him.

She'd seen other cats of other colors and professions, some the "good" professions, who had rotten hearts. Surely folks of any race would be able to see the same need - to learn each person by their heart, and go from there.

But it seemed that this truth wasn't widely known. Some were judged merely by their race or chosen lifestyle. And it was many, not few, who judged this way. Pouncibelle couldn't understand why, but found more and more who held this strange idea that they knew a person by their race or profession.

Pouncibelle sighed. That was a sad truth she must record for posterity.

She wrote it faithfully, then looked about her. The empty hall is making me ponder overmuch, she thought. The emptiness is weighing heavily on my spirit. I need to get out and see some other wonder, to clear my mind. Or perhaps at least I can find or write a new song, she mused.

Pouncibelle looked about, tidying up, and decided she'd go see what her brother was doing. Fresh air would do both of them good. They'd stayed in one place for too long. She would return, faithful to her task, but right this moment a short trip was very much needed ...
Alliance Reborn
Pouncibelle had been unable to persuade her litter-mate to leave his cooking, even for a brief roam. This had bitterly disappointed her, so she'd wandered about aimlessly for as long as it took many individuals to grow in skill.

Yet nothing seemed to satisfy her, and she began to feel guilty for being away when there might be others come to the inn and need to know what was happening.

She turned her steps back toward the favored meeting-place of the members of Winged Alliance, where so few still visited. Expecting to find only passers-by among the patrons, Pounce was surprised to hear many voices raised in conversation before she even opened the door.

Checking beside the door as she came in, she discovered that someone had torn down her notice. Mildly irritated, and concerned that guildmates might feel abandoned without any message, she turned toward the loudest voices.

And stopped in her tracks, stunned.

There, at the best table in the house, sat Truebeliever, Rgyle, Jarsa, Jordanesque, Aelseren, Hegegroll ... and Aundra?!?

They were so busy celebrating and clapping each other on the back, that Pouncibelle's arrival went unnoticed. Her feline tread was nearly silent, though any small sound she might have made would have been entirely lost under the sound of their jubilant conversation.

Their words began to fade, and Aundra began to speak. Thankful to see her alive and well again, Pouncibelle had no desire to interrupt.

Pouncibelle quietly moved near, to hear better because she needed to record the tale accurately. When she reached a place that seemed suitable to her, she settled herself to listen.
