Galadri Leafshimmer

Once upon a time ...

A tall elf-maiden with reddish-gold hair answered the call of the Paladin.

Galadri Leafshimmer, granddaughter of the respected city elder Aelfiera Leafshimmer, entered the Paladin Guild hall.  She asked the guard at the door where she might find the general.

"His lordship is currently tending the documents of his office," she was told.  The guard gestured to the east.  "The second door to your left will be his."

She thanked him, curtseying, and then walked gracefully toward the specified door.  Finding the door partly open, she knocked gently upon its carved frame.  "Enter," said an authoritative voice from within.  She obeyed.

Though currently seated, it was clear that he must be a tall elf.  Silver streaks ran through his rich chestnut hair, bound at his nape in one of the more traditional fashions.  His chest and shoulders, broad as some humans', were covered by etched and gilded plate.  His eyes, as vividly green as the leaves in early summer, raised their intensely penetrating gaze to look at her.  "Yes?"

Though belonging to one of the lesser houses among the Elvish nobility, Galadri had the confidence of her class.  "I have come to offer myself in the service of our Lord and our Nation," she said politely though firmly. 

"Indeed?" he said.  "Very few are the noble women who are called to wield swords."

"So I have been told," she replied, "yet I think I may be one of those few."

"How did it come to you?" he asked.

Galadri had not anticipated that question, though she knew he was within his rights to inquire.  "It is difficult to explain," she said.  "Perhaps it is best described as an intense awareness of the need for knights, and a burning desire to contribute to that need -- even unto my very life."

His dark brows drew together in concentration.  "Your dedication is admirable," he said, "and your desire is worthy."  He paused, looking down toward his desk as he sat back in his chair, folded his arms, and raised one hand to rest his chin against his calloused fist.  "Sometimes the calling does come as an intense desire," he said at last, almost as if thinking aloud.  "Other times, the desire is not a calling.  In most cases, only the passing of time can define which is which."

He raised his gaze to her again.  "Your family has no objections?" he inquired sharply.

"They know I am here," she said precisely, "and they did not forbid my coming."  They had not exactly been thrilled about her choice, though, Galadri knew.  Yet they had sufficient faith that, like it or not, if she had a true calling they had no moral grounds upon which they could prevent or forbid her from becoming a Paladin.

One side of his mouth quirked upward.  "I see," he said, almost as if he had read her unspoken thoughts.  He pressed his lips together into a firm line, took a deep breath, released it, and then said, "You understand that we are not a place for the young to run to, in order to escape their families.  Flight solves nothing; one must face the battles that life brings."

"I am not at war with my family," she said.  "Yet your words make me wonder: would flight never be acceptable?  Even if one knew she could not win?  Would there never be a time when one should retreat, to gather her strength or summon allies, and then return to a more equal contest?"

The last trace of his half-grin evaporated.  "Retreat is not flight," he said firmly.

She nodded, waiting silently.

"You also understand that you are entering service to the Temple?" he asked.  "You understand that Paladins shall be required to live in spiritual and physical purity, and not waste time or energy indulging the lusts of the flesh?"

"Yes, milord," she answered firmly and without hesitation.  Her family was just prominent enough that those of lesser bloodlines often sought her acquaintance.  Far, far too many had sought to promote themselves by using her.  Galadri had learned, while still quite young, to recognize a true friend from a false one.  She would never be hurt by that type of betrayal again.

The fortune-seekers had been bad enough; but she had later discovered that there were even worse forms of selfishness.  Since puberty had reshaped her figure into striking proportions, Galadri could no longer appear anywhere in public without someone saying, either blatantly or subtly, that she was physically desirable.  She saw through such false flatteries, and recognized the concealed insult.  Those who said such things sought only their own pleasure, and thought nothing of her - to them, she was only a convenient means to their selfish ends.  They viewed her as a tool, not as a person.  This elfess had no interest in being used that way, either.

A sacred marriage would always be an option that was both permitted and blessed by the Church; but to the priests and paladins, temporary physical alliances were forbidden.  That suited Galadri perfectly, as she knew full well that most who sought her bed had no interest in lifetime partnering.  She had no interest in temporary alliances. 

Once she was known to be in Temple training, Galadri hoped that the puerile lascivious would finally accept her "no" as final.

At least there were few such rude ones among her own people.  The worst offenders were outsiders, visitors to the Elvish Capitol.  They would gawk, ogle, and make rude noises or lewd invitations whenever any elfess was seen.  Unfortunately, the city law enforcement officials had enough to do without serving as bodyguards to every elfess within the city's walls.  So this problem had no easy solution in sight.

The general turned in his chair to open a bronze-bound chest near his desk.  He looked at her again, an intensely measuring look.  Then he sorted through the various items within the chest.  After a moment, he gave her a moderately padded training tunic and a beginner's sword. 

She lifted the one-handed sword, surprised at how heavy it was.  She had seen paladins who swung their swords about as if the blades weighed nothing at all.  This weapon was solid steel, and weighed roughly half as much as she did.  Holding it up one-handed was no easy task.

As the elfess stood testing the weight of the weapon in her hand, she was told to go out and fight the enemies that plagued the forest surrounding the city.

Galadri had known, intellectually, that she would eventually be expected to defend the city.  However, she had not expected to be sent forth so soon, nor without any substantial preparation.  She stood blinking at the general in disbelief.  "I thought I would receive training ere venturing forth against enemies," she said.  "As yet I have no skill with the sword."

"Wise," the general said, nodding.  "You pass the first test.  We cannot have paladins who fight without thought.  What is your name?"

"Galadri Leafshimmer, milord," she said.

"Leafshimmer?" he said softly.  After a thoughtful pause he told her, "Go put on your training tunic, and then return to me for further instruction."

Galadri entered the female Paladins' bathing area for the first time.  She removed her cloak, unwound her sash, unfastened her platinum shoulder-clasps, and let her plain green silken gown drop as she pulled the coarse training tunic over her head.  She continued wearing her under things, since no replacements for those had been offered.

Here was her next surprise: the training tunic's hem stopped just slightly above her knees!  Her tunic was three or four palm-widths too short for a woman of her station.  She wondered if the general had accidentally or deliberately chosen an incorrect size as she hung her gown on a nearby hook.  Then again, Galadri reminded herself, she did run taller than average for even a high elfess.  Cautiously, she returned to his office.

He rose wordlessly and led her to a practice yard.  Once there, introduced her to another Paladin apprentice.  "Face each other," he ordered.  "Stonn, you attack; Galadri, defend.  Slowly, now - the point is to learn how to do it correctly, not to surprise and overcome your training partner."

The general beckoned to the master of the yard, who came to monitor their progress.  After a brief conference between the two, the general had bowed to them and departed.

The rest of that day was spent in the practice yard.  All the squires were commanded to practice both attacks and defenses.  Over and over again, they rotated and practiced within new pairings.  When Galadri asked why, she was told it was to keep all their skills sharp.

"Imagine how it would be if there were not rotation," the master of the yard explained.  "If one among you should excel, then only his or her partner would benefit from such excellence.  The rest of you would learn less, from never personally facing one with a higher skill.  If one among you was unusually weak, their partner would have to be always teaching instead of learning for them self.  By using partner rotation, the strong can teach the weak.  All benefit."

She nodded, returning her focus from his words to the actions required of her.

There were no other noble women.  In fact, there were few other women at all.  As the hours wore away, she began to understand why. It was long, grueling labor.  Galadri had never felt so weary in her life. Her arms ached as if they might fall off her body.

Yet she persevered until the sun drew near to the western horizon.  At last a trumpet sounded, and all were released for the night.  The yard master inquired if she would need a place in the barracks, or would be returning home.

She found herself pondering, not sure how to answer.  It was unusual for her to fail to think ahead.  Mayhap her mind was numb from all the physical exertion in the heat of a summer's day.

"I think I shall return home, at least for tonight," she answered at last.  "Mayhap tomorrow I shall know where I will be living henceforth."

He bowed and walked away.  The yard had largely emptied even as they spoke, and she found herself standing there alone.

She returned to the women's wing to wash up and retrieve her gown.  She washed quickly, but even so found herself half-inclined to doze in the warm water.  Rousing herself with a reminder that she did not wish to miss the evening meal, she hurriedly dressed and departed.

O how surpassingly good it was to see home again!  The family mansion had never before seemed as lovely as it did in the golden light of that evening's setting sun.

She passed in by way of the main entrance, under the silvery letters etched into deep green marble that proclaimed her family's name in an ornate, artistically rendered Elder Elvish script. 

She stepped into the hallway, upon its wide marble squares of alternating pale green and deep green.  The walls were formed of pale green marble with deeper green veins.  Beautifully carved pillars supported the slightly arched ceiling at intervals.  Yet the hallway was not bare: carpeting, paintings and tapestries adorned it.  It was exquisitely appointed, yet displayed enough restraint to avoid ostentation.

To the weary young elfess, it was home.

She took a step aside, to hang her deep green cloak on a peg in the family cloak room.  Stepping back out, she looked again through the windows that flanked the doorway and judged the angle of the sun.  No, there would not be time to change, she realized.  The gown she currently wore would have to do.  She hurried down the hall from whence the faintest wisps of appetizing aromas were emanating.

The dining hall appeared almost like a forest at sunrise or sunset.  Plentiful beeswax candles lent both light and a pleasing scent.  They were scattered about the room almost like fireflies among the branches of the illusory trees carved into the walls.  She paused a moment to enjoy the familiar walls, formed of varying shades of carved, colored marble.  Brown marble columns formed tree trunks, green marble formed leaves and grass, and beige marble formed winding "paths" on the walls and floor.  How often had she taken for granted this work of a master craftsman, or mayhap a troupe of master craftsmen?

The long central table was made of a hardwood of a peculiar hue.  The grain was accentuated by a glaze, which also protected the wood from any spills that may occur.  Woven mats of softly dyed plant fibers were distributed around the table, upon which were laid out place settings of silver flatware beside dishes of fine porcelain.

Galadri took her usual seat, one away from the right hand of her grandmother's chair.  Even as she estimated that the others must arrive very soon, she heard her brother's step in another hallway.  She composed herself as best she could, knowing that he would certainly note any flaw in her appearance or bearing.

Kalrin often arrived first, Galadri reflected.  His was a very exacting personality.  One day he would be head of House Leafshimmer, and he took great pains to behave in a manner that would prove him responsible and worthy of that honor.  His hair was as deep an auburn as their grandmother's had been, and at times he could be equally solemn.  Other times, the mischievous twinkle in his deep blue eyes seemed the calmest part of his personality.

Her younger sister, Abree, arrived next.  Her sister's deep golden hair was caught up into an elaborate style twined with pearls and numerous semiprecious stones.  Her lavender gown was more tailored than Galadri's, and was ornamented with elaborate embroidery outlined in silver threads.  Platinum filigree ornamented her upper arms and wrists, her throat, her waist, and dangled from her ears.

Galadri felt positively drab as she looked upon her sister's everyday finery.  She still felt it would have been less than polite to wear such ornamentation to the temple for paladin training, as it might have appeared she was flaunting her nobility to the common folk who were called to service.  However, she began to regret that she had not brought suitable ornaments in her carrying-pouch to put on for dinner.  That was the second time today that she had neglected to look far enough ahead.  She hoped she could cure herself of that fault ere it grew into a bad habit.

Her grandmother, matron of the household, arrived in attire similar to Abree's.  The immediately noticeable difference was that Aelfiera's silken gown was dyed and embroidered in many shades of green, instead of the lavender and silver that formed her younger granddaughter's.

The aging priestess' hair was liberally sprinkled with silver strands among the deep auburn.  She bore herself gracefully, though some minor stiffness was apparent to an astute observer.  Her vivid blue eyes seemed to echo depths upon depths of her soul and mirror both past and present in nearly equal measure.  All in all, however, she bore her four millennia with dignity.

When the current guests had arrived and seated themselves around the table, Aelfiera led them in evening prayers followed by a benediction.  Once the benediction was complete, a sincere prayer of thanksgiving for the food was pronounced.  As they raised their heads, smiling servants brought in the evening meal.

It was Galadri's favorite; a well-done roast with fruit and nut glaze and garnished with root-vegetables.  Her mouth watered as she returned the servants' smiles and thanked everyone from her heart.

     *     *     *     *     *     *     *

Galadri spent years studying in the Paladin hall ere she was pronounced fit to apprentice among the city guards.  Among them she had to study for several more years ere she began to learn prayers of power that would heal friends or confuse foes.  She progressed well, up to a point. 

Once she reached that point, however, it seemed that her ability to learn combat decreased and her capacity to learn prayers increased.  A strange situation, this odd imbalance in her learning capacity: especially since a knight needed both skills in great measure.  Either skill without the other could mean needless defeats.  Unfortunately when a knight was defeated, those (s)he protected were most often harmed also.

Over a lengthy period of time, an odd pattern in Galadri's battles emerged with increasing clarity. She began suffering more and more defeats - instead of less, as would be expected when one grows more seasoned. This troubled her exceedingly, so she began earnest prayers to learn what was amiss.

When Galadri had completed her 24th season of instruction, she finally knew what was awry. She had to admit an embarrassing truth. Denying truth is unnatural, even when it hurts. And this particular truth was so very painful that Galdri had hidden it from herself for a very long time.

That truth is that Galadri had grown far better at praying than she was at fighting. She had worked hard, with the aid of many friends, to win a sacred sword. She had hoped to prove herself worthy of the holy blade, and with it to gain great victories.

But the holy sword was not enough to cure her own inadequacies. Finally, deeply regretful, an evening came when Galadri laid aside her beloved blade on the guard walk of the city wall. She knelt in prayer, and waited. 

The elfess had misunderstood her calling.  She had not been called to knighthood, but to priesthood.  Priests were forbidden the use of blades, as their first calling was to heal - not to kill.  Only blunt weapons were permitted, and it was encouraged that they be used only for defense.

A guard approached.  "Seeking illumination in the sunset?" he asked. 

"Mayhap," she allowed.  "Else mayhap I ought best to speak with the general or the high priest.  I know not which I ought to seek," she added miserably.  "I need their counsel."

"I shall see if they are willing to speak with you," he said kindly.  She had trained with this one, but to her embarrassment she could not recall his name at that moment.

"Thank you," she said softly.  She heard his footsteps retreat.

Shortly thereafter, she heard soft footsteps approaching.  Surprisingly soft footsteps, considering they belonged to two elves in plate armor.  Galadri knew the sound of those footsteps, and that it meant the guard had sent the ones she needed instead of summoning her to them.  She remained kneeling, waiting for them to speak first.

"What ails you, my child, that you request we meet you here?" a deep voice asked.

Galadri's eyes misted with unexpected tears.  "I..." she began, finding the words more difficult than she had imagined.  "I may have sinned," she said brokenly.

"What is this sin that troubles you so?" the same voice asked. 

That voice belonged to the high priest, she realized.  The general, though present, remained silent.  It was said the two were related, and indeed they did look and sound much alike.  It had taken her this many words to know who had addressed her.

A long pause followed, during which Galadri struggled for composure.  "I believe that... I may have misunderstood the call placed upon my life, and thereby brought dishonor to the Temple," she blurted out somewhat abruptly.  Tears slipped silently down her cheeks as she confessed.

She heard the general shift position, felt a hand on her shoulder.  "You have not dishonored the temple," he assured her.  "You have put your whole heart into learning the skills needful for a paladin.  No more can be asked of anyone, for none can do aught more than his or her best."

"It is no sin to be mistaken," the priest added, "provided you do not persist in the error after learning the truth."

The elfess nodded, trying to accept this absolution.  "What then shall I do?" she asked.

"What do you believe your calling ought to be?"

"I think that mayhap," she began hesitantly, unsure of herself since she'd been mistaken once before, "well, that mayhap I am called to heal instead of fight."  There, it was out.  The decision was theirs; in a manner of speaking, her life rested in their hands now.

"That calling is no less difficult."  The voice was completely neutral.  She wasn't sure which of them had said it.  "Come to the altar, and wait tonight in prayer.  We shall pray with you.  Mayhap ere daybreak illumination will come."

With head bowed, Galadri rose and followed two of the most important elves in creation to the temple's main altar.  There all three knelt, and waited.

She prayed silently but with intense sincerity.  She praised the Holy Maker, for all the wonders bestowed.  She repented and sought forgiveness.  She humbly requested wisdom and understanding. 

Time passed, the candles flickered as they burned low.  Her prayers became more heartfelt and less fully formed as words in her thoughts.  She continued to feel prayerful, yet her thoughts slowly began to merge together and drift...

A strong yet gentle voice echoed softly in her mind.  "My healing power shall flow through you when you pray.  Know that all are my children - be not too proud to heal one who does not acknowledge me.  Condemn deeds where needed; but never condemn the doer.  Let your life be your sermon - example teaches better than preaching.  Serve me faithfully, with your whole heart, and I shall be with you always."

Galadri awoke with a start, feeling guilty for having succumbed to a weakness of the flesh when she had such a great need in her spirit.  She looked quickly to either side and found that both men were looking at her with concern.

"I am to heal," she said, "leaving judgment to others."

"My spirit sings as she says it," the high priest said.  "I confirm the message."

The general nodded quietly.  "I, too, sensed the presence of the Maker while she slept," he said.  "She shall serve as she is called."  He took her sword hand, squeezed it gently, and then laid it in the high priest's hand.  "I shall miss a promising squire," he said in a tone of voice that made it uncertain if he were serious or in jest.  "Yet I believe we shall gain a priestess worthy of the calling."

Again the water stood in her eyes, as Galadri tried to find words to thank them for their patience and support.

Galadri continues to devote herself to prayer and learning, and to healing others. When all are healthy and an opportunity to bludgeon an enemy presents itself, she seldom bypasses it ... she still has a bit of the Paladin in her soul.



 		 

Goblin Mines

"But I am a healer," came the unexpectedly articulate voice from the bear. "I have no need of a healer, I need a fighter." With that the overlarge bear began to turn away.

"I can fight," Galadri replied, the memory of her days as a Paladin suddenly fresh in her heart. "Try me."

The bear looked over its shoulder, considering. Finally it said, "very well. Join me for a little while, and we shall see if you are up to the task."

They were in a mountain keep, where a mine tunnel had gone too near to the goblin warrens. Vicious goblins poured into the mines, so much that the minors had fled for their lives. Miners were not fighters.

So it came to pass that many assorted peoples, from many parts of creation, would come to the mines. Some came merely to test their skills against the goblins. Others, like Galadri, came from compassion for the miners. She wanted to help them, and everyone seemed to mock her instead of welcome her or tell her how best to render aid.

Galadri unslung her shield from her back, and steadied it on her shield arm. She hefted her club - as a priestess, the sword was forbidden her - and she prepared herself to do battle. She glanced uneasily at her new ally, uncertain if this odd bear was truly to be trusted or not.

A goblin appeared through a crack in the corner and raced toward them! Wasting no time, Galadri charged as she once had when she was a Paladin. She swung her club and bashed with her shield ... and had pleasure in seeing that she had not lost all her battle skills.

The bear had a pet wolf, who charged beside Galadri. Together, they defeated the goblin and it seemed that her skills improved even as she fought. When the goblin fell, she brushed a stray strand of red-gold hair from her eyes and looked back at her ally.

"Let's try another," he rumbled, and looked pointedly at the corner again. Galadri turned as another goblin emerged, and joined the wolf in the attack. Club, bash, pray to stun the enemy, club, dodge, club, bash, pray that the enemy be smitten, club, club, dodge, club, bash ...

Again they were victorious, and she recognized this was partly due to the bear's prayer-spells. Galadri had also prayed, knowing that the club would be less damaging than her previous swords had been.

This time the bear only nodded, then turned. "They seem to alternate. When an attack from one side is repulsed, they begin to come from the other."

And so it was. Battle after battle followed, some from one side and others from the other. Then there was a call for help from deeper in the mines. A fighter was being overwhelmed, in a deep room where goblin raiders kept pouring in. Galadri and her new acquaintance went to help. Soon they were joined by one who knew powerful prayers invoking fire and ice against the goblins.  With his help, they had occasional times of peace between battles.

Then the bear was called away, but Galadri and the others were not yet done. Another healer joined, this one a dark elf. "Need makes strange alliances," Galadri reminded herself. She neither knew nor cared overmuch why a dark elf had come, for she was already growing battle weary. Yet Galadri did know that such a one could be a powerful ally - or adversary, depending on their bent.

Words from her calling came back to her mind: "Know that all are my children - be not too proud to heal one who does not acknowledge me.  Condemn deeds where needed; but never condemn the doer."  It was difficult, with this enemy of her people; yet she was determined to be obedient to the commands of the Maker.

Though she always watched carefully as she was able, with the ongoing battles to beat off the goblins, Galadri saw no sign that the dark elf planned betrayal. They hunted there, some coming, others leaving, but always Galadri -- and the Dark Elf -- battled together, for the rest of that season.

Club, bash, club, dodge, club again ... see a companion falter, step back and pray for healing, turn again to the enemy and attack...

Those who were of fighting professions looked askance at both of them. Galadri wondered which amazed the fighters more - that two priests of such opposing views would group together against a common enemy, or that two priests were fighting. For most had never before seen priests fighting alongside a Warrior, Bard, Rogue, Monk or a Paladin. But the fighters were always too few, and the need was too great.

Finally, Galadri's strength waned and she knew that she must withdraw for a time. "Farewell, success and victory," she bid her recent associates, looking at what appeared to be an endless stream of goblins.

"Thank you for fighting with us," one said. Galadri bowed in response. She prayed a last time for their protection, then withdrew as quietly as possible to avoid attracting more enemies in the direction of herself or her recent associates.

She escaped to the keep, and took a room at the inn. Galadri would rest for now, but soon she intended to return and fight goblins for another season or two. After that, who knew?

There would always be a need for a healer somewhere. And, mayhap, there would again be need of a fighting healer. Galadri had every intention of learning all her skills as best she was able. Doing anything less seemed unnatural; in fact, it seemed almost an insult to the designs of her Creator.

Changes

Galadri Leafshimmer walked slowly through the twilight streets of the Elvish capital city, her feet feeling nearly as heavy as her heart.  A drizzle was just lifting as the sunset colors faded from the sky.

 

She was more than just battle-weary; she was heartbroken

 

The priestess had thrown herself into battles and raids, trying desperately to learn and grow stronger.  She had done this in the hope that she would either find the one cure that mattered... or, failing that, to lose herself in forgetfulness. 

 

Neither had happened.

 

Oh, she had gained strength and skills from her constant battling of enemies to the army she had joined.  She gained acquaintances, she learned about a few areas where the enemies were so tricky that one learned fast from battling them.  She learned how to join with other healers to form a chain of healing when a stout warrior was leading the attack against an uncommonly difficult or powerful enemy.

 

But it did not teach her how to heal the afflicted ones who meant the most to her.  Nor did it save her army from dissention.  She had sensed unrest recently, and seen fewer coming to the raids.  She was worried, for she had heard that one leader was ill and knew that the other was still seeking his missing wife... even the third leader seemed too often called away, often unable to rally the troops when need arose.  She hoped the officers would find a solution swiftly, and was dimly aware of a guilty sense of relief that she did not need to bear that burden also.

 

Galadri walked softly into the door of her grandmother's house, the Leafshimmer estate, and hung her leaf-green cloak upon her assigned peg.  Hanging next to it, folds arranged with military precision, were Eowenna's dark blue cloak, Kolya's earth brown cloak and Abree's silvery grey cloak.  All were slightly damp, indicating that the paladin must have taken them out recently.

 

Galadri closed her eyes, willing herself not to weep till she reached her own chamber.  A deep breath or two, and some rapid blinking, enabled her to leave the cloak room and move into the house proper.  Well-paid household servants nodded politely to her as she walked swiftly toward her own room, remembering the day she'd found Eowenna weeping at Kolya's grave.

 

It replayed swiftly in Galadri's memory, the day she had gone to garland his gravestone, and found the human there weeping.  It had taken a bit of healing to help her speak coherently, but it seemed she had only just come from the high priest's house, where she had seen Kolya living. 

 

Eowenna had poured out the tale of how Kolya's body lived, yet his spirit seemed absent, and the high priest had demanded she not tend him alone.  She knew not what arrangements would suit him, and feared she would be incapable of meeting this demand.

 

Galadri had marched her straight back to the high priest's manor, and investigated herself.  Unfortunately, Eowenna had not exaggerated.  There sat Kolya, apparently healthy, yet without any volition or recognition.  Just like her younger sister, Abree.

 

Her pace quickened as she felt the tears threatening again.  Her memories continued unabated... It had been the work of few moments to check with her grandmother and obtain permission for both paladin and afflicted elf to take up residence there as long as might be needed.  The entire household would care for Kolya in Eowenna's absence.  Yet, in truth, the human paladin seldom left his side.

 

Galadri reached her room, swiftly and silently closing her door behind her.  Just as she was about to release her tears, she remembered her manners.  She must clean up, and inform her grandmother that she was home.  It would also be well for her to check the patient and his keeper.

 

Throwing herself into routine, and relying upon that to help her maintain composure, Galadri removed her heavy battle armor and laid it in a chest.  From another chest she withdrew a simple silken gown, and donned that in place of the armor.  She snatched up a hairbrush from her dressing table and unbound her red-gold hair, then brushed it out till it flowed unhindered by tangles.

 

Checking her reflection to make sure she was presentable, Galadri saw that her chin quivered.  No, she thought strictly toward herself, not yet.

 

She checked first on her sister, to find Abree was asleep.  One might almost think naught was amiss with her, to see her sleeping so peacefully.  Sadly,

Galadri knew better.  She kissed her sister's forehead and softly left the room.

 

She paid her respects to her grandmother, embracing her fondly and thanking her for a welcome home.  Aelfiera remained straight and strong, even with the many centuries she had lived.  Aside from some minor stiffness, and the silver threads in her fiery red hair, one might almost mistake her for an elfess of only two or three centuries.  Galadri wondered, fleetingly, if her own destiny would be to weather years equally well.

 

Since the evening meal was already cleared away, Galadri made only a brief token visit to the kitchen to obtain a slice each of bread and cheese.  She ate them daintily as she slowly walked toward the most difficult part of her homecoming.  She swallowed the last bite as she reached the doorway almost directly across the hallway from her own.

 

The priestess tapped gently upon it.  Discovering it unlatched, she pushed it open whispering the Paladin's name.  When she opened the door enough to see inside, she understood why no answer had come.

 

Kolya lay on his bed, resting but with his eyes open.  Eowenna's bed remained made up with her usual military precision nearby.  On the other wall was the table that nearly disappeared under books, parchments, scrolls, and maps. 

 

There, amid the research materials, Eowenna sat slumped over in sleep.  The human wore her sleeping gown, apparently wishing to check yet another scroll before turning in.

 

This was not the first time Galadri had discovered the Paladin in that position. 

 

She walked over to her softly, and stroked her fine silvery brown hair.  The gentle priestess noticed how there were a few silver strands among the pale brown now, strands that had been brown until Kolya's affliction.  She also noted, again, how Eowenna's ear lengthened slightly where an elvish ear would draw up to a point.  Galadri wondered just how much elvish blood flowed through this human's veins as she whispered, "Eowenna?"

 

The paladin startled awake, reaching for her sword.  Then she saw the priestess, and looked embarrassed.  "Pardon, milady," she said.  "How may I serve you?"

 

"You must care for yourself also," Galadri chided gently.  "Rest now; the library shan't reclaim anything before tomorrow."

 

Eowenna nodded, and then looked wistfully toward Kolya.  When she looked back toward the priestess, her eyes spoke for her.

 

"I know," Galadri said.  "Still, you must sleep sometimes.  Making yourself ill won't aid him."

 

The paladin looked down, her shoulders slumping.  After a moment she stood, then moved to Kolya's side.  "Rest well tonight, and heal," she wished him as she gently kissed his forehead.  Then she took three steps to her own bed, pulled back the blankets and sat down.

 

Galadri also went to Kolya's bedside, and kissed his forehead and prayed over him.  She gently stroked his hair away from his face, and prayed again.  "I must rest also," she whispered for Eowenna's sake, and as a way of taking leave.

 

"Of course," the paladin whispered back.  "If you would be so kind as to blow out the candle on your way out, please?"

 

"Certainly," Galadri answered wearily, but without any ill will from the request. 

 

The two had become so united in their concern for Kolya that station and race had become meaningless between them.  Such small, intimate details as this had become normal for them to share when none were about to take offense.

 

Galadri blew out the candle, as Eowenna slipped under the blankets into her own bed.  The priestess crossed the hallway to her own room, and saw that someone had brought her a few pieces of recently delivered mail.

 

She sat at her table and opened them.  Some had no matters of great urgency. 

 

Galadri knew she was only trying to dodge the rain of tears that was welling up inside herself by reading these missives.  For the last she saved two marked as official from the leaders of the army she had joined, and was now on leave from.

 

The first was from the primary leader of the army, and said that the decision had been made to disband the army.  Galadri gasped in astonishment, clapping one hand over her mouth to stifle the sob that she could not contain.  No!  It must be a mistake, surely?

 

She reread the letter twice, thrice, four times... unless her swimming eyes were changing the meanings of the words, she had caught the intent correctly the first time.  The army would march no more.

 

The combined pain of her dear ones' affliction and the loss of her army was too much; Galadri knew she would weep too loudly and disturb others.  She fled to the cloakroom and snatched up her cloak, then dashed out into the streets again.  Her steps took her quickly to the small graveyard, where she hoped she might find release undisturbed.  Reflex or instinct or habit took her to Kolya's grave... where, to her surprise, she found another mourner.

 

A young wood elfess knelt clinging to Kolya's gravestone and sobbing brokenheartedly.  Galadri thought she recognized her, though she could not recall seeing the ranger since taking the oath of priesthood.  She had hunted with her, a little, back during her days as a Paladin.

 

Had the young ranger only just learned of Kolya's... condition?

 

"Legolia?" Galadri called softly.

 

The elfess spun into a crouch, as if ready to attack... though she wielded only a single dagger, and that a rusty one.  After a moment her eyes focused on the priestess' face, and she whispered as if disbelieving, "Galadri?"

 

"Yes," the priestess answered.  She wondered briefly at the rumors that Legolia had joined an unorthodox army, which would certainly have put her out of favor around the elvish capitol if it were known.  "Would they not let you see him?" she inquired.

 

"See him?"  the ranger asked, looking and sounding thoroughly bewildered. 

 

"But the stone says he has lain here these several months..."

 

Oh no, Galadri thought, she doesn't know.  Tears misted her eyes again, as she knelt by Legolia and put an arm around her.  Softly, gently, she explained Kolya's true condition and why the empty grave had remained undisturbed.  The result was predictable.

 

"Please, take me to him?"  Legolia whispered, half whimpering.

 

Galadri nodded, no longer trusting herself to speak.  She stood, offering a hand to the ranger, and then led her to House Leafshimmer.  She hung her cloak in the cloak room, and only then realized that Legolia wore only a leather tunic and leggings.  Holy Maker have mercy, Galadri thought, suddenly understanding fully why the other elfess was shivering.

 

She led Legolia to Kolya's room, lighting a new candle and taking it in with them.

 

The younger elfess saw Kolya and dropped to her knees beside his bed, wrapping her arms around him and clinging to his shoulders as she wept softly.  Galadri cleared a corner on the study table, and then slipped out to bespeak lodgings for Legolia in a nearby room.  That tended, she brought a wrap for the younger elfess so she need not take a chill.

 

The priestess returned to the room softly, noticing that Eowenna had been sufficiently worn out that Legolia's soft weeping had not wakened her.  "Come Lia," Galadri whispered, wrapping the shawl around the ranger's shoulders, "you need rest, even as he does.  I have arranged for you to have a room nearby.  So you will see him again tomorrow, even if Eowenna doesn't take him out."  When the other resisted, she added, "Please, come with me."

 

Galadri understood and appreciated Legolia's reluctance, but knew that Eowenna needed her sleep too.  She retrieved the candle, then gently but firmly steered the ranger out of Kolya's room and into the one prepared for her.  Once there, she saw to it that the other elfess was inclined to stay before taking her leave.

 

Returning to her own room and pulling the door to, she was reminded that there remained an unopened missive marked as official for the disbanded army to which she had belonged.  Galadri went to the table, and lifted it.  This was a small package, heavier than one might expect.  It was from the secondary leader of the army, explaining that he could not remain in the army and watch it dwindle and die; it was too painful for him.  For reasons only he would ever know, he had sent her his own badge and asked she look after it.

 

Galadri could no longer with hold her tears.  They came like a waterfall, flowing down her face as sobs welled up in her throat.  She tried to muffle her sobs by burying her face in her hands, but knew not if she was sufficiently effective.

 

She knew not how long she wept, nor when exhaustion claimed her and she slept.

 

Aelfiera discovered her granddaughter still sobbing in her sleep at daybreak.  The House Matron gently guided the younger priestess from chair to bed, and then tucked her in.
