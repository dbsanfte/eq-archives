The path of the Warrior is chosen ...


Ldari, a Barbarian lass, counted the ways of magic too strange for her. While her own brother seemed to have the gift, Ldari herself had little interest and less skill. Meerc sometimes teased her about her "limited view" of the world, but always gently. They were close, Meerc and Ldari. She didn't want this to change.

Ldari had had no dispute with the rogues, yet she did not find herself inclined to join with them. She lacked the stealth they so highly prized. So she joined the Wolves o' the North, and became a warrior. Her strength and coordination were well suited to this profession.

Halas was her home, nestled in the canyons of Everfrost mountains. Ldari was exceptionally fortunate, for a kind passer-by gave her a full set of patchwork armor almost as soon as she had chosen her profession.

This gift was especially welcome to her, as she had previously owned only minimal indoor clothing and the winter was bitterly cold. Her family was poor, and lacked the means to properly provision her for a life ouside their small town. All the adventuring supplies they'd ever owned were given to Meerc when he set out some seasons past. Except for a rather poor sword, which Meerc's calling had forbidden him to use.

As others had before her, Ldari ventured out into the world armed with her sword and seeking adventure. One hope she cherished was to find her beloved brother Meerc, and share adventures with him.

She quickly learned how easy it is to get lost in the canyons, and thereafter stayed very close to home. The young Shamans loved her - be they lad or lass - for her strength, courage, and skill in weapons. She never lacked for company in her early adventuring days. And she gradually came to respect the skills they had to offer as well.

When Ldari reached the age that required her to venture a bit farther from home, she wandered too far. She was defeated by a cowardly attack from an Ice-boned Skeleton, who was invulnerable to Ldari's poor rusty sword. Ldari knew she could never retrieve her meager posessions alone, so she looked around for anyone who might have the wisdom and strength to aid her. She had only seven seasons of hunting experience at that time.

Ldari checked to see who was in the area, and received word of a wood-elf druid who had been adventuring for nearly 20 seasons. She turned toward the canyon, wondering where she might find him or if she even could, or if he would be willing to aid her. To her surprise she saw a wood-elf standing beside her. A polite inquiry informed her that he was the very druid she sought.

She humbly begged Kolya for his assistance, which he graciously granted. He made her invisible, caused her to float, and led her to her posessions. She was thrilled and relieved, after retrieving her belongings she felt more like herself again. She offered many thanks, and offered to do him any service she could. Of course Kolya snickered, for one of his exalted level hardly needed anything from anyone so young as Ldari. He did offer to sharpen her sword, which offer she gladly accepted.

Kolya, the elfin druid, was closely bonded to two barbarians: a shaman and a rogue. The shaman turned out to be her brother Meerc, who was now sufficiently skilled that he could wear his family name with pride.

The rogue, however, had limited skills of approximately Ldari's own level. Kolya summoned the rogue, and together they hunted around Everfrost. The young rogue taught Ldari where the maps were hidden, which helped her tremendously in her quest to avoid getting lost.

In following days, the rogue seldom appeared. However, Ldari's beloved brother Meerc, now a renowned Shaman, visited often and extended his protection over Ldari so she could hunt in safety. With the help of his magical skills, Ldari grew speedily in strength, wisdom and skill. It was not terribly long before she approached a skill in arms nearly equivalent to her brother's skills in healing and magic.

Oddly enough, the first time she hunted with her brother as an equal, both were defeated. They were in a desert region, near what was reputedly the only oasis. They had followed rumors of a camp of marauding orcs, and had gone thinking they could cleanse the land of this scourge.

Sadly, their first effort sent them back to freeport in defeat ... stripped of their posessions. Though humbled by this setback, they returned more determined than previously. Meerc was able to hide them from the orcs' sight, and thereby enable them to recover their posessions. Armed and equipped for battle once again, they approached more cautiously.

One at a time, they picked off their enemies - without repeating their former mistake of underestimating them. The two gained the support of a few others, and forged a brief alliance which enabled them to cleanse the camp quite thoroughly. When darkness fell, and there was a rumor of more orcs coming, the brother and sister withdrew to a safer area. They were weary after their day of battle, and needed rest. They would fight again another day.

Meerc and Ldari can still be found about Norrath, or sometimes visiting Luclin or the Plane of Knowledge. Both have reached and passed into their third decade of skills. Both continue adventuring, when Meerc is not tending to his second love of brewing. He has gained grandmastery in that skill, and Ldari is proud of her brother's achievement ... though, truth be told, she sometimes wishes he'd brew less and hunt more.

Ldari is seldom seen hunting away from her brother's side, for she is slow to trust strangers. But she is reputed to be a loyal and courageous warrior by the few who know her. As her skills increase, Ldari hopes to win greater renown for her brother, herself, and their people.



 

A Tragedy Strikes ...


A tall Barbarienne warrior, with deep golden tresses wafting freely in the soft breeze, turns her troubled blue eyes toward the door of the council chamber yet again. She knows that the Council are meeting within, and she is not so ill-mannered as to intrude.

She folds her plate-clad arms across her crafted breastplate, looking down toward her plate-shod feet. Her weapons are sheathed at her sides, peace knots tied.

"Can they aid me?" she whispers, a question that has the sound of being oft repeated. "Or will they even have me, that I might aid them?"

A wave of dizziness assails her, and she sits on a nearby rock. Waiting for the weakness to pass, her thoughts wander.

Ldari thinks back on her own history, a child raised by grandparents with those she'd believed were her natural siblings. Only recently had she been taken aside and informed that they were, in fact, her cousins. Third cousins, to be precise ... all of her natural family, though of the same clan as those who raised her, had perished or been lost.

She had run to Meerc, her eldest "brother" ... and the one to whom she'd thought she had the closest tie. She begged of him to confirm the truth of the matter.

Meerc admitted the truth of what she'd just learned, saying she was such a wee small thing when she'd been brought in, they'd not the heart to make her any less part of the family than to be known as their own.

With tears flowing down her face, she'd asked how long he'd laughed at her for believing herself his natural sister ... when in truth she had no such claim?

Meerc had instantly gathered her into his arms. "Sweet Ldari," he'd said softly, after kissing her forehead, "I came to love ye as a sister when we were children. Now, as we are not such near kin, we can be family in another way ... if ye are so inclined."

It had taken her some few moments for the signifigance of his words to sink in. She had pulled back in the embrace just enough to see his eyes ... to be certain he wasn't teasing her, as he had done so many times in their youth.

Unless she was imagining it, Meerc was in earnest. She clung to him anew, weeping in relief and gratitude as she tried to express her thanks and joy that he would choose her over others he'd known.

Ldari blinked back tears from the memory. They had married quietly, later that same week, and made their home in a small hut near Halas. After too short a time, their advance stock of provisions had run out. Meerc had kissed her and said he'd return soon.

Then tragedy had struck. Ldari heard Meerc call her name in her mind, and then his thoughts had gone silent.

Ldari had armed herself and run out to Everfrost to find her beloved Meerc. She knew where he normally hunted, so was not long in discovering his breathless form on the frozen river just at the bottom of a slippery bank. He lay at an awkward angle, she remembered ...

She had lifted the large man and carried him to Qeynos, traveling all night. The priests there had driven her from the temple and performed a ceremony lasting almost two days, after which time Ldari had finally been permitted in to see Meerc. He lay quietly on a pallet the Priests of Life had provided. He was breathing, the hue of life had returned to his flesh.

But his spirit was not restored.

Ldari blushed to recall her rage at this apparent mockery of resurrection ... and the Priests of Life had apologized profusely, not understanding themselves why Meerc's restoration had not worked properly.

Ldari had taken Meerc, beloved as both brother and husband, back to their humble hut. She cared for him as she might a newborn, feeding him, dressing him and cleaning him. She went to Halas and Qeynos, or hunted in Everfrost, to provide for them.

Ever since Meerc's ... accident, however, Ldari was constantly tired. It seemed she kept needing more and more rest. She assumed that her pain and fears regarding her beloved Meerc were the cause, and did her best to ignore the signs of weakness. Surely, in time, they would pass.

Her brief marriage began to feel more and more like a pleasant dream from which she had wakened too soon. She fell easily back into the habit of thinking of Meerc as her brother, the way he had been for most of her life.

Then she had heard a whisper on the wind, that there was a destruction of life coming to Norrath. A Council sought to stem the tide, and preserve those they could.

Mayhap, she'd thought, what befell Meerc is a part of this? It was but a short transition from thought to deed ... if they would have her, and her emptied brother, Ldari would join their cause to resist this destruction of life.

She knew her hope that Meerc would return to himself was likely futile, but believed that no others were as likely to aid that hope as these folk.

She returned to her pacing, quietly, wondering how long it would be ere any would emerge from the Council chambers to hear her offer to join them.
