"Curse them, we're too late!" muttered the leader of the expedition.

"Indeed," responded another, the sound coming through clenched teeth.

Their horses stomped and tossed their heads, not liking the stench of the smoke still rising from the decimated village. There were perhaps 15 riders, all told. Each was wearing dark green clothes and leathers stained in dark browns. With minimal effort, they could melt into a forest setting nearly unseen.

Here, where the ground had been cleared and burned, they stood out plain as rocky islands in the sea.

Lia stayed still and silent. She was huddled deep in the bushes edging the smouldering rubble that had been her home village. Her lavendar eyes watched them closely, her delicate pointed ears catching every word they uttered, every sound from the horses.

Most of her clothes had been stained and tattered into dirty rags. Lia's only recognizable garment was the tattered, adult-sized, dark green cloak wrapped around her entire body. Beneath it, her fair skin was completely coated in smoke and grime. The filth nearly covered her incompletely healed scrapes, bruises and burns. Her hair, normally a medium golden brown, appeared much darker from the soot clinging to it.

She scarcely looked like the very young elf child she was. Instead, being so small and grime-coated, she bore more resemblance to the dark brownies that plagued the region.

Lia waited and watched. She didn't know yet if these elves were safe.

This last week had nearly consumed the young child. Her memories of the peaceful existance prior to the destruction of her home village were fading rapidly, as she turned increasingly feral in order to survive.

She recalled her name, Legolia Greenleaf. She knew the Elvish tongue, which these strangers spoke. She knew this smoking ruin should be her home. She wanted to believe it was all a bad dream, but she didn't know how to wake from it.

Vague impressions remained of a finely crafted archer's bow, prominently displayed. An elvish woman, with hair of the deepest gold, pale and trembling weakly while leaning on the case. Her father's deep baritone rumble, saying, "Fear not, my little Lia. Your mother is weary from delivering your brother, but she will be fine in a day or two. She just needs to rest now."

The sound of many footsteps, as most villagers gathered in the streets. Lia closing a window over her mother's bed, to protect her. "She needs rest now," Lia had whispered, repeating her father's words.

There had been a low rumble of conversation, but Lia had paid it no heed. Father would be home from hunting soon, he would explain everything then.

Suddenly it had gone quiet, too quiet ... then there was a shout followed swiftly by a loud, rhythmic pounding on the main gate.

Lia had wakened her mother then, when she became afraid that some danger was coming. Her mother had listened, then motioned for silence. She picked up the tiny baby, and Lia had brought her mother's walking stick.

"You remember where we hang our washed clothes to dry?" Her mother had asked in a soft whisper. "The little wall-door?" Lia had nodded, too frightened to speak. "We will go out there, and hide in the forest. This may be nothing, but ... " Her mother had begun walking, gesturing for Lia to follow.

They had tried to sneak out of the village, but her mother had tripped, and cried out, just as there was a terrible crash from the main gate. "Run Legolia," she whispered urgently, still cradling the newborn in her arms, "fast as the wind. Go out the wall-door, and hide nearby. I will find you."

Lia had run to the wall-door, a gate carefully made to look like part of the wall. Even the latch was a curious matter. Legolia had reached up and opened it unconsciously, her fingers knowing the latch of their own accord. So many times her mother's arms had been too full to operate the latch, that Lia had unlatched it for her. Even though she could barely reach the latch that was waist-height to an adult elf, she knew it reflexively.

Closing it softly behind her (also from ingrained habit), Lia had scurried to the familiar embrace of the forest. She had played here so many times while her mother hung the wash on the friendly branches.

But there was no playing today. Lia knew how far to go into the forest, so she wouldn't be easily found. She'd played hide-and-seek here enough times to know the needed distance by instinct. She went to her favorite hiding place, where only her mother had ever found her.

Lia's hiding place was just deep enough into the forest that she had been unable to see or hear all of what happened to her home. There were the harsh barks of masculine voices shouting in an unknown tongue. There was the sound of stone and wood cracking.

She'd heard a scream, but the inarticulate wail was cut off so quickly that it could scarcely be recognized as a woman's, let alone whose. It didn't occur to Lia that it might have been her mother. Not long after the swiftly silenced scream, the flames had begun.

Lia rushed toward her home even as the pillagers had mounted up and had begun to ride away. They missed seeing or hearing her, in the sounds of the greedy flames claiming what had been a peaceful village only an hour before.

She had seen her father return, swathed in his hunting clothes. He wrapped his cloak around her, and told her to wait there. He would attempt to learn what had happened.

That was 8 suns ago, and her father had not returned. Nor had her mother or the other villagers been seen. Only these strange elves that came with the sunrise.

"It's unlikely that anyone survived that," the leader said sorrowfully. "But we should look anyhow. Maybe some escaped before their enemy got in."

He gave a hand signal, and all dismounted. Each was tense and alert, as if they expected to be attacked by whatever force had destroyed the moderately-sized village.

Ash was plentiful from the village to the bush that was young Legolia's current refuge. As the riders walked about, seeking signs, they stirred up small clouds of ash. Lia felt the itch and tickle growing in her nose and throat. She pinched her nose shut, to prevent making the sound of a sneeze.

But the treacherous tickle kept growing in her throat as the light tread of the elvish horsemen probed the edges of the ruin. They were hoping to find signs of what befell the villagers, they had scarcely dared hope to find a survivor.

Lia's body refused to be denied release. The small sound, quickly muffled, snapped two arrows to bowstrings as the two nearest swung her direction. Fortunately, they did not fire.

"Sir!" one shouted. The leader, distinguishable only by the finer fabrics and embroidery on his clothing, hurried over as one of her discoverers began reaching for her. "Worry not, small one, we mean you no harm."

Lia struggled, protesting "No! Mother said I must wait here, she will find me! And Father went to find her, he will be back soon!" Her father's hood fell back, revealing her singed hair and delicate but sharply pointed ears.

One of the men abruptly dropped his bow and turned his back, covering his face with his hand. His shoulders shook.

Her benevolent captor soothed little Lia, calming her with tone of voice and gentle words. Then he asked, "Have you looked for her, small one? How many suns have crossed the sky since the fire began?"

Lia's bedraggled clothing, and the burns on her hands and feet mostly answered his question. "Eight suns, and just because I couldn't find her doesn't mean she won't find me! My mother never lies!" But she was beginning to sob, for she feared what he was suggesting might be true.

"So the rumors were true. There were elves here." The leader spoke flatly. "If only we'd learned of this village sooner! But alas, it seems knowledge often comes too late." He heaved a great sigh, and was silent.

"What would you have us do?" asked Lia's gentle captor.

"Search for further signs. If we find no others, we will take her the long journey back to Kelethin with us. 'Twould be beyond cruel to leave her here alone," he decided. "She cannot be more than 6 summers old."

Many murmurs of agreement answered their leader's words.

They searched for two days. Although the only body found in the village was that of a newborn infant, they found no sign of the other villagers. So when they abandoned their search, they bundled up young Legolia and took her back to Kelethin with them.

==============(10 years later)

Legolia Greenleaf tended to be shy. Her instinctive love of life, and equally instinctive hatred of the elf-slaughtering orcs, drew her down from the treetops of her adopted hometown Kelethin. She tried to learn the ways of the Greater Faydark on her own, to avoid troubling anyone. But she needed their assistance, and finally began asking for advice or aid when she was lost.

She always found herself drawn to the Ranger guild when she needed instruction. One day, the honored guild leader took Lia aside. "I see you coming often, with questions for my trainers. I have watched you in the forest, and observed your woodcraft. With proper training, you could become a fine Ranger. Is this your wish?"

Lia was surprised, but had been around Mr. Trueshot enough to know he favored a direct approach. She simply hadn't anticipated he'd want or accept her into his Rangers. Lia, the child all claimed but none wanted. Lia, the lost girl not of Kelethin. Lia, the maid with no family.

"Thank you sir, for your kind words," Lia replied formally. "If you think I can become a Ranger fit for your guild, then I would be honored to accept any instruction you would offer."

===============(a few months later)

Her training was taking less time than most, Lia overheard her teachers commenting to each other one day. To them, she appeared to be remembering instead of learning. They pondered what manner of people would train a child so young as she had been when they found her.

Lia realized they thought her a safe distance away. She could not recall when she had realized that her hearing was more sensitive than was normal for Wood Elves. Her unknown history was deeply troublesome to her at times. Once again, as she had done so many times in the past, she pushed aside that which she could not solve through pondering. Until more was learned, her original home would remain an unsolved mystery.

One teacher approached now, and she looked up as if newly aware of her approach. "We have taught you enough that you should be able to survive if you hunt near our town. At this point in your training, the best way for you to continue learning is through actual hunting and combat." Lia had heard this said to others, and knew what it meant.

Each apprentice must prove himself or herself in the forest, before advanced training would be offered. It was time for her to be tested. She accepted thankfully the guild tunic, and the short sword. She proceeded to the nearest elevator and descended to the forest floor.

Lia found the ground level to be disorienting. She had difficulty keeping track of her sense of direction. She knew she could run to the guards, who were much more seasoned than she was, if she stumbled into a group of enemies she couldn't defeat. But when she lost track of where the guards were ... that could be deadly.

Lia was still shy, always disliking imposing upon others. Yet she quickly discovered that she lacked the prowess to travel far alone. She would watch for others who shouted an interest in grouping together for hunting, then she would humbly offer her services.

She primarily offered her minor assistance to other elves. This was partly because her natural shyness made her feel more comfortable around those who spoke her native toungue.

Most often, if they knew her not, the ones forming into groups did not choose her to accompany them. She seemed too delicate, to the untrained eye, to be an effective huntress.

Lia kept her golden-brown hair tied back, revealing her delicate yet sharply pointed ears. Her lavendar eyes always seemed gentle, unless she saw an enemy. She was a bit fairer skinned than the wood elves of Kelethin, and ran slightly more slender. Some had speculated that there had been intermarriage with a high elf in Lia's ancestry, but she had no way to know if there were any truth in that notion.

Since her delicate appearance caused some hunting groups to shun her, Lia did the best she could alone.

============================

One day she was accepted into a group. A wood elf druid, named Kolya, was foraging and said another pair of hands would be useful. He was far more skilled than she, but still accepted her company. They spent a few days together harvesting the roots, berries, herbs and the occasional cinnamon sticks. At the end of that time, and very much to Lia's surprise, Kolya chose to adopt her as a younger sister.

Lia bashfully accepted this countryman's aid, and his instructions, and began to grow in strength and skill. He took her to other places, there to hunt evil beings or dangerous animals, and she learned and grew more. Lia was so pleased to finally have someone who was "family." The world seemed less lonely simply from knowing he was somewhere in it.

She met another druid, about her own level. His name was Sashe. Lia hunted with him and his friend bard for a season or two, then the bard moved on. A few days later, Lia met yet another wood-elf druid -- this one a lady.

That lady druid was Aundra Windhover, who was forming an Alliance to fight against evil. Lia considered the invitation she was given, and chose to accept. Suddenly she belonged to two guilds - one, her home guild of Rangers; the other this guild of allies to hunt with.

Her primary disappointment was that her adopted brother had chosen to join a different secondary guild. He told her it was ok, he'd stay in touch.

Through her new guild of allies, Legolia met Sashe's twin brother Arundel.

Arundel, a noble Ranger with much wisdom, was only a few seasons beyond her own skill. Lia and Arundel hunted together a great deal over much time. Even when circumstances forced them to hunt separately, they continued communicating through the curious means that Rangers have to keep in touch with one another.

Then, rather abruptly, the missives from Arundel quit arriving. It was especially odd that Arun had not even transmitted any messages through her adopted brother Kolya, which he had done frequently in the past. Lia was not especially concerned at first, since he'd disappeared before but always returned later.

But as the days turned into weeks and the weeks grew into months, her concerns deepened and grew ...

It is also possible that he was killed as a follower of Tunare in sacrifice to one of their dark gods. "It appears I may have been spotted by an Ogre patrol this morning, as they have abandoned their usual patrol route and seem to have found my trail. How this is possible, I do not know. Come nightfall, I will take to the trees and circle back to get a better look."

"There is a dark elf among them! This explains how my trail was discovered. He is using the dark vision to see through my trackless step illusion. I will need to take him out if I wish to end this game of cat and mouse. If he is killed the skinbags will pause to select a new leader, and during their squabbling I will shall make good my escape."

"Blast! Another patrol is approaching from behind me this morning to join with this one and I am stuck in the middle. Though I remain undetectable in my element, I cannot chance moving for fear that the dark elf may detect my presence."

When Lia received Arundel's journal ...
... she was shocked and amazed. She sat, stunned, clutching the weathered tome and weeping softly.

Lia had studied and trained hard, hoping to prove herself a fit partner to hunt (and sometimes adventure) with the noble Arundel. There was so much she could learn from him ... and mayhap even a few things she could teach him.

He had spoken often of faraway places he'd found maps for, and wished to explore. He'd invited her to accompany him to some of them, but left it as a "someday" instead of setting any specific time.

It had never entered her wildest imaginings that he could, so abruptly, be gone.

Must it be forever?

Lia had previously worried that Arundel might have ventured into dangerous territory, and left her behind in a kind but misguided effort to protect her. Alas, if only she had been there! Mayhap he would not have been taken? They could have alternated watches, confused the trail more thoroughly by using greater diversions, two may have found ways to forage along the way ... Oh why had he gone off alone?

Arun had always been so very resourceful, Lia could not find it within herself to believe him utterly defeated. While life remained, there was hope of escape ... though Arundel was also aware of the habits and practices among the Dark Elves. It was possible he would not permit himself to be taken alive. Still, his last words were of weariness and resting ... and his pursuers would surely want such a prize captured alive.

Lia shuddered at the thoughts of rumors she had heard about what Dark Elves did to prisoners. She knew they would try to break him first ... and Arundel was a very strong personality. In that there was hope.

Yet, strong as he was, he was a living being. He so cherished his freedom, he so loved the sunlight, she feared the very confinement in their dark caverns might be his undoing.

Lia also knew that they would not be content merely to confine him. Indeed, hard labor in their mines was not uncommon as a daytime activity. Though the rumors of their night time activities did not bear contemplation. Not for Arundel!

Even knowing this quest may be beyond her abilities, Lia could not forsake her friend, mentor and companion! Was there any other she could trust to aid her?

Being alone is the way Arundel was caught. Going alone, herself, would be to risk the same fate.

This matter would take much serious thought and prayer. In all probability, it could require more training. But if he lived, time was short. She dare not delay overlong ...

* * * *

As Lia begins to overcome the shock of Arundel's capture, she continues pondering Arundel's probable fate. After a time, she comes to a minor decision. She will not waste time searching among the Ogres.

Since it took a Dark Elf Shadowknight to overcome Arun, the likelihood is that vile creature claimed the prize for himself and his dark brood. He would certainly have taken Arundel to a Dark Elf stronghold. The only question remaining was "Which Dark Elf stronghold?"

There were rumors of Shadowknights and Necromancers deep within the Gnollish lair of Splitpaw. That would, perhaps, be the best place for her to search first. Hunting gnolls would also be a good way for her to gain some of the skills she would need as she searched farther.

She would probably check the ruins of Old Guk next. The most likely route for the Dark Elf villain to take, in carrying off Arundel, would be: through Innothule Swamp, the Ro desert, through the tunnel to Commonlands, and through Nek Forest to his own people.

If Arundel managed to escape early, he may have fled to the Froglok ruins. The labyrinthine passages would be adequate hiding, especially since the frogs hate the Dark Elf kind passionately. Frogs have no love for Ogres either, so both halves of that foul alliance would be equally unable to pursue him there.

Lia would search there for a season or two, hunting crocodiles as she searched among the areas where the live Frogloks held sway. She reckoned that, in his weakened condition, Arundel would not chance the deeper ruins where the vile Undead haunted the passageways. Though she planned to check there at least once before searching elsewhere.

She would check briefly in Befallen, in case the foul Shadowknight attempted to hide him there. But somehow her instincts made her feel that she was unlikely to find him in that old haunted monastery.

She would search through Najena's stronghold, as that was a more likely place than Befallen. There were plenty of dark caverns there where they might attempt to break the noble Ranger. She hoped not, but realized it was a likely location to find him. Another place to search, if he was not found in Najena's keeping, would be Mistmoore Castle.

If she did not find him there, she would continue hunting. She knew it was slightly possible he might be taken elsewhere, and she worried what may happen to him if she misguessed and searched in the wrong place.

Arundel had already been missing for months. She must not delay any longer. Splitpaw and Guk she could probably search with very little aid. Though she hoped to find him there, she realized it may be only a futile hope.

Nonetheless, she set her gear in order and began her journey. Perhaps along the way she would find a fit companion to help her in her search. Maybe she would go through a series of companions, each helping her for a season or two, until she found her lost friend.

But she would not stop until she either rescued Arundel from the clutches of his captors ... or found absolute proof of his untimely demise. Legolia fervently hoped she would find him living.

Doubts assailed her nearly every step of the way. Arundel would not have been treated well ... she might find him weak as a newborn babe. After being imprisoned so long, without opportunity to practice his woodcraft, he may have far less skill than formerly.

But none of these things deterred her. If Arundel must begin again, Legolia would aid him in any way needful to get him back on his feet. She would assist him in any retraining he needed. Lia would even help him find more weapons and armor, if his own were lost or taken.

Though Lia would certainly discourage any solo hunting against Dark Elves anytime soon ...

Legolia was touched to learn that one of Arundel's last thoughts, before his capture, had been of her. She hoped Arundel would know that he could count on her to seek him out! Almost as if he might hear her, Lia whispered, "Hold on Arundel! I'm coming ... "

* * * *

Lia searched many places, finding no traces of Arundel. But there were rumors ...

Many rumors about a piece of bark found near the Feerott druid ring, that had been taken to the Ranger's Guildmaster in Kelethin. But when Lia went to the Guidmaster, who had governed both her own and Arundel's earliest training, he would say nothing.

Lia had previously heard rumors of strange portals. Not the usual type, cast by Druids and Wizards, that lead to other places in Norrath. But unusual portals that lead to other worlds outside Norrath. Many of these were said to be one-way portals, and some only appeared for a short time ... thereafter to disappear forever.

One who had returned from such a journey described a place where it seemed the Gnomes had taken over - all was mechanical, like clockworks, including the distance weapons. Instead of bows, they carried mechanical tubes that belched fire or energy.

From the beginning of her search, Legolia encountered rumors that Arundel had departed to one of those other worlds. She had initially discounted them entirely, since Arundel too wise to wander near such a portal. But she began to reconsider. If Arun were being pursued and was weakened, and in fear of his life ...

Lia had searched for five seasons, finding no sign of either Ranger Arundel or his twin (the Druid Sashe Ravenstorm). There was only the written word of Arundel's flight, and the strange rumors of the clockwork world where he'd been seen.

If no further sign had come after this much time, it seemed likely that the strange portal rumors must have had more truth in them than the possibility of Arundel's capture. And after so much time, that trail would be very cold. Lia wasn't sure she could adapt to such a strange world, even if she could find a portal to it that still functioned.

Given the choice between adapting to another world he might not be able to leave, and accepting capture by the evil races ... Lia guessed it possible that Arundel may have fled through such a portal if he saw one. If, as appeared increasingly likely, he had abandoned Norrath for another world ... further searching would be equally fruitless.

The leaves and the trees no longer speak Arundel's name. The wind bears no tidings of his movements. The water and the land bore neither his scent or the marks of his passage. It was as though Arundel had vanished from Norrath all together.

Legolia would mourn her lost friend for a long time.

Lia joins a new army
Lia spent many seasons in her adopted home forest of Greater Faydark.

She would bless young elves with toughened skin, or with the ability to run with the speed of a wolf. She foraged while wandering about aimlessly, or double checked that she still knew how to track prey.

Several seasons passed in this manner. Others she had known continued to gain strength and skill, while Lia mourned her lost friend. Kolya, her adopted brother, was wandering far and wide seeking pelts to improve his tailoring skills. Or other times, he was foraging for the hidden ingredients his Barbarian friend needed to brew fabulous magical drinks that empowered people to greater defenses against attacks, to greater strength and stamina, or to greater levels of knowledge.

She exchanged few messages with him, he seemed too busy for her now. As had seemed to be the way of most of her life, Lia was alone again.

Those who had been her peers were now her seniors, to all appearances, because their skills had continued to grow while Legolia remained mourning in Greater Faydark. The forest was the last place she'd seen Arundel or Sashe alive, and staying there was partly to be nearer to memories of them ... partly foolishly hoping against hope that one might someday return or some word be learned concerning their fate.

Yet the seasons had passed, one after the other, and she remained at only the 34th level of skill. Until one of the senior Rangers of Kelethin had taken her aside this morning.

"The tale of your life should not end thus," he had told her. "Arundel would not want you to waste your days mourning him. He would have wanted you to grow to your full potential."

Lia had wanted to dispute this, but remembered well all the many times Arundel had forced her to stretch her own limits, so that she could be all that nature had intended she become. She nodded reluctantly, to indicate her agreement with his statement.

"We have all been worried about you Lia," he said gently, "and we believe that one thing lacking in your recent days has been a purpose. While we may not be able to fully restore your desire to live fully and grow, we can at least assign you a duty."

Lia forgot to keep her lavendar eyes respectfully downcast when he said this. She was surprised by the statement, enough that she looked up to search his face. Was he implying that she'd failed in her duty?

"There is trouble in the region of Freeport. Several Rangers of Karana have been traveling from Surefall Glades toward Freeport, with 3 wagons of supplies and reinforcements. It is our wish that you go with one of our druids to Rivervale, and meet up with the caravan and see it the remainder of the journey to Freeport."

This sounded simple enough, and she understood the implied message also: he wanted a Kelethin Ranger to view for the guild precisely what was afoot in that region. Humans could, after all, be trusted only so far. They generally meant well, but their limited senses caused them to miss things that may be extremely important. She wondered if the halflings would be joining, or only a waystation for the humans.

Legolia bowed to her superior, accepting both the spoken and the implied task. "I will go," she said simply.

He nodded, and she left to fill a pack with what she'd need for the journey. Once packed, Legolia found a druid porting to Rivervale. They shimmered out of existance from the Kelethin platforms, and re-coalesced at the druid ring near the home town of the halflings.

The three wagons had arrived there a day sooner than anticipated, so they were preparing to leave town about the time Legolia arrived.

She was directed to join the reinforcement fighters in the third wagon. But as she approached it, she was filled with a nameless dread. She tried to convince herself she was being silly, that her discomfort at being among strangers so far from home was playing tricks upon her imagination.

But she couldn't shake the sense that something wasn't right, that there was a danger. It seemed worse whenever she aproached the group of humans. So Lia sought the caravan leader again, and requested permission to sit in one of the supply wagons instead.

He sneered at her, and directed her to the center wagon. Lia nodded, and climbed in. The wagons ambled out of Rivervale, through the pass out of the last of the high cliffs, and into the beginning of Kithicor Forest just as dusk was settling over the land ...

* * * *

Legolia knelt by the cot of the sleeping druid, pondering the events of the evening. She felt confused, with so much to absorb all at once. Lia looked at ... what was it they said his name was? Oh, Treybor.

The young druid was pale, his breathing somewhat shallow. He had spent himself summoning magicks beyond him. Mayhap not much beyond him, but beyond him none the less.

The magicks he'd summoned had burned many of the undeads where they ... was "stood" the correct word? They hadn't exactly been sitting, but they weren't really standing straight either. Lia shrugged, the precise word she wanted eluding her. No matter. She focused her gaze again on the prone wood elf, and made a minor adjustment to the thin blanket covering him from chest to toes.

This Treybor reminded her somewhat of her absent adopted brother. Oh, if only Kolya were here! He could help her figure out all these confusing things, and help her know what to do. Lia hoped she was doing the right thing.

Against all reason, the caravan of wagons she was assigned to help guard had departed Rivervale near sunset. They were ambushed in Kithicor Forest, appearantly by undead minions sent by General Avimoth's uncle.

Yet at the same time, it seemed that the "skin riders" had already claimed most or all other individuals who were a part of the caravan. The Thexians had slaughtered them all, claiming they had only destroyed reanimated corpses.

Lia had been so frightened, alone facing enemies and possible enemies on every side. The dark Commander, two shadowknights, another a wielder of blades ... a rogue, perhaps?

And that last one, the one who kept grinning at her as though he were enjoying some private joke ... that one frightened her the most. He emanated power, in ways she had seldom seen before. He bore only blunt weapoons, so it seemed likely he must be some form of clergy.

Then the undeads had begun rising from the ground. Lia had drawn her swords, only to hear the general's warning that her blades would be useless. Mayhap, she'd thought to herself, yet I shall not be overcome without dealing a single blow!

Scarcely had the thought crossed her mind, when a dagger embedded its tip into the wood by her leg. The Thexian commander had thrown it, then whirled to confront enemies nearer to himself. Disinclined to trust the stranger so quickly, Lia had turned toward the abomination on her other side. She attacked, with a shouted prayer to the Maker for aid and protection.

She'd been knocked backwards shortly after, catching herself with one hand near the dark elf's dagger. Lia had hacked and slashed at the vile creature she faced, but in truth her onslaught had seemed to be without effect. She quickly sheathed her offhand sword, and gripped the dagger.

And then the wagon she sat upon had been severely jostled, and Lia had tumbled to the ground. She'd landed with only minor injury - hitting her head on a rock, just enough to knock her senseless for a few minutes. She'd awakened to find the Druid nearby, and undeads on every side.

Some instinct or reflex had kept her weapons in her hands, and she laid about herself as best she could from her seated position. Suddenly, by chance or skill, Lia had achieved a decisive blow: she cut the back of the creature's knee with the magical dagger from the dark elven commander.

That caused the abomination to fall enough that she could finish it, again using the commander's dagger.

To Legolia's immense surprise and relief, she was mostly unharmed. Just a few scrapes and bruises, and most of those from falling off the wagon.

Others had not escaped so undamaged. It was about this time that the wood elf druid had collapsed in front of her.

Lia had quickly sheathed her primary sword, and absently cleaned the magical dagger on the grass before thrusting it between her body and her weapons belt. The belt fit snugly enough over her ivy armor that the slim dagger was held in place.

She shifted from her posterior to her knees, pulling bandages from her pack but finding no injuries on him. She had begun to draw weapons again, when the dark ones aproached him. But Treybor had forbidden her, saying these dark elves were his trusted friends.

Reluctantly, Lia had obeyed his stated wish to permit these dark elves to tend him. Yet she felt responsible for him, and refused to leave his side through the journey to the Thexian's camp.

Avimoth had spoken at length of their ways, and generously offered her a place in his army. Lia was uncertain if this were the best course, but she feared being sent away if she did not join. She had looked at the crest she wore, and hesitated. Would they shun her, if she ceased to be a member of their army? Would they feel betrayed? She hoped not, for she counted many there as being her friends. Yet, in the end, Lia could not forsake this other wood elf. She feared for his life if he remained alone among these dark ones.

She hoped her fears were groundless, but Lia could not trust the dark ones. Not as it seemed that Treybor did. At least not yet.

How on Norrath did a Tunarian Druid come to keep company with Dark elves? She tried to remember all that Avimoth had told her, but her recollections were growing fuzzy from exhaustion.

Lia never realized when exhaustion overcame her. Her head slowly dropped onto her arms, on the side of Treybor's cot, and she was lost in uneasy and restless dreams.

* * * * *

Lia had been in the camp with the Thexians for many suns now, yet she remained as jumpy as a plump rabbit near a stewpot.

Uncertain if she should consider herself a member, a prisoner, or a guest, she tried to be as unobtrusive as possible. When hungry or thirsty, she would forage around the camp. Legolia frequently cast her eye in the direction of Treybor's tent, and kept her ears tuned for the cry of distress she kept expecting would come.

She was restless, nervous, uncertain ... half inclined to bolt, make a run for the nearest dock and take ship anyhwere. Yet if their claimes were true ... Lia tried to wait, to watch and learn. The next battle would most likely prove their words true or false.

By instinct, somewhat reflexively, Lia foraged as she paced. She looked down and saw berries in her hand. She was a little hungry, and they appeared ripe. Still distracted by the thoughts and concerns that troubled her, she abently ate them without further scrutiny.

She snared a brace of rabbits, found some leafy vegetables growing among the grasses, and dutifully took them to the camp cook. After bowing to the cook and leaving, she saw the berry juice on her hand and realized it was giving off a peculiar odor. Had the berries been overly ripe?

She raised her hand to her face, to smell better. Oh no, those were medicinal berries. Foolish, Lia thought to herself. How very foolish ...

Legolia realized she hadn't eaten enough to die from an overdose, but she was going to be very ill for a little while. The first wave of dizziness came, and she searched about frantically for a suitably quiet corner that would be out of the weather till the fit passed.

She found one, in a supply tent behind bundles of animal fodder. There were some spare soft goods, that she used for pillow and blanket. Then she sank into a restless unconsciousness troubled by dreams of delirium ...

She awoke just past midday, drenched with sweat but whole. Lia estimated it should be 2 or three days since she ate the berries. She had a terrible headache, and was still mildly dizzy and feeling weak.

Slowly an awareness pierced her misery. The camp lacked its usual bustle. What had happened here? She staggered toward the sanitary facilities, then got herself cleaned up a little. Still this uncanny silence.

Legolia looked in Treybor's tent, he appeared to be worse than he had been before she was incapacitated. Oh no, Lia fretted, what have they done to him?

She staggered away, still weak and dizzy. She scrounged up some food and drink (no berries this time) and couldn't help noticing the heavy tracks leading from, then back to the camp.

They have battled again, she realized. And I was of no assistance ... nor was I able to observe and learn if they spoke truth or lies!

When she felt she had eaten enough, she staggered back to her quiet corner in the supply tent and collapsed again, tears streaking down her face. She could not recall a previous time in her life when it had been so critical for her to be alert, and she had been incapacitated.

Why had she been so foolish? Why had she been so careless as to allow incapacitation to come from her own hand?

As soon as she recovered fully, she must speak with the other wood elves and learn what had happened.
