Once upon a time ...
... there was a fair young elf-maid with reddish gold hair who answered the call of the Paladin. Galadri entered into the Guild hall, and was awarded her training tunic and given instructions to go out and hunt enemies - especially orcs.

She quickly discovered that orcs were too strong for her, so she gained experience by fighting bats, decaying skeletons, wolves and whatever other enemies she encountered that she could actually defeat.

At that time, she had no maps and frequently lost herself in the mighty Greater Faydark forest. She had no reliable sense of direction to aid her, and did not know that she could take her location by coordinates. Many times when she fell in defeat, all her posessions were forever lost to her.

Then other elves took pity on her plight, and began to teach her the ways of the forest. They also began to teach her the ways of Norrath. By learning well what she was taught, she grew strong enough to hunt enough orcs to please her guildmaster.

She also learned from a barbarian warrior that there were maps to be found at the strange-sounding locations of eqatlas.com and castersrealm.com. Obtaining those maps helped tremendously as she began to travel farther from her home in Felwithe.

Many seasons later, she had attained enough experience to hold Orc Hill alone for a full hour. How wonderful! She had not expected that place of challenge and trials could ever be vanquished alone! Shortly after descending from the hill, still glowing with her many victories, she met a Wood-elf druid named Aundra.

To Galadri's amazement, she was invited to join another guild called Winged Alliance. She inquired - what was the goal of this guild? She had heard of guilds that no honorable Paladin should rightly join, and had learned caution. Aundra assured her that the name expressed the guild's purpose: an alliance to give wings to those who fought the evils lurking about Norrath.

Aundra's answers pleased Galadri, so she joined forces with this young wood elf and her alliance. She hunted with these new allies for a few seasons, and learned many things during that time. It wasn't until much later that Galadri learned that she was herself the very first one that Lady Aundra had asked to join her. Learning that made her blush in astonishment, for she understood that this was indeed a high honor.

In time, an odd pattern began to emerge in Galadri's battles. She began suffering more and more defeats - instead of less, as would be expected when one grows more seasoned. This troubled Galadri exceedingly, so she began earnest prayers to learn what was amiss.

When Galadri reached her 24th season, she knew what was awry. She had to admit an embarrassing truth. Denying truth is unnatural, even when it hurts. And this particular truth was so very painful that Galdri had hidden it from herself for a very long time.

That truth is that Galadri was better at praying than she was at fighting. She had worked hard, with the aid of many friends, to win the mighty Ghoulbane. She had hoped to prove herself worthy of the holy blade, and with it to gain great victories.

But the holy sword was not enough to cure her own inadequacies. Finally, deeply regretful, Galadri laid aside her beloved Ghoulbane on the Guard walk of Felwithe's wall. She knelt in prayer, and waited. In a short time she seemed reborn, and was accepted by the High Priest Bronzeleaf for Cleric training.

She seemed to have forgotten much. Galadri would need to relearn everything under the High Priest's guiding hand. But she had also gained much.

Galadri met Aundra again. Aundra Windhover had grown into a much more powerful druid, while Galadri felt that in many ways she herself had lost ground. She felt extremely small and humble, and scarcely dared to approach her former friend.

However, Aundra understood the need for the change and was kind. Galadri became again a member of two guilds - her Cleric guild where she gains instruction, and Winged Alliance with whose members she hunted.

Until the Lady of the Alliance went missing. The allies were not overly concerned at first, but as the absence grew longer they discovered that their guild had no meaning without their beloved leader. The members of the alliance also discovered that they were too few to challenge the stronger evils that were beginning to invade Norrath.

Winged Alliance had a number of allied guilds. Someone did research and contacted the allied guild that appeared to have almost exactly the same goals as their own.

Galadri wasn't present at the historic meeting where the decision was made, but she discovered that all of her friends and associates from Winged Alliance could now be found in Eviternity.

(She ignored the rumors that this stronger alliance was partly negotiated by a Dark Elf - nobody seemed to have any proof to back up that vicious rumor, and all seemed happy with this newly forged union. Surely no agent of darkness would work anything so beneficial to the forces of Good!)

Galadri rejoined her guildmates in Eviternity, and has found no cause to regret that decision.

Galadri continues to devote herself to prayer and learning, and to healing others. When all are healthy and an opportunity to bludgeon an enemy presents itself, she seldom bypasses it ... Galadri still has a bit of the Paladin in her soul.
  Highkeep mines "But I am a Shaman," came the unexpectedly articulate voice from the bear. "I have no need of a healer, I need a fighter." With that the overlarge bear began to turn away.

"I can fight," Galadri replied, the memory of her days as a Paladin suddenly fresh in her heart. "Try me."

The bear looked over its shoulder, considering. Finally it said, "very well. Join me for a little while, and we shall see if you are up to the task."

They were in high hold keep, where the mine had gone too near to the goblin home of Runnyeye. Goblins poured into the mines, so much that the minors had fled for their lives. Miners were not fighters.

So many assorted peoples, from many parts of Norrath, would come to the mines. Some came merely to test their skills against the goblins. Others, like Galadri, came from compassion for the miners. She wanted to help them, and everyone seemed to mock her instead of welcome her or tell her how best to render aid.

Galadri unslung her shield from her back, and steadied it on her shield arm. She hefted her club - as a Cleric, the sword was forbidden her - and she prepared herself to do battle. She glanced uneasily at her new ally, not certain if this odd shaman in bear form were truly to be trusted or not.

A goblin appeared through a crack in the corner and raced toward them! Wasting no time, Galadri charged as she once had when she was a Paladin. She swung her club and bashed with her shield ... and had some guilty pleasure in seeing that she had not lost all her battle skills.

The bear summoned a spirit wolf, who charged beside Galadri. Together, they defeated the goblin and it seemed that her skills improved as she fought. When the goblin fell, she brushed a stray strand of red-gold hair from her eyes and looked back at her ally.

"Let's try another," he rumbled, and looked pointedly at the corner again. Galadri turned as another goblin emerged, and joined the wolf in the attack. Club, bash, pray to stun the enemy, club, dodge, club, bash, pray that the enemy be smitten, club, club, dodge, club, bash ...

Again they were victorious, and she recognized this was partly due to the Shaman's prayer-spells. Galadri had also prayed, knowing that the club would be less damaging than her previous swords had been.

This time the shaman only nodded, then turned. "They seem to alternate. When an attack from one side is repulsed, they begin to come from the other."

And so it was. Battle after battle followed, some from one side and others from the other. Then there was a call for help from deeper in the mines. A fighter was being overwhelmed, in a deep room where raiders kept pouring in. Galadri and her new acquaintence went to help. Soon they were joined by a wizard, who cast powerful spells of fire and ice against the goblins, and they had occasional times of peace between battles.

Then the Shaman was called away, but Galadri and the others were not yet done. Another Cleric joined, this one a dark elf. "Need makes strange alliances," Galadri reminded herself. She neither knew nor cared overmuch why a dark elf cleric had come, for she was already growing battle weary. Yet Galadri did know that such a one could be a powerful ally - or adversary, depending on their bent.

Though she always watched carfully as she was able, with the ongoing battles to beat off the goblins, Galadri saw no sign that the dark elf planned betrayal. They hunted there, some coming, others leaving, but always Galadri - Cleric of Tunare - and the Dark Elf - Cleric of Innoruuk - battled together, for the rest of that season.

Club, bash, club, dodge, club again ... see a companion falter, step back and pray for healing, turn again to the enemy and attack ...

And those who were of fighting classes looked askance at both of them. Galadri wondered which amazed the fighters more - that two Clerics of such opposing views would group together against a common enemy, or that two clerics were fighting. For most had never before seen Clerics figthing alongside a Warrior, Bard, Rogue, Monk or a Paladin. But the fighters were always too few, and the need was too great.

Finally, Galadri's strength waned and she knew that she must withdraw for a time. "Farewell, success and victory," she bid her former group, looking at what appeared to be an endless stream of goblins.

"Thank you for fighting with us," one said. Galadri bowed in response. She prayed a last time for their protection, then withdrew as quietly as possible to avoid attracting more enemies in the direction of herself or her former group.

She escaped to the keep, and took a room at the inn. Galadri would rest for now, but soon she intended to return and fight goblins for another season or two. After that, who knew?

There would always be a need for a healer somewhere. And, mayhap, there would again be need of a fighting healer. Galadri had every intention of learning all her skills as best she was able. Doing anything less seemed unnatural, and an insult to the designs of her Creator.
