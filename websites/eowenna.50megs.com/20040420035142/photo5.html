A choice ...
Somehow the clink of metal on metal echoed differently, and it wakened the youngest refugee almost instantly.

Little Kelejathenne had been living in the basement of the Hall of Truth for nearly a month now, with her siblings. She knew the room well, though she had only seen four summers. She sneaked toward the hallway where the Knights patrolled, away from the sound of the lock being picked.

She nudged each sibling as she passed: Eowenna, the eldest and possibly the strongest; Elarion, whose devotion to prayer had caused their parents to hope for another Cleric in the Family; Janiessa, who immediately summoned a small spurt of flame to ignite the candle on the wall; Valarick, who immediately looked about for a weapon, and Frodhyrid who muttered "a pox on them!" quite bitterly.

In the dim light of the candle, Eowenna had gathered them toward the hallway. But before she could open the door, the militia guards were upon them. Six children, unarmed, against 10 or possibly more of the corrupt Militia.

Valarick and Eowenna, the two eldest, pushed back the younger ones in a futile effort to protect them. Janiessa froze the shoulder of one soldier's sword arm, and caused the tunic of another to catch fire. Val and Enna disarmed each of the wounded militia men and engaged those guards on nearly equal terms.

Eowenna screamed for the Knights, but the Militia had the children pressed against the door and the Paladins were unable to enter. The battle continued, the children fighting for their lives and the Knights shouting orders and running around to the outer doorway.

Elarion was praying for the healing of his siblings, and little Kelejathenne and Frodhyrid attacked with their bare fists. Janiessa continued trying to burn and freeze them, but it was inevitable that the children could not prevail against such hardened and seasoned warriors.

Janiessa was cut down, shortly followed by Elarion. Kelejathenne had been near to her brother when he fell, and one of the soldiers pushed her back at that same time. She fell, and was so frightened she lay still as death ... covered in her brother's blood. She could hear the knights coming, but they had not yet arrived. One of the militia ran off with two of her siblings' still forms.

Valarick fell just as the Knights of Truth burst into the room. Frodhyrid was also lying still. One of the remaining militia grabbed the bodies of Valarick and Frodhyrid, and somehow escaped the vengeance of the Knights. All the other attackers were cut down without mercy.

Eowenna was injured, but ran instantly to her young sister's side and checked her for injuries. When she found nothing worse than bruises and scrapes, she'd clung to Kelejathenne and cried.

=========================

Kele'en awoke, sitting bolt upright, bathed in sweat. Was it the thousandth time, or the ten thousandth, that night had replayed in her dreams? Always she wished there had been more she could do, yet nothing would return her lost siblings.

It was the morning following the remembered horror that she'd been shipped off to Qeynos, for her own safety. Eowenna had sent word a few days later that the bodies of Janiessa, Elarion, and Valarick had been discovered outside the freeport gates ... shortly after it was too late to resurrect them.

Frodhyrid had never been seen again, living or dead. Eowenna had suggested that Kelejathenne's name be shortened to Kele'en, to make it more difficult for the militia to trace her. The Qeynos family who now sheltered the young refugee had agreed.

Kele'en shook off the memories. It was time, she decided. Eowenna was expected today, it would be good to have it done before her sister arrived. Kele'en had come of age this week, and needed to make a choice. Somehow, the nightmare reviving that night of horror had helped her decide.

She no longer believed in the Truthbringer, not the way her elder sister did. If he had been worthy of reverence and worship, he should have heard the prayers of her parents and siblings. He should have rescued them. But they had died, and he had permitted their bodies to be held until no restoration was possible.

Kele'en recognized that there were several powerful spiritual entities, but none seemed more worthy than that one had proven himself to be. She wasn't sure precisely when she'd become agnostic, but she had come to recognize this in herself and accept it.

This agnostic philosophy barred her from certain professions. The young woman considered briefly, and knew that the arcane arts would never appeal to her. Her deceased sister had a natural affinity for such things, an aptitude that Kele'en knew she lacked. Besides, she wanted to fight evil more directly, to do better than she had been able to do on that night of horror.

Kele'en also realized that she didn't like the idea of being burdened with heavy weapons and armor, nor did she like the notion of being dependent upon such equipment to be able to function. She had few posessions, and nothing from before her flight to Qeynos. And she found that having few posessions had become comfortable to her somewhere along the line.

Yes, it was clear to her now. The young woman who had been named Kelejathenne turned her steps toward the monastary. She walked through the small structure, nodding at each inhabitant, and out the back door. She followed the path through the back yard, and visited the Master's study.

"Would you accept me as an apprentice?" Kele'en asked directly yet politely.

He had looked her over, asked a few pointed questions, then approved her admittance to his order.

==========================

Later that day, she'd informed Eowenna and shown her the training tunic she'd been awarded. Kele'en was a bit excited, but also nervous. Would Eowenna approve?

Eowenna had looked Kele'en up and down, arms folded across the heavy armor she now wore constantly. "I'll be back shortly," the Paladin had said. Then she turned around and strode away quickly.

Kele'en wondered where Eowenna was going, and if she'd left because she was angry. Kele'en waited, disappointed that one of their rare visits had been so abruptly cut short. She paced around, exterminating those rats and snakes that proved too aggressive, and wondered if "shortly" meant "today" or if it meant "next time I'm in town."

It was hours later, but still before sunset, when Kele'en saw her sister coming toward town again. She ran toward Eowenna, then paused wondering if hugging the Paladin in public would be considered undignified.

Eowenna smiled, almost as if she read the thought. Then she held out three odd-looking bags. Kele'en accepted them, and began opening them as Eowenna said, "I checked with a master, and I believe these bags and items may prove useful to you. My goodness, the Bazaar was crazy today! I'm sorry it took me so long to return to you."

One bag was a Rallic pack, another a Shralok pack, the third a tailored bag. All were magical, light weight, and reduced the weight of the contents. They contained food, drink, weapons, Wu's armor pieces and a Robe of Living Fungus - all appropriate for members of the monk class.

Kele'en nearly wept for joy - her sister was not angry with her! In fact, Eowenna was willing to spend her own hard-won income to equip her younger sister decently.

Dignified or not, Kele'en hugged Eowenna for a long time. Eowenna smiled and hugged her back. "No sister of mine will be seen in a dirty old worn-out training tunic!" she teased. "No matter our differences, we will always be family."

Even though love of possessions was discouraged in the ascetic philosophy of the monk class, Kele'en would treasure these items for many seasons to come.

Sleeping Paladin
Keleen had often wanted to find a proper way to thank her sister for the gifts that got her started. Twenty seasons she had studied, and learned, and she still relied heavily upon her sister's gifts.

Keleen had difficulty finding anything useful to her sister, that she could afford. At long last, there came a day when she found a possibility. There was a "city wear" tunic, dyed a rich burgundy ... almost wine-colored. Keleen knew her sister liked that color well, and it was hard to find.

She purchased the soft leather tunic and had it wrapped. Then she hurried to the nearest port stone and visited the recently discovered city of New Tanaan. She went to the room her sister was currently renting, and pulled out her key. But the door was unlocked ...

Curious and somewhat concerned, Keleen went in. She saw her sister asleep in an awkward position on her bed. A strange glow surrounded her. Eowenna was wearing some of her plate armor, and all of the ringmail underlayer that protected the areas where plate couldn't. That was unusual, since even the heavily padded leather that supported the ringmail was uncomfortable to sleep in.

Keleen tried to wake the Paladin, but was unsuccessful. So she unbuckled the last two pieces of plate armor, and eased her out of them and the ringmail under them. She straightened her sister's body so she was stretched out in a position that looked more comfortable. Keleen put her sister's head on the pillow, and unfolded the quilt at the foot of the bed and stretched it over her.

She penned a quick note, to let her sister know she'd been there and that the package was for her.

As there seemed nothing else she could do there, she left her sister's room and locked the door behind her.

Odd, that so much movement should fail to wake her sister.

Keleen needed to return to Qeynos, and to complete tasks assigned to her by her instructors. She didn't know when she could return to visit her sister again.

She thought hard, being young in the ways of distance communication. It took serious concentration, and frequently gave her a headache. She accepted the word from her trainers that it would grow easier in time, but for the moment it was mighty uncomfortable.

"Hello?" she called out to the guildmates she and her sister shared.

"Hail and well met," came a reply.

"Do any stay near my sister, in or around New Tanaan?" Keleen asked.

"I have a room in Selia," one responded. "Does Eowenna need anything?"

Keleen shifted from the guild communication to an individual conversation. Those usually didn't give as severe of headaches as the wider broadcast.

"She's asleep, and I cannot wake her," Keleen informed the one who tenanted a room nearby. "That's unusual, she's generally a light sleeper."

"I will check in on her, at least until this mystery is solved," came the reply. "It has been a week since any had seen her, we were growing concerned too."

"My thanks," Keleen sent. "I nearly reached her mind, but I think my inexperience is against me. Mayhap you can communicate with her, even in her sleep, where I have failed."

"Then I shall try that also," was the response.

"My thanks again," Keleen sent.

"Not a problem, we take care of our own." It was a reminder, but also reassuring.

Keleen attempted to transmit a mental smile, but wasn't sure if it went through. She informed him where Eowenna's spare room key was concealed, in case it was needed.

Feeling better, Keleen walked to the Qeynos stone and triggered the translation.
