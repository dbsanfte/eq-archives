Intrepid Spirits
This guide is based on the various opinions and preferences gathered through experience, discussions and message boards such as The Ranger’s Glade. Although, I have attempted to provide a good guide for new rangers based on the general consensus, ultimately most of decisions you make in creating a new ranger should be the product of personal preferences. To be clear, NO DECISION YOU MAKE WILL HAVE THAT DRAMATIC AN IMPACT. A ranger is as good as his/her tactics, experience and friends.

Races

A Ranger can be either a human, wood elf, half-elf or halfling. Other than a few stat differences and the night blindness of humans, the differences between each race option is strictly role-playing related. I made a human ranger, as I had the legendary Strider in mind from the Lord of the Rings. Other players selected the wood elf, seeing their stealth, swiftness and affinity with nature as an integral part of a rangers character. Others chose the half-elf, the quintessential outsider, at home only in the solitude of the hunt. As long as you are happy with your choice, any of the races will suffice.

Stats

The following section explores the initial distribution of stats. It should noted that opinions and suggestions are based on equipment currently available (as of November 2001) and that Hit Points and Armor Class is of the highest importance (in other words, items with AC are preferable to items with stats only).

A debatable topic for any class other than wizards, who max out intelligence, there has been much discussion on the distribution of ranger starting stats. Once again, it should be noted that initial stat distribution does not make a dramatic difference over the life of your character. Further, since most rangers agree that Armor Class and Hit Points make a much bigger impact that stats anyway (‘AC/HP is King’) the impact of increased stats is trivial at best.

So how do you distribute your 20 points? There are basically four schools of thought regarding initial stat distribution:

Pump Stamina: Increases in stamina add to the amount of hit points available, and decrease the amount of fatigue resulting from battle. Followers of this school of thought believe that in the long run having more hit points is preferable to anything else. Also, stamina is one of the hardest stats to raise with items, thus it is important to start with a solid base. From a fatigue point of view, it seems that getting your stamina over 100 is all that is needed to counteract the effects of a long fight.

Pump Strength: This is recommended in the Caster’s Realm creation guide, as well as in other online guides. This appears to be suggested due to effect of strength on damage output and amount of weight one can carry. However, it is the general consensus that pumping strength is the least favorable of all the options. Strength is one of the easiest stats to raise with items and as a ranger you get a strength buff anyway. Further, the overall effects of adding a few points to strength on damage output is not nearly as dramatic as once believed.

Pump Wisdom: Adherents to this school of thought point out that each point of wisdom can add a lot of mana to your mana pool, especially at higher levels. This is even more important to Half Elves, who start with such a low wisdom base. However, critics argue that ranger spells do not play a dominant role in high-level encounters, and that most of ranger’s life will be spent face to face with the enemy, weapons drawn.

Pump Everything: This school promotes evenly distributing the points over 2 or 3 of the stats. For example, Half Elves should put 10 points into wisdom and 10 points into stamina. Basically, you try to keep all stats relatively high without favoring one.

Although any of the above strategies is acceptable (stats aren’t that important, remember), it is my opinion that ‘pumping stamina’, or ‘pumping everything’ are the best choices. In fact, I favor distributing the bonus points over a couple of stats to even things out as long as stamina is one of them. It is very, very true that raising stamina with items without sacrificing AC is hard, even at high levels, so added points to stamina is a smart thing to do. However, adding all the bonus points to stamina is not really necessary, especially since you may have one or two stats lagging behind (like the wisdom base of Half Elves, for example). Further, having high numbers in stats like dexterity can have a nice effect when using your bow. With this in mind, I offer the following distribution suggestions:

Humans: +15 to Sta, +5 to Dex 
Half Elves: +10 to Sta, +10 to Wis
Halflings: +15 to Sta, +5 to Str
Wood Elves: +15 to Sta, +5 to Str
