My Biography

Once again I find myself running through the sandy scrub surrounding Freeport. Returning to this my second home after an adventure in the volcanic dungeon called Solusks Eye, a home to many goblins of evil nature and a strange gnomish outpost. The sun warm on my skin and as I ran I began to remember my first visit to this sprawling city, and the events which led me to my entering the Brotherhood of the Ashen Order.

It was a warm summers morning when I arrived in Freeport. My long journey from Highpass Keep in the company of a merchant and his group of guards and retainers had been rather boring. Only the distant view of a hill giant busily eating a bear or perhaps a wild boar gave any excitement.

I was an orphan who had the lucky, or perhaps unlucky, chance of catching the eye of Captain Bosec, leader of the inner guard in High Keep. One day while I was playing in the courtyard with some other children I accidentally kicked our makeshift ball through the open doors of the keep proper. As the oldest, and the one at fault, I was responsible for getting the ball back. Knowing that children were not allowed past the portcullis without an escort I was rather wary as I mounted the steps. Listening intently for the footfalls of guards, who would not harm us but would report the intrusion possibly banning us from playing in the courtyard for another summer.

Stealthily I slunk around the corner, keeping my body low and as close to the concealing shadows as I could. Upon entering the foyer I spied our ball resting just this side of a large ornamental vase. I smiled to myself thinking I had succeeded in my venture and reached for the ball. I had just laid my hands on it as a black leather clad boot came to rest atop the ball. I slowly lifted my gaze to see the guard who was about to give me a dressing down.

It was no simple guard.

"Young man, you know the rules of the keep do you not?" It was the Captain himself.

"Yes Sir and I am sorry but I just came in to get our ball, I accidentally kicked it through the doors and it bounced in here." My voice was shaking in fear.

"Hmm, so you are taking responsibility eh? At least you have the presence of mind to accept the blame. The last boy I caught in here blamed it on a guard who had stolen their ball. Your parents shall hear of this and be told to keep you home for a while. Who are your parents boy?"

"I have no parents sir, I am an orphan." I replied. "Then who cares for you son?" the captain asked, some of the scowl leaving his face. "I sleep in the stables sir, in return for helping groom the horses, and the priests and merchants give me enough coppers to eat on."

"A beggar are you?" he said, his voice rising. "No sir! I run errands for them in return for the money! Ask Quothos the priest, he gave me three coppers for helping him weed his garden."

"Good." He said "I had my fill of beggars during my stint as a guard in Qeynos. How old are you boy? You look a fair size, getting near time for you to apprentice isn’t it?"

"Yes sir, though in truth I have few prospects here. I suppose I am either going to stay here in the stables or leave Highpass as I have few skills to impress anyone to grant me an apprenticeship, and less money to buy one."

"Well it falls to me to ensure you are punished then. What is your name boy?" he said. "Chiun sir."

"Well Chiun, tomorrow morning after you have finished your chores in the stables, you will come to the guard house and I will have thought of a proper punishment for you. Enjoy the rest of this day boy, and I had better not hear of any other incidents of unescorted children in the keep. Now off with you!"

As I ran back to the courtyard I was nearly shaking with fear. Captain Bosec himself was going to punish me! Bosec was known as a hard but fair man, but being uneducated and scared from the confrontation my mind went wild with visions of being flayed or shackled in the dungeon and left for the goblins which I had heard occupied the mines below the keep.

Morning came early the next day, and I, having been the victim of nightmares of my coming doom, was sleepy and bleary eyed, slowing my chores. It was near noon when I arrived at the gate that leads to the guardhouse. As I walked toward the gate it opened and a small detachment of guards came out in formation. Their polished blades and armor reflected the noon sun in a glittering display of light and steel. After they passed the guard holding the gate saw me.

"You! Boy! What are you doing here?" he shouted in harsh tones.

"I am here to see Captain Bosec sir." I stammered quietly, becoming more nervous by the second.

"Who sent you boy? Any message you have for the captain I can take to him, so as not to keep you from your master."

"I have no message sir, the captain told me to come this morning."

"No message? And he told you to come eh? Ha! What would the captain want you for? From looking at you I would say you are a thief trying to steal a weapon or two! Now get back to whatever little hole you came from, the Captain has no time for the likes of you."

"Please sir, Captain Bosec told me to be here this morning." I pleaded" Just tell him I am here, I don’t want to be in any more trouble than I already am. He’s sure to think I am late as it is!"

"I said leave now boy, before I show you the back of my hand!" he shouted as he lifted his hand threateningly.

"Guard Rogar!! What are you doing?! And what’s all the shouting about?" Captain Bosec said in a commanding voice.

"Sir! I caught this young scoundrel trying to sneak through the gate and was just sending him on his way, and he tried to lie saying you had told him to be here."

"I did." The captain stated flatly, cutting off the guards tirade. "I told him to come, now back to your post!"

"Sir! Yes Sir!" he barked and saluted before turning to leave.

"Perhaps if you were quicker at your chores this could have been avoided boy. Where have you been?" his voice was ominous sending a chill through me.

"Sorry sir, I didn’t sleep well and I woke up late and…."

"Silence! The world wants results not excuses," he barked. I nearly fled, but knew if I did whatever punishment he had in store for me would suddenly become much worse.

"Follow me boy, your punishment awaits." He turned and walked into the training grounds with me quick on his heels.

"Chiun, this is Brother Micah, a monk of the Ashen Order. He comes from Freeport to help train the guards here in hand to hand combat. I have decided that your punishment is to be his assistant while he stays here. You will report to him every day as soon as possible to help him with whatever he may require. This includes fetching water mending clothes or being his demonstration partner during training."

I was taken aback, to say the least. This punishment seemed too easy. I was sadly mistaken.

Brother Micah was a small fellow compared to the captain. He wore no metal armor and held no blade. All in all I was not impressed at first glance, yet upon further inspection I noticed the way he moved. Quick and sure his feet seemed to almost float above the ground. His voice, though quiet and composed could somehow be easily heard over the din of clanging armor and weapons. And though he wasn’t heavily muscled he had no fat on him, and seemed perhaps stronger than other men his size. The most intimidating thing about him were his eyes, ice blue and at short distances seemingly colorless, at night almost seeming to glow.

"Come young Chiun attend me." Micah said. So I followed him out to the middle of the practice field. There were a few guards sparring here, their swords flashing as they swung in wide arcs and back to a ready position. Micah produced a small gong from a pack and bid me to chime it three times. I took the small mallet and quickly struck the gong as I was told, and the guards turned to look at Micah and me. "Form up!!" captain Bosec bellowed.

The guards scrambled into position each man at arms length from the next in several tight rows.

"Gentlemen, this is Brother Micah. He is here to instruct you in hand-to-hand combat. Some of you will remember another of the brothers who was here a few years ago when the orcs assaulted the keep. He helped us in the defense, and actually equaled the number of kills I made in the battles. I used my sword, he used nothing but his hands and feet. Listen closely to Brother Micah and learn what you can, it could save your life and the lives of the citizens here."

With that the captain turned and walked to the keep, leaving the guards in Micahs hands.

"Chiun, go collect their weapons and place them out of the way." Micah ordered. So I went through the ranks gathering the various blades and stacked them in a neat pile to the side.

"Students attend my words. You are warriors, steel is your discipline, your weapon and your guardian. Steel is strong but only as strong as the hand that wields it, and as quick as the mind which guides it. These are the truest weapons. The hand and the mind." I sat in rapt attention, it seemed so true yet I was accustomed to thinking of warriors with their blades, but what is a blade without a hand to hold it and a mind to control it?

"So you are here to teach us to fight eh Micah?" it was Rogar "We know how to fight. In true battle you need a sword, just using your hands makes it a bar room brawl rather than a battle! The only way to win a fight is to have a better weapon than your opponent. And I believe my sword is a much better weapon than anyone’s hand."

"Then you are a fool." Micah stated calmly. Rogars eyes narrowed and his fists clenched "Give me my sword and I will show you who is the fool!" Rogar shouted, yet made no move.

"Chiun, get him his weapon." Said Micah quietly. I ran to the pile of weapons and grabbed the first longsword I saw and took it to Rogar who took it so quickly I was nearly cut.

"Now student, show me the might of your blade. Cut me down if you can."

The rest of the guards formed a large circle around Rogar and Micah. Rogar paced back and forth trying to size up his opponent. Suddenly he leapt at Micah, his sword in full extension thrusting toward Micahs heart. At the last second Micahs hands became a blur, one moving up to the flat of the blade and deflecting it the other in a direct line to Rogars face catching him solidly on the cheek with a sharp slap that sent Rogar spinning to the ground. He rolled and regained his feet, roared and swung his sword with all his strength in a wide sweeping arc meant to cut Micah in half. Micah deftly stepped back out of harms way, then back in toward his opponent after the blade had passed. He closed in as Rogar began to reverse the cut. Micah caught Rogars wrist and twisted. The sword went flying, making several guards have to doge out of the way. Rogar roared again and started pummeling Micah, who easily blocked or dodged all his blows. Once in a while stepping in and again slapping Rogars face. Finally Micah tired of the game he was playing and stepped back out of reach. Rogar, in a fury at being treated such took two steps and tried to tackle Micah who again dodged and brought his hand down on Rogars back, thrusting him face first into the dirt.

"There sir, you see that with or without your blade my hands and mind are all I need. Also notice I chose not to harm you, Quellious teaches that to bring unnecessary harm is to stray off the path of the tranquil. You would do well to follow this teaching.

Rogar stood, red faced and breathing hard. As Micah turned he brought both hands up above his head and brought them down for a crushing blow on Micah back. The blow never struck. Micah leaped and spun in midair his foot flying into Rogars chest with a meaty thud. Rogar flew back a few feet and landed hard gasping and unconscious.

"Students, that is the first lesson for today. Know when you have lost the battle, learn from you own weaknesses and your opponents strengths. Now call the clerics to attend his wounds.

The men fell back into their formation quickly and quietly. Rogar was still gasping for air and blood trickled out of his mouth as he lay there unmoving.

The days rolled by as they do in the summer. Every day I hurried to finish my chores at the stables and ran to meet brother Micah. He seemed to enjoy my company and he taught me many things. At the end of his stay he and captain Bosec took me aside.

"Chiun has been of great service to me during my stay here captain. And he has been an apt pupil as well."

"Aye, he’s a good lad brother Micah, honest and brave. Though his service to you was supposed to be a punishment I can see he turned it into a chance to learn. Micah, I have a boon to ask of you. Chiun is an orphan, and soon to be of age to apprentice in a craft. Sadly I cannot take him into the guards, and as he explained to me he has no real ties to other trades. I fear he will be left as a vagabond. I like the boy and wouldn’t see him come to that fate. You said he was an apt pupil, could you take him to Freeport to train with the brothers of the Ashen Order?

"Sadly captain I cannot. I am on my way to Qeynos to meet with the master of the Silent Fist. But I can write a note to Master Closk on the boys behalf, that is if Chiun wishes it." Micah looked at me with those penetrating eyes a hint of hope in his smile.

"Yes sir! I would love to go to Freeport!" I said excitedly. A chance to go to Freeport, see the Ocean of Tears, the Desert of Ro! Perhaps one day to travel to fabled Feydwer, land of Elves, Dwarves and Gnomes!

Soon Micah had written a note on a thick piece of parchment and handed it to me. Give this to Master Puab Closk in the house of the Ashen Order near the Theatre of the Tranquil. Then your instruction in the arts of the monk will begin. Fare you well young one, have a safe journey. I must be off now to Qeynos. May Quellious guide you Chiun.

"Captain, there is a merchant caravan that has just arrived, they are heading to Freeport when their trading here is done. Take this and buy him safe passage with them." He handed the captain a small jingling bag of coins. "I can’t take your money Micah" the captain protested. Micah interrupted "Monks have few needs sir and we travel lightly these coins would perhaps slow me in a fight but they will help speed Chiun on to Freeport. Perhaps Chiun will prove stalwart enough to bring honor to the brotherhood, this token would be paid back a hundredfold.

Micah dropped the small purse into Bosecs hand, bowed deep to both the captain and myself turned and walked through the gate without another word.

Bosec and I spoke shortly about my coming trip, and then rallied the guards for a foray through the small pass, which lead to Kithicor forest before the caravan was to leave.
