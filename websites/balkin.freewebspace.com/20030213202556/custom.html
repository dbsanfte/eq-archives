WELCOME TO BLACKSMITHING 101!!!

Welcome to the world of the blacksmith. Pull up a stool, cause this is going to take a while. I'm going to write this the CHEAPEST way possible to get your skill from 0 to 242 in blacksmithing. And if you want just FAST, then I'll put in some addendums as well. I'm modeling this guide for use in Freeport as virtually everything you need to get to 242 blacksmithing (accept leather padding) can be found in the city, or one zone out of the city.

OK, first things first. Your chance of SKILL INCREASE is based on the higher of your INTELLIGENCE or WISDOM. STRENGTH is a secondary attribute in blacksmithing, so a high STRENGTH will increase your chances of skill increases as well. NOTE: it seems STR has to be above 162 to gain a bonus. I have NOT confirmed this personally.

If you have the available cash, I strongly recommend, you invest in some INT/WIS and/or STR gear. There are some people who believe a very high strength, in excess of 255 will give you the same result as a high INT or WIS but I cannot personally verify that.

Keep in mind, when blacksmithing this way, you are selling any successes you have (accept metal bits of course) to a merchant to get a bit of your cash back. This can make or break a young blacksmith with limited funds. While we're at it, if you have available cash, I suggest you invest in CHARISMA gear as well. Get your charisma up to the 105-110 range. With indifferent or better faction, you're getting the best prices you can get.

0-21 METAL BITS

2 small pieces of ore, 1 flask water

Believe me, you're going to need them. Lots and lots of them! Backpacks full of them! IF you have the training points, and would rather not spend the money then by all means, put 20 training points into it. I personally had no training points when I started blacksmithing, so I did it the hard way.

I suggest, you start with one backpack full or small pieces of ore (the stackable kind), and a half backpack of water. Even if you did training points to 20, you'll need them, so crank out some bits! If you're doing it the hard way, you should still reach a skill of 21 easily during this step. Remember, the higher your skill, the better chance you have to succeed with trivial items. We'll start with however many bits you managed to make in that first run, and go from there. Then, it will be back to making more bits. Once you get a bit above 41 I recommend you make approximately a backpack of bits at a time. That is, until you get REAL close to 135, where bits will no longer help advance your skill. 2 backpacks of stackable pieces of ore, 1 backpack of flasks of water will make about a pack full. Actual bits made will be a bit less than a full pack, taking failures into account.

22-41 SCALERS

1 metal bit, 1 scaler mold, 1 flask water

No, there is absolutely no use for them in game, but they sure are good to skill up with!

ADDENDUM! If cost is REALLY tight, and you need a cheaper way, you CAN make toolboxes from 21 to 51 instead of 41-51. HOWEVER!!! Toolboxes are containers, and must be carried in one of your main inventory slots. That being the case, they are a real pain as you have to keep closing the forge, going to a merchant to sell, and going back to the forge. If money is a major issue then do it, but I'd recommend the scalers otherwise.

42-51 TOOLBOXES

1 metal bit, 1 flask water

You though you were going to get away from them didn't you? Nope. Though they are a pain, they are absolutely the cheapest route to 51.

52-68 LANTERNS

1 metal bit, 1 lantern casing mold, 1 bottle, 1 flask water.

A bit more annoying, but everything will stack accept the mold, so it shouldn't be bad.

69-115 SKEWERS

1 metal bit, 1 skewer mold, 1 flask water

WOO HOO!!! At skill 101, you are officially, a master blacksmith! Congratulations!

116-122 POTS

1 metal bit, 1 pot mold, 1 standing legs mold, 1 flask water

This gets a bit annoying. You have 2 different molds that won't stack, but it's the cheapest method to get above 115.

You CAN start making some of the mid range banded trivial items to skill up if you wish, and have the cash. Banded boots are 1 sheet of metal, and trivial at 135, so it's up to you. Personally, I'd do the pots.

123-135 SEWING KITS

1 metal bit, 1 needle mold, 1 thimble mold, 1 flask water

This is your last step of metal bits. Again, you have 2 molds that don't stack. AND sewing kits are containers. So, again, you can only make as many as you have open main inventory slots before you have to go sell. IF the cost of increasing your skill is truly the deciding factor, then by all means continue with sewing kits to 135. Personally, I got tired of the bits and molds routine, and I moved on to banded boots to 135.

136-168 BANDED MANTLE

2 sheets of metal, 1 banded mantle mold, 1 flask water

WOO HOO!!! No more metal bits! You are officially an armorer!! Banded gauntlets are also trivial at 168, and the mold is a touch cheaper, but the sell back prices, and the difficulty on gauntlets make mantles the way to go. Trust me.

169-175 BANDED CLOAKS

2 sheets of metal, 1 banded cloak mold, 1 flask water

Once you hit 175, you have my heartfelt congratulations. Take a break. You deserve it! The next step becomes exceedingly painful.

FINE STEEL PLATE ARMOR

Here's where the truly dedicated (or crazy, depending on your point of view) shine. 175 in blacksmithing is reasonably painless to attain. Your most expensive skill attempt is about 3.5 PP. To Exceed 175 though, you have to do fine steel plate collars, which will run you MINIMUM 22 plat PER skill attempt! ARGH!!!!

You now need a smithy hammer, purchasable for 11PP if you have good faction. This item is reusable. You also need 1 leather padding per attempt. Leather padding is only made via Tailoring. 1 silk thread combined in a sewing kit with a LQ Wolf/Cat/Bear pelt. Use a skinning knife (made by you) to reduce the quality of pelts as well. A HQ wolf pelt, combined with a skinning knife in a tailoring kit makes a MQ wolf pelt. Combined again, the MQ wolf pelt is reduced to LQ and is now usable to make leather padding.

You can buy padding from other players, but be prepared to spend stacks of platinum. The going price varies from 50PP to 100PP per stack depending on server. Insane, but to blacksmith past 175, you absolutely have to have it. You can hunt pelts and threads yourself if money is tight of course, but the tedium is maddening.

OK, to get ready for Fine Plate blacksmithing, you need to make

MEDIUM QUALITY FOLDED SHEETS

Smithy hammer, 1 block of MQ ore, 1 flask water

Once you have a few stacks of MQ folded sheets, it's time to make some plate!

176-182 FINE PLATE COLLAR

Smithy hammer, 1 sheet MQ folded metal, 1 plate collar mold, 1 leather padding, 1 flask water

183-195 FINE PLATE VISOR

Smithy hammer, 1 sheet MQ folded metal, 1 plate visor mold, 1 leather padding, 1 flask water

196-208 FINE PLATE BRACER

Smithy hammer, 1 sheet MQ folded metal, 1 plate bracer mold, 1 leather padding, 1 flask water

WOO HOO!!! At skill 201, you become a GRAND Master Blacksmith! Truly, an astounding accomplishment!

209-228 FINE PLATE VAMBRACES

Smithy hammer, 2 sheets MQ folded metal, 1 plate Vambraces mold, 1 leather padding, 1 flask water

229-235 FINE PLATE GAUNTLETS

Smithy hammer, 2 sheets MQ folded metal, 1 plate Gauntlets mold, 1 leather padding, 1 flask water

236-242 FINE PLATE CLOAKS

Smithy hammer, 3 sheets MQ folded metal, 1 plate Cloak mold, 1 leather padding, 1 flask water

THAT'S IT! YOU'RE DONE!!!

That is, unless you happen to be an Ogre, High Elf, Dark Elf, Human, or Dwarf. These races have cultural blacksmithing open to them to achieve 250 skill. That's a whole different ballgame, and I direct you back to EQ traders to research your cultural armor, if you have it.

ADDENDUM! On Luclin there is a new armor, called Shadow Scream Steel Armor that can be created by skilled blacksmiths. It's sufficiently difficult to apparently take even non cultural blacksmiths to 250 in skill. As with cultural armor, I direct you back to EQ Traders to research Shadow Scream Armor.

ADDENDUM 2!! There is a new (Tinkered) device out there called the "Geerlock" hammer. It gives you a +5% chance to succeed when blacksmithing. Average price seems to be between 500 and 1500 Platinum for it, depends on if you provide extra ingredients to the GRAND MASTER TINKER *eyes bug out* when you ask to have one made. NOTE HERE, that is' +5% to success, not skill up chances. So, I'd say use whatever WIS/STR items you have to skill up, and the Geerlock when you're really just trying to make something.

Good luck on your quest to become a master, and even a GRAND master blacksmith!
