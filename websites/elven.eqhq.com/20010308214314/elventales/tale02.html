Birth of a High Elf Paladin
By Aildaar, Knight of Felwithe

It was still hours before sunrise when I awoke to the light knock on my door. I rose swiftly, stepped to the door, & opened it, seeing Corporal Swiftmoon standing there, smiling. "Thy graduation day this morn, young knight -- I trust thou art ready?" She said these traditional words with a smirk, knowing that, if ever there had been an apprentice more ready than I, he or she had not yet been heard of. I merely knodded; to do otherwise would be to show needless vanity. She smiled again, more gravely, & said, "Come then, young knight -- thy destiny awaits!"

I followed her to the ceremony room. Each of us graduating would meet Master Tynkale alone; each of us would present our final words to him -- our thoughts, reflections, & prayers, written down during our long, final vigil. When it came my turn, I was nervous -- who would not be, Master Tynkale is nearly legendary, & this was the first time I had ever even seen him -- but I stood tall & firm, as expected. He read what I had written without expression, seemingly never taking his eyes from my own as he read. It seemed like hours, but finally, he looked at me -- REALLY looked at me -- & he smiled, slightly. "Thou art truly ready, Squire Aildaar. Step forth, & claim thy destiny!" & so it was done.

I followed Corporal Swiftmoon to the armory, where I was issued my uniform, a sword, & some rations of water. She then assigned me duties, showed me the corridor that led out of the castle, instructing me to return every so often & report, either to her or to Master Tynkale. I saluted her, she returned my salute, & she was off again, just like that. I made sure my uniform fit properly, hefted my blade, & set forth into the Greater Faydark.

I had heard tales of madness & murder, but still I was not prepared for what I saw. Renegade Feir'Dal were running about, murdering other youth like myself in cold blood. It was truly frightening, causing me to wonder if these were perhaps Teir'Dal infiltrators in disguise -- such a thing has happened before, & one can never be too careful, when those fiends are about! -- but no, these criminals were, indeed, Feir'Dal like myself. Well, not like myself, I said grimly, & set forth to do battle with them. Of course, they were older, more skilled in their craft, & so I was beaten down many times -- but never will I retreat in the face of danger, nor flee from evil, & so each time, I rose, dusted myself off, & threw myself back at the murderous traitors. Finally, they tired of beating me up, & left for more accomodating parts. Fine. As long as they leave my people alone -- fine. But some day, they will pay for their crimes.

Aside from this, there is no more to say. I hunted the beasts of the forest, & learned much. I am well on my way, I think -- I even have a job, carrying mail for the Antonican Bards Society. The pay is excellent, & the work quite easy. I am learning much of the trails from Felwithe to Kelethin in this task, & I have been told my efficiency in carrying these missives has caught the eye -- favorably, or so I hear -- of men as far away as Freeport, & even Queynos. I did not expect to become a courier, when learning the ways of The Mother -- but it is good, honest, work, & I am proud to do it well.

I wonder what the future will bring.
