Zifeer's Travels        

Wanderings in…Greater Faydark

All good journeys begin at home, and for me, this is Greater Faydark. Almost all of the elves begin their lives hunting and wandering in this vast forest, so much of what I describe will be known, but I shall continue regardless, for the foreign visitor.

Greater Faydark is a misty, gloomy place to the visitor, yet to the elves who live there, it is a comfortable place, made bright by Infravision and made safe by the regular patrols of the Kelethin and Felwithe militia. Near the trails, you are often no more than two minutes frantic sprint from safety.

The forest has few places of note, the principal of these being the Tree City, Kelethin, home of the Wood Elves. Those wanderers who are unfamiliar with the woods often find themselves hopelessly lost in the vastness of the trees, bereft of landmarks, and needing to shout for directions. To these, my advice would be, using map and compass, head for the co-ordinates 0,0; from there the lifts up to the platforms of Kelethin are visible. Better advice still, would be stay on or near the trails until you are more familiar with the terrain.

Kelethin itself can also be confusing to those unused to it, the combinations of turns and ramps needed to reach a place often twisting like a snake in the mind. The key is to know your way to a common point of reference, I use the Priest of Discord (or "pod") Lift as a starting point.

To the Bank: Across, Up Left.

To the Ranger’s Guild: Across, Up Left, Down Right.

To the Bard’s Guild: Right.

To the Druid’s Guild: Across, Up Left, Down Right, Down Across.

To the Orc Lift (And thence to the Orc Camps and Crushbone): Right, Across, Right, Hard Left.

To the Tapped Barrel Tavern: Right, Across, Up Left, Large Building Ahead.

(Note: Check the opening hours. Jarus, the proprietor, keeps unusual hours. I’ve yet to find the place open! Also, he has yet to affix a sign to the building, in my opinion, bad for business!)

There are many other places of note in Kelethin, but I’ll leave you to discover those, try a little wandering of your own. The most important thing to note about Kelethin, is its altitude. The platforms have no guardrails and are often hundreds of feet above the ground. I’ve seen many a drunken reveler plummet to his or her death, and think it’s time someone raised the matter with the Elven Council. Be careful up there, and walk, don’t run, until you get used to it.

Most goods and services are available there, with two notable exceptions. Unsurprisingly, no Ore or Sharpening Stones are available here, the nearest being sold in the Steamfont Windmills, although imported Sheets of Metal can be found if you look hard enough. Also, for a town with so many taverns, brewing supplies are in surprising shortage.

Back on the ground there are several other places of note.

The Henge: Near to the Priest of Discord, there is a Stonehenge. This area abounds with lesser creatures; Giant Wasps, Bats, Orc Pawns and the like. These are all hostile, yet easy to defeat, explaining why so many young elves choose to hunt in this area. (If possible, try to leave the Black Wolves alone, they mean no harm really, and will actively aid endangered Druids) This area is also popular because a quick run over the hill brings you to armed and friendly Kelethin Guards, who will swiftly dispatch those creatures that are a little too much to handle.

Felwithe Gate: To the Southeast of the forest lies Felwithe, the City of the High Elves. A little too ostentatious for my tastes, it is none the less a friendly port of call for the wanderer, and the area in front of it’s soaring towers is another popular hunting venue for young elves.

The Bandit Camp: Tucked away deep in the forest, between Kelethin and Felwithe is a permanent camp of bandits. The competent cutthroats are often hunted and dealt swift justice by more seasoned Elven hunters. You will need to leave the trails to find it though, no small task in itself. It is unwise to venture here alone.

The Orc Camps: To the North of the forest, along a trail from the Orc Lift, lie many tents and Orc Centurions, and the occasional Oracle. These are the sentries for the heavily fortified Clan Crushbone, further north, and these blue skinned scum have waged war on the Elven peoples for as long as I can remember. Many more seasoned hunters carry the battle on today, mounting attack after attack on these camps, but the Orc reinforcements seem inexhaustible.

The Fay Gate: Along the southern trail sit four massive stone pillars, tingling with magical energies. These markers allow Wizards of sufficient power to teleport here from anywhere on Norrath. In time, the wanderer gets used to seeing strange figures in flowing robes pop out of thin air here.

Elsewhere: From Greater Faydark, it is possible to travel to the High Elf city of Felwithe to the east, the brooding Orc castle of Crushbone to the North. To the west are the Dwarven mountains of Butcherblock and to the south, lies the forest of Lesser Faydark, an eerie place of spiders and fairie folk.

The forest of Greater Faydark is provisionally ruled by the newly appointed Elven Council of Faydark, a democratically elected body of three Wood Elves, three High Elves and one Half Elf. They preside over affairs of the forest, it’s inhabitants, and they consider the Elves place in the world beyond these trees. They are, I believe, a force for good, although there are many within the woods that do not acknowledge their authority, or are simply unaware of their presence.

The council’s authority and will is enforced by the rapidly growing Elven Guard, a self appointed military force dedicated to stamping out injustice and murder in Faydark. They do this with increasing efficiency, and I do think the forest is beginning to see the benefits of their work. There also a number of other guilds dedicated to fighting for justice, including the Femme Fatales, and the Vengeance of Eldarr, among others. If you find yourself a victim of attack, be sure to inform a member of one of these guilds, they will often help the unfortunate.

Never the less, the forest can still be a dangerous place. The forces of madness and death, in my opinion the true enemies of all civilized races, still penetrate these woods and kin-slayings are still a major problem here. In particular, the two training areas noted above are often beset with petty bickering and fights, making progress in training difficult to begin with. My advice would be to find someone you trust, and watch each other’s backs, murderers usually only attack single, lone targets.

Once seasoned and able to move on, the major source of concern becomes the area around the Orc Camps, particularly, the area nearest Crushbone itself. Typically casters, some unscrupulous individuals will take advantage of poor health or overwhelming odds and seek to kill and loot the unwary. This is a place to be wary and not to show weakness. Again, a watchful friend is the best defense. At times these camps can get very crowded.

(Note: I’m stabbing myself in the foot here; but consider Lesser Faydark as an alternative to the Orc Camps once at this level of skill, it is quiet, target rich and on the whole, kin-slayer free!)

For the foreign visitor, Greater Faydark is largely a welcoming place, with a few exceptions:

Dark Elves, Trolls and Ogres are usually here with only one intention, killing Elves. Sad but true, so wanderers of those races are required to inform one of the Elven Guard of their presence and intentions if they wish to travel unhampered. Even then, this is no guarantee of complete safety.

Dark Elves in particular will have a hard time convincing the Elves of good intention, since numerous recent raids, by Teir’Dal wizards teleporting in through the Fay Gate, have not done good things for race relations.

Gnomes are largely accepted, but are nearly always treated with suspicion. Not so long a go, a series of escalating raids between Kelethin and Ak’non, led to much bloodshed, and only recently tensions between these two lands have eased off. Even so, a fair number of known Elf killers hail from this race, and old memories are slow to fade.

Wanderers from other races are generally treated well on the whole.

(Note: Wanderers from afar will be glad to know that the entire of Greater Faydark is considered a city for the purposes of the spell Bind Affinity, although finding someone to do it for you is another matter!)

Nearest Wizard Gate – Greater Faydark

Nearest Druid Ring – Steamfont or Butcherblock

Nearest Bind – Greater Faydark

If you want to know more, give me a tell! All my wanderings end, and begin, here!

Zifeer the Wanderer
