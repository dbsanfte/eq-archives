Taeladar paused next to a large Oak tree to rest, for just a moment. He quickly looked around and confirmed his fears. There was no doubt about it, he was lost. Taeladar felt foolish. Why hadn’t he listened to his Father and stayed with the others? It had seemed like a good idea at the time, just a quick break from the other boys, a moment to look around. Now it didn’t seem so wise, lost and alone in The Faydark. Taeladar sat next to the tree, in the deepening shadows as night approached. The others would be completing their initiation rites by now. He should have been there with them, he though bitterly. Now lost and alone in the woods he had little hope of being found. He had marked several trees as he passed them, only to find himself doubling back again and again. Most disturbing of all, he had found footprints. Orc footprints. Should they find him before the others he knew he would die. To die before his initiation would be horrible indeed, to wander forever in Tunare’s forest as a boy! Best not to think of it.
Taeladar quickly looked up. Something was headed this way. He did not think it was an animal; it was making too much noise. Too much noise and trying to be quite. Taeladar grabbed a large branch that had fallen from the tree, in a pinch it would do as a club. The creature that emerged from the woods was horrible indeed. The stuff of nightmares. Easily a foot taller than him and nearly twice as broad, the Orc was as terrible as the stories told. Clutching his makeshift club Taeladar swung the club in the beasts direction. A futile gesture, the creature seemed to laugh and scream at the same time. It advanced on him with murder in its eyes and a wicked blade in its hand. Taeladar gritted his teeth and prepared for the worst. If he were going to die, he would make this Orc pay in blood for his head.
.........
Taeladar sank to the ground, his every muscle aching with exhaustion. He was badly cut and bleeding freely. How he managed to survive he still didn’t know. During the battle he had simply felt somehow protected. He had seen his wounds close before his eyes. How such things were possible he did not know. He had heard of such things from the old women of the village, but had laughed at them. He wasn’t laughing now. He felt different than he had before; somehow more at home in the wood. As confused as he was he knew one thing. The voice he had heard while near death was unmistakable. It could only belong to one being

Tunare
