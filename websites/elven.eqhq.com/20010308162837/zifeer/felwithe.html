Wanderings in…  Felwithe

Located on the eastern edge of the Greater Faydark, the marble towers of Felwithe are an awe- inspiring sight. Felwithe is the home of the High Elves, and a center of learning, art and culture. It is also a welcoming haven for the wanderer.

The city is steeped in ancient history and dates back from the time of the mass relocation of the High Elven peoples from Antonica, or Tunaria, as it was then known. The solemn hush that shrouds this place can be quite disconcerting for the visitor, particularly those used to the rowdy Kelethin or the bustling Freeport. (Personally I find it a bit too austere for my tastes, but I never turn down a friendly city at the end of a long run!)

The city is divided in to two districts, North and South. The Southern district contains most things of note, the temples and guilds, the bank and expansive shopping, although you’ll be hard pressed to recognize this serene area as a marketplace. Southern Felwithe also contains a freshwater lake with some of the best fishing in Faydwer, and most of my skill in this sport comes from practicing here as a youth. (Check out the Felwithe Fish House, cuisine second to none!)

Yes, a digression on fishing, I think. I would seriously recommend this sport to anyone, regardless of profession, although Druids are particularly adept at finding bait, others will need to buy it. The initial outlay is minimal, a rod for two gold, and bait for a few copper. In the time it takes to wait for the boat to Freeport, you easily can haul two or three gold’s worth of fresh fish out of the deeps. Good way to unwind too. Anyway, those wishing to take this up, try Felwithe Bait and Tackle, next to the Fish House, tell ‘em I sent you.

The Northern district of the city holds little of interest to the wanderer, but is worth seeing anyway, since it houses a few small shops, and the Nexus of the Art; the combined guilds of the High Elven Enchanters, Wizards and Magicians. The first time I saw it, I just stood staring like a, well, Wood Elf. The entrance hall is a remarkable structure, not for it’s fine lines or detailed workings, although these are impressive, no it is the fact that the entire building is suspended in the air by a pillar of rock no thicker than my arm! I tentatively ascended, but the thing was as steady as a rock. I decided not to meddle in the affairs of wizards further, and left.

One other person of note here, is Tacar Tissleplay, a Wood Elven Bard, found loitering just inside the city. He is the Felwithe contact of the Antonican League of Bards; a group of individuals who tirelessly help keep communications open across the whole world. The young wanderer can do a lot worse than helping this fellow out, since he and his colleagues are one of the few ways I have so far found, to make wandering profitable, and even now, I regularly ferry mail around the globe for them.

(Incidentally, I have recently proved that our world is flat, or at least very, very big. It goes like this…In Erudin, on Odus, it is midnight; I use Gate to return to Kelethin, it is still midnight. Either we stand on a truly immense spinning ball, which goes around the sun, or we stand on a small series of square panels around which a small ball of fire spins. I still have nightmares about the time when, high on the north side of the East Commonlands, I saw over the edge of the world! I remember little about it, save running around the Commons like a madman, blathering on about how "It goes on forever!" I think most of the Dark Elves hunting there were too curious to kill me.

I’ll leave it to you to decide which is the more far-fetched, but I’d love to hear from any Gnomes who might have any theories about this…)

Onward travel from Felwithe consists of going back outside to the Greater Faydark.

And on Rallos Zek…

Visitors here, who have just come in from the forest outside, will be immediately struck by the quiet of this city. There seems little to keep anyone here for long periods of time, most people simply restocking with equipment, selling loot and heading out again, so the place is almost always empty of adventurers.

This is perhaps one of the reasons for the almost non-existent murder rate here. Another is the ruthless efficiency of the many guards in this city. They are not noted for their tolerance.

Troll, Ogre and Dark Elven wanderers would be best advised to give this city a miss altogether, regardless of their intention, along with others who follow the art of Necromancy, aside from that, most other people will find this a welcoming sanctuary from trouble.

Nearest Wizard Gate – Greater Faydark

Nearest Druid Ring – Steamfont or Butcherblock

Nearest Bind – Felwithe
