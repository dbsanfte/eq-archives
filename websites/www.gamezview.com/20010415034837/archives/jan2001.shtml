Heart Of Winter Preview Posted Thursday, January 11, 2001 by skrip

Orphen Review Posted Saturday, January 6, 2001 by jcsmith
ESCMag has placed up a review of Orphen: Scion of Sorcery for the Playstation 2. Quotage to follow...

In one of the most hilariously pointless additions to a game ever, treasure chests are scattered about Chaos Island in hard-to-reach places. Almost without exception, the chests hold non-descript little bells or lanterns that heal 10-40 HP. Interestingly enough, you can’t use these items in battle and there’s no need to use them after battle as your HP is always fully restored. By the end of the game, I had at least 15 of each variety of lantern, incense and bell, all of which basically did the same thing. I wound up using a few sometimes like when I fell in a lava pit 10 or so times progressively and my health was half-drained (!!!) as a result, but mostly, I found myself figuring out how to get to a hard to reach ledge containing treasure only to open a chest and groan upon seeing yet another stupid inventory-clogging space-filler. There are also items that "attract flying enemies" and perform equally pointless tasks. If some secondary ending was added as a reward for opening every chest in the game, or even if some kind of percentage was shown to give those RPG completists a little extra gameplay, the system would have made more sense. As it was though, I think it would have had greater meaning if the chests contained various novelty items such as a whoopee cushion of dragon slaying or an electric hand buzzer of +2 ogre zapping.

Built on a new graphics engine, Delta Force™: Land Warrior™ uses the power of today's 3D accelerator cards to produce smoother, more attractive visuals while retaining the unique advantages of the voxel-based engines that drove the original Delta Force™: Land Warrior™. The result is a game that gives players the best of both worlds: the long viewing distances and vast outdoor battlefields that distinguished the first two games, plus large, complex interior environments previously unseen in a Delta Force™ game.

Further realism is added by new weapon effects like reduced accuracy while walking or running, recoil, and even sniper scopes that are affected by a soldier's breathing. In turn, games that only allow high-ranking players to join will mean that the top online soldiers can always find a challenge equal to their skills.

Oni Preview Posted Tuesday, January 2, 2001 by bullseye
GamePen has posted a preview of Oni, Bungie's upcoming third person game that will hit stores this month.

    Oni is a new kind of third person action game that for the first time combines hand to hand combat and gunplay into a single, intense experience. Oni tells the story of Konoko, an elite cop and one-woman SWAT team whose approach to fighting crime is a devastating combination of rational calculation and fighting fury. She is an agent of the Tech Crimes Task Force - a woman whose tough exterior hides a soul haunted by dark secrets from her past called "oni" ("ghosts" or "demons" in Japanese). Oni's gameplay transports the player into the midst of an intense anime action thriller. Massive, futuristic cityscapes, indoor/outdoor environments and a huge variety of characters give it the sweeping scale of an adventure game, but the gameplay is pure action.
