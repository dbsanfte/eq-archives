Illegal Drugs?
 Planetside - 13:33 EST - jcsmith

Stryker popped in to this thread on the Planetside forums to say no to drugs...

Yeah, serums were reconsidered. Part of the reason we ended up not really wanting them was that we felt if we had performance-enhancing serums -- even with bad side effects -- everyone would feel compelled to 'boost up' before going into each battle, which isn't the what we want to see happening. We don't want players to feel like, "Well, in order to be equal or better than my foes, I need to take Serum A, B, and D, THEN go into battle!"
We still have medical equipment, our specialty system, and implants, but these won't really interrupt the overall gameflow for players. Specialties you've learned as well as implants you've purchased and installed are permanent -- you die, you come back with them, so there's 'reconfiguring' time (unless you want to swap implants).

Part of this all has been just been due to ongoing refinement, the 'living' design document. As I've stated in the past, it's a really delicate balance when your primary mission is to remain first and foremost a first person shooter, but at the same time you want to add layers of roleplaying and strategy (depth). You have to make sure that none of the extra depth/content undoes the main goal -- creating a massively multi-player FPS.

Mimesis Online Interview
 MMORPG - 13:21 EST - jcsmith

A Mimesis Online Interview is available from RPGPlanet. Here's a clip...

RPGPlanet: What level of object interaction can players expect in your world? There will doubtless be terminals and weapons, and doors, but can we expect interactive furniture, tools, and so forth?

Derek: As you said, there's biocom terminals, weapons, doors, pieces of equipment like Medikits and miscellaneous items like jewellery or animal body parts, but we resigned from the idea of a totally interactive environment. Basically, it's a question of minimizing unnecessary client-server-client data transfer. Full interactive furniture proved to be something that slowed down gameplay and clogged things up. You have to make those kinds of tough decisions in game development - resign from something you think would be cool, fun, attractive, whatever, in favour of things that make the overall experience better - faster reaction times, better graphics. What we do have is upgradeable equipment - your equipment can learn new tricks, be rebuilt and added to, and so on.

Rogues Are In
 DarkAgeofCamelot - 13:00 EST - jcsmith

The long awaited thieves went in to Dark Age of Camelot yesterday. The thief classes are handled in an interesting sort of way. The primary combat oriented goal of these classes is to sneak up(using the stealth and distraction skills) on their opponents and mount a sneak attack(beginning with a backstab) on them. Thieves also have a form of range attack each(be it spells, throwing knives or bows) as well as their special abilities. It's too early to say much else regarding the classes, but it is certainly an interesting change.

Potential Melee Changes
 Everquest - 14:05 EST - jcsmith

Some huge melee changes may be on the horizon, especially for Hybrids. It's a huge thread, here's a sample...

The effectiveness of these weapons falls slightly below our desire for them. This tends to make those that rely on them most often (shadowknights and paladins) also fall slightly behind our desires for their damage output, at least at levels beyond 50. We'd also like to see a broader range of weapons used by higher level warriors, and it would be nice to see two-handers as a more viable option.

Therefore, we will be increasing the minimum damage of these weapons for all classes once they reach 50th level. There will be an increase in the minimum damage of two-handed weapons based on the wielder's level and delay of the weapon. The higher level the character and the larger the delay, the bigger the bonus. Obviously we're not going to give out exact numbers, but this should result in a solid increase in damage output when using two-handed weapons.

We also believe that there has been a bit of disparity between the very high-end one-handed weapons and their counterpart two-handed weapons. The ultra-high end one-handed weapons are comparatively better than their two-handed counterparts. We will, on a case by case basis, be improving many of the high-end two-handed weapons. Because this is essentially a fix for an oversight on our parts, we will be making these changes retroactive (meaning you won't have to trade the items in or hunt for new ones, existing weapons will just change).

Lag Explained
 StarWarsGalaxies - 14:02 EST - jcsmith

Raph Koster covered tackling lag on the Star Wars Galaxies forums. Here's a piece of his mind...

  • Send only essential data. We don't send you facial expression packets for someone who's face is too small for you to really get the benefit of seeing it. We don't show emotes past a distance, or even the person's presence. The server makes the decision as to whether or not to send you packets of info based on your relative positions, what you can see, etc.
  • Interpolate! We work to make sure that your movement on your side always feels smooth. For other people, we send fewer and fewer updates on their position based on how far away they are. We use dead reckoning and interpolation code to make their movement appear smooth on your client, so if your client thinks they "teleported" away because of lost packets, we in fact have them walk to that new position.
  • Load balancing server architecture. One of the slowdowns that can occur is if the servers themselves are bogged down looping through all the people in a tight area. Our servers are smart enough to dynamically split up people to multiple server processes regardless of the georgraphy. Hence no zones, not even "dynamic boundary zones" like what Asheron's Call uses. Basically, there are no lines in the sand used to determine a "server boundary." This means that we can crowd more people onto the same spot without overloading the server processes, because multiple processes can actually manage the people in the same piece of land.
  • Use a smart network protocol. We rolled our own that uses both UDP and TCP, depending on whether or not something is critical info or not. It is in use by several projects here at Sony Online, in fact.

    Progress Report
         StarWarsGalaxies - 13:33 EST - jcsmith

    Boshek dropped this Star Wars Galaxies progress report on us...

    Now that everyone has seen the trailer from E3 it would be a good time to talk about our progress since then. We have come a long way with regards to our terrain and our playable species. E3 was really a technology demo and we wanted to show how immersive Star Wars Galaxies is going to be.

    7. Implementing some of the UI elements like chat, radar, combat, character customization.
    8. Implementing character customization (It looks awesome – I spent an hour just having fun making characters.)

    MCO Interview
         MotorCityOnline - 13:18 EST - jcsmith

    There is a Motor City Online Interview today at HomeLan Fed. Here's a sample of what's inside...

    HomeLAN - Creating a online persistent racing game is certainly very different than the typical online RPG. Despite this difference, did you learn anything from how games like Ultima Online and Everquest handled their games?


    Andy Beaudoin - The other fantasy online games were a good source of learning on a number of issues. They have provided us insight as to how to manage large groups of online players. They have demonstrated the importance of community to the gaming experience. From observing these games we have identified some pitfalls to avoid and some strong mechanics to adapt. However, a racing experience is very different from a fantasy role-playing experience. So, the trick with Motor City has been to identify those game elements that apply to all communities while leaving behind those that cater more to a fantasy role-playing environment. Players familiar with these games may see some familiar features in Motor City, but they will likely find a significantly greater number of features never before seen in either a racing game or an online PSW.
