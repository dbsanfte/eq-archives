Class Interdependecnies in Sovereign
 MMOEverySite - 1:35 EST - jcsmith

Froech made a lengthy post to the Sovereign forums where he talked about class interdependency in Sovereign, and in MMO games in general. Here's a piece of his mind...

Everyone can play EQ by themselves for the first several levels without too much difficulty (it's always easier in groups, that's a given). However, the older you get, the more you tend to need to have a group of friends to flesh out your hunting party. You need that tank, a healer, and a magic user, just to make sure that you can hunt effectively.

The way I see it, that's good game design. You're encouraging "community" amongst the players. They need to group together, and help each other out, deepening the reward for playing the game, and reinforcing each other. There is no class in the game that can “do it all, and do it all well”.

His contention was (and I tend to agree) that you need to do the same kind of thing in any massively multiplayer game. Start them off fairly well rounded, but as they go up the tech tree (in our RTS model), build in reliance on other classes as well.

It's an interesting topic but I am of the frame of mind that one of Everquest's biggest failings was the dependency on certain classes. I love to group with other players, and in a game like Sovereign I can see the good in a system such as this(similar to in Shattered Galaxy). As far as all MMO's though I have to disagree there.

There was nothing worse(well maybe a few things) in Everquest than sitting at the Sebilis entrance for an hour because you couldn't find an enchanter and/or a cleric to go in any further. There was nothing more frustrating than the fact that in order to do much of anything in the game you had to set hours of your time aside to find a group and then stick with that group for long enough to make it worth everyone's while. I think it's great to give every class it's own unique feel and I think it's great to give players reasons to work together. When you tie the entire game in to that premise however it can ruin the game for a lot of people who may be limited by time.

Phillips Acoustic Edge Review
 SoundCards - 14:38 EST - jcsmith

Audiophiles might be pleased to hear that 3D Spotlight has given their review of the Phillips Acoustic Edge sound card. They gave it a 9.2/10 rating. Clippage below...

One thing of interest during installation was the custom SPDIF & Line Out cabling which the Acoustic Edge used. The Line Out consisted of 1 9 Pin jack & cable which split into 3 other jacks - Center & Subwoofer, Rear Left & Right, Front Left & Right respectively. In a similar vein the SPDIF jack cable consisted of both SPDIF In & Out jacks. It's fairly nice & innovative design, certainly an efficient one. No more reaching around your PC to mess with inputs. You simply have 1 cable to deal with which can easily
be placed around the side of your system.

    Terminal Reality Interview
         Action - 12:53 EST - jcsmith

    3DAP has put together an interview with Terminal Reality. They discuss the latest versions of the Nocturne engine in detail. Here's why it can go toe to toe with the next generation Unreal/Quake engines...

    Real lights and shadows. The cornerstone of the Nocturne technology is that we start a scene completely black and then add lights. Where no light shines, the world is dark. Just like in real life. That realism in the lights increases the believability of our environments. Future Nocturne titles will take better advantage of this lighting technology to allow characters to hide in shadows or feel the blistering hot death of raw sunlight on their supernatural skin. We've got bump mapping and specular mapping and detail mapping and moving lights. Everything the new Doom engine has. Also, artists have tremendous power over the NocturneX engine. We don't have a watered-down custom editor for environments or characters. Everything is done in Lightwave or 3DStudio, powerful tools that no in-game editor can hope to match.

    Rails Across America PR
         RealTimeStrategy - 17:10 EST - jcsmith

    In a press release earlier today Strategy First announced that they had entered in to an agreement with Flying Lab Software for the release of Rails Across America in North America. If your curious as to what Rails Across America is, then check this out...

    "Rails Across America takes an exciting era of cutthroat competition and crams it into a dynamic two hour multiplayer slugfest," says Russell Williams, Executive Producer, Flying Lab. "We’re thrilled to be working with Strategy First. They’re enthusiastic about the genre and they have a great line of products."

    Rails Across America depicts a network of hundreds of tracks with thousands of trains crossing the entire continent. Players focus on strategic decisions, such as where to expand, where to compete, and when to engage in a well-timed price war. Rather than simply operating individual trains, players must build and monitor the rail network, moving cargo from one destination to another. Similar to the “SimCity” model, the level of direct control is limited, but the subtleties that arise are immense, which makes it possible to control large numbers of trains.

    While freeing players from managing the "train dispatcher" functions that are common in games such as Railroad Tycoon 2, Rails Across America allows them to focus on higher-level decisions. While many games use money as their sole victory condition, Rails Across America players will compete for ‘prestige’, which is earned by good railroad management and specific goals for different eras. Multifaceted victory points will mean players will be less likely to find economic loopholes by which to dominate the game.
