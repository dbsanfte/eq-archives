    The Developers Answer Questions
         DarkAgeofCamelot - 17:58 EST - mage

    After the posting of the brand new screenshots of the Norse Troll and Norseman, there were some questions on the Round Table Boards the developers decided to clear up.

    Matt on PvP balancing: Actually, that's not quite true, although I can easily see why you think so. Basically, our monsters are designed to be set up exactly the same as a player of that level would be (armor factor, damage done, type of damage, etc.).
    This way we know that a 20th level monster will fight almost the same as a 20th level player (although not quite as intelligently, of course). So, if we make sure that all the monsters are set up correctly, then we know that we're at least in the right ballpark, so far as the numbers go.

    After that balancing occurs, then we have to take into account all the "methods" players will use that monsters can't, such as strafing, running away, ganging-up, and so forth. So we will still have work to do, but like I said, at least we'll have a good baseline to work from.

    On the troll's natural armor: This is to answer the question about Trolls wearing armor:

    The troll skin is not rock -- it is regular skin that is thicker and "rocky looking" than human skin. Hence, they will need to wear armor to protect themselves. They will get some natural protection from the skin, of course.

    Copper on the proporation of women employed by Mythic Entertainment: Mythic has 20% RL women.

    Skills in Star Wars Galaxies
         StarWarsGalaxies - 14:34 EST - jcsmith

    Raph Koster made a new post to the Star Wars Galaxies forums to describe how skill points work in Star Wars Galaxies. Here's a piece of that post...

    You start with all the skill points you will ever have. Let's say you start with three points.
    You decide to pick up a skill, Blaster. You find that it's cheap--only one point. So you go do the things you need to learn it and pay that 1 point.

    You decide to advance more in that profession, to Advanced Blasting. It costs two, and you have two left, so you can afford it. No, whoops, you can't get it yet! You have to QUALIFY to advance further. Phooey. To qualify you have to use Blaster regularly, and go get some training, and do some mission for the trainer, and sign up for a local blaster firing range and stuff.

    So you decide to try some other cheap and easy skill that doesn't require you to qualify, alike say, Wookiee Grooming. Yay! You can learn that. It also costs you two, because you're diversifying professions. But it's a low level skill, so it has either no or almost no prerequisites.

    On Planetside Network Code
         Planetside - 14:26 EST - jcsmith

    Veto spilled the beans on Planetside network code earlier in this thread...

    Ok, so I hear there are some questions about the network code in Planetside. Who could possibly care about network code. What could it possibly matter how good it is? (Just kidding, don't start shooting yet.) ;-p
    Planetside is using a hybrid hit resolution system that is both client and server side. The client will sending data on what it thinks it hit. The server will then verify this data with it's version of reality to see what deviance there is due to latency from the client. This deviance is then used to modify damage results and other things based on target ping times and other heuristics from the network layer. We are trying very hard to alleviate the problem of latency differences between players as well as keep the system robust against attacks.

    Hacking of code and network packets is always an issue; an issue that is not easily resolved. We have done our best to prevent this in both areas, but as anyone in security knows when someone really wants to hack something they will. That being said, we have developed many other systems to detect hacking attempts and log them. One of the hard things about hacking is you never get it right the first time. We will detect these attempts and take appropriate action. Hackers may have an advantage for a short period, but they will be caught.

    Clan Involvement in Planetside
         Planetside - 14:14 EST - jcsmith

    Danny "Blackbird" Han made a response to this thread on the Planetside forums today. He talks about clan involvement in Planetside. Here's the scoop...

    Yes, you should be able to keep tabs on members of your Outfit with a device that's similar to an IM system.

    I can't comment on the odds of another Outfit messing up your plans, unfortunately. We're going to do our best to keep continents populated but not overly populated, so the number of 500 people per continent is definitely subject to change. We sorta used a very rough calculation that considered how much "comfort zone" a person should have on a continent, and part of our calculations were based on EverQuest's population and size of their landmass per server. Here's some food for thought. A PlanetSide continent measures 8k x 8k, or 64,000,000 meters. With terrain obstruction, objects, etc., half that number may be "unusable" so let's take it down to 32,000,000 meters. Eliminating half the continent may STILL be a low figure (the unusable terrain could actually be -more-), but for the purposes of this illustration, it seems plausible. If you have 500 people per continent, that gives each person a "comfort zone" of 64,000 meters. And that's just one continent out of 13 (and we're not even counting our Empire home continents and smaller campaign continents). So with all this land, I'm hoping that people will be well spaced out. Of course, people will focus their efforts on points of interest (e.g. bases and facilities) so clustering will occur, and our nice, comfortable 64,000 meters per person in breathing room really becomes a different number.

    Fighting Legends Preview
         FightingLegends - 13:40 EST - jcsmith

    There is a preview of Fighting Legends at Women Gamers. Check this out...

    One of the most intriguing aspects is the game play. Players may control up to 16 units, much like an RTS, but those units are not necessarily of their own creation. Instead, users may trade units, indeed, will have to collaborate if they are to complete certain quests for the game. Ideally, this will foster friendships and strategic alliances, as reciprocity benefits both parties in the transaction. Each player can clearly see on the screen what units are being transferred, and the exchange is not complete until both sides have fulfilled their part of the agreed-upon bargain. Additionally, such trades will force players to communicate with one another, and will help the commerce aspect present in the game world: players can swap tips, units, relics, and world resources like wind crystals, shadow magma and power ore. In other words, help your friends with mutually beneficial swaps, and they might help you face off as a team against a player who is being obnoxious. While player slaying is nothing new in online games, Fighting Legends varies considerably from the typical solo avatar scenario by providing the aforementioned numerous units for a competitive face-off, complete with individual animations. However, in a game world populated with characters as goofy as the Hurler (a Wee clan member that tosses tiny, wriggling dwarves as projectiles), it's hard to think of a serious confrontation as much more than an intensely-tinted parody of an Old West showdown.

    First Look at Jumpgate
         Jumpgate - 18:22 EST - jcsmith

    After testing the game over the past month I decided that it was time to take a first look at Jumpgate. Jumpgate is a massive multiplayer space simulation that is scheduled to be released later this year. Here's a taste test...

    Missions are handed out at space stations. You acquire a mission by first selecting from a base type such as delivery, combat, or mining. After choosing a base type you will be presented with three missions of that type. Each of these missions will vary in difficulty and reward. You can then select which of the three you would like to undertake(if any). You would be advised to start off with the easier missions such as deliveries or cargo based missions as they are relatively safe and will net you the cash necessary to upgrade your vessel for the more difficult endeavors. More dangerous missions have a higher experience and cash compensation, but also carry substantial risk.
