Games Cause Violence? Posted Monday, April 24, 2000 by skrip
Read over at fgnonline an interesting article regarding the infamous 'video game causing violence' issue. Here's is the whole enchilada:

In a new report Professor Karen Dill claims to have found a definite link between games playing and aggressive behaviour. "Aggressive behaviour and hostile thoughts are significantly increased in players of violent videogames, " she said. "They are more harmful than violent television or movies, because they are very engrossing and require the player to identify with the aggressor. In a sense they provide a complete learning environment for aggression. These result are very, very worrying."

Dill, who has published various reports on the subject of the media and violence, teaches at Lenoir-Rhyne College, North Carolina, a school affiliated with the Lutheran church. The report, details of which are published in the Journal of Personality and Social Psychology today, comes a year after the Columbine High School shootings, which focused attention on the role of games in an increasingly violent society. The two teenagers responsible for the atrocity were keen players of first person shooters.

A parallel study, led by Dr Craig Anderson, focused on games players who had already shown violent tendencies. "Young men who are habitually aggressive may be especially vulnerable to the aggression-enhancing effects of repeated exposure to violent games," he said.

One of the tests cited as proof of gaming's effects involved game players punishing opponents post-gameplay, with various noise blasters. Players of shooters were more likely to punish harder, than players of Myst, by pressing the buttons for longer periods. Anderson concluded that long-term effects are "likely" to be longer lasting.

Today's London Guardian asked a spokesman for the British Psychological Society for his view. "There have been a number of attempts to set up experiments like these and they have got absolutely nowhere. There is a world of difference between pressing a noise buzzer for longer, and shooting at your classmates," he said.

Psychologist Dr Guy Cumberbatch, an expert in media violence, said the report is far from conclusive. "You cannot simulate in a laboratory the complex social problems that people are concerned about, and overall the actual evidence supporting a link between media violence and real violence is very weak," he said.

New Black List Posted Sunday, April 23, 2000 by jcsmith
There is a new edition of The Black List up at GA-RPG. The Black List is a bi-weekly column by J.E. Sawyer and Black Isle Studios. Quotage below...

Well tough luck, smacksters! 2nd Edition AD&D is about as intuitive and sensible as an RPG based on correlations between random IRS tax forms. Sure, it started out innocent enough. The core rules are relatively straightforward, if antiquated, and they were built upon the foundation of 1st Edition. Over time, the inclusion of eight-zillion kits, the Skills and Powers book, and a bunch of other random optional (?) rules, the system became so bloated, unwieldy, and complex, that a player had to dedicate a VAST amount of time to learning the game.

Here's where I assert my big important opinion: AD&D 2nd Edition is not a rule set. It is an *exception* set. If you think about it, the 2nd Edition game system is not about making broad rules that apply equally to all characters. It's about making exceptions for characters. Every class advances at a different rate. Every class has different THAC0s, skills, and abilities which they *alone* can possess. When they stacked Skills and Powers on top of AD&D 2nd Edition, I likened it to putting a Lamborghini body on top of a car that's essentially a Gremlin. It might look good on the surface, but underneath, the engine is really kind of screwed up. You would have been better off just leaving the Gremlin alone.

A dwindling PC game market and diminishing ad sales turned out to be our biggest enemy, a demon that slashes away at every other game mag on the market. It just didn't make good business sense for us to continue.

If you have any problems, DON'T e-mail us because we have no control over subscriptions. Write our customer service department at custserv@pcxl.com for any subscription questions.

It might be unusually sentimental for a hard-edged PCXL editor to admit, but working on this magazine has been a real labor of love for me. If it were up to me, I'd stick with it until they had to pry the joystick from my cold, stiff fingers.

So, we go out not with a bang or a whimper, but a loud, ugly thud. Even in death, we've always tried to do things a little bit differently. Until we meet again ...

Chuck Osborn, Associate Editor

Hook on Carmack Posted Friday, April 7, 2000 by jcsmith
In the latest edition of Ask Hook(formerly ask Grandmaster B) Brian Hook responded to one users disagreements on his recent article entitled Getting Into John Carmack's Head which dubbed John Carmack as the Michael Jordan of the gaming industry...

This is purely a subjective call, so feel free to disagree. There are many people in this industry that are important for many different reasons. John, in my opinion, has simply done more for the technological aspect of gaming in the past ten years than any other single programmer. His contributions do not consist of a single piece of technology or one article, his contributions consist of a combination of: delivering awe inspiring products that are technological tour de forces; demonstrating new ways of doing old things; and publicly talking about his techniques instead of keeping them close to his chest as if they're some kind of "secret sauce recipe".

There are other programmers and designers that have had a huge influence the past few years. Chris Hecker may not have shipped a game, but his articles and talks at GDC have probably been used in one way or another in hundreds of games the past five years. Doug Church is one of the pre-eminent designers of our time. Mark Kilgard has done a huge amount of work educating programmers about OpenGL and special effects in general. Gary Tarolli and Scott Sellers revolutionized 3D gaming with Voodoo Graphics. And, as you point out, Michael Abrash has been an important part of 3D graphics in the gaming world for his early articles in DDJ and DDJ Sourcebook.

Obviously there are a huge number of contributors in the world of 3D graphics that have done important research, and my comments were not meant to belittle their achievements and accomplishments.

John Carmack Speaks Posted Thursday, April 6, 2000 by jcsmith
If it were anyone elses .plan file I wouldn't mention it. On those few occasions where id Software's John Carmack speaks however it always makes news. So here it is the latest .plan update from JohnnyC...

Whenever I start a new graphics engine, I always spend a fair amount of time flipping back through older graphics books. It is always interesting to see how your changed perspective with new experience impacts your appreciation of
a given article.

I was skimming through Jim Blinn's "A Trip Down The Graphics Pipeline"
tonight, and I wound up laughing out loud twice.

From the book:

P73: I then empirically found that I had to scale by -1 in x instead of in z, and also to scale the xa and xf values by -1. (Basically I just put in enough minus signs after the fact to make it work.) Al Barr refers to this technique as "making sure you have made an even number of sign errors."

P131: The only lines that generate w=0 after clipping are those that pass through the z axis, the valley of the trough. These lines are lines that pass exactly through the eyepoint. After which you are dead and don't care about divide-by-zero errors.

If you laughed, you are a graphics geek.

My first recollection of a Jim Blinn article many years ago was my skimming over it and thinking "My god, what ridiculously picky minutia." Over the last couple years, I found myself haranguing people over some fairly picky issues, like the LSB errors with cpu vs rasterizer face culling and screen edge clipping with guard band bit tests. After one of those pitches, I quite distinctly thought to myself "My god, I'm turning into Jim Blinn!" :-)
