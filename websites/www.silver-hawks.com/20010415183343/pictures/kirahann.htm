Sir Kirahann

We stood at the freeport docks looking after the departing ship. To bid our brother farewell had been a hard task and the look of our faces was depressed. A week ago Kirahann, second in command of the Silver Hawks, had announced that he would go on a Holy Quest. He couldn't reveal the details of this quest as it was secret. All he told us was, that it was a dangerous task and that his safe return was not granted.

Looking in the faces of my brothers I remembered Sir Kirahann. Strange, but I never thought about him just as Kirahann, it was always 'Sir' Kirahann. Everyone seemed to call him so. It was something you couldn't explain but everyone knowing him, his appearance, his deeds and particularly his heart called him Sir Kirahann.

There were many tales that could be told about this great knight, but there was a special one I remembered. It was while Kirahann hunted in the Feerrott, seeking and killing lizards. When all of a sudden he heard a cry in the distance. Wondering what that could be, he sneaked towards a clearing and saw a little ogre child, surrounded by several lizard warriors. Kirahann didn't hesitate and rushed on the clearing and took the lizards by surprise. The first two lizards fell within seconds and after another minute the battle was won.

The child was only slightly wounded, but it was terrified and looked anxious at Kirahann. Step by step he moved forward, speaking softly to the child. Once again the paladin's open hearted nature achieved it's purpose. The little ogre calmed down, and with his little knowledge of the ogre tongue Kirahann found out, that the little one had got lost.

The way back to Oggok was uneventful. The closer they came to the ogre town, the more Kirahann worried about meeting the parents of the ogre. There was no open war between the humans from Freeport and the Ogres. But the two people hated each other and it was never heard of a human visiting Oggok and coming back alive. When Kirahann came into sight of the village he stoped and waited for the ogres to come out. It didn't take long and a group of ogres, armed to the teeth rushed towards him. Thanks to his hard training as a holy knight, Kirahann kept his self control and didn't move a feet, letting his sword in the sheath on his back. Ten feet in front of Kirahann the ogres halted and stared at him, telling him to leave. Unimpressed he pointed at the child and towards the village. The group leader recognized the child and sent one of his men back to the village.

Some minutes later an ogre woman, at last Kirahann thought it to be one, rushed out of the village towards the child. Ignoring the paladin, the mother hugged her child comforting and babbled in a unexpected soft voice. As if just noticing him right now, she turned towards Kirahann and looked him deep in his eyes. There he could read suspiciousness and fear, but looking deeper he also recognized amazement and gratitude. It seemed like this moment stayed forever. Finaly she turned around and motioned the guards back to the village. Reluctant the guards turned around and returned to the village looking back from time to time.

I believe that was one of these moments that shaped Kirahann. Later he expressed it in his famous motto we all know:

'A Paladin's value is not measured by the number of people that know his name. But by the number of hearts he has touched'.

And our hearts you have touched, Kirahann. We wish you all the best for your Holy Quest and we hope to see you again in the world of Norrath.

Arthon, November 2000
