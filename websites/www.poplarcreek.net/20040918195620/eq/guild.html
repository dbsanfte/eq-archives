Councillor Sergo addresses the complaints of some of our newest    members.

Numerous times in recent weeks I have heard the pleas of an anguished young hobbit in the thicket.  Often our younger members are harassed and sometimes even killed and robbed by the miscreants with whom the gods have decreed we must share this realm.  These fledgeling adventurers naturally often turn to their elder guildmates for assistance.

Our guild is going through some growing pains now.  Founded just 5 months ago by three young, ambitious hobbits, none of whom had reached their tenth rank yet, we are now a prosperous guild with a good reputation and a solid core of councillors.  However, now that those three young hobbits and their cohorts from those early days of recruiting have claimed their family names, they roam farther and farther from the Rivervale, seeking adventure, glory, and booty for the guild's treasure chest..  As adventures become more distant and demanding, it becomes more and more of an effort for these guild members to help younger ones back home in the Misty Thicket.  This also affects recruiting, since the Thicket is the area where most of you first heard about the Patrol and signed up.  All this leaves our youngest members feeling a bit left out. 

I once left a fine guild for the same reason.  It seemed like a club of high-level people who all new each other and really didn't want to get to know me.

Younger guild members are sometimes disappointed when their calls for help are not answered.  I remember the frustration of being young and subject the the violent whims of Norrath's ill-formed minds, and I sympathize with the desire for protection and revenge.  The Patrol spends more time than any other guild I know of aiding its young recruits.  Councillors Sergo and Toddin are in the Thicket at least once a week, healing and blessing young hobbits who face danger.  Other members also stop by the Thicket any time they're at home and aid young members.  However, this does not mean that higher level guild members are "on call."  The mightiest Patrolmen have their own goals, their own quests, and their own battles to fight.  They will not  leave a good group and gate halfway across Norrath every time they are asked, nor will you, once you claim your family's name and begin to carve your own niche in hobbit history.  (But not into the trees!  That REALLY upsets our Karanan followers.)  While you, as a new member, will not be protected all the time, neither will you be obliged, when you become more powerful, to drop what you are doing to aid another.

One reason adventurers join a guild is to make it easier to find friends to travel with.  Some have been disappointed in their inability to find guild members to group with.  Of course, our membership is limited because of our racial requirement as well as our screening process.  We don't accept just anybody into the patrol.  And while this means fewer patrolmen to potentially group with, it also means you won't be grouped with anyone who doesn't know which end of the mace to hold. 

Both of the above problems can be allieviated with a little more involvement on the part of every Patrolman. 

First, all patromen should make an effort to take an interest in members  they don't know.  While I don't advocate taking all your time greeting people  in mystical guildspeak, every now and then when your adventures are slow,  look to see who's in Norrath (/w all hairwill do it) and strike up  a conversation with someone you don't know.  Could be as simple as "How  go your battles today?"  If they're busy, they'll let you know, but it still  makes the guild a friendlier place.  (Corrolary:  You may have noticed some  of us do not wear the cloak of anonymity.  It makes it easier to find groups  with guild members, and it's just friendlier.  Worried about PKs?   Most  of us can't tell a difference.  Worried about beggars?  It's okay to ignore  them.  And, as Gercheck has found out with his teleporting, being visible  can be good for business.)  And send in your picture to be put here on the magical nexus, so people can get to know you.

Second, young hobbits should become active recruiters for the guild.  Read the recruiting guidelines in the Council Chamber, and be ready to interview people yourself, then call for an officer to issue an invite.  When you group with someone you think would make a good patrolman, then you are ready to help add to our ranks.  (OOC - Keep in mind we are a roleplay guild.  Make sure you're not talking to a powergamer )  Be ready to refer interested hobbits to this magical nexus so they can learn more about us.  Of course, your recruits will reflect on you.  While a few murderous gankers get by the most careful screenings, you should be a careful judge of who you invite.  We are certainly interesting in quality of our members over quantity.

Thrid, the Council needs to consider promoting some younger hobbits onto the Council.  These members would be more likely to be near the Rivervale when an invite or assistance is needed.  Helping other members is a great way to get the Council's attention if you want to become an officer of the Patrol.

Fourth, we need help from those hobbits who have graduated from the Thicket's school of hard knocks and are now battling the goblins or orcs that menace nearby lands.  When you return home to resupply or bank, take the time to help out a young patrolman with a blessing, a couple of coins, or a few minutes of your watchful eye as the battle a difficult foe.

Fifth, the most experienced hobbits will continue to spend some of their time in the Thicket, blessing, encouraging, and spreading the word about the Patrol.  In fact, I'm off to my stump right now, if I could just find that cheddar I had a moment ago...
