Definitions
  1. Authorized Representative of the Department of Environmental Management -- is an employee or agent of the Department of Environmental Management who has been delegated authority to enforce the provisions of this Chapter.
  2. Bay Island - the islands of Prudence, Patience, Hope, Dutch, East & West Island (off Sakonnet Point), Little & Big Gould, and Hog.
  3. Blank Gun - any firearm designed to discharge only blank cartridges and is incapable of propelling any type of projectile.
  4. Bow - long bow (straight, limb, reflex, recurve, and compound bow)
  5. Camping - the erecting of a tent or shelter of natural or synthetic material, preparing a sleeping bag or other bedding material for use, parking of a motor vehicle, motor home or trailer, or mooring of a vessel for the apparent purpose of overnight occupancy.
  6. Crossbow - a bow fixed perpendicularly on a stock having a trigger mechanism to release the bowstring, and often incorporating or accompanied by a mechanism by which the bow is drawn, held, and/or trigger released.
  7. Designated Areas - any part of a public reservation specially set aside for one or more purposes and identified as such either by the posting of a sign or signs or so posted at the public reservation headquarters.
  8. Disabled Person - an individual who has been issued by the Registry of Motor Vehicles, and whose vehicle displays a current certificate issued pursuant to RI General Laws 31-28-7 entitling that individual to parking privileges afforded to a disabled individual.
  9. Domestic Animals - animals, which through extremely long association with humans, have been bred to a degree which has resulted in genetic changes affecting the temperament, color, conformation, or other attributes of the species to an extent that makes them unique and distinguishable from wild individuals of their species and as listed in R.I.G.L. Title 20, Chapter 18, Section 4 of the Importation of Wild Animal Regulations of the Department of Environmental Management.
  10. Firearm - any machine gun, pistol, air rifle, air pistol, and "blank gun." "BB gun." So-called, or other instrument from which steel or metal projectiles are propelled, or which may readily be converted to expel a projectile, except recurve, compound, or longbows and except such instruments propelling such projectiles which instruments are designed or normally used for a primary purpose other than as a weapon. The frame or receiver of any such weapon shall be construed as a firearm under the provisions of this section.
  11. Float Tube - any device manufactured and sold for use as a fishing float tube.
  12. Group - a collection of at least 10 persons, which may be military, public or private, may or may not be chartered or incorporated, profit or non-profit and having a formal name, with or without officers and/or dues.
  13. Management Area - public lands that are owned by the state of Rhode Island under the jurisdiction of DEM's Division of Fish & Wildlife or Forest Environment or in conjunction with each other or subject to the enforcement authority of the DEM.
  14. Management Councils - internal DEM coordinating group, which includes representatives from the divisions of Fish & Wildlife, Parks & Recreation, Planning and Development and Forest Environment. The councils are responsible for issuing use permits, land use issues, improvements, maintenance and projects in management areas.
  15. Motor Vehicle - every vehicle that is self-propelled including, but not limited to, every vehicle that is propelled by an internal combustion engine or electric power, but not operated upon rails, or upon water.
  16. Migratory Waterfowl - per RIGL 20-3-34, all waterfowl species in the family anatidae including wild ducks, geese, brant, and swans.
  17. Native Wildlife - animals occurring naturally, either presently or historically, within the boundaries of the State of Rhode Island
  18. Off-Highway Recreational Vehicles-any motorized vehicles which were manufactured or modified for use on terrain other than highways and are not registered with the registry of motor vehicles or its equivalent agency in a state other than Rhode Island for operation on highways.
  19. Official Written Permission - written permission obtained from an Authorized Representative of the Department of Environmental Management.
  20. Open Air Fire - any fire in the outdoors or in a structure not completely enclosed by walls and a roof, including charcoal grills and gas stoves.
  21. Park - any property under the jurisdiction of the DEM Division of Parks and Recreation.
  22. Person - an individual, firm, corporation, society, association, partnership, or private or public body.
  23. Public Reservation - any property under the care, control or custody of the Department of Environmental Management.
  24. Rock Climbing - activities associated with a person moving upon, along, or across a non-horizontal rock surface, including but not limited to scrambling, bouldering, free climbing, assisted climbing, and technical climbing.
  25. Snowmobiles - a motor vehicle designed to travel over ice or snow, and supported in whole or in part by skis, belts, or cleats.
  26. Special Event - any activity in which seventy-five (75) to three hundred (300) people are proposed or registered to participate.
  27. Special Use Permit - an authorized written permission for groups between 10-75 people or research studies to use the management areas.
  28. Trash - garbage, rubbish, waste papers, bottles or cans, debris, litter, oil, solvents, liquid waste, or other discarded materials.
  29. Vehicle - every device in, upon, or by which a person or property is or may be transported or drawn on land, except snowmobiles including devices moved by human power or used exclusively upon stationary rails or track.
  30. Wildlife Food Patches - any parcel of land that is physically planted or manipulated to provide food and/or cover for wildlife.

Animals, Including Horses and Dogs
  1. No person shall bring into a state park property or picnic area any animal unless under control at all times on a leash of not more than six feet [6'] in length. The handler of all animals is responsible for cleaning up and proper disposal of all animal wastes.
  2. In management areas, persons may walk leashed dogs and domestic animals. The leash shall not exceed 25 feet in length with animal under control at all times.
  3. All dogs must be wearing a collar with valid license tag. See Section 7 for additional dog and dog training regulations on management areas.
  4. No person shall leave an animal unattended in a vehicle with the exception of dogs that are being used for hunting or dog trials between September 1 and March 31.
  5. No person shall bring onto any state bathing beach, East Beach/Ninigret Conservation Area, or Burlingame picnic area any animal from April 1 through September 30.
  6. Requirements for using the Reynolds Horseman's Area, Arcadia:
    • Horses shall be allowed only on bridle trails and in designated areas. All users will clean up manure at campsite and dispose it is bins provided.
    • Ring is to be used for riding or attended exercising, not to be used for turn out or hitching.
    • No camping permitted around ring.
    • Fencing must be self-supporting with no nails or fasteners allowed in trees
    • Horses are not to be tied to trees.
    • Cemetery is not to be used for horse turnout.
    • Only one site can be used at a time.
    • Camping allowed in designated areas only. Also, see Section 12 Fires
  7. Horseback riding is prohibited in the Great Swamp Management Area during the established small game season and the established shotgun deer season.
  8. Horses shall be allowed only in designated areas and bridle trails, except by official written permission.
  9. During the established small game and shotgun deer hunting seasons, horses are prohibited from the Arcadia Dog Training Area with the exception of Thornley and Midway Trails.
  10. The exercising, training, or running of dogs from March 15 through August 15 is prohibited except by official written permission, or in designated areas. Also, See 16.12 Dog Training and field trials.
  11. Cross-Country skiers are not allowed to bring dogs on designated cross-country ski trails.
  12. The release of any animal within any State Park, Beach, or other public reservation is prohibited.
    See also Section 16 Hunting Regulations and Section 7 Camping for additional animal regulations

Annoying or Questionable Activities
  1. Loitering is prohibited in or around restrooms.
  2. Activities, which interfere with designated activities, are prohibited. It is unlawful for any person to engage in disorderly conduct as described below: person commits disorderly conduct if he or she intentionally, knowingly, or recklessly Engages in fighting or threatening or in violent or tumultuous behavior; or In a public place or near a private residence that he or she has no right to occupy, disturbs another person by making loud and unreasonable noise which under the circumstances would disturb a person of average sensibilities; or
    • Directs at another person in a public place offensive words which are likely to provoke a violent reaction on the part of the average person so addressed; or
    • Alone or with others, obstructs a highway, street, sidewalk, railway, waterway, building entrance, elevator, aisle, stairway, or hallway to which the public or a substantial group of the public has access or any other place ordinarily used for the passage of persons vehicles, or conveyances; or
    • Engages in conduct which obstructs or interferes physically with a lawful meeting, procession, or gathering; or
    • Public nudity is prohibited.
  3. Operation of sirens or any noise audible to a person of reasonable sensitive hearing at a distance of 200 feet from its source and other noise making devices or broadcast loud or raucous sounds or noises by use of an electrical amplifying system or sound track, is prohibited except by official written permission. Engine powered model airplanes, model boats, rockets and model cars shall be operated only upon receipt of official written permission from individual Park Facility Manager.
  4. The possession or use of a paint ball gun or any other mechanism, which propels a projectile, is prohibited, except as otherwise provided in these regulations.

Archaeology
  1. No one shall excavate, disturb, or conduct field investigations or any site or underwater historic property, nor shall anyone disturb or remove any specimens from any property under the care, control or custody of the Department of Environmental Management without first obtaining the written approval of the Director and a permit from the State Historical Preservation Commission.
  2. All archaeological sites, underwater historic property and archaeological specimens as defined in the Antiquities Act of Rhode Island, R.I.G.L., ch. 42-45.1, are the property of the State of Rhode Island.
  3. Metal detectors and other location devices are restricted to designated areas during specified time periods.

Bicycles, Operator Propelled Vehicles
  1. No person shall operate or ride a bicycle, scooter, skate board or other operator propelled vehicle or device in any unit or portion thereof, of the Division of Parks and Recreation after the Regional Manager has made a finding that conditions are unsafe for the operation of such vehicles and has issued an order prohibiting such activity.
  2. Ice-skating is permitted only in designated areas and at designated times.
  3. Cross-country skiing is permitted, except where specifically prohibited.
  4. Downhill skiing and sledding is permitted only in designated areas.
  5. Windsurfers must launch and land only from designated areas.
  6. During the established small game and shotgun deer hunting seasons, the use of mountain bikes is prohibited from the Arcadia Dog Training Area with the exception of Thornley and Midway Trails. Also see 16.7 fluorescent orange requirement.

Boating Regulations
  1. Boating is prohibited within the impoundment created by the Great Swamp dike, except with official written permission. Official written permission is not required from July 15 through October 15. All motors are prohibited at all times.
  2. Boats are prohibited on the following ponds:
    • Round Top Pond, Burrillville
    • Little Round Top Pond, Burrillville
    • Peck Pond, Burrillville
    • Gristmill Pond, Glocester

    • Frosty Hollow Pond, Exeter

  3. The launching, hauling, and operation of boats with motors of any kind is prohibited from state-owned and/or operated ramps on the following ponds:
    • Upper Roaring Brook Ponds, Exeter
    • Asa Pond, South Kingstown
    • Alton Pond, Hopkinton
  4. The launching, hauling, and operation of boats with motors of any kind, except electric trolling motors, is prohibited from state-owned and/or operated ramps on the following bodies of water:
    • Spring Lake, Burrillville
    • Union Pond, Burrillville
    • Clarkville Pond, Glocester
    • Lake Washington, Glocester
    • Shippee Mill Pond, Foster
    • Pawtuxet River (Hope), Scituate
    • Carbuncle Pond, Coventry
    • Silver Spring Lake, North Kingstown
    • Barber's Pond, South Kingstown
    • Blue Pond, Hopkinton
    • Simmons Mill Pond, Little Compton
    • Browning Mill Pond, Richmond
  5. The use of motors in excess of 10 horsepower on boats launched, hauled, or operated from state-owned and/or operated ramps is prohibited on the following bodies of water:
    • Wilson Reservoir, Burrillville
    • Keach Pond, Glocester
    • Smith and Sayles Reservoir (Sand Dam), Glocester
    • Bowdish Reservoir, Glocester
    • Woonaquatucket Reservoir (Stump Pond), Smithfield
    • Indian Lake, South Kingstown
    • Tucker Pond, South Kingstown
    • Asheville Pond, Hopkinton

    • Chapman's Pond, Westerly
  6. No person shall launch any motorboat with a motor in excess of 10 horsepower on the waters of Pascoag Reservoir at the state-owned launching ramp on Schoolhouse Road, Glocester, between the Saturday before Memorial Day and Labor Day of each year inclusive.
  7. The use of motors in excess of 10 horsepower on boats launched, hauled, or operated from the state-owned ramp on Wakefield Pond in Burrillville is prohibited at all times. The use of any motor on a boat from this state-owned ramp, except electric trolling motors, is prohibited from June 20 to September 20 of any year, inclusive.
  8. Power boats are prohibited on the waters of Olney Pond in Lincoln Woods State Park on Saturdays, Sundays, and holidays from June 15 through September 15.
  9. Power boats owned by persons other than the State of Rhode Island with motors in excess of 10 HP are prohibited upon Olney Pond except with official written permission from Lincoln Woods Park Manager.
  10. The use of motors in excess of 10 horsepower on boats launched, hauled, or operated on the waters of Breakheart Pond in Exeter and John L. Curran State Park is prohibited.
  11. Only vehicles launching or landing a boat will be permitted to park in designated boat trailer areas.
  12. The mooring of boats, the parking of vehicles or engaging in any other activity on land or water so as to obstruct the use of a boat-launching ramp by others is prohibited.
  13. Boats shall be launched or/hauled only at designated areas .
  14. Boats shall be tied up at state boat ramps for no longer than 30 minutes.

Camping for All Camping Areas
(see Section 8 for Specific Areas)
  1. The washing of clothes, cooking utensils and actions relating to one's personal hygiene shall be limited to a designated campsite or to facilities provided for that purpose.
  2. Camping is allowed only in designated areas with official written permission from individual Park Facility.
  3. The person to whom a camping permit is granted shall be 18 years of age or older, shall be an occupant of the campsite, shall state the first and last names of members of the party, and shall be responsible for his or her party's compliance with the rules and regulations of the Department of Environmental Management and all other applicable laws. Only those members listed shall use the campsites. Only one family (immediate family) is allowed per campsite. Non-family groups shall be limited to six persons per campsite. Any person or persons voided on a camping permit are not allowed to reenter any camping area for the duration of the camping season.
  4. All campers shall check in with the caretaker at the beginning of the camping period and shall surrender their camping permit to the caretaker at the end of the camping period, where applicable.
  5. No pets are allowed in camping areas, unless with written permission, with the exception of working assistant dogs.
  6. All visitors shall be out of the campsites by 10:00 PM each night. Campers shall be quiet between the hours of 10:00 PM and 7:00 am.
  7. All campground permit holders must be 18 years of age or older. No person shall occupy a campground for more than a continuous two-week period, with a seven-day minimum break prior to any reoccupancy. Permit renewals are limited to three within the two-week maximum period and must be obtained prior to 8:30 am on the date of permit expiration. Charlestown Breachway, Ninigret Conservation Area are excepted from this rule.
  8. Each campsite must be occupied on the first night of the permit and must be occupied at least four (4) nights during each week of the camping period. Noncompliance with the above is grounds for immediate revocation of the permit.
  9. Public nudity is prohibited. No person shall disrobe other than in tents, campers or other properly designated structures.

Camping for Specific Areas
  1. Camping for Specific Management Areas
    • Reservations for backpack areas and shelters will be accepted prior to stay. Payment for shelter reservations must be received within fourteen working days of the date reservation is made and before the date of use. All permits must be picked up in person from Arcadia Forestry Headquarters.
    • Camping permits run from 3:00 PM on date of permit to 2:00 PM at end of camping period, except for the George Washington camp ground where camping permits run from 2:00 PM on the date of issue to 11:00 AM at the end of the camping period.
    • No person may occupy a backpack area or shelter for more than a continuous 3-day period with a 7-day minimum break prior to any reoccupancy.
    • No person may occupy Escoheag Horsemen's Area for more than a continuous 4-day period with a 1-day minimum break prior to any reoccupancy, unless with official written permission from Arcadia Forestry Headquarters. Use of this area is limited to campers with horses.
    • No person may occupy canoe campsites for more than one (1) night, with a minimum break of seven (7) days prior to any reoccupancy, unless with official written permission from Arcadia Forestry Headquarters.
    • In the Arcadia and George Washington Campgrounds, no more than two (2) cars shall be parked at each campsite. Vehicles are prohibited in backpack areas, unless with official written permission from respective Forestry Headquarters.
    • Visitors are prohibited from backpack areas and shelters, unless with official written permission from respective Forestry Headdquarters.
    • Campsites must be occupied each night of the camping period in backpack and shelter areas and Escoheag Horsemen's Area.
    • No more than two (2) tents per site shall be allowed in Arcadia and George Washington Campgrounds and backpack areas, unless with official written permission from respective Forestry Headquarters.
  2. Camping for specific Parks properties
    • No more than two motor vehicles shall be parked at each campsite.
    • Camping permits shall be issued at the parks (with the exception of Fishermen's Memorial State Park) on a "first-come, first-served" basis. Campers shall be ready to set up camp on date of issuance. Permits shall not be issued by phone.
    • Camping permits shall run from 1:00 PM on the date of permit to 11:00 am at the end of the camping period as indicated on the permit.
    • Reservations shall be accepted for Burlingame State Park Cabins by mail ONLY from April 1, at Burlingame State Park, 1 Burlingame State Park, Charlestown, RI 02813.

Dunes
  1. Walking or crossing over the dunes is permitted only in designated areas.

Fees
  1. The fee structure designated "Rules and Regulations: Certain Fees for the Use of State Beaches, Parks, Recreational Areas, Management Areas, and Fees for the Use of Any Services or Facilities under the Jurisdiction of the Department of Environmental Management", as submitted to the Secretary of State May 6, 1991, and amended, shall remain in effect and are incorporated herein by reference.
  2. For persons with disabilities. - No fee shall be charged to any person with a disability in accordance with the provisions of current State Law, RIGL 32-1-17. For purpose of this section fees shall include all fees for parking, admittance, or other user-fees for playing golf. The term fees shall not include licensing fees; camping fees, picnic table fees or specialized facility use fees, including but not limited to, fees for the use of equestrian areas, performing arts centers, game fields, and mule shed.

Firearms and Archery Equipment
  1. Possession of a firearm or bow and arrow is prohibited in the state parks unless otherwise authorized in these regulations, see sections 11.1, 16.13 or with a special use permit issued by the director or designee.. In addition to above, blank guns are permitted for the purpose of dog training in designated areas from January 1 to September 30.
  2. Target shooting is prohibited except in designated areas by and in accordance with official written permission from Fish and Wildlife Field Headquarters.
  3. Possession of a loaded firearm, or a firearm from the magazine of which all shells or cartridges have not been removed, in or on any vehicle or conveyance while in or upon any part of a public reservation is prohibited.
  4. No person shall hunt or trap or carry firearms within a public reservation unless in possession of a valid hunting or trapping license and other official written permission as required. See Section 16 Hunting Regulations and current Abstract of Wildlife Laws
  5. Hunting is prohibited within areas designated with appropriate signage
  6. Hunting is prohibited except in designated areas.
  7. No hunting is permitted within 500 feet of camping areas, picnic areas, ball fields, or occupied dwellings or structures, with the exception of archery hunting for deer with a safety zone of 200 feet. Carrying cross bows at any time is prohibited in parks.
Also, See Section 28 Target Range Regulations

Fires
  1. No person shall kindle an open fire, including charcoal fires, except in designated areas or with official written permission. From March 15 through May 15, no open fires are allowed between 10:00 AM and 5:00 PM without burning permit issued by local fire department.

Food, Beverages, Vending
  1. No person shall possess, drink, sell or offer for sale any alcoholic beverages except as follows: Permission maybe requested from the Director of the Department of Environmental Management or his or her authorized representative to consume, serve, sell or offer for sale alcoholic beverages at events held at Fort Adams State Park, and at the Annual Octoberfest Celebration held at World War II State Park. Requests shall be submitted in writing to the Director, or his/her authorized representative sixty (60) days in advance of the proposed date for the event. Permission may be given at the Director's discretion only as a part of a formal license agreement between the State of Rhode Island and the entity requesting permission. If granted, permission shall apply only to areas and times designated by the Director at his/her discretion and specified in the license. The Director may impose additional restrictions which he or she deems necessary.
  2. No person shall offer for sale any goods or services without official written permission from the Director of the Department of Environmental Management or his/her authorized representative.

Groups, Meetings, Distribution of Printed Matter
  1. Persons may distribute or exhibit printed or written material, and or hold public meetings only in accordance with an official written permit which shall regulate only the time, place and manner of such exhibit, distribution or meeting.
  2. Groups of 100 persons or more utilizing public reservations under the jurisdiction of the Division of Parks and Recreation, scientific collectors and groups of 10 or more in other public reservations, must submit prior written request to and have official written permission for a Special Use Permit from the Director or his/her authorized representative.
    See Section 24 for Large Event Permits for group between 75-300 people
  3. Any group or organization bringing youths under the age of eighteen (18) into any State Park, Beach, Recreation, or Management Area must provide adequate adult supervision for these youths and their activities.

Hours of Operation
  1. All Management areas are closed from one-half (1/2) hour after sunset to one-half (1/2) hour after sunset. except for fishing, hunting, launching and/or mooring of boats, snowmobiling on established trails in Arcadia and George Washington-Pulaski Section (see Section 20), or as provided by a written permit issued by the Director or his authorized representative, or as otherwise provided under regulations promulgated in Section 32-2-4. No person or vehicle shall be allowed within the limits of a state park or beach area between the hours of sunset and sunrise the following day except by official written permission or unless an area is designated otherwise by written regulation.

Hunting Regulations - Management Areas
  1. The possession of a valid Rhode Island hunting, or combination hunting and fishing license, shall serve as a permit to hunt State Management Areas, and Tidal Lands pursuant to Titles 20 and 18, ˜and Sections˜ 20-18-2 and 20-18-3 of the General Laws.
  2. This permit as stated in 16.1 shall be valid only from the first day of dove season in September through the last day of February, except when check stations are in operation for upland game at the Great Swamp, Carolina, Durfee Hill, and Arcadia; for turkey hunting; when special permits are required for deer hunting; or for waterfowl hunting on state lands. (See current abstract for check station dates). Bow hunters and muzzleloading hunters hunting deer on state management areas do not need to check in or out.
  3. The carrying of firearms on state management areas at any time is prohibited except as provided in Management Area Regulations.
    Also, See Section 11 Firearms.
  4. Individuals must check in, and out, of state management hunter check stations each day they are open for small game season. Check station dates will be listed in the current Hunting Season Abstract.
  5. Dog training and all hunting, except for bow hunting for deer, and waterfowl hunting, as provided, is prohibited on state land during the five (5) day period prior to small game season, unless otherwise provided.
    See also Section 8
  6. From the period April 1 through August 15, annually, there shall be no trespassing, disturbing, or molesting of any species of nesting birds on Hope Island in the West Passage; Gould Island in the Sakonnet River, East Island and West Island off Sakonnet Point, or Gould Island in the East Passage, except by special permit. All cats, dogs, and other domestic animals are prohibited at all times upon these Islands, except that hunting dogs may be used during that period set aside by the Department for hunting of game birds and mammals on Hope Island only. No person shall engage in any activity that interferes or interrupts lawful hunting on a management area.
  7. Fluorescent Orange Requirement: Fluorescent orange safety clothing is required during the hunting season statewide for all hunters. To meet this requirement, safety clothing must be solid daylight fluorescent orange. Fluorescent camouflage does not meet this requirement. The hunter orange must be worn above the waist and be visible in all directions. Examples which meet the orange requirements are a hat that covers 200 square inches or combination of hat and vest covering 500 square inches. The following orange requirements apply:
    • Two hundred (200) square inches by small game hunters during the small game season.
    • Two hundred (200) square inches by muzzle-loaders during muzzle-loading season
    • Two hundred (200) square inches by archers when traveling to/from stands during muzzle-loading deer season
    • Five hundred (500) square inches by all hunters (including archers) during shotgun deer season
    • Waterfowl hunters, archery deer hunters (except during muzzle-loader and shotgun deer season), turkey hunters, and first segment dove hunters are exempt from orange requirements.
    • All other users of State Management areas, including but not limited to: hikers, bikers, horseback riders, are required to wear two hundred (200) square inches of solid daylight fluorescent orange from the third Saturday in October to the last day of February, annually.
  8. A hunting license is required when training dogs on wild game on state management areas, except when participating in state-sanctioned field trials.
  9. Complete dressing of game birds and mammals on state property is prohibited. Field dressing (removal of entrails only) is permitted. Head, skin, feathers, wings, tails, etc., shall remain attached to the carcass.
  10. Dogs on State Management Areas allowed only under the following rules. No dogs shall be trained, run, or exercised or be permitted to run at large on state management areas from March 15 to August 15, inclusive, without written permission of the Division of Fish and Wildlife except as otherwise provided in these regulations.
  11. Dog Training Areas -- Dog training areas shall be allowed on designated areas only, between January 1 and September 30. During this period, dogs may run freely provided they are under the command of the trainer by voice, training collar, or check cord.
    • Areas designated for training dogs are: the Black Farm Management Area; the Nicholas Farm Management Area north of the Trestle Trail; and the Arcadia Midway Trail Area confined to the developed field complex east of Brook Trail, south of Austin Farm Road and west of the established field edges comprising the Midway fields area. Field trial areas are as depicted on maps drawn by the Division of Fish and Wildlife.
    • Training hours shall be sunrise to sunset.
    • All persons using areas for training shall possess a hunting license.
    • All dogs using areas shall be licensed and have proof of rabies vaccinations.
    • Training with live birds (pheasants/quail) is permitted provided the bird is released unharmed and all necessary permits are obtained.
    • Shooting of live birds for training purposes is prohibited.
    • Sanctioned field trial events may be held at the following locations by requesting a special use permit application from the Applicable Management Councils: Arcadia, Carolina, Durfee Hill Marsh, and Burlingame (Durfee) Pond, and Nicholas Farm.
  12. JOHN L. CURRAN STATE PARK/MANAGEMENT AREA
    • All hunting shall be within the inner boundaries of the buffer zones marked as posted by the Department.
    • Only shotguns and bow & arrows may be possessed or used while hunting.
    • The hunting of and/or possession of Mourning Dove or Wild Pigeons (Rock Dove) is prohibited.
  13. Permits for waterfowl hunting within the Great Swamp Impoundment may be obtained only from the Great Swamp Field Headquarter
  14. Permits for trapping all management areas may be obtained from the Division of Fish & Wildlife at the Great Swamp Field Headquarters
  15. SIMMONS MILL POND MANAGEMENT AREA -- Only archery equipment, bow and arrows, may be possessed or used while hunting white-tailed deer within the Simmons Mill Pond Management in Little Compton.
    See also Section 11 Firearms

Miscellaneous Regulations
  1. The playing of golf or the driving of golf balls is permitted only in designated areas at designated time.
  2. Bicycles, mountain bikes, and horses shall only be used on designated trails and roads for said use.
  3. Horses, bicycles or motorcycles are prohibited in streams, rivers or bodies of water except at designated crossings (trails and roads.) Exceptions are granted to hikers, fishermen, boaters, hunters and swimmers using official designated areas in an appropriate manner.
  4. Closure of Management Areas and/or roads and trails may occur if environmental conditions warrant.
  5. Washing of vehicles, bicycles, or animals is prohibited.
  6. The feeding of all wildlife, migratory waterfowl, or any feral animal is prohibited within any State Park, Beach, or Management Area.
  7. There shall be no traveling by vehicles in streams, rivers or bodies of water except to cross at designated crossings (trails, roads).
  8. Wildlife Food patches can only be used by hikers, hunters, and for running of dogs. The use of food patches is prohibited between June 1 and August 15 except for the running of dogs in designated areas.
  9. Rock climbing restricted to designated areas with official written permission. Participants must sign a liability waiver form. Designated areas for rock climbing are Lincoln Woods, Wickaboxet Management Area and Durfee Hill Management. Permits are available from the office at Lincoln Woods, Arcadia Headquarters and George Washington Headquarters.
  10. All users of State Management Areas must wear at least 200 sq. in. of solid daylight fluorescent orange material such as a hat or vest, visible from all directions, from the 3 rd Saturday in October to the last day of February. Exceptions include boat launching sites, raccoon hunters at night, archers in tree stands or waterfowl hunters as provided.

Mooring
  1. No person shall moor, dock or berth a vessel, boat, or any other object overnight except in designated mooring, docking, or berthing areas.
  2. The Regional Manager may, by posting of an order, specify time limits for the mooring, docking, or berthing of a vessel, boat or any other object at mooring, docking, or berthing facilities. No person shall moor, dock, or berth a vessel, boat or any other object in violation of time limits so established.
    See also Section 27 Swimming, Bathing

Motorcycle use in Arcadia Management Area
  1. Motorcycle use is only allowed within designated areas of Arcadia Management Area subject to conditions 20.2 through 20.6 listed below. Town and state roads within the Arcadia Management Area which are not part of the designated route may be used at any time, provided all local and state motor vehicle regulations are adhered to. Designated trails for motorcycle use are subject to change or correction by the Department of Environmental Management if conditions warrant.

    The designated trail (18.9 miles total loop) for motorcycle use shall consist of gravel roads and fire trails as delineated on the Arcadia Trail Map and described as follows: Arcadia road; Austin Farm Road; Brook Trail; Barber Road; Blitzkrig Trail; Bliven Trail; Frosty hollow Road; Mt. Tom Road; Summit Road; Old Ten Rod Road (White Church Trail).

  2. Only street-legal registered motorcycles operated by riders who hold a valid motorcycle operator's license may use the area. Riders must also maintain liability insurance according to Rhode Island State law.
  3. No riding shall be allowed behind any gated gravel or fire trail unless said roads are designated for motorcycle use. Motorcycles are not allowed on hiking trails and may only use single-track trails when authorized through special event permits or special use permits.
  4. No new motorcycle trails shall be cut or created within the Arcadia Management Area.
  5. Trails may be periodically closed by DEM to use by motorcycles when habitat or natural resource protection issues, weather, or road conditions warrant. Such conditions may include, but are not limited to, high/extreme fire danger or seasonal muddy conditions. There shall be no motorcycle riding beyond gated portions of the loop during March, April, and before 1300 hours during May.
  6. Speed limit on the designated trail shall be 25 mph or as posted.
  7. Use of "Trials" motorcycles in the Arcadia Management Area is covered under a separate Memorandum of Understanding (MOU) between the Rhode Island Trials Club and DEM. The MOU designates the portion of Arcadia where Trials riding can occur and the time of year that use is permitted. The use of the management area by RITC and the MOU will be evaluated by DEM on an annual basis to determine if changes are necessary in order to protect the natural resources of the Arcadia Area.

Motor Vehicles
  1. The traffic laws of the State of Rhode Island are fully applicable unless these regulations provide otherwise.
  2. All motor vehicles must be legally street registered.
  3. Motor vehicles shall be operated only in designated areas, no motor vehicles are allowed off roads or trails where prohibited, except with official written permission.
  4. No person shall park a vehicle so as to block any road or trail or to prevent access to others, nor shall they block another vehicle in a designated parking area, nor shall any vehicle be parked in any area, where prohibited.
  5. The speed limit is 25 miles per hour unless otherwise posted.
  6. The operator of a motor vehicle shall grant the right-of-way to all pedestrians, persons on horseback and who are using or crossing roads, trails or highways.
  7. Airborne conveyances such as balloons, gliders, engine-powered airplanes and ultra lights shall not be operated without official written permission from Individual Park Facility Manager.
  8. Off-highway recreational vehicles shall be street legally registered and used in accordance with law. Their use shall be limited to designated areas or trails at designated times.
  9. No person shall wash, polish, clean, change oil, or repair any motor vehicle or motorcycle in a public reservation.
  10. Snowmobile Operation -General-Snowmobiles shall be allowed at designated areas and at times designated for those areas.
    • Snowmobiles must operate within the posted speed limit for that area
    • Operators shall grant the right-of-way to all pedestrian, horses and vehicular traffic when operating and crossing roads or trails.
    • Snowmobiles shall not operate in a manner intended to or reasonably be expected to harass, drive or pursue any wildlife.
    • In addition to firearm restrictions in RIGL 31-3.2, carrying of bows and arrows-all arrows shall not be notched and shall be secured to the bow or in a quiver or other container
    • Snowmobile Operation-Management Areas
      • Hours of operation are one-half (1/2) hour before sunrise to 11:00 p.m. in Arcadia and George Washington-Pulaski Area, only. During the period one-half (1/2) hour after sunset to 11:00 p.m., the buddy system must be used.
      • Hours of operation are 1 one-half (1/2) hour before sunrise to one-half (1/2) hour after sunset in Burlingame Management Area, Wickaboxet, and Woody Hill, only.
      • During the established shotgun deer season, snowmobiling is not permitted in management areas
    • Snowmobiles Operation-Parks
      • Hours of operation are sunrise to sunset in Burlingame, Colt, Lincoln Woods and Snake Den.
        Also, See RIGL Chapter 31-3.2 Snowmobiles
  11. The vehicle patterns as established by the filing on May 5, 1972 Regulations of the Department of Natural Resources for Colt State Park, which became effective on June 1, 1972 shall remain in force and are incorporated herein by reference
  12. The Les Pawson Loop shall have a one-way counter-clockwise traffic pattern with two exceptions. When the gates across the road are closed, traffic shall be two-way. That section of road, which lies between Twin River Road and the North Parking Lot area, shall be two-way.
  13. Commercial vehicles in excess of two and one-half [2-1/2] tons gross weight are prohibited on the highway running through Veterans Memorial Parkway in East Providence, Dean Parkway in Cranston, and Narragansett Parkway in Warwick, except by official written permission.

  1. No refunds are allowed unless authorized by the director or designee.

Special Events
  1. Arcadia and George Washington Management Areas
    • Special Event permits are required for any group of 75 participants or more, and shall not exceed a total of 300 participants.
    • Permits will be issued during the period of June 20 through September 30 ONLY.
    • Exemptions from the limitation on the maximum participants and/or time of year restrictions may be granted by the DEM for educational workshops, use of picnic/beach areas, and use DEM boat launching ramps for fishing tournaments.
    • The Arcadia and George Washington Management Areas will be granted four (4) special events permits each during the designated period. These permits will be granted for these Management Areas ONLY.
    • Applications to hold a special event must be submitted to either the Arcadia Management Council or Northwest Management Council for review and approval. Applications must be submitted from January 1 to the last day of February of the year that the event will take place. Applications will be reviewed by DEM to determine if they meet the requirements specified by this policy. If necessary, permit applications will be selected by a lottery drawing that will be held on the first Monday in March at 1200 hours (noon). In the event that all the allotted special event permits are not filled by the first Monday in August, remaining permits will be issued on a first-come, first-served basis. No more than two special event permits will be issued per year per individual recreation use type to allow fair access to other user groups.
    • DEM, at its sole discretion, will determine the suitability of a proposed event of the management areas requested and may refuse permission if the event threatens the integrity of the management area's natural resources, including but not limited to soil, water, fish, wildlife, and forest and DEM's responsibility to provide for availability to the public for multiple use of the area.
    • DEM at its sole discretion can impose restrictions or refuse to permit an event during periods of high or extreme fire danger or where environmental, public health, and/or safety concerns so dictate.
    • Special event organizers shall be responsible for the following in running an event:
      • Paying for adequate DEM law enforcement personne l during events.
      • Notifying and obtaining written permission for said use from all local towns/cities affected by the event. Written permission must be submitted before the permit will be issued.
      • Notifying state and local police, fire, and rescue of the event.
      • All event organizers shall provide a liability insurance policy of $1,000,000 and must sign an indemnification of the state and all its employees.
      • All participants must sign a liability waiver and release for the State.
      • Event sponsors shall be responsible for repairing any damage to the management area caused by the event.
      • Event sponsors are responsible for removing all signs, markers, and trash from the management area caused by the event within one week of its conclusion.
      • Event sponsors shall provide to DEM an estimation of the number of spectators that can be expected for an event.
      • DEM reserves the right to include additional special conditions on any permitted event if conditions so warrant.

Target Range Regulations
  1. A range permit is required prior to use of shooting range by any individual.
  2. Range may be used from 8:00 A.M. to ½ HOUR BEFORE SUNSET, seven days a week, provided the shooter has a valid range permit.
  3. Range is closed to the public during all hunting seasons.
  4. Individuals using the range will assume responsibility for their actions.
  5. Misuse of the range will result in the revocation of the range permit.
  6. All minors (under 18 years of age) must be accompanied by an adult responsible for the minor's actions.
  7. All shooters are to police their firing points for empty casings when they have completed their firing.
  8. Park in designated areas only.
  9. Guests will remain behind the firing line at all times.
  10. PERMITTED FIREARMS:
    • Rifles - Standard velocity .22 caliber rimfire only, using .22 long, short or long rifle ammunition only. Stingers, expediter, yellow jackets or .22 caliber magnums prohibited.
    • Shotguns - all gauges, using shot ammunition only. Rifled slugs may be used only when sighting in at the designated firing point.
    • Muzzle-loading firearms - all calibers and gauges.
    • Handguns - .22 caliber long, short or long rifle. Larger handguns permitted with wad cutter ammunition only Stingers, expediters, yellow jackets or .22 magnum prohibited.
    • No firearm is allowed to be discharged more than one shot every two (2) seconds.
    • Use of paper rifle and handgun range targets and clay pigeons on shotgun range only. No metal target stands or targets.
    • Keep the muzzle of the firearm under control and pointed in a safe direction at all times. Treat all firearms as if they were loaded.
    • Actions of uncased firearms are to be left open and safety on except when on the firing line ready to fire.
    • Firing on all ranges will cease when it is necessary for a shooter to go downrange to the targets.
    • When the 50-foot firing line is in use, there will be no firing at any other distance.
    • Firearms being transported from the parking lot to the range will be unloaded and cased.
    • Shooting at objects on the range other than the prescribed targets will be construed as destruction of state property and will be cause for revocation of the range permit.
    • Primary use of the range is for Hunter Education Student Training. Range may be closed without notice for training. Range may be closed without notice for upkeep, maintenance, or safety. Secondary use is practice live firing by the general public.

Trash, Litter
  1. No person shall bring into any public reservation any trash, ashes, garbage or other material for the purpose of disposal.
  2. All trash and waste material shall be deposited in receptacles provided for that purpose and shall not be left on the grounds or waters.
  3. In areas designated as Carry In/Carry Out , all trash must be carried out of the area for proper disposal.

Trees, Shrubs, Buildings
  1. No person shall cut, remove or damage any tree, stone walls, natural element or feature, shrub or vegetation except with official written permission, nor shall any person deface or alter any structure, sign, or other public property or improvements.

Area Specific Regulations
  1. Bay Islands
    • Camping in the Bay Islands is prohibited
    • From the period April 1 through August 15, annual, there shall be no trespassing, disturbing, or molesting of any species of nesting birds on Hope Island in the West Passage; Gould Island in the Sakonnet River, East Island and West Island off Sakonnet Point, or Gould Island in the East Passage, except by special permit official written permission from authorized Representative of the Department of Environmental Management. All cats, dogs and other domestic animals are prohibited at all times upon these islands, except that hunting dogs may be used during the period set aside by the Department for hunting of game birds and mammals on Hope Island only.
  2. Beavertail State Park
    • The Rules and Regulations for the Development and Operation of Beavertail State Park, Jamestown, Rhode Island, which were received by the Office of the Secretary of State on August 15, 1980 shall remain in force and are incorporated herein by reference.
  3. Charlestown Breachway
    • Camping permits shall be issued at the Charlestown Breachway on a "first-come, first-served" basis. Camping is limited to fully self-contained vehicles. The permits shall run from 1:00 PM on date of issuance to 11:00 am at end of camping period.
    • No canvas top camping units shall be allowed in the day use areas or camping areas.
    • No camping shall be allowed in the Day Use Lot.
    • At Charlestown Breachway the maximum period of permit is seven days. There shall be a four-day minimum break prior to any reoccupancy.
    • All visitors to the camping area will pay the daily parking rate and park in the day use parking lot.
    • Only one additional non-towed vehicle is allowed to be parked in the camping area per site. Vehicle pass is required.
  4. Fisherman's Memorial State Park
    • Reservations shall be accepted for Fishermen's Memorial State Park by mail only from January 15 at Fishermen's Memorial State Park, Point Judith Road, Route 108, Narragansett, Rhode Island 02882.
  5. Galilee State Beach
    • The parking lot at Salty Brine State Beach is for the use of State Beach Patrons only. All vehicles using the lot must fit within the designated parking spaces and cannot protrude or obstruct any traffic within the parking lot.
  6. Ninigret Conservation Area/ East Beach
    • No more than 30 day use barrier beach vehicles, DEM inspected, are permitted beyond the beach parking lot at any one time.
    • Camping permits shall be issued at the Ninigret area on a "first-come, first serve" basis. Camping is limited to self-contained vehicles, properly DEM inspected and licensed. Only 20 camping permits may be issued at any one time. The permits shall run from 1:00 PM on the date of issuance to 11:00 am at the end of camping period.
    • At the end of a consecutive 72-hour period, self-contained units must leave the Ninigret area and empty holding tanks at designated dumping stations. This operation shall be verified by the clerk on duty at the dump station.
    • No person may occupy a campground for more than a continuous four-day period with a three-day minimum break prior to any reoccupancy.
    • Parking shall be allowed only in designated areas. All holes and ruts caused by immobilized vehicles shall be filled, and the area left in good condition by the operator of the vehicle causing damage.
    • All persons and vehicles must vacate the designated beach parking area by sunset. Parking is prohibited in the beach parking area before sunrise.
    • Day use vehicles displaying a season pass or paying the daily beach fee will be allowed only in the beach parking area designated for this purpose, unless given official written permission to use the Back Track.
  7. Purgatory Chasm
    • Parking is prohibited from sunset to sunrise the following day.
    • Parking in excess of 30 minutes is prohibited.
  8. Salter Grove
    • No person or vehicle shall be allowed within the limits of the State Park at Salter Grove, in the City of Warwick, between the hours of sunset and sunrise the following day except by official written permission by City of Warwick.
  9. Meshanticut Grove
    • No person or vehicle shall be allowed within the limits of Meshanticut State Park, in the City of Cranston, between the hours of sunset and sunrise the following day except by official written permission.
