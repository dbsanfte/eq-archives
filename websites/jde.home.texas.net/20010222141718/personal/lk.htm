Lyrren El' Tanneth

Order of the Fang

 

Keila

Order of the Eye

 

 

 

The boat crashed through the violent sea, at one moment perched precariously on a peak, the next dwarfed by the towering waves as it plunged into the troughs. In the pouring rain the mighty sorceress Tanneth stood rooted to the deck, her robe whipping and flapping in the gale. The masts groaned and bent threateningly above her but she ignored them, intent only on focusing her powers, helping the boat to battle the raging storm. Tanneth was unique in Norrath, a sorceress who was a master of not one, but two of the paths of magic. She was called both Enchanter and Wizard, and hence none was her equal. Although the seas were notorious for disrupting the powers of the mages of Norrath, most were not able to cast at all while on board, Tanneth had no such trouble. In this storm her powers were greatly reduced however. Normaly she would never have travelled by boat at all, there was no need, the powers of the Wizard allowed her to gate instantaneously to anywhere she willed. But she and her guild were accompanying a precious cargo, an ancient library she had unearthed in the ruins of a once mighty Elven city, now known only as Befallen and home to the undead. Hundreds of books, a wealth of knowledge that the Erudites would need in the coming battle against the darkness. That the darkness was coming she had no doubt, for more than half her life she had travelled Norrath, searching for knowledge to help her people prepare, and she had seen the signs. If they were not prepared even great Erudin, the fortress city of Odus, would fall to the dark hordes.

But it seemed the darkness had learned of her discovery. Erud's Crossing was notorious for rough seas, but this storm was not natural, she could sense it. The will driving it was not that of Karana the Rainkeeper, or Prexus the Oceanlord, but something far more malevolent...perhaps Innoruuk himself was conspiring against her. If that were true, the boat would not survive. Alone she was no match for a God, and no other on board was able to cast in such a storm. The darkness had bided it's time, waited until she was most vulnerable. Not yet willing to give in, to sacrifice the library that could be their salvation, she continued to enchant the boat, strengthening it against the onslaught of the waves. Her staff glowed white hot with the power she was channeling through it to the deck beneath her. But even so the timbers were screaming under the strain as the gigantic waves crashed against the hull again and again.

She could have gated to safety at any time, but that would have meant abandoning the library, her companions and the crew of the boat to the murky depths of Erud's Crossing. Instead Tanneth continued the enchantment and wished Innoruuk would show himself so she could fling at least one mighty firebolt at his hideous face. It was a vain hope, it may not have been Innoruuk at all, the darkness had many faces and they were nearly always hidden.

Too late Tanneth heard the crack as a bolt of lightening splintered the massive centre mast. Exhausted from her efforts to keep the boat together, she had no time to react as it fell directly towards her. "Far too convenient" she thought, and then the darkness claimed her as the mast crashed down...

* * * * *

Some time later, how long she didn't know, she regained consciousness. She was roped securely to one of the solid doors that had once sealed the cabins of the boat. The door was floating now under a bitterly hot sun, all evidence of the storm was gone. Of the boat there was no sign at all. In despair a low moan escaped her lips, which came out more as a croak and she realised how desperately thirsty she was.

"My Lady", a voice whispered beside her.

With a start she tried to sit up but realised that was impossible so instead turned her head to see a young acolyte sprawled on the door beside her. He was too weak to even lift his head.

"Larman...", she croaked. "Where are the others?"

"All dead my Lady", he whispered.

"And the library?", even though she already knew the answer.

"Gone".

Tanneth could see tears starting to well in his eyes, and every word was too obviously an effort. He was close to death, and she knew she was not far herself. Only the spark of her power was keeping her alive, and that spark was too small to save them.

"How long?"

"Four days, I think".

Tanneth groaned. Four days without food or water. She had no way to regain her powers in such a weakened state. But she should not have survived at all. Unconcious she should have drowned.

"Thank you Larman...for saving me".

"It was not enough...my Lady", was the last thing the young acolyte ever said.

Tied to all that remained of the boat, alone and too weak to free herself, the greatest sorceress Norrath had ever known floated helpless under the merciless sun and felt her life slipping away. Her entire guild were dead, her friends, many mighty Wizards, Enchanters and Magicians who had followed her teachings, scouring Norrath for knowledge to use against the coming darkness, all were gone. In one fatal blow, The Circle of Tanneth had become no more than a memory. For the long journey from Befallen she had thought it wise to protect the library with the full strength of the Circle, only now did she realise her folly. Using the last of her power Tanneth cast a spell to protect herself from the heat and perhaps survive a little while longer. It would not be long now though she thought, as the darkness reached out to claim her again....

* * * * *

She awoke to the sound of children laughing. As she struggled to open her eyes Tanneth thought she had finally passed beyond. Then she realised she was still tied to the door. It had drifted ashore, but where? She couldn't focus and her head was throbbing making it difficult to think, but she could make out two small figures further down the rocky beach. They appeared to be fighting, and yet laughing.

They obviously hadn't noticed her. She probably looked just like another piece of driftwood or seaweed. Desperate to attract their attention Tanneth tried to cry out, but the sun had finally taken her voice. She searched within her for the last spark of power that must be there. She was still alive, against all odds, some power however small must remain, somewhere. It was there, at the very core of her being, that strange merging of Enchanter and Wizard that seemed impossible to extinguish. But so very very weak. With all her strength Tanneth called forth a tiny frost bolt, and sent it floating towards the children.

* * * * *

Lyrren yelped as she ducked the blow her sister had aimed at her head. Strictly speaking trainee Paladins were meant to avoid the head when practicing, but Keila often tried the unexpected to catch her older sister off guard. It often worked too. With the heart of a true warrior Keila relished the joy of physical combat. There were no warriors on Odus of course. Erudites had renounced most typical forms of warfare in favour of the arcane arts. Their mastery of those was unsurpassed, but they were not so foolish as to rely entirely on magic. They were only too keenly aware of its limitations. And so although they were few, Erudin valued it's Paladins greatly.

Their father had heard the call of Quellious early in life. Rennick had trained hard and was now greatly respected as a leader of the Paladins sworn to the defence of Erudin. As a proud father he had watched his daughters join the ranks of trainee Paladins and follow the path he had once travelled. Although neither had yet been called by the God, Rennick was not concerned. Many were called later in life, and the training always stood them in good stead. The power to heal would come in time.

And so Keila and Lyrren were practicing hard on the beach outside the little fishing village they called home, hoping to earn their father's respect and be ready to serve when Quellious called.

Keila laughed as she was forced off balance to dodge a particularly nasty swipe. Their blunt weapons very rarely connected during practice, the objective was to learn to avoid them. Instead of righting herself, she allowed herself to fall and roll, aiming a blow directly at Lyrren's exposed legs. It hit home with a dull thud.

"Ow!", Lyrren yelled as she bent down to rub her shin. "That hurt".

"Got you!". Keila grinned mischievously and did a quick victory dance, then let out a yelp of surprise and spun around quickly.

"What?" Lyrren asked, still a little peeved.

"Something hit me. A frost bolt I think, but a very weak one". Keila was scanning the beach, looking for her attacker. Even a trainee Paladin should have been able to determine the exact direction an attack had originated from.

"Probably one of the Wizard acolytes playing games", Lyrren said.

"I can't see them", Keila sounded puzzled.

"Maybe they're invisible", Lyrren suggested.

"An acolyte? You know they can't do that till they're much older. No wait...what's that?" Keila was pointing down the beach at what looked like a pile of rags, almost imperceptibly it moved. "There's someone there".

"Maybe we should go and get Father", Lyrren said, eyeing the rags dubiously but Keila was already running down the beach.

"We're not in much danger if that bolt is the best they can do, anyway he's at the temple", she yelled over her shoulder.

Lyrren sighed and headed after her sister, besides she was curious too and she could hardly let Keila go alone.

* * * * *

Tanneth allowed herself a moment of hope as she recognised the guild tunics of the Paladins running towards her. But too soon she realised they were only trainees, neither had the power to heal. Her life was ebbing fast, there was no time left.

Keila skidded to a halt and looked aghast at the woman in front of her. She was tied to a piece of wood, and Keila wasn't even sure she was alive. She bent down quickly to start untieing the ropes.

"Wait...", Lyrren said, as she ran up behind her.

"Don't you recognise the robe?", Keila asked struggling with the knots that were swollen with sea water.

It was faded, torn and soaking wet...but all Erudites knew the robes worn by the Circle of Tanneth, the most powerful guild in Erudin.

"Tanneth!", Lyrren said.

"Yes, the Circle of Tanneth", said Keila not looking up.

"No, it is Tanneth herself", Lyrren said in awe, not moving.

"What?!", Keila jumped back quickly.

"Water", Tanneth croaked. She had to tell them about the darkness and quickly. There was so little time left to her.

Hurriedly Lyrren pulled a flask of water from her belt and gently dribbled a little into the woman's cracked and dry mouth. The once mighty sorceress struggled to swallow, but it was enough so she could talk.

"You must listen to me." Her voice was less than an whisper, Lyrren and Keila had to lean close to the old woman to hear her over the crashing of the waves. "The darkness is coming, not now, I do not know when, but they are gathering their forces. And when they are ready, they will strike. We must be prepared or all will be lost."

Lyrren and Keila looked at each other confused. What could two young girls do about the gathering tide of darkness?

With her dying breath Tanneth continued. "The library from Befallen is lost. The darkness has struck down the Circle of Tanneth. We are no more. They knew what we had found. Somewhere there is a traitor in Erudin, maybe on the Council itself".

She fell back with nothing left to give, but still the power would not let her die. It held her life, refused to release her, there was something else to do...but what? Many times in her life Tanneth had been guided by forces she did not quite understand. At times she had felt merely a tool of some greater power, but who or what had never become clear to her. Now, at the moment of her death, looking up she thought she could see figures moving in the sky behind the worried faces of the two girls bent over her. Try as she might she couldn't focus, couldn't see who or what they were. Then she realised she was looking in the wrong place for her answer. She studied the faces of the two girls, the older one with a fierce nobility and a fire that burned deep within her, and the younger so charming and deceptive but with the heart of a warrior. It was obvious. She reached up and gripped each of them by the arm.

There was only one thing left to say.

"Remember this if you remember nothing else. Ignorance is the only true evil. Seek the light of knowledge and it will banish the darkness".

She felt her power leave her, and as the mighty sorceress Tanneth died she saw Quellious and he was smiling. Two girls never destined to be Paladins now carried her legacy. Lyrren the Wizard and Keila the Enchanter. When they became masters of their powers and could protect themselves they would take her name in defiance of the darkness which had destroyed her. The Circle of Tanneth was no more, but her name would go on.

Together the sisters would be vital in the coming war, if they survived to see it.
