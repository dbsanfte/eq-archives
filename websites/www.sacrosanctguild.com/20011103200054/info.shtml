Code of Conduct

We are a professional guild. The best way to visualize this is by likening Sacrosanct to your 
job and the relationship you have with your guild members to your co-workers. The bottom line 
is you respect each other, your class leader and the council. If people have problems with each 
other, they are worked out in personal tells. If there are further issues, contact a council 
member to assist you. There will be no pissing matches in any channel outside of personal tells. 
There will be no exceptions to this. If you have an issue with something related to the guild 
as a whole, contact a council member. 

Relations with other guilds on the server will be managed exclusively by the council member 
responsible for public relations. If you become aware of an issue with another guild let the 
public relations council member know and s/he will handle it. In situations where relations 
between Sacrosanct and another guild are strained, it is very important that this policy is 
followed. Again, the bottom line is we are a professional guild and issues are handled in a 
professional manner. As individual members of Sacrosanct, we do not flame other guilds or attack 
them publicly. If a situation requires a heated public discussion, it will be managed by the 
public relations council member.

Sacrosanct is a raiding guild. Members are individually responsible to get to level 60 and 
this should be a priority for them. Playing an alternate character is not prohibited, however, 
we strongly encourage players to focus on reaching level 60 with their primary character before 
playing alternates. While it is acceptable to ask for ports, rezes and groups in guild chat 
outside of a raid situation, and we encourage members to offer these services, it is not the 
guild's responsibility to provide them. Whining and spamming the guild channel regarding these 
issues will not be tolerated.

In order to prevent a revolving door policy and the use of de-guilding oneself to express 
disapproval of some issue, members that remove themselves from the guild will not be allowed 
to return unless a unanimous exception is made by the guild council and at least two weeks has 
passed. We take deguilding oneself very seriously. In cases of guild tag bugs, please contact 
a council member for re-tagging.

Discipline issues will be handled collectively by the council. The process is as follows. If 
a guild member is acting in a way that requires discipline, the council will discuss the issue 
and vote on whether or not to give the member an official warning. If the voting is unanymous, 
the member will be officially warned. The official warning is just that, a warning. The member
 will be contacted and informed that s/he is being officially warned regarding his or her conduct. 
If the problem continues another vote will be taken to suspend the member for one week. If the 
vote is unanymous, the member will be deguilded for a week and will not be permitted to attend 
guild functions. After a week, the member will be reguilded. If the problem continues to persist, 
a third vote will be taken to perminently deguild the member. If the vote is unanymous, the 
player will be informed of this and removed from the guild. The bottom line here is be professional. 
If you are acting in an unacceptable fashion, you will be informed of that and eventually removed 
from the guild if you do not change for the better.




Raid Etiquette

During official guild raids we expect everyone to follow these rules: 

- Be on at the designated time and place that is set for the raid. Either camp your character at 
the designated place the night before or get on early to make sure you get a ride. If you know 
that you will be late, speak with the raid leader to see if arrangements can be made. If you don't 
arrive in time, two things can happen. If the raid leader believes the raid will be successful without 
your presence, the raid will start and you will miss it. If the raid leader believes the raid cannot 
continue without you, you will be penalized on the amount of DKPs you earn from the raid. The actual 
amount is dependent upon how long people waited for you to get on. Bottom line here is, respect your 
fellow guild members by thinking ahead and planning ahead. One person 15 minutes late in a group of 
30 is 7.5 hours of wasted time. (Note: The DKP penalty will only be associated with raids that have 
been planned for ahead of time with a defined start time and meeting location. Impromptu raids and 
the like will, of course, not be tied to such a rule.)

- Act professionally in any situation, whatever it may be. We will have really good raids and sometimes 
we will have really bad raids. This is especially the case when we do new encounters for the first time. 
When things get nasty, act like an adult. We aren't a bunch of bratty children and you are expected to 
act professionally, even when the crap hits the fan and everyone is pissed off.

- Raids are not about gaining exp. You will often lose more exp than you gain. Groups will be organized 
to maximize the efficiency of the raiding party and often times there will be only one or two groups that 
will get any exp. Please do not complain that your group never gets the kill, but instead focus on helping 
the raid to be successful.

- Have any equipment or resist gear that is needed on you at the appointed time. We don't want to hear 
people asking to borrow gear or have jewelry made for them at the last minute. It's your responsibility 
to be prepared ahead of time. No Gear - No Go!!

- Everyone is doing his/her job during the event without complaints or whining. If you are a porter you 
will port people to the event scene, if you are a buffing class you will buff people. If you are a tank 
and things go badly, you will die so a cleric can camp. This is your job.

- No one but the Raid Leader or someone designated by him/her invites other people from outside the guild 
along. If you have suggestions inform the Raid Leader.

- Once a raid has started the organizers will make groups and keep you informed on tactics and important 
orders. We expect everyone to shut up and listen. We realize sometimes people are zoning or miss their 
group list so these orders will be repeated. At this point please control jokes, horsing around and repeated 
emotes. This helps not only the organizers but also everyone else who's trying to pay attention. Feel free 
to relax and mess around before or after a raid but once we're in raid mode please control yourselves.

- The Raid Leader or the person assigned to make the groups will list the group leader first in a grouping list. 
Please do not argue with them about whom you're grouped with. We realize some people have slow computers or 
poor connections and the Raid Leader will try to make sure these people aren't made group leaders. Each group 
leader is responsible for letting the Raid Leader know the status of their group - when they're all together, 
mana status, rez status, LD status, lost corpses, etc. - any important information will be relayed to the Raid 
Leader by each group leader. If the group leader goes LD on a raid the remaining members will regroup ASAP and 
that new group leader will relay their status to the Raid Leader. This means that no other person except the 
group leaders will be giving information, talking on channel or sending tells to the Raid Leader.

- Do not question guild leader or raid leader’s decisions during guild events. If you have questions, 
suggestions, or comments about any decisions, or people, please contact the appropriate person after the 
guild event.

- You should attend scheduled guild events if you are online during guild events’ time period. Remember
 that we are a raiding guild and every member is needed at guild events. 







Council Roles and Responsibilites

Council - The council as a whole is responsible for establishing and guiding the strategic 
direction of the guild. They act collectively as the "Guild Leader." They set the guild policies 
and make changes as they see needed. Changes to guild policy requires unanymous agreement among 
the council members. The council also handles guild disciplinary issues and must act unanymously.

Individual Council Member Responsibilities:

Guild Banker:
- Store guild items
- Track the guild items and maintain a list of what items we have and which loot pool they belong 
(guild only - main character, guild only - twink, for public sale)
- Track platinum reserves
- Manage sale of unneeded items
- Manage process for receiving and delivering items and cash to and from guild bank (reimbursements etc.)

Guild Raid Planner:
- Manage raiding calendar
- Publicize raid events
- Research new areas and stratagies
- Choose raid leaders and ensure raid leaders are properly trained and instructed
- Train guild members who wish to learn how to lead raids
- Announce who is rolling for a drop and announce winner in DKP Manager's absence.

DKP Manager:
- Track individual guild member DKP bank balances and purchases
- Maintain DKP data
- Report DKP data to guild
- Maintain DKP item price list
- Maintain DKP raid value list
- Announce who is rolling for a specific drop and call the winner of the random

Epic Coordinator:
- Manage tracking of individual guild member epic progress
- Understand each epic quest and involvement of the raids
- Work with raid planner to get epic raids scheduled
- Motivate individual members to complete non-raid portions of epic quest.
- When SoL released, research epic armor quests

Public Relations Specialist:
- Manage guild public relations
- Develop and maintain positive relations with other guilds and groups
- Resolve issues and complaints
- Manage inter-guild negotiation


Class Leaders - Class leaders are responsible for the 
guild members of their particular class. 
They work with these players individually to train, motivate and ensure that they are adding 
value to the guild. They also are responsible for recruiting new members of their class.

Class Leader Responsibilities:

- Track talent level of players of their class
- Initiate recruitment process for players of their class
- Research and train guild members of their class
- Ensure players of their class understand their role and responsibilities on raids -- especially guests


Raid Leaders - Raid leaders are the players who actually 
run our raids. We have 2-3 raid leaders who run the majority of our raids, but we encourage 
all our members to develop raid leading abilities as this is good experience.

Raid Leader Responsibilities:

- Work with the Raid Planner to fully understand the raid strategies
- Manage assigning of groups (do or delegate)
- Lead the raid
- Ensure all loot is delivered to guild banker 




Looting Policy

Sacrosanct uses a DKP loot system. The system has been patterned closely after Afterlife's 
system. In order to maintain fairness across the varying levels of player's raid time, DKPs 
or Dragon Kill Points are awarded to players when they participate in killing raid level mobs. 
These points are then used to weight the randoming between players when loot drops. When loot 
is won, the DKP value associated with that item is subtracted from the winning player's DKP 
bank. Through this method, even though a player may be killing a raid level mob that does not 
drop an item that character would use, the player still benefits as he or she receives some 
predetermined DKP amount. This DKP can be used in the future to more or less purchase an item 
the character desires.

In order to mitigate the "bad random" scenario, a scenario where a person that has been raiding 
for a long time loses a random to a person who's brand new, there are DKP tiers. Tiers have 
been developed to enable players with similar amounts of time invested (similar DKP levels) 
to random for loot while those with significantly less time invested (lower DKP levels) are 
excluded. The DKP tier that an item will be randomed in will be defined as the highest interested 
player's DKP level minus 10% of his DKPs or the highest interested players DKP value rounded up 
to the nearest 5 minus 4, whichever is larger. In cases where this number is the same, the latter 
of these is used (round up to nearest 5 minus 4.) 

For those that would rather not do the math, this is summed up as: if the highest interested 
player's DKP level is 50 or less the tier level is 1-5, 6-10, 11-15 etc. wherever the highest 
players DKP level falls. If the highest interested player's DKP level is 51+, then the highest 
interested player's DKP level minus 10% is the defined tier range. As this can be confusing, 
here are several examples to help you better understand:

Example 1:

An item drops and Player A desires to have the it and has the most DKPs of all interested at 
23 DKPs. As this is less than 50 the tier would be 21 to 25. Thus, all players with 21-25 DKPs 
who wanted the item could random against each other for it. If there were no other interested 
players in this tier, Player A would get the item and the DKP cost of the item would be subtracted 
from his DKP bank.

Example 2:

An item drops and Player B desires to have the it and has the most DKPs of all interested at 43 
DKPs. As this is less than 50, the tier would be 41 to 45. Thus, all players with 41-45 DKPs who 
wanted the item could random against each other for it.

Example 3:

An item drops and Player C desires to have the it and has the most DKPs of all interested at 
86 DKPs. As this DKP amount is 50+, the tier range will be 86-8.6=77.4 -- 77.4 to 86. Thus, all 
players with 77.4-86 DKPs who wanted the item could random against each other for it.

Example 4:

An item drops and Player D desires to have the it and has the most DKPs of all interested at 
256 DKPs. As this DKP amount is 50+, the tier range would be 256-25.6=230.4 -- 230.4 to 256. 
Thus, all players with 230.4-256 DKPs who wanted the item could random against each other for it.

As you can see, the larger the DKP level for those interested, the larger the range. This is 
intended as players whose DKP values are within 10% of each other have invested similar amounts 
of raiding time.

Players can only random for items that their character would use. These items cannot be used by 
their twinks or transferred to any other player except through the proper process. If nobody 
present wants an item that drops, it is added to the guild bank and offered again at the next guild raid.

A player must have enough DKPs to purchase an item in order to be eligable for the random, that 
is, s/he cannot go negative. If nobody has enough DKPs to purchase an item, the item is banked 
and re-offered at the next raid. If the item is NO DROP, however, the item is offered at tier 
zero and all players with positive DKP values who need may random for it. The winning player will 
then go negative to purchase the item. If the item is NO DROP and no players interested have 
positive DKP values, the item is offered at tier sub zero and all players in need are allowed 
to random regardless of how negative they are. Items that have been in the guild bank for one 
week or more have their class restrictions removed and they can be purchased with DKPs for player's 
alternate characters. Items that have continued lack of interest can be liquidated at market value 
to players outside the guild for raid money or sold to members of the guild for 66% of market value.

Qualifying items (raid level items) that are upgraded at a raid can be offered to those present 
using the same process as if the item dropped at that raid. In this situation the winning player 
transfers the DKP value for that item to the previous owner. If no players desire the raid level 
item that was upgraded, the player may sell it to the bank for one-half it's DKP value or keep it 
and offer it again at the next guild raid. Non-raid level items acquired by the guild which are 
upgraded will be returned to the guild bank to be given to a member in need or liquidated for raiding 
funds.

Keys quest components (trak teeth etc.) are not considered loot and will be awarded to players 
based on total DKPs earned, level, class* and percentage complete. Once an item like this is 
awarded, the player must make completing the key quest a high priority as the commitment being 
given to that person by the guild is large. *The keys will be awarded in an order to ensure balanced 
groups.

DKPs will be awarded at the end of each day's raiding and be available to purchase items for the 
next day's raiding. The difference between this and actual real time awarding of DKPs in minimal 
compared to the resources and time needed to track and maintain a real time updating program. 
Each player will know exactly where he or she is at for DKPs when the raiding begins and these 
are the numbers that will be used that day.

A list of items and their associated DKP values as well as the DKP values for various raid level 
encounters will be published and maintained for your reference.





Recruitment Policy 

Joining Sacrosanct is not a simple task. We seek the most talented and gifted players and are very 
excusive in who we recruit and eventally grant membership. The role of each class leader is to know 
the talent level of other players of their class. It is up to each class leader to initiate the 
recruitment process for a given player. Other guild members, including the council, may make suggestions 
to the class leader regarding potential recruits, but the class leader must work with the potential 
recruit to gauge his/her level of ability and officially initiate the process before recruitment will begin.

The process is as follows: 

1. The class leader of a class the guild is currently recruiting finds someone whose skill level and 
character level meets the guild's requirement. Generally speaking, we are looking for players whose 
skill level is at the 90% percentile or higher.

2. The class leader or a member of the council contacts the potential recruit to see if they are interested. 
If the potential recruit is interested, the class leader initiates the recruitment process for a given 
player by officially posting so on the recruitment board.

3. A poll is created where all full guild members vote "Yes", "Abstain" or "Veto." Veto votes require 
a written explanation as to why and may be directly submitted to the council via email. The council 
will review each "Veto" vote and judge it valid or invalid. The poll will be up for one week and if 
no valid "Veto" votes have been cast and at least 50% have voted "Yes", the member will be granted a 
trial membership. If there is a valid "Veto" vote or less than 50% voted "Yes" the potential recruit 
is informed that recruitment will not proceed further. The class leader must wait one month before 
re-initiating the recruitment process for a person not granted trial membership.

4. Trial membership lasts for two weeks. During this time period, the trial member is required to 
attend all guild raids (baring RL issues) and the guild members must attempt to group with the trial 
member. Raid leaders will do their best to group the trial member with various guild members to assist 
with this. During trial membership, a trial member has no looting rights, but does acquire DKPs which 
can be used if he or she becomes a full member.

5. After two weeks of trial membership another poll is created to grant full membership. This poll 
also lasts one week and full guild members cast "Yes", "No" and "Abstain" votes. If, after one week, 
90% of the votes are "Yes", the trial member is granted full membership. Either the class leader of 
the trial member or a member of the council can individually veto the trial member at this time. If 
the trial member does not meet the 90% "Yes" requirement or is vetoed, they are informed that they 
will not be granted full membership and removed from the guild. The class leader must wait two months
 before re-initiating the recruitment process for a player previously not granted full membership.
