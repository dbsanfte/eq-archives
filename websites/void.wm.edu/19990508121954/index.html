So, you want to be a tradesman? You want to know what it takes to make that steel breastplate, just how high your smithing skill needs to be...

Bad News

It'll never happen in EQ. No, this game isn't another UO where armor can be produced after a relatively modest amount of investment. Well, at least, not most types of armor, and certainly not steel armor. Fact is, you can never make steel armor, ever.

Still Interested?

Then read on, fellow traveler. These pages detail what you need to know to be both a tailor and a blacksmith, though you should be aware that at the moment the best advice is to be neither, and save those precious coppers to buy armor in the store.

Basic Tailoring

To be a tailor, you'll need a sewing kit, like this one . Sewing kits come in two sizes, small and large. Small kits cost about 6 gold, large ones are well over a platinum piece in value. Initially, you'll want to purchase a small sewing kit, since you'd never succeed at making anything with a large one you couldn't make with a small one anyway. These are available from your local tailor. Check out the city maps on eqvault or stratics for the nearest tailor in your city. You'll also want to pick up the tailoring book at the shop, even though its pages are reproduced here.

Did you know? Having the how-to books in hand increases your chance of success at trades. Never practice your trade without them.

You'll also need some tailoring skill. Use a few practices with your guildmaster to increase your tailoring skill to at least 3 or 4. More is better, but practices at low levels are precious, so don't spend too many on this skill.

Next, you'll need a ruined pelt and a pattern. There are three types of ruined pelts that are relevant for our purposes, and they make three different sizes of armor. See the chart below, which is ripped straight from the tailoring book.

There are a lot of different wolves, cats, and bears in Norrath, but people seems to find it most difficult to locate cats, or at least cats that can be slain by relatively low level characters. Look thee to West Karana, one zone east of Qeynos Hills. Therein are young lionesses, which may be slain by a fourth or fifth level character, or groups of lower level characters. West Karana is dangerous though, do not venture too far from the guard towers.

Pelt, kit, and skill in hand, you lastly need a pattern . Many patterns are sold by your local tailor, though there are other that aren't (bracers, masks, belts, cloaks, and shoulderpads come to mind). Start with something easy, like gorgets or sleeves. These patterns cost about 6 silver apiece, and are stackable, unlike pelts.

Right click on your sewing kit to open it, then put both the ruined pelt and pattern inside. Make sure your tailoring book is on your person! It increases your chances of success. Cross your fingers and click combine. You'll probably get this message:

Get used to it. But you may get lucky and make something, or even better, your skill might increase. Repeat until your skill gets up into the twenties. Patchwork sells to shopkeepers at about 12 silver per. Note that tunics are harder to make, but sell for the same price, to shopkeepers. Your best bet is to sell to other players, since patchwork protects almost as well as leather, though it's heavier. It affects player appearance, unlike cloth armor, which is another selling point. A good sale price is 2 to 3 gold, though you're welcome to ask more if you've got a lot of takers.

Advanced Tailoring

So your tailoring skill is in the twenties, and you're thinking you're ready to move on to something harder. Especially considering you're probably starting to get the message patchwork is now trivial to make (which indicates your skill can no longer improve by making it). Think again. Quit now, before you blow a lot of money with little results.

Still reading? Well OK, but you were warned.

As noted in the tailoring book, there are two other kinds of leather armor a tailor can make besides patchwork. Studded and reinforced. Studded leather armor, as of beta 4, offers the same exact protection as regular, store bought leather. On the other hand, reinforced leather is the best non-magical leather armor in the game.

In order to make either of these, you're going to need to trade in that small sewing kit for a large one. These are a little more difficult to locate, but if you've been tooling around Norrath at all you've doubtless found someone who will sell you one.

Did you know? Your large tailoring kit is a container with 8 slots. You can keep stuff in it at the bank. Just be careful to NEVER hit the combine button when using it in this way, it'll destroy all your stuff!

To make studded leather requires, well, studs . See the blacksmithing section for details on how these are created if you can't find someone to sell them to you. And believe me, you won't. Different items require different amounts of studs. See the chart below (again, straight from the tailoring book) for the numbers.

Put these studs together in the large sewing kit, separating each stud from the others, along with the required medium quality pelt and the pattern itself, as in the image below, and combine as before.

Well, you probably failed. And your skill probably didn't go up either. And if you made those studs yourself, you might be crying about now. Well, dry your eyes, you were warned. Make some more studs, and repeat this exercise over and over until your skill gets up into the 60's. When your tailoring skill gets sufficiently high, and the 60's is only a guess, you may be able to make reinforced leather.

Reinforced leather requires a large kit, pattern, and high quality pelt of appropriate size. These high quality pelts seem to be easier to locate than the medium quality stuff, or at least they were in beta 4, so that's not the hard part. Instead of studs, though, steel boning is required.

Don't worry, though - steel boning is actually about the same cost to produce as studs, and it doesn't take as many to create stuff. It'd be a better choice to raise skills at lower skill levels, except you have no chance of success and your skill does not go up if attempted at low skill levels. Basic Blacksmithing

As with tailoring, the first thing the budding smith needs to do is spend a few practices at their guild on blacksmithing. More is better here, since little having to do with this trade is cheap, though as before, low level practices are precious and should be spent on other aspects of character development. In fact, you don't even want to think about getting into blacksmithing until you're level 6 or so. Consensus is you can practice up your smithing skill at the guild to about 20, beyond that, you've got to burn coin working at it. You'll know when this time has come, since you'll see a message like "You must learn advanced trade skills in the field."

When the idea of losing those rusty weapons you get from orcs, gnolls, kobolds, and the friendly decaying skeleton without getting a penny for them no longer makes you flinch, it's time to start smithin'.

Did you know? Everything in trade combination containers such as the forge is consumed on any attempt to combine items. Bad combinations always consume everything and fail, correct ones sometimes succeed, but any attempt always destroys all container contents.

You'll want to start by sharpening weapons. It's the cheapest way to get your skill up. Ever thought about fishing? It's a good way to get rusty daggers to sharpen, and sharpening anything else weighs heavily on the mind because of the loss of potential sales capital. Keep in mind that like tailoring, any failure results in the loss of all items.

Sharpening stones are always sold by the same shopkeepers that sell ore These are few and far between, but you can find one in High Keep, one in North Freeport, and one in Ak'Anon. I've heard there's another in the land of the dark elves, but cannot confirm it. There may be others still, but I don't know about them. Note that there's nothing in Odus or the Qeynos side of Antonica, so that's probably not a good home base choice for the smith-to-be. These stones stack, and cost a few coppers, so they're easy to accumulate for ready use.

To sharpen a weapon, left click on a forge, and put a single sharpening stone and rusty weapon inside. If you don't fail, you'll end up with a tarnished weapon, which is a couple of speed points faster than a rusty weapon and has the exact same value when sold to NPCs. Not a real moneymaker. But sometimes, your skill will go up, and if you've used a rusty dagger the cost to you is relatively low. When sharpening, or undertaking any other kind of smithing activity, always make sure to have your basic blacksmithing book in your inventory, as it increases your chances of success.

Eventually, sharpening weapons will start being "trivial" for you, which means your skill is no longer capable of increasing whether you succeed or fail. I've heard that when this point is reached, two handed swords do not yield the same message. Whether this is because they have a higher skill improvement cap, or is a game bug, remains to be seen.

Once your skill is in the low teens, and you've got some real money to waste, you can shoot for making some other stuff. I'm not going to reprise info you can read in the blacksmithing book (for once), but you'll want to start with making metal bits from ore pieces. You absolutely must separate the ores in the forge, and use a store bought flask of water to smith! Failing to do either will result in a botched attempt.

When you've made a few metal bits, you can think about making something useful from them, like studs. Unfortunately, before you can do this, you'll need to make a file, as detailed in the blacksmithing book. Alas, files require metal bits and file molds, and file molds are notoriously hard to find. There is a vendor who sells them in West Karana (the east section), one in East Freeport, one just outside of Kaladim, and another in Kelethin. The molds are inexpensive, about a silver apiece, but they do not stack, so unless you're close to the source you'll want to make large purchasing runs, since they are consumed on each attempt to create a file.

Did you know? Freeport is the only city in which all components for basic blacksmithing are available (that I know of, anyway). If you're looking to be a blacksmith, you may want to settle there.

If you're lucky, you'll make a file. Here's a picture of a file being examined (it's under the cursor arrow):

As you can see, files weigh a pound apiece and do not stack. By combining them with metal bits or a large piece of ore, one can create studs or steel boning. It isn't unreasonable to start making studs with your skill in the high teens, though twenty is better since they're expensive and you'll fail a lot. See the smithing chances chart below for an idea how often you'll succeed at skills levels of 10 and 20. You'll not be able to increase your skill past 20 or so making metal bits or files.

Advanced Blacksmithing

As stated in the armor and weapons book, a book which as of beta 4 did not actually contain any information whatsoever on making weapons (I never saw any weapon molds, either, so my guess is weaponsmithing is not yet implemented), there is but one type of player made metal armor, and it is banded.

Making banded armor requires the proper type of mold, available in specific sizes at the appropriate venues throughout Norrath (small in the dwarven city of Kaladim, etc) and some number of metal sheets. Notice no mention is made in this book of folded metal sheets, though basic blacksmithing includes instructions on their manufacture. We can only assume, again, either this will be put in later (or it's arcana I was unable to determine in the beta).

Players actually claim to have had suits of banded armor made for them by other players, on the chat boards. This was certainly on the test server, and the smith whose handiwork this was must have been extremely flush to be able to afford to ascend to such lofty heights of prowess.

Feedback Corner

Thanks to the helpful feedback of several players, I now have some greater insight to the mystery of banded armor. It is possible to make this stuff at lower skill levels, you just need to be prepared to fail most every time. One player reported making seven different banded items, including two tunics, with a skill of 22. He reported having to burn over 60 platinum to do so, but it's still cheaper (and better) than chain.

As for the test center, another player reported that for a while there were no skill caps for trades at the guildmaster on the test center, and a few players used this little oversight to sink a lot of practices into a trade. With a skill in the high 30's, this player reported only two successes in 24 attempts overall in making first a gorget, then bracers. He also reported going one for nine on a regular server with a skill of 21, which sounds about the same as the report above. Another player reported someone succeeding only 40% of the time on banded armor with a blacksmithing skill in the 80's!

If you're still thinking about smithing, here's one final quote from a player you might wish to consider... "after smithing about 100pp or so worth of things (well, most were fails but it still counts toward skill raising), raised 1 skill point to (22)."

Food for thought... do dwarves have a natural ken for making armor? These Verant guys are strong on game and mythology traditions, so I wouldn't count the possibility out.
