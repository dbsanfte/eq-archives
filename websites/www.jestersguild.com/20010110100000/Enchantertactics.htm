Enchanter Tactics

By

Nong Ninipuss

 

I. Intro

One of the hardest things about being an enchanter is simply, well, not being a class that many people are at all familiar with. Everyone knows the basics (we get clarity, augmentation and some other "can I get" buffs, as well as come crowd-pleasing illusions). But few people are aware of how best to work with the enchanter's other abilities.

Simply put, the enchanter is the ultimate grouping class. Unlike any other single class, enchanters can turn hopeless fights into easy wins. But unlike any other class, the group must adapt its overall strategy to suit the enchanter and not the other way around. That can be a problem. Most stranger groups are simply too impatient to go over tactics 101. They just want to kill, and kill now. And you can hardly blame people. They are paying to play, not sit and get a lecture about group tactics. But as a result, the enchanter usually ends up adapting to the group's standard pull, beat, nuke and pray strategies, which is a shame. Why? Let me give a quick example.

Group in aviak city gets jumped by a stray avocet while fighting a rook. Avocet beats on player. Player tries to flee (while I am frantically trying to direct the group, to no avail) and does not make it. I try to explain to the group that with one spell, which cannot be resisted, and a bit of cooperation I can virtually ensure no one ever dies from such an attack. Falls on deaf ears. This player dies two more times in exactly the same way before the wizzie I am with yells to the group "shut up and listen to Nong unless you want to keep getting killed!" No one dies for the rest of the day from stray mobs. The spell, by the way, is memory blur. It completely wipes the hate list of any mob, except a few immensely powerful ones which have immunity to it.

All i needed was two things: the group to move the rook away (wizzie nukes it) from the avocet, and, the player to stop beating on the avocet. I blur, and the avocet walks away, player is saved. So getting back to this, let's assume your enchanter knows what the hell she is doing. That leaves number 2, getting the group to adapt its overall strategy. This isn't rocket science, and it won't take a lot of effort and planning. Actually, it's all pretty damn simple. Best thing is to have the group leader barking out commands, telling the enchanter what to do, and to what. As long as everyone else knows what to do and what NOT to do, things are fine.

For example, if the enchanter is going to charm, people have to know which one is being charmed and not attack it. (I can't tell you how many times I have had to scream to group members to stop attacking/wasting mana on a charmed mob.) If the enchanter is doing a group mez, the group needs to focus on one mob at a time and assist the leader or puller. Not that complicated.

Before I get into all that, a few words about what enchanters are not good for.

1) We can avert utter disaster. But that's when things go right, and they don't always. Someone described enchanters are half demigod/half rube goldberg, and that is not too far from the truth. When things go right, it's a work of art. When things don't, well … it gets ugly. No class experiences more spectacular wins and losses than enchanters. So enchanters shouldn't be used as reasons to go after Mobs that the group otherwise could not touch.

2) Enchanters have great buffs, which means we are useful in groups higher level than we are. But we are just as useless against deep reds as any magic class would be (except a mage or necro pet.) We can use some spells (memory blur, for example, can be used to distribute damage . . . see further down). But our most powerful crowd control and damage spells are resisted just as much as an equal level wizzies burns would be in these cases. We get no special benefits there at all. And there is a serious downside to even attempting to burn a deep red (see below).

3) When enchanters do burn, it is as a finisher, not as a nuke. Enchanter direct damage spells are good. But they have three drawbacks, two small and one huge one. Small ones: They are fairly mana inefficient compared to wizzies (as it should be) and they are all or nothing, meaning no such thing as partial resist. Any resist means no damage at all to the mob. The BIG disadvantage: Our spells come with a MEGA taunt. And i mean mega. Even on resists. Experimenting in High Keep, in a group with two free-burning 30+ wizzies, I could taunt onto me a half dead gob warrior with one single dd spell. Therefore, enchanters are finishers only, and in a good group would not be burning at all. At 29, we get a decent DoT that does not have the mega taunt problem, so I would use that or nothing at all. I actually dream of the day when I can go with a group and not waste a single spell slot with an offensive spell.

II. So what are enchanters good for?

We are like plumbing. When things are going well, you will hardly notice us. You won't see big fiery special effects, gonzo explosions and stuff like that. You may even see is sitting and meditating during fights. This is what an enchanter should be doing. Here's what you WILL notice: - tanks will notice they are attacking faster, getting hit less often, and the mobs are moving much slower (tepid deeds, alacrity, augmentation). - Nukes/healers will find their mana bars filling up faster (clarity) and downtime is significantly reduced. - Healers won't have to heal as often because mobs won't be doing as much damage (many spells) - Rogues who know what to look for will find mobs frozen in place long enough to get off one or two completely free back stabs (whirl/dyn's dizzying) - Monks will notice they have become whirling dervishes of death, and other classes with fast weapons will find their attack speeds markedly improved (alacrity) -Tanks and nukes won't have to worry about stray mobs jumping them during battle (mezmerize/color flux/color shift). - Nukes will notice their spells hitting for full damage much more often (tashani). - If a puller brings a few too many, you might notice one mob turn and fight another (charm) or you may notice the entire group of them suddenly stop and wait politely for their turn to be killed (mesmerize/mesmerization). - Or you may see the pack of critters suddenly immobilized while you pound away on them (color flux/color shift) - If you are fighting something with a pet, you may notice they pet suddenly turn tail and run, never to be seen again (chase the moon). - If you are meleeing a mob, and you are getting low on hits, you may notice as you back away the mob inexplicably stop its frenzy attack on you and turn onto some other tank (memory blur). You won't necessarily ascribe these things to the enchanter, because all you see is a blue light and silly message. Just like the plumbing, you will notice it more when it's not around or not working right.

III. "So…what do you do?"

I have been asked this more than I can say. People just don’t know that much about enchanter spells. Here’s a few that everyone should know.

Clarity - (no explaination needed) Augmentation – Great buff for melee or casters. Ask for it every time. It increases AC by a few, attack speed by 15% and agility by 20 points. It does not stack with quickness or alacrity. Alacrity – upgrade of quickness. Speeds attacks by up to 40%. Short duration, a few minutes. Works best on monks or on those with really fast weapons.

Mesmerize/Mesmerization – Freezes a monster for 30 seconds or so. Any damage will break the mez. Color flux/shift – Stuns a monster for 3 to 6 seconds. Cheap mana. AE spell. Can be dangerous (see below). Whirl/Dyn’s Dizzying Draught – It "spins" the target for a few seconds. Basically, it is a range, single target version of flux/shift. Mob stops attacking for up to 8 to 10 seconds. An observant rogue will use this opportunity to duck around its backside and BS it. An observant enchanter will see this, and keep casting.

Memory blur – wipes the mobs hate list. Basically starts the whole encounter over. If you are standing next to a hill giant, it will attack you. But if you were being chased by a HG and it is out of aggro range when blur is cast, the giant will forget all about you and walk away.

Obscure – An upgrade to the crappy enchanter buff. Adds 16 AC points to target. Not bad.

Rune II – Targetable spell that makes target immune to a certain amount of damage, usually something like 140 points. Costly tho. Each cast requires a bloodstone. Used only in emergencies.

Tepid deeds – Slows the monster’s attack speed by over half. An amazing spell.

Chase the moon – upgrade to fear. Never worry a pet of any kind with an enchanter around. One cast of this spell, and the pet runs away never to be seen again.

IV. OK, some tactics

1) The "fluxing fool" This has become my default strategy of late, because it requires the least group cooperation. It is quite dangerous because it is area effect and a resist will taunt a mob onto the enchanter. Because the enchanter must stand literally in the middle of the fray to do this, it is hard for others to see that he/she is getting creamed -- and what is doing the creaming -- until too late. But when this works, it is very effective for controlling large crowds.

Color flux and color shift each are area effect around the enchanter and stun for something like 4 and 6 seconds respectively. That does not sound like much, but it is. Casting them one on top of another, an enchanter can keep the crowd stunned for almost as long as he or she wants to. Also, both spells are insanely cheap spells to cast manawise (20 and 40 mana) and they are hard to resist, even by reds.

Here's how it should work: At the beginning of battle, the enchanter tashanis the main mob, and whatever other people are pounding on first, then crosses his fingers, jumps into the fray, and fluxes. If it works, most anything around her will be completely stunned for enough time to tashani the rest, and then punch shift before anything wakes up. If one or two resist, usually I can just keep trying to shift/flux to stun them, then blur each one so they get off me and onto a melee type. It does not always work. Even tho these spells have a very quick casting time, you can get interrupted. And that can get ugly fast, especially when no one is keeping tabs on you. (Welcome to high keep.) Ideally, the enchanter would have a melee type spying her. If the enchanter gets a resist, and gets jumped by a stray mob, all it takes is a hit or two by the melee type to pull the mob off for the rest of the fight. Once it has taken physical damage, the mob won't care about being stunned or fluxed. Even better is someone who can hit an entire group of mobs with an ae spell after the enchanter stuns, especially a bard. That way, the one or two resists won't pull those critters onto the enchanter. (It will give them a few free shots at the bard . . . but hey, that's what all that armor is for.) The big drawback is that the mobs must remain in fairly close proximity to the enchanter. If the fight gets too spread out, then this strategy is harder to employ.

2) Mesmerize/Mesmerization This needs more coordination by group members, but not a lot. This should be the default enchanter group strategy, but it is especially good for those "ooops" pulls, when you bring the two or three blues you wanted and a boatload of greens you didn't. Here's how it works. Puller brings a group of mobs, and the entire group uses the /assist function to focus on the same monster. Everyone focuses on one critter, and pretty much ignores the rest. The enchanter's job is to tashani the mob getting hit by the group, and mesmerize the rest. When that one is done, they move to the next, which the enchanter tashanis while keeping the rest under control. This works best when the group starts with the toughest monster and moves down from there (which it should be doing anyway, but not all groups do). That leaves the enchanter to mez mostly lower levels and probably some pesky greens.

This strategy absolutely requires one person to watch the enchanter's ass, usually the cleric or designated healer is the logical choice. Mez can be resisted, and that will mean an untouched critter will come bolting after the enchanter and needs to be pulled off him. The cleric is a good choice for this job because he is not usually needed right away in a group battle when most folks are at full health. If the enchanter is efficient, even a resist or two won't cause more than a few seconds delay getting things under control.

Once the crowd is controlled, it's a cakewalk thereafter. The advantages to this strategy are many. For one, the enchanter can stand away from the main battle, where she should be. If a creature is pulled onto her, she has to time to react with a flux or shift before it hits. Mesmerize lasts a long time like 20 seconds. If the enchanter begins to remez after 10 or 15 seconds, there is room for error. A resist will not draw the mob onto her, and she can cast again before the initial mez wears off. Mez also lasts long enough to allow the group to bug out of a hopeless situation. Flux/shift only last a few seconds. Mesmerization, the area effect mez spell, is incredibly mana efficient. For like 70 mana, an enchanter can mesmerize literally dozens of monsters within a certain radius. And it's a pretty big radius too. Spread out battles are less of an issue here than they are with flux/shift. And if things do get spread out too much, the enchanter can just mesmerize individual monsters and group mez the rest.

The disadvantages: The group has to be absolutely steadfast in focusing on one monster at a time. Even if they are being beaten up by another one, they have to have faith that the enchanter will do the job and mez it off them. That means they may have to let the enchanter know, and back off a battle for a minute to let the enchanter target the mob that is on them. In a good group, tanks should be calling for "mez" as freely as they call for "heals." If the group is not disciplined, as most stranger groups are not, they attack anything and everything at the same time. People jump on and off mobs. It's a total free-for-all. In that case, it's not worth even bothering with mez. It would simply be wasting mana. Any damage a mob takes will break the mez, and that is pretty much mana down the crapper. That also means no area effect spells as well, which can be a drawback for bards.

3) Charming Charm is a great spell for pulls that are maybe a bit bigger than intended, but not one of those "holy crap" runnyeye caliber pulls. It is good in situations where you have a few moderately high level mobs doing lots of damage, say four or five blue dry bones or aviaks or cazic-thule lizzies. Again, the group needs to be coordinated and again you need to have someone spying the enchanter, probably the cleric is a good choice. The enchanter will yell to the group that she is charming, then tashanis the mob everyone is dutifully focusing on and charms another. She then commands it to kill one of the other untouched mobs. You can see how quickly the math can turn in your favor. A group of 6 against four becomes 6 on 2, with two critters beating the crap out of one another. If the enchanter can mez one, that's 6 vs 1. I like those odds. Ideally, you polish off the creatures not being attacked by the charmed monster, then wait while the charmed mob polishes off its foe, and with luck, gets beat up pretty badly in the process. Then the enchanter breaks the charm, and the group polishes off the formerly charmed monster.

Advantages: Charm works well on tougher creatures, believe it or not. High blues and evens. Some yellows too. I had a dry bones charmed for several hours one afternoon (it was high blue to me at the time) hunting in rathe mountains, and man is it great watching your "pet" help you and your partner slice and dice a barbed bone skele or skele monk. It can salvage a disastrous pull and give you time to get away from one really nasty critter if you charm, put your charmed pet on it and run.

Disadvantages: Very risky. Charm has an unpredictable duration. It can last for 10 minutes or more. Or it can last for 30 seconds. The normal time is more than enough to finish off a battle, and enchanters are quite prepared to deal with the critter once the charm does wear off. But this is where that Rube Goldberg thing comes in. There are times when the charm wear off sooner than expected, and the formerly charmed mob and the one it was fighting are a gonna come lookin for a certain red-faced enchanter, and they aren't going to be happy. That's when it is critical to have someone spying the enchanter to get those things off him. Usually, I can flux and mez one, while someone else takes the other off me.Usually. But I have died a few times this way when no one was watching my ass.

The other problem with this is the same problem that used to affect magician pets. If you tell a charmed creature to attack something too many levels higher than it is, it will balk. It will walk a few feet away and pace back and forth for the entire battle. I learned this the hard way, incidentally.

4) Distributing damage - The amazingly useful memory blur This is a truly under-appreciated spell, much more powerful than people realize. It is the one spell an enchanter can use against deep reds. What it does, as I said earlier, is wipe the hate list. So, in the case of monsters and animals that are non-aggressive by nature (spiders, aviaks, etc) the effect of the spell would be that the creature would stop what it was doing and walk away as long as you do nothing to aggravate it -- e. g., keep bashing it with your sword.

Against aggressive creatures, the same is true as long as you are out of range (say you root it, back up then blur). This spell is a lifesaver in other ways as well. You know how mobs go into that "frenzy" mode when they have you to low hit points? Nothing can taunt them off you hardly? Well, blur can. Lets say the group is attacked by a very tough high red mob. What an enchanter can do is blur the mob as it starts to frenzy on someone. The blur will erase the frenzy, and the entire hate list. It will attack the NEXT player who does damage because for all intensive purposes, blur starts the fight over again as far as the critter is concerned. In a group that couldn't care less about enchanter tactics, it still works. The odds are good that the next player will not be the one in trouble. But if the group is on the same page, the person who is getting frenzied on would first turn off auto attack when they call for a heal. This is the signal for the cleric to heal and the enchanter to blur. The mob will blink and turn onto someone else. I have used this spell in exactly this way before, and it ensures that damage is spread evenly among the melee types and no one player is taking the brunt.

This spell also has a powerful side effect. It wipes the runny tally of damage done. That means, it can be used to steal kills or steal kills back. If a creature is, say, down to 1/4 health, and an evil enchanter/wizard group wants to steal, there is nothing anyone can do to stop them except kill it faster than they can cast. If the enchanter blurs right before the wizzie casts, and the kill is theirs. Of course, enchanters only use such powers for good, never evil. There are some downsides. Since the spell essentially wipes the damage tally at the time it is cast, very very very occasionally a mob will die without giving experience. This happens when DoTs are cast BEFORE the blur. The dot will continue doing damage even though the monster has "forgotten" who cast the spell. Where the dots end up doing the majority of damage after the blur, since the damage comes from a non-specified source (the caster has been wiped from the list), the experience is not assigned to the group. This is a very rare. But it can happen.

5) Ahhh clerics So, what the hell does the cleric get out of all this, except the satisfaction of saving the enchanter’s ass a few times. Later in the fight, the enchanter gets to return the favor. Just like enchanters, clerics tend to draw unneeded attention to themselves if they heal. That tends to annoy unengaged mobs, which then come after our happy healer. By then, this should not be an issue, because our enchanter has the crowd completely under control. But every once in a while, one slips through. And it is the enchanter’s job to make sure the cleric (or designated healer) is not about to get flattened by a stray mob. The enchanter should be doing the same for nukes as well, who tend to draw attention occasionally.

In a good group, the healer, enchanter and wizard/mage work as a team, and that frees the other members to focus on the bad guys, rather than trying to strip frenzied mobs off the casters or watching their health bars plummet while the healer tried to get an uninterrupted heal off. Oh, there are so many others I could cover. But this is getting way too long.

IV. Bottom line Do groups have to play this way? No. Enchanters can work just fine as second-class nukes and clarity factories while using those crowd control tactics that are not dependent on group coordination. Personally, I think that’s like asking your tank not to use armor or your level 30 wizard to cast only level 20 spells. But groups will do what they will do. If there is one thing I wish groups would do when I am with them it is this: focus on one mob at a time, and have the leader announce which critter that will be. That alone would make your enchanter so much more effective.
