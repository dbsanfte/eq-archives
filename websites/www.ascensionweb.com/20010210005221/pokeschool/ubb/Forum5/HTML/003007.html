Here's my damage swap deck. I've only lost once on Apprentice with it. I wouldn't go into a DCI tournament with it though because stall decks just take too long to play. With a 20 minute time limit, I'm sure to lose since my opponent will take more prizes than me in that time frame. But in straight games, it's a frustrating deck to play against.

DAMAGE SWAP DECK

POKEMON
4 Gastly (Fossil of course)
3 Haunter
2 Gengar
4 Abra
3 Kadabra
2 Alakazam
2 Mr. Mime
3 Chansey

TRAINERS
3 Professor Oak
3 Computer Search
2 Pokemon Breeder
2 Pokemon Trader
3 Pokemon Center
2 Mr. Fuji
2 Energy Retrieval

ENERGY
2 Double Colorless Energy
18 Psychic Energy

STRATAGY
I wanted a deck to be able to damage swap when needed and dish out damage as part of its stalling tactics against slower decks.

Wounded Pokemon have a hard time fighting against this deck.

Keep track of your opponent's Oaking strategy. If he/she Oaks 2x, then try and Oak only 1 time. If he/she Oaks 3, then it's okay to Oak 2x. You'll still be 7 cards ahead. Without Bills or too many Searches, this deck will stay ahead.

Of course it's slow going, but one shot by Chansey on a big Pokemon then a retreat and a Pokemon Center, Gengar's swapping abilities can slows down opponents!

Needs that extra something I can't figure out yet.

Thanks for help!

I tried a deck based on much the same principles (except I *still* haven't got an Alakazam.... , so I was using Slowbro) The biggest problem I can see is that with both Gengar, and Alakazam you have a lot of evolves to use. I would suggest that you remove Gengar, but keep the Gastly and Haunters (excellent stall). With the two extra cards, I would suggest that you add a Computer Search and a Pokémon Center. I would also advise replacing the Mr. Fuji's with Scoop Ups, in case you're opponent uses the worst (second actually (behind Goop Gas Attack/Fossil Muk)) card possible, GOW. Annother alternative is that you could attempt to add in one more DCE. More Mimes and maybe one more each Kadabra and Alakazam might also be usefull.
