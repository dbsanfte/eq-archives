Part II (Bills and Oaks)

I received a response to my article (How to Build a Winning Deck) that stated: "You said 3 bill & 2 Prof oak? If you use a lot of draw cards you might lose due to decking. also, with so few pokemon, you might not get maybe basics right away and then you'd lose due to no bench. I fell that around 22 or 23 is a good number of pokemon for a deck. Don't take this the wrong way, like I'm saying your way is dumb. Its just feedback."

My response to the above statement is:

This article is based on averages from Tournament Winning Decks found in magazines and websites played at DCI ranked tournaments, online sponsored tournaments and tournament reports throughout the world.

Actually 3 Bill and 2 "Oaks" alone is a very small amount of draw cards to gain card advantage. Most trainer engines have to include Computer Search, Item Finders, Energy Retrievals, etc. Which means you have to discard cards from your hand for a better card.

It's all about card advantage. Without card advantage, your game is doomed. If you play all of these 5 cards, you have about 27 turns to win the game. That's plenty!

I'm not saying 3 Bill and 2 Oaks is something EVERY deck should use. Some decks have 4 Oaks yet never use more than 2 in the game. It's there for ratios. Sometimes 4 Oaks are needed in order for you to get at least 2 in your hand during game play.

I run most of my decks with 2 Oaks. Maybe HALF the time I use only one, yet sometimes during the beginning of a game I need to exhaust both Oaks in order to get the bench or cards I need.

If you're a tournament player you'll find people go "Oak crazy" at times. What I'm saying is, some players use 2 Oaks per turn, sometimes 3 in a two turn span just to get the bench and hand they need right away. This way they are ready for battle earlier than their opponent. Sure, decking is a problem, but usually you deck against someone who can stall. A majority of decks out there aren't stall decks. They're fighting decks - made to go out and do damage. So whoever does the most damage first wins!

Example of using Oaks.

~RAINDANCE - 3 Oaks minimum should be in a Raindance or Evolution deck. If you are going up against anyone who has a faster deck, you're in trouble. You need to run an Oak early to get an evolution you need or a Computer Search in your hand right away.

~WigglyHaymaker - If you're running a Wiggly Haymaker, you better draw cards fast before you're opponent gains an advantage. Get Wiggly evolved in 2 turns in order to attack fast. Use Oaks to get the basics to fill your bench and get that Wiggly evolved by turn 2 because by turn 3, IT MIGHT BE TOO LATE!!!

~Anti Evolution Decks - Aerodactyl is the key. So if you don't get Aerodactyl evolved by turn 2 or 3, forget it. His power is useless if someone has a faster deck. Oaks speed things up. Use 'em to get that Computer Search or hopefully Aerodactyl in order to get the advantage.

I wrote this article before Brokaw's new book came out - Pokemon Trading Card Fossil Guide. And in his book he has a section called, TOURNAMENT WINNING DECKS.

Here's the Bill/Oak ratios for those decks:
Woodworth - 4 Bill 3 Oak
**Recursive Scavenger (stall deck)- 2 Bill 0 Oak
Fossil Raindance - 4 Bill 4 Oak
Fossil Haymaker - 4 Bill 2 Oak
Psychic Buzz - 4 Bill 3 Oak
**Wildfire (stall deck) - 0 Bill 0 Oak
Kaminari - 3 Bill 2 Oak
Monster Swap 0 Bill 0 Oak
Ferocious Commons - 4 Bill 4 Oak
Cursed Swap 0 Bill 3 Oak
 
**Note: The only decks that don't really use this high Bill:Oak ratio are Stall Decks.
 
So if I do averages just on Brokaw's published decks omitting stall decks like in my article, I get:
23 Bills and 21 Oaks out of 8 decks.
 
That's an average of:
3 Bills(2.88) and 3 Oaks(2.63) in the deck. Higher than my average based on 20 decks.
 
But this result is an important example that screams... CARD ADVANTAGE with Bills and Oaks are KEY to building a Tournament Winning Deck.
