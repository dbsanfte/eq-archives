David Johnson #242E

BIR, June 18 - 30, 2004

I have never seen three such perfect racing days, weather-wise, and I have never seen so many track bozos. Does too much sunshine make people reckless? Do pleasant temperatures cause undue boldness? Or is it simply that bozos, like sunflowers; only show their faces when the weather is nice? In any case, the "conditions" led to some pretty hairball antics and spectacular carnage. And it must be said: I was not completely innocent of hairballiness this time.

 

Cell Division Resumes!

I am (surprisingly) somewhat on the mend relative to my usual state of cellular disarray, which helped me go a little faster, be a little braver, and drink beer with my right hand for a change. Talk about livin’ large! It’s like Viagra for the soul! Fourteen months have passed since I was injured crashing that beeatchy GS500, and it’s been 7 months since it was allegedly put right with the "allograft" operation (a process similar to making a box kite from raccoon parts, shoving it inside a bunny rabbit through a one-inch slit, and calling it a functional mammal). I have been hoping to get back up to my previous level of ineptitude since last April, and it finally felt like progress. My only complaint was a strange pain message I kept getting in turns 8 and 9, which made me run wide onto the red and white bumpy strips of Teflon death thoughtfully provided by the BIR folks. Yes I had fear! Yes I had doubts! But I punched through the pain, rode the burn, put my head down, maxed the envelope and so on! Hooray for repair enzymes, uplifting nonsense slogans, and (especially) those inspirational speeches from Cdr. Sponge Bob JenSpeed!

Suck it up, you wussies!

 

Lightweight Supersport – Calling all Goobers!

The Supersport race on Saturday could not have been more eventful if it had been a troop of baboons on unicycles. It began at the start (well duh!) when I suddenly discovered that my clutch had worn to the point of being just an on/off switch. I actually got a freaky-good start, but the paper-thin clutch engagement point had me riding an involuntary wheelie all through first gear. I hurtled right through the row ahead, craning my neck to see around the now-vertical tank, and steering around the inert speed-bump guy in front of me with nothing but ass magic. Despite my good start, all the cheaty-power folks caught up and clustered around me like gnats as we entered turn one. Gad! I hate that! Unfortunately, you can’t spray for ‘em, so there I was, at full throttle, near top speed, in a big moving cluster of bowling pins. And naturally, there was one oaf who fancied himself the ball. He was weaving back and forth menacingly, and finally, as he was observing something off track that was appare ntly more important than his life, he came bouncing into me. At a buck thirty. But even that failed to get his attention. He just kept fat-assing over while he gazed at the pretty flowers. I actually had to throw him an elbow to get his attention. He looked over at me like he wasn’t expecting anyone else to be out there. I passed him as quickly as possible, while reconsidering the idea of using pesticide. I finally managed to scrape that gum wad off on some slower riders in the infield and was looking forward to a nice clean run through turn one on the next lap. But NOOOO! Just as I was at what I thought was maximum lean angle for turn one, a new bumbler swerved wildly toward my line like a drunken Shriner! I cranked the hell out of the bars, shoved the tank over with my knee, and prepared to blow my ballast tank. It is very difficult to make quick corrections at that speed, but I did just enough flopping and butt-clenching to go ripping past him at his speed + 30 mph, nicking him with my foot peg and hopefully scaring the crap out of him. My on-board bozometer was now reading off the scale, but there were still more of them! Before I could completely extract myself from the remains of the oaf cluster, there were two separate crashes in turn four and a giant hog pile crash in turn three. Red flag! Everyone back to the pits! Just as I was dreading a restart with all of the same goobers I had just ditched, things went straight to weird. The CRA decided to call the race, and further proclaimed that all of the busted-ass dirt miners laying around like road kill had won the race! That’s right... people laying face down in the grit with a load in their racing shorts finished ahead of me and everyone else who was still on the track. HELLOOO? Does that make sense? Does anyone understand what this means? It means the best approach for me is to get as far ahead as I can in a race, then stop, lie down in the middle of the track, and flop around like a sunfish. The race is officially declared finished and I get whatever place I am in. OK then. That should save on tires.

Figure 1. When the Bozometer is pinned, you must be ready for anything.

 

SuperTwins – Why am I Seeing Sky?

The clutch situation did not magically improve between races, despite my careful ignoring of it. One millisecond after the green flag flapped, I was looking at sky. Not the little patch of clouds above the horizon, I mean the deep blue, satellite-filled vastness of outer space. I felt this was an invalid race strategy, so I grabbed the clutch and fell back to earth like a lead muffin. This was also an invalid race strategy, as evidenced by the "everyone is going into hyperspace but me" effect that I observed. For some reason, this angried up my blood, and I got a few good laps in before the "super sticky" tire I had chosen decided to become a banana peel. I managed to finish 8th, even without falling down and flopping like a sunfish, which I considered acceptable.

 

A Note about Tires - Try Again, Michelin Man

This week, the local trackside Michelin dealer recommended a new rear tire for us. He said "It grips like a soft, but it wears like a medium." After careful study, I concluded the following: HORSE BISCUITS! It grips like a soft because it is softer than a soft, and it wears about as well as an incompletely baked jelly roll. My guess is the dead dinosaurs used to make this tire were poorly refined and reverted back to their prehistoric tar-like state immediately upon contact with the ground. I was down to the baloney skin stage of tire wear by the middle of my second race (SuperTwins), which meant sliding the back end around in all the right-hand corner exits. I hate that. Maybe tires like this work for AMA racers who throw them away after six laps, but here in Squalor-Town I need to get a weekend out of one tire. I like the Michelin Pilot medium rear, thank you.

Figure 2. The latest from the Mars probe? No... this gumball is my tire after morning practice and two races.

 

Lightweight Superbike – Why am I Seeing Ground?

I have been lucky up to this point with bike parts not wearing out. No longer. My two previous starts had me worried about the grabby clutch and the sky I kept seeing. I resolved to do a better job. The result: I started the Superbike race with the sort of jump I imagine one would get by dumping the clutch on a ’76 Renault. This meant roaring into turn one surrounded by all the pests again. And yes, the same fat-ass with the faulty guidance system was menacing me once more. Note to self: Never mind the fly spray, bring napalm. Somehow I extracted myself from the bustling hoard, and was on my way to semi-glory (like 12th maybe?), when I suddenly noticed my brakes were not working. Of course I was coming into turn three at 120-something at the time, trying to scrub off enough speed to live. I squeezed ‘em, I had plenty of lever feel, but the bike did not slow down. More squeezing, and suddenly the front wheel locked and pushed, and I slid to almost a complete stop sideways in th e middle of turn three. I got the hell out of there just before the rest of the angry hive arrived, only to have the exact same experience in turn four. After two corners of near death, I backed off a little and wondered "was that just me?" Meanwhile, my ever dingo-ish teammates Tate and Billy were stalking me while I was trying to comprehend why some new laws of physics were being applied to me. I did two more laps that seemed OK, and I began to suspect it was just some sort of dementia. On the last lap, Billy got a better drive out of turn nine and edged by me. "It’s all coming down to turn ten" I thought, "just like on TV." I quickly moved over behind Billy and had the perfect draft 10 inches off his tail. I was being pulled along using about ¾ throttle, thinking about dinner and preparing tales of victory for the adoring pit bunnies. I even had time to think "He really needs to get some advertising back here." As we came up to the brake markers for turn 10 , I had perfect position. I pulled out to do what should have been a simple brake and pass maneuver. I grabbed a handful of brakes and...nothing. I grabbed for more, and suddenly there was a vise on my front tire, and I was looking at asphalt. My face shield slammed into the windscreen. It felt like a huge stoppie, and when I saw my feet in front of the windscreen I quickly decided this was not going to work out. I released the brake, but unfortunately I had already secured the coveted inside line. I surged past Billy just as he was turning in and we slammed together like enraged Battlebots. Then there was that sound - that horrible sound that you could only mimic by dragging a dozen vacuum cleaners behind your truck. Scruuutttch! Billy was down! Remorse turned to fear, which turned to AAAAAAAHHHHHHHHH! I straight-lined the corner and launched off the lip of the banking, still going 80-something towards the fence with my ankles up around my ears. Then I remembered the soothing words I had installed in my brain after last month: "Don’t touch the @#$%*!$ front brake in the grass, you moron!" I didn’t. I foot-flailed at the rear brake pedal, doing my best impression of a guy not falling down. I stopped right at the fence and waited for my stomach to arrive. I felt like a treed cat – all agitated with no where to go. During a normal crash, there isn’t time to get excited before it’s over. In an incident like this, where the "almost crashing" phase lasts for ten seconds, the little adrenaline squirter becomes a fire hose. I’m sure this must have been handy for my cave-dwelling ancestors, but with no mastodons to slay I just had to simmer in my own hot brain chemicals. As Billy came walking over, I was hoping he wasn’t about to do the Aaron Yates Daytona Monkey Dance at me, since I was still sitting on my bike with no where to run. Fortunately, he was pretty much unharmed and strangely calm. I have got to get some of whatever he is taking

 

Autopsy

When I got back to the team’s vast service facility, I began the delicate process of "What the #*%@ happened?" I noticed there was a giant skid mark on the front of the lower fairing where my forks had actually flexed enough (like 4 inches!) to let the front tire rub. Jeepers! That was some impressive braking force! There was also a big skid mark on my lowers from Billy’s tire, the shift lever was mashed from contact with his brake rotor, and something folded the heel guard over about 45 degrees. Fortunately my foot was not on the peg at the time, or it would be about the consistency of a pot pie right now. I disassembled the front brakes to see what evil had crept in there and caused all of this. To say the brake pads were glazed is something of an understatement. They were frosted like a wedding cake, with what must have superheated brake rotor plasma.

Figure 3. Mmmmm..... glazed! Compare to normal used pad in background.

I saved the pads for my scrapbook, and noted that, for once, I knew what had happened and why. I also evicted the calipers, brake fluid, and rotors, and I shook my beads and rattles over the whole thing just for good measure. Ooooga Bunga! Fixed!

 

Figure 4. The forks flexed enough during the hideous stoppie to let the tire burn half way through my lower fairing. It’s fixed now, thanks to modern technology.

 

Fine Print

Thanks again to Sage Homes (www.mysagehome.com) and JenSpeed (www.jenspeed.com) for giving us money, useful things, and inspiration. I hope I am providing entertainment value or something. HellOOOooo? Internet? Anybody on this thing? Please go buy stuff from them so I can keep writing this twaddle.

 

Summary:
  • I saw entirely too much sky while seated on the motorcycle. New clutch please.
  • I saw entirely too much earth while seated on the motorcycle. New brakes please.
  • The pesticide sales group at Dupont says there is nothing labeled for control of leather-clad dolts. I will have to improvise. Hello – Defense Department?
  • Teammate Billy must have some dynamite tranquilizers. I want some.

Lightweight Supersport 11th (Saved by my Spidey sense)

SuperTwins 8th (On a wing and a banana peel)

Lightweight Superbike 15th (And glad to have it)
