New 1998 GSX-R600 for the 1999 Season!

Oct 3rd, 1998

I got too good of a deal to pass up on a new 98 Suzuki GSX-R600 ( MSRP $7799 and the dealer sold it for $6525 ).  I'm going through its break-in period and still haven't got enough miles on it to feel the power.  I can say that it handles like its on rails.  The front end is very light, responsive and provides excellent feedback.   I wasn't really planning on buying a new "race" bike for the 99 season,   but after taking it for a ride...I just had to put it on the track.  :-(

The current plans for the 99 season are as follows:

  1. Get the GS-500E into the shop to have the engine rebuilt and a few more ponies eeked out of it.  The goal is 50+ HP.
  2. Buy race plastic, pip & jet kit  for the GSXR.  Probably not until February 99.
  3. Take the FREE ( with purchase ) Suzuki race school, hopefully with both bikes.
  4. Take Keith Code's school with both bikes and a group of  local wanna go faster friends.
  5. RACE! Focus on the GS-500 w/ the GSXR there for a few "fun" races.
  6. If I feel comfortable/don't crash on the GSXR and can get my lap times at BIR into the low 1:50's to 1:40's,  race the 99 AMA Pro 600 SuperSport race at BIR.

GSXR Update#1

11/22/98:  I had a little highside in WI.  I was out enjoying the unusually warm weather and came upon a downhill left.  Everything was going good until I noticed that the turn was full of marble size gravel chunks.  I grabbed the brakes and even used the rear a little ( maybe good, but probably bad ) to shed as much speed as possible.  I set myself up for the turn and released the brakes to try and roll thru the gravel.  The rear grabbed and spit me off the right side of the bike.  The pipe looks like someone took a ballpen hammer to it,  the mirror & front blinker are toast, right side plastic has some good roadrash, and the right footpeg bracket is bent/cracked.  It looks like I've got some work to do & more money to spend.   Most of the stuff I was planning on removing/replacing anyway...so it's not that bad.  I was hoping to keep the stock plastic perfect for when I took the race plastic off to ride the bike on the street between races.  I did find a few good sources for used stock body work.  Mostly from racers who had taken the stock stuff off.   The $1000-$1300 that it would have cost to make my bike stock mint again...just seems to take too much from the race budget.

12/7/98:  I ordered some Harris Performance rearsets from the U.K.  I got a 20% racer discount from Harris.  The rearsets ended up costing ~$280 with shipping.  They bolted on pretty easy and the adustability should come in handy. Seeing how the frame got some gravel nicks, I also ordered some Carbon Fiber frame guards from ART Exotic Motorcycle Accessories ( U.K. )   I hope they will help protect the frame from getting further cosmetic damage during my '99 championship season! :-)  I also picked up a used race take-off mirror and blinker for $40 from a racer in MD. I'm glad the GSXR600/750 share so many parts and that he was willing to sell them to me! My next purchase will be the pipe & jet kit, closely followed by the race plastic. 

New Plans for 99 season:

  1. Sell my GS-500E
  2. Concentrate on the GSX-R600
  3. Maybe find a "slower" bike or even Dual Sport to ride on the street.
  4. Get comfortable on the GSX-R600 by taking advantage of the local & cheap practice track.
  5. If my BIR race laptimes get low enough, try and qualify for the AMA Pro race at BIR.

Mounting a W.E.R. front mount steering Dampener to a 98 GSX-R600

Mounting the dampener wasn't as easy as the instructions made it out to be.  The instructions seemed to be made for the 750.  I had to grind about an inch off the stock instrument mount bracket to make room for the dampener.  After that,  the dampener would bump into the fuses located on the left ram air tube.  I moved the fuses into a little space below and to the right of the ram air tube.  So...now that I have it mounted how does it feel?  I don't really know.  I don't feel any resistance in the steering and I have it set .5 or 1 turn back from MAX.  Maybe W.E.R didn't put 10W oil in it, it has air in the thing or I just don't know what a dampener is supposed to feel like.  I hoping that it is the latter.

Harris Rearsets ( Left side )

Notice the range of adjustablity.  I might even use it!  :) I like 'em,   but dealing with Harris in the U.K. can be a pain. It took 'em about 1.5 months to fill an order for some spare pegs. About a month after I initially placed my spares order with Harris, I figure that Harris lost my order.  I find a U.S. distributor for Harris stuff  and I order some spare pegs from them too.  2 weeks after that,   Harris ships my original order...so now I have 4 spare pegs on the way.  I guess you can never have too many spares.

More Bike Prep and Frenzied Parts Ordering

3/29/99: A new subscriber to the GSXR list has his 98 GSX-R600 stock plastic for sale.   He breaks down and agrees to sell it to me for a mere $500 plus shipping.  I can't pass a deal like that up...so to the mail a check goes.  I find out that he is also a dealer for Pro-Tek.  I need some re-enforced case covers and he offers to sell 'em to me at well below retail prices.  I'll check with him before I list this good source for parts.  Sorry,  folks.  Anyway...the plastic arrives later in the week and it is good.  Now my bike can be seen in public without a huge pavement scar on its right side.  Now the question is....why spend another $500-750 for race plastic?  I'll just use the original and scratched stuff to race and put the undamaged "street" stuff back on between races when I want to make it pretty for the street. 

4/18/99: After a ride with some friends Sunday morning, we end up back at the house in time to catch the AMA Superbike race at Willow on Speedvision.  A few more friends came over and one of them was an amateur motorcycle mechanic.  He ended up "volunteering" to help me install my Factory Jet kit.  I pulled the carbs and then we both poured over the flimsy instructions that came with the kit.  A few things with the kit just didn't make sense after looking at the carbs.  The main jet sizes were all wrong.  The mains that came with the kit were all smaller than the current mains.  That didn't seem right.  Sooo,  after spending 20 minutes comparing things, I notice that the instructions say that the kit is for the "US,CA Suzuki GSX-R600 97 & 99" model.  Hmmm...I wonder if having a 98 model is what the problem is?   I decide to give up on the carbs until I can call Factory on Monday.  I end up getting a few more of my friends to lean the bike to the left, while I  install the Pro-Tek right side engine cover.  It went on pretty easy, feels stronger than the stock one, and even more important...it looks cool!  Now my bike beginning to look like a race bike.  :-)

4/19/99:  I confirmed that the Factory Kit was the wrong one.  I also find out that Factory doesn't make the Race Jet kit for the 98 GSX-R600.  Bummer!   Now Trackstar wants to charge me a 20% restocking fee!  I think that I'm going to have to contest that fine...seeing how it wasn't my fault they ordered the wrong kit. I guess that I'll just have to try the Yoshimura MJN Carb System kit that  fellow GSX-R600 pilot and new Pumpkin, Matt Hanson, is raving about.  It uses the "emulsion tube" principle vs. a pilot needle.  Yoshimura claims that it's the "best" thing since sliced bread.  $300 on the card later...I have one on order from www.motodepot.com. 

I finally developed a sense of urgency and got busy prepping the bike for racing tonight.  I stopped by the Ace Hardware store on my way home from work and picked up some hose clamps,  catch bottle hose and an extra long Philips head screwdriver for the carb removal and installation.  Big purchase!  :-)   I rig up my catch bottle in the tail section "trunk" from an 1 quart plastic oil bottle.   The 1/2 inch tubing is pretty easy to route from the tail section to the engine breather thing.  I vented the catch bottle, then inserted a 1/4 diameter hose in the vent hole and routed that out the back.  That way...if the engine total spews and somehow manages to fill the catch bottle...the overflow will safely miss my back tire.   At least, that's my plan. 

I take a break for dinner and plan on being lazy for the rest of the night, but NOoooo, Todd Poindexter ( R-1 Expert Racer CRA# 116 ) decides to come over. :-) Back in the garage and out come the safety-wire pliers & the drill.  I get most of the right side hose clamps wired.  While I'm doing that I get Todd to pull my front race wheel with it's shiny new slick out from under the kitchen table ( yes..I am single ). He goes to town pulling the perfectly good stock rotors off and mounting the PFM rotors that I got from YoyodydneTi.com.  Todd makes the appropriate jealousy noises as he's putting them on the wheel.  I then make the mistake of showing him the Speedbleeder® bleeder valves.  He wants to try them. :-(  I pull the braided lines out from under the kitchen table and we proceed to get the floor full of brake fluid.  The installation of the Galfer lines (  YoyodydneTi.com ) was relatively easy...once we figured out that both hoses connected up top vs. down on the right side caliper like the Suzuki had it setup.   Bleeding the lines was a little tricky, until I discovered that the hose that runs from the reservoir and the master cylinder was collecting all the air bubbles and preventing the fluid from reaching the lines.  I then primed the lines by removing one line at a time from the caliper and "bleeding" the lines until fluid started to flow.  Reconnected the lines to the calipers and then those wonderful speed bleeder valves did the rest.   Get 'em if you can!

4/20/99: I give Metro Hitching Post in St. Paul a call and I try and order the same HJC Helmet and the same color that I got last year.  HJC doesn't make the fashionable Orange solid colored helmet anymore.  I find a design/paint scheme that I like at the Motorcycle Accessory Warehouse's website,  but the parts guy at Metro can't find it in his catalog. So...after about 20 minutes on the phone with most of that time spent on hold... I decide not to order the big ticket item from them.  I settle for ordering the Suzuki GSX-R600 shop manual from them instead.   I then promptly call the MAW order line and place an order for the HJC LT-12 "Inferno" helmet.  It was the only one that had orange in it's graphics design.  Sigh...more money.   :-(

Jetting and the benefits of following the instructions

4/23/99

     I got my 98 GSX-R600 jetted. I had some "fun" while doing it. I got a Factory Race Jet Kit from Trackstar. The only problem is that it was for the 97 & 99 model. My bike is the 98 model and the carbs are different. Good news is that Trackstar is giving me a 20% discount on parts, bad news...they charged me a 20% restocking fee to return the thing. I understand the purpose behind a re-stocking fee, but in this case I disagree with being charged $25 for their mistake in ordering the parts. Needless to say...I'll probably be ordering the bulk of my future parts online. Enough of that...So I end up looking for a different jet kit and the Yosh MJN kit came highly recommended. It's $279 at motodepot.com. Ouch! It uses "emulsion tubes" instead of needles. It sounds like a good idea. Anyway...now for the "fun" part.

    One of the new for 1999 Evil Pumpkins, Matt Hanson, has a 97 GSXR600. He was the one who pointed me to the Yosh kit. The Main Air Jet is a "pressed fit" jet and the 97 kit required drilling the Main Air Jet, extracting it and plugging the hole. It also required drilling the mixture screw plugs out and drilling the pilot needle hole out to 3.8 mm to make room for the emulsion tubes. To get a jump on the work while I waited for my kit to arrive...We ( Todd & I ) drilled out the mixture screw plugs and drilled & extracted the Main Air Jets. Just in case the emulsion tubes for the 98 kit where different, I decided to wait for the 98 kit before drilling the pilot needle hole. I should have waited for my kit before I did any drilling! When I got the 98 kit, I was unpleasantly surprised to see that the 98 kit didn't have the Main Air Jet step! :-(

    Panic time! I called Steve Guynes & left an email with Yoshimura ( no reply...yet ) Since the MAJ's are pressed in at the factory...The only way to get new Main Air Jets is to buy new carb$. Luckily I didn't drill deep enough into the MAJ to screw up the tiny opening on the bottom of the jet. Per Steve's advice, I scraped out all the junk the drilling put in the jet and tried smoothing out the worst damage. I then tapped the suckers back into the carbs. I installed the rest of the kit and got the carbs back on the bike. The bike started ( that was a minor relief ), but by this time it was 11pm and I was leaving for Texas the next morning. No time to test it.

5/2/99

    I finished getting the bike back together yesterday. I start it and take it out to see if I screwed things up too bad to race. It seems to rev okay, but it's a bogging down. Kinda like it's running out of gas. I'm thinking time consuming float height and mixture screw adjustments...new carbs & the missing my first race. To relax....I decide to watch the Japan 500 GP race that I taped while down in Texas.

    After that, I go out to the garage and start pulling things apart. I pull the airbox out and I notice that the hose that runs from the front of the airbox, splits in two and connects to the top of the carbs wasn't connected to the airbox when I pulled it out. Not sure what it does...but I guess it helps fuel flow somehow. I pray that this is the answer ( I like simple solutions to complex problems ) I put the airbox back in and make sure that the hose is attached this time. Start her up and take her out to terrorize the housing development with a high rev sub orbital speed pass. The hesitation is gone and she's revving smooth and strong!

    I then start putting the "race plastic" back on and finish safety wiring everything. I put the brake light back on so that I can legally ride it to the "track". I'm taking the bike out to a local "practice" session at a 1.1 mile 15 turn track a couple of miles from my house tonight. I also decide to check my WER dampener out and make sure that I have it installed and adjusted correctly. While I'm moving the handlebars back in forth in an effort to adjust the dampening level, I here a squishing noise. What the hell is that!? I look down and notice oil squirting out of the dampener. :-( It's leaking onto the fender and my tire! Damn it! Can't anything go right? No dampener and less than a week to go before my first race on a new bike. I'll be calling WER this morning...maybe I'll get lucky.

    WER is sending me a rebuild kit ( $29.95 ).  The only other option was to ship them the dampener overnight,  get it rebuilt and hopefully sent back to me before I left for the track Thursday night.  If it leaks after I rebuild it....WER is going to get some bad press...and I'm definitely planning on letting you...my faithful reader(s) know about it.  :-)

5/3/99: Team Absolute Riders Practice - Dakota County Technical College Track

    Today was the first time that I've had the bike out for a good thrashing.  The short version is:  1st couple of sessions were slow as I got used to the bike and found my brake markers while chewing up the transmission by having it in the wrong gear most of the time. By the end of it...I was feeling real good,   dragging a knee or two and having a blast. 

    Longer version:  I have the GSXR ready for the track...with the exception of the headlight and taillight.  I left those on the bike so that I could ride it the 3 miles from my house to the track.  I got to the track around 5:15, registered and paid my $50.  I signed up for the Medium group.  Glen had the session sign-up sheets marked as Advanced ( slow ), Expert ( medium ) & Master ( Fast ).  I found the euphemisms kinda humorous. 

    During my first session, I spent most of the time getting used to bike & track.  It wasn't too hard to find the brake markers and turn points,   but figuring out what gear I needed to be in was a pain.  Too high and the bike bogs...too low and the bike is bouncing off the rev limiter half way thru the corner.   I missed a few down shifts and put her in neutral going into turn one a few too many times.  Especially since turn one is where most of the people watch from. :-)   I did have one semi-close call during the first session.  I was behind 3 bikes and gaining on them while coming down the back straight.  The off-camber, downhill left was coming up and the bikes in front of me decide to park in the turn.  I had to drop anchor and remind myself to turn before I ran off the track while trying to avoid the last of the terrible trio's rear end.  I paid MUCH more attention to everyone while going into that turn for the rest of the night.  The downhill off-camber is the corner that sees the most crash action on the course,  so many riders take it slower than they need to.

    A new rider on 98 GSX-R600 came over and we started talking about what gear we where in and when.  I was never really sure about what gear I was in.   I think that I would downshift into 2nd going into turn one.  The main reason I think that I was in 2nd was due to the many neutrals that I accidentally found myself "enjoying" on the entrance to that turn.  I tried leaving it in 3rd for turn one, but it just didn't feel right going into such a hard turn, after a braking from the front straight without doing a downshift.  I usually up-shifted as I was making the right turn onto the back straight.  About 1/3 of the way before the off-camber I'd hit 4th?, then touch the brakes going into the off-camber.  Cruise the off-camber with my knee touching down every now and then, and then down-shift a gear going into the decreasing radius ( kink ).  Coming out of the kink and onto the front straight, I'd upshift into 4th and then just before the chicane, I'd loose some speed by grabbing the brakes for a second.  A quick left-right accelerating out of the chicane, then HARD on the brakes for turn one and downshift.  I think that I NEED to pay more attention to what gear I'm in throughout the course.  It makes it hard to compare notes and trade advice when your doing the shifting by feel.  Oh well...
