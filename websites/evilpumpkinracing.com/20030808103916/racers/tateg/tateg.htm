Tate's Corner

Time to Upgrade

BIR, June 20-22, 2003

It was time to upgrade the race bike platform from a GS500 to an SV650, and I had about 6 weeks before the next race weekend. No problem. I once learned a semester’s worth of Abstract Algebra in 4 days (well…learned it well enough for a C, anyway), so how hard can this be? It’s not like I’m trying to figure out women or something…

So the order was sent to Paul Jensen of Jenspeed Racing (www.jenspeed.com) and I waited for the parts to arrive. I learned a lot about the world of aftermarket parts. ALMOST NOTHING FITS OR WORKS RIGHT!!

  1. Forks: The settings for the preload are determined by how large a spacer you cut from a piece of pipe, and the damping is set by a small screw inside the forks. So set it now, and then disassemble the forks and cut more pipe bits to make adjustments?!? I was saved this frustration by Brad Frey from Two Bee’s Tuning since he called me with the settings that he’s found to work best.
  2. Shock: Why the heck would you make an aftermarket shock for an SV that isn’t threaded for the same lower mounting bolt? Given my anorexic supply of nuts and bolts, I now have 1 English bolt, and the rest of the bike is metric on the SV.
  3. Digital Tach: Since the stock tachometer is also part of the stock speedo, I needed a tach and opted for the digital one. Sure would have saved a lot of iterations of installation attempts if the Japanese translator for the installation instructions wouldn’t have skipped the day when his English class talked about "positive" vs. "negative".
  4. Shift light: Worked perfectly. Of course, I just spliced the tach wire to connect to the light, so I never checked the instructions…
  5. Braided Brake Lines: You’d think someone would have checked the thread pitch of the brake line bolts on the calipers to make sure they’re right, but nnnnooooooo. Also, if you’re ever race prepping an SV, be sure to give some thought into whether you order parts for an SV or an SV-S, since once you put clip-ons on your SV, the length of brake line needed is more like that of an SV-S. I now have brake line poking out from behind the sides of the number plate, like arm pit hair on a French woman poking out from behind the edges of her tank top.
  6. Rearset Foot pegs: There’s no way anyone tried mounting these at the factory where they’re made, because the shift shaft drags on the frame. But, with a little help from the handy-dandy Dremel I was able to make a properly sized spacer. On the other side, I just had to accept the face that there was no way to mount the return spring on the brake pedal. I … uh … guess I don’t need it?
  7. Frame Sliders: Ok, Goober – don’t send instructions for which of the sliders mounts to which engine mounting bolt or anything (yes, the 2 sliders are different sizes, and there are different engine mounting bolts they could mount to).
  8. Number Plates: In my attempt to control cost, I opted to not buy full bodywork, and just mount number plates. So about 5 days before racing, I began looking. Guess what? Nobody carries them in stock, and Lockhart-Phillips "discontinued that part number". I finally found some on the web for way to much money.
  9. Temp Gauge: Worked perfectly.
  10. Bellypan: Good luck finding a bellypan that can contain fluid. I found one that at least could be modified to contain fluid, but then it turns out that the mounting bracket doesn’t fit with an M4 exhaust. Dr Dave Johnson, his grinder, and countless hours of bending metal finally resolved the mounting problem.
  11. Exhaust: First, I removed the old one, which was way more difficult than I expected. Once that was done, I opened the box for the new one, and the directions said "Do not remove the stock rear cylinder header pipe". Ooops…too late. Glad I wasted that effort.

Anyway, I need to thank Dr Dave, Dr Dave’s grinder, Clint Lee, Paul Jensen, Brad Frey, and my credit card for their help with the SV project. I never would have made it in time without their help.

Now it’s OFF TO THE RACES!

Friday’s practice was great fun! The SV runs about 20mph faster on the top end than the GS did, so it took me quite a while before I was keeping any real speed through turn 2. But after a while I was having great fun dicing with teammate Billy Hewitt. I was enjoying the good brakes on the SV, and had fun stuffing Billy in turn 3 a few times (comes back to haunt me later). Ok, I’m ready for the races.

During the Lightweight Supersport race, I had a wonderful launch at the start, and was running closely behind the "fast guys" through the first lap. "Wow, this is great". But then I hit a false neutral during my downshift in turn 10. Since I tend to shift poorly even on the street, I knew that soon after the false neutral, there was a good chance that the transmission would find the gear on its own with a big "clack!" soon, so I held in the clutch to keep that from messing with my rear wheel during the turn. Problem was, now I can’t raise my left hand to warn the other riders that I was now traveling at a really sucky speed. So I just tried to hold a predictable line and went way to the outside of turn 10, and once the mob had passed me I searched through the gearbox until I found one that would work. I could see Billy and Dr Dave way ahead, and I knew what my mission was. With just a couple laps to go, I had the pleasure of surprising Dr Dave by passing him. "Gue ss who’s back, Dave?" Once again, I was having great fun racing with someone. Then, we got passed by a couple of really fast Novice riders (bastards!), so I tried to pick it up a notch so they wouldn’t get away from me. Coming into turn 9, before we were even braking, they passed an Orange Shirt on the right and the good news is I saw some room on the left of him where I could also pass. Bad news is that the Orange Shirt moved left as those ahead of me passed him on the right, perhaps out of some misplaced effort to be safe. My passing lane was now shifted solidly into the grass. Traveling at race speed on the grass is tough business. I had the presence of mind to not grab front brake in the grass (been there before, don’t want to do that again), so I was working the back brake. Each time it felt like it was sliding to the side, I’d ease up and wait for the bike to straighten out. However, I did not have the presence of mind to remember to pull the clutch in, so the motor stalled, and I had no w ay to get out of the back tire slide I was in. Hello, HI SIDE! The bike vaulted me into the air, and I landed on my right shoulder and head. I tumbled forever, and then got up and sat behind some trees wondering what that ringing sound was.

The SV took the crash really well, all things considered. The frame sliders worked wonderfully, but the tank took a hell of a hit. It was kind of depressing to see how wrinkled, scratched, broken, and dirty the once pretty machine had become. But I had the same experience sometime in my early 30’s looking in the mirror after a shower, so this was certainly not something that was going to be too depressing since it can be fixed.

So, with a new helmet in hand, I was ready for the Superbike and GP races on Sunday. In the Lightweight Superbike race, I had some weird type of petit mal seizure or spasm or something while waiting for the green flag, and unconsciously jumped the start. I stopped within just a few feet, but then the flag went and I wasn’t ready yet to go again. So the entire pack took off without me. Most of that race I was just racing myself. Not much fun. But with all that time by myself out there I realized that I needed to make a gearing change. The 44 tooth sprocket was too tall, so I made a mental note to up that to a 45 for the next weekend.

During the Lightweight GP race, I was stalked incessantly by Billy Hewitt. Although I know you shouldn’t do it, I kept looking behind me to see if I was getting away from him. No. As the last lap started, I was wondering where he was going to make his move. He obviously had plenty of time to figure out where he is faster than I am, and would make his move there. It just sucks when you know it’s coming. Sure enough, just before turn 2, he drafts past me and I couldn’t get back in his draft and return the favor. "But I’ve been able to out-brake him coming in to turn 3 every time", I though to myself (insert evil grin here). Unfortunately, Billy’s a quick learner and it was not to be. He blocked the inside line, and was plenty good on the brakes. He did the same thing through turns 4 – 8. Often when you are protecting against the pass really aggressively, you aren’t really going through turns as fast as you would otherwise. So when I saw he was protecting the inside pass again in turn 9, I took a new strategy – corner exit speed. I got on the brakes relatively early, and set up for an early apex and early acceleration (the whole time really really hoping there was no one waiting to pass me, since it would have been easy to take the inside line on me with this approach to the turn). Hey! It worked! I was able to carry much more speed exiting the turn and I passed him. In a rare display of calmness, I managed not to get too psyched out in turn 10 and keep my position ahead of Billy. I’m the man! I OWN … 8TH place. Or … 10th … or … 12th … whatever it was. Ok, so I’m really not "the man". Nonetheless, it was some of the most fun I’ve had racing.

Lessons Learned:

  1. New riders wear orange shirts for a reason. As Dr Dave put it, "I wouldn’t have tried that outside pass on an Orange Shirt. You can’t count on them to behave reasonably." But truthfully, the fault lies with me for picking the passing line that I did – It’s the passing rider’s obligation to safely make the pass, and I didn’t.
  2. Novice? Expert? It doesn’t matter. There are some very fast novice riders (my hat’s off to you!), and there are a few slower expert riders.
  3. Fellow Evil Pumpkin Rich Nelson (aka, the Great Pumpkin) is busy prepping his new SV for racing. Once he joins Billy and me, and once Dr Dave has heeled a little more, there’s going to be some great racing (at the back of the back?) between us.
  4. As our benevolent sponsor, Eric of Sage Homes (www.mysagehome.com), stated in a note left on my banged up SV tank, "Fans love a good crash". Glad I was able to add to your enjoyment of the weekend.
  5. Apparently there were some pictures taken of me while I was taking a nap. No, really! I was asleep! So if you see these pictures defaming me, know that I was asleep and Photoshop was most likely involved.

Well, I’m off to see the bodywork guy to see what he can do with my tank and tail section. We’ll talk again after the July races.

I really enjoy riding motorcycles

BIR, May 2-5, 2003

A person spends an awful lot of his time doing things out of obligation, but a motorcycle ride is solely for you. It doesn’t matter if it’s trail riding, motocross riding, street riding, or roadracing – it’s always just you and your machine having fun with your buddies. But life has an incessant and wicked sense of humor, which requires an all-too-often HUGE dose of cycle maintenance, which I really do not enjoy at all

It was my turn for that misery while I was putting my engine together just before the May 2003 CRA races at Brainerd. After several trips to the machine shop (long story, don’t get me started), the GS500 engine was ready for assembly. Teammate Clint Lee was very gracious in his services in assisting me in assembling the motor in his basement, and explicitly warning me to "be sure an cover the openings before trying to put the pistons on, otherwise you’ll drop the lock ring into the hole." OK, so noted. At least for the first piston. I forgot on the second piston, and dropped the cursed ring into the great abyss. I actually did a good job of not introducing his children to a level of foul language that one only really embraces after a military tour. After an eternity with an extend-o-magnet, I was able to fish it out of the bottom end of the engine, and was referred to as a "lucky shit." Funny – didn’t really feel lucky. Fine. Get over it. Then two of the eight cam cap bolts also decided to strip the threads. A week later, I guess I can see how Clint thought this was so funny.

Now, the bike is running and IT’S OFF TO THE RACES! Dr Dave accompanied me to the races, although he still has an injured flipper so he would just be practicing and not entering any races. So we left really early on Friday morning to get up for a day of practice. Since I didn’t race during the first race of the year, it really took me an entire day of practice before I was able to ignore all of the voices in my head and really ride like a person should ride on a race track.

After a day of practice, it was time to relax over a beer or two. But, again, it’s never that simple. I met a lady, who I will call Ducati (748cc set of jugs) who was justly proud of her new set of … uh … rebuilt twins. I handled myself very properly . While the rebuild on the set was a tribute to some physician’s countless hours of study, it was nonetheless awkward to be evaluating them in the presence of her husband. But hey, in the name of medicine, I obliged.

During Saturday’s races, I learned that teammate Paul Jensen really knows how to ride the hell out of a bike – pissed me off getting stomped by a relative newby to roadracing, but he’s a good guy so I’ll forgive him. I also learned that I don’t want to keep racing a GS500. Too much maintenance, and I’m just tired of dealing with trying to make a bike that was never meant to be raced behave properly on the racetrack (OK, and Paul doesn’t have one yet so I won’t have to get beat by him if I have one). So, stay tuned while I try to convince Chase that I deserve a higher credit limit to buy an SV650.

Racing Lessons Learned in May:

  1. Just because no one is closely behind you at lap 3, if you relax the pace you will definitely give up 2nd place by lap 7.
  2. If you feel an odd tugging on your arm at 100+ mph, don’t panic – it’s just Bruce McGee pulling your arm for fun.
  3. The stock GS500 rear shock sucks while racing. Paul Jensen referred to my bike as the "pogo stick" after watched my bike wallow around in turn three during the 17-lap trophy dash race – it was scary
.

Well, I’m off to find an SV650 – hell, the kids can find their own way to pay for college … and food … an clothes …

June 23-25th, 2000

Although it was the second race weekend for the CRA, it was my first racing of the year since I had scheduling conflicts with the first CRA weekend. So, as you can imagine, I was just chompin' at the bit to get out on the track. Friday's practice sessions were going well, until..."wait, is that oil I see on the side of my bike?" Sure enough, I had a leaky head gasket. I sat there staring at it for a long time trying to decide if it was leaking enough that it could cause me problems (like oil on the rear tire). After all, it really wasn't leaking much. Dr Dave provided his insightful comment of "yeah, but how much oil does it take on the rear tire to cause problems?"

Good point, but I decided to continue practicing and just watch the leak to see if it got any worse. It seemed fine through the practice sessions friday, and the bike was running really well, so I just tried to goop on a bunch of liquid-gasket-sealant-stuff in hopes of slowing the leak (yeah, I know...it didn't work for me either). Hell, the bike's feels fast (well, for a GS, that is), and I'm feeling pretty good - I'M RACING!! Saturday I raced the ultralight supersport sprint and trophy dash races, inbetween the all the red flags and oil cleanups that the middle weight races were good enough to provide. Both races played out basically the same. My usual blazing starts put me about dead last into turn 1. So I just figured I would have to get past this pack of people in front of me and try to dice with the fast guys up front. Then it became obvious to me that in the expert class the ENTIRE pack of racers in front of me were fast guys, as well as a couple of novices who probably should be experts. OK, time to get brave. Coming into turn 3 I went very late on the brakes to get past one guy. I was just feeling pretty studly, when I realized that someone was out

late-braking me also! ARE YOU KIDDING?!?!? Both races concluded with me finishing about 5th or 6th, having turned my best lap times ever. It was great fun though, since there were about 8 guys all of whom were VERY fast riders. It made for extremely fun racing since most corners involved passing or getting passed. Although I do question how another supersport legal GS500 can pull away from me down the front straight when I am drafting on him...

Sunday morning, I felt ready to race. I had a new appreciation for how competitive the races would be, and I was really looking forward to them. Unfortunately, after one practice lap in the morning, my head gasket blew. The bike started bucking, and there was oil on my leg and boot. &#^%@! I was done for the weekend. So I just moped around the pits packing up my stuff and watching the morning races. Looking forward to the July race weekend, I have replaced my head gasket, and am confident that the bike will hold up. Now, if I can just get my newly leaking fuel line rigged up properly over the next week....

Going fast and vegetables on pizza  ( July 16th-18th 1999 )

"Time for another race weekend...I suppose I should call Dave and see what kind of mechanical crisis needs fixing before we can go,", I said to myself on about Monday. "Ignition locator pin" was this weekend's fix. No problem. We turned Clint Lee (future pumpkin?) loose in his trouble shooting mode, and voila - a nail of the same diameter cut to size and a little assembly and Dave's race bike runs again.

Up to Brainerd we go. After Thursday's drive up there (through a tree - ask Dave for details) and a good night's sleep (Days Inn north of BIR is WAY better than the one in Brainerd) we were ready for Friday Practice. Both our bikes ran well, and it was obvious to me that Dave was going to be a force to be reconned with so long as his steed held up. Good day of practice on Friday - I just kept repeating to myself "hands loose on the handlebars, look way ahead - past the riders in front of me, and feel where the seat is against my body on the left-hand turns to make sure I am actually hanging off". Generally, when I'm not feeling good on the track it's one of those things going bad on me.

Saturday morning's practice was FANTASTIC. CRA changed the practice so that there are 3 practice groups rather than 2. That meant we were practicing without those annoying 600's out there with us. It was the best practice time I have ever had up there. We were actually able to ride the lines we wanted without worrying about bikes with largely different speeds hitting us.

Saturday's Ultralight Supersport race had Dr Dave, Mike Conlin, Rob Duerscherl(sp?), and me fighting for the first 4 spots. Each of us had the lead at some point in the 5 lap race, and passes were happening with each turn. HUGE FUN!!! Going into turn 10 on the last lap, Mike was in 1st, Rob was in 2nd, I was in 3rd, and Dr Dave was in 4th (off track excursion hurt his position). I tried to stuff on the inside of Rob and late brake to pass him on turn 10. It worked, and I was able to get on the gas early, but Rob still managed to catch me and beat me by 0.034 seconds by the time we passed the timing beam. Next time I think I'll mount the DBCOM further forward on my bike.

The Ultralight Trophy Dash was a duplicate of the supersport sprint except it went on for 14 laps instead of 5. My previous best lap time was a 2:05, but during that race the same 4 of us were doing consistent 2:05's with several 2:04's in there. Can't believe none of us crashed! Unfortunately, Mike, Dave and Rob slowly were pulling away from me with each lap. Mike went off the track on turn 3 with only a couple laps left, so I was in third. With Dr Dave and Rob's big lead and lap traffic coming up, I never was able to reel them in. Another 3rd place finish.

Sunday can be summed up in one word. RAIN. By 2:00 we decided it would not let up and just packed up and went home. I didn't run any of the 3 races that I was scheduled to - not worth crashing in my book.

Nonetheless, Saturday will go down as the most fun racing thus far for me. The practices were terrific, the races were fun and fast. Can't wait for next time. Especially since I'm going to finally move past the stock motor and exhaust in search of another 4-5 hp to keep with those other 3 guys on the straights. Tune in next time to see if it really makes any difference...

What about the "Vegetables on Pizza" thing?  Let's just say that Tate was "awarded" a Butterfinger Sunday morning for his take on having stuff other than Pepperoni & Cheese on a pizza.  Very entertaining and thought provoking.  Ask Tate for details!  

BTW:  Dave drove through a downed tree on the way to the hotel.  A kindly motorist tried to warn Dave about a fallen tree in the road by flashing his high beams at Dave.  Dave,  not liking to have anyone tell him how to drive, cursed at the motorist as he passed by him.  Dave then found out out what the motorist was trying to warm him about as he drove through the upper branches of the tree.  The truck suffered a small scratch while Dave & Tate got a good adventure story to tell.  :-)

There's a first time for everything... (June 25th - 27th)

This is my first contribution to the evil pumpkin site, although Dave Johnson (aka Dr. Dave, aka Super Dave) and I have been hanging around the pumpkin patch for about a year now trying to pick up tidbits of wisdom on this whole racing thing. I missed the first race weekend, so I was really fired up for the second weekend. I was going to try out the advise of Reg Pridmore who, the week before at the CLASS cycle school, had talked so matter-of-factly about trail braking well into the start of a turn. It made me realize that I had been braking quite early going into turns (that explains why Dr Dave passed me each time coming up to turn 3). After a week's worth of experimenting with that on my VFR during the daily commute at the cloverleafs of hwy 36 and Snelling, I was ready to give it a try on the track.

Practice sessions Friday and Saturday morning were not very helpful for "practicing" this since there were always a herd of bikes with terribly different speeds at every turn (practice sessions definitely need to be broken up better between different types of bikes). But I was ready for the Ultralight Supersport Sprint race to let 'er rip. Unfortunately, the clouds that were threatening rain all day let loose around lap 2 of the race. I was right on the Dr's rear tire when someone else stuffed inside of me on turn 10. Too bad for him, he low sided right next to me. Being the humanitarian that I am, I chose to avoid running over him and his bike and ran off the track. By the time I got back on track I was left in the dust by the leading group. 6th place finish for that race. Damned rain - still no REAL good experimenting with this late braking thing.

The track had dried up pretty well by the time I was at the starting line for the Trophy Dash. I was feeling pretty good during the race until, you guessed it, the rain started again. I did enjoy the front row seat to another low side in turn 4 by the guy directly front on me, but still there's no getting around the fact that I don't like racing in the rain. I was just grumbling to myself about that, when the race was red flagged. Apparently there was a getting to be quite a pile of crashed bikes on turn 10, so they ended the race early. Somehow, I was 1st in my class when it ended. WooooHoooo! Still, I had not really had a chance to really feel like I had good track time.

Sunday's Ultralight Superbike race found cooler but dry weather. After a typically poor start for me (hate it when I miss 2nd gear!) I was towards the back of the pack heading into turn 1. It took me a couple of laps to get past the slower bikes, and then just focused on passing the bikes I came up on one at a time. The late trail braking thing works GREAT!!! No one ever passed me by out late braking me, and I got passed several people by doing it to them. Thanks Reg! I was reeling in the last guy in front of me, when I noticed the white flag. Only one lap left to get past this guy. I didn't catch up to him until turn 7, and couldn't pass him in 7 or 8. Coming up to turn 9 I decided to only go down 1 gear rather than 2 and try to take turn 9 very fast so I could have high exit speed and pass him down the following straight. Don't try this at home, boys and girls. I was able to get the bike back on the track from the grass with both tires still underneath me, but just barely. OK, that was scarey...nothing wrong accepting 2nd place.

Finally, the Ultalight GP race. I felt decent through that race, but I also had it in the back of my head that it sure would be nice to go home from the weekend with me and my bike in tact. So I wasn't overly aggressive that race. Had loads of fun, no near crashes and finished 4th. Almost had 3rd, but it took a serious late brake in turn 10 to get in front of the guy, and then I was going to hard into the turn to get on the gas until very late. He just passed me right back exiting the turn. Oh well, I had to try it.

Overall, a very good weekend for me, specially when compared with Dr Dave's mechanical woes, Brian's crash, etc. I still haven't seen lap times for all the races, but I did see my best lap during the Ultralight Superbike was a 2:05.6 which is a personal best. It was over 3 seconds faster than the best lap by the guy who won the race, which just goes to prove how poor my starts are. My GS500 is still bone stock except for upgraded fork springs, so I was frustrated by people leaving me on the straight after I'd worked so hard through the infield to make up time on them. I'm going to have to get more power out of that bike if I'm going to have any luck fending of Dr Dave and the bike that will be born from mating his two GS500's.

Sunday evening muster for the pumpkin GS500's showed Dave with 2 broken bikes (and a beat up checkbook), Brain with a scraped up and oil leaking bike, Mark feeling exhausted after only his second race weekend ever, to the point of not running the last ultralight race, and me feeling good about my first race weekend of the year (yeah...that probably means my turn is coming for a very troubled weekend).
