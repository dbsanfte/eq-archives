New Pumpkins Invade the Track! Run away!!

May 5th:

The day started sunny and hot and ended sunny and hot.  Perfect weather for riding a bike around the track at high speeds.  Today was the CRA's New Rider's training day and I had volunteered to be an instructor. We had around 54 or so new riders to show around the track and hope that they stay on two wheels.  I started the day by going out for few laps to dust off my track knowledge before I had to start leading the new riders around.  It sure felt good to be out on the track again!

At 12pm, we piled all the new riders into some pickup trucks to show them around the track.  This being their first time to see the track, there were a few questions, we went back over some of the track procedures for entering and exiting the track as well as lines and reference points.   I'm hoping that the number of times we told them to not drop down into turn one when exiting the pits took for everyone.  After the pickup truck ride it was time for everyone to mount up and let the instructors lead them around. It was kinda fun to ride around super slow and still have to slow down every now and then to let them all catch up to you. Riding thru turn one with one hand while the new riders are all tucked behind the bubble.  Makes you feel fast, until you go out for your fast expert practice and get schooled again. :)  We had very few crashes and the new batch of pumpkins seemed to be fairly smart.  I think the warm weather helped to reduce the crash count more than anything else.  Having the tires warm up fast is a good thing for a new rider.

May 6th:

Again, Saturday was very warm and sunny too.  Perfect tire weather! Trying to avoid the big grids, I registered for the 750 SuperSport Sprint and the Middleweight GP 17 Lap Trophy Dash.   I sprint wasn't too memorable. I got my traditional bad start, was almost last going into turn 1 the first time, but I managed to pass a few experts, get passed by the winning novice 750 rider, and not finish last.  I was a little disappointed, but I did turn a few 1:53's.  Not great, but a few seconds faster then my average lap time from last year, but also not the 1:50 that I managed to get a few of last year too. 

The trophy dash was much better!  There were only 5 bikes in my class, and I somehow managed to get a decent start!  I was right in the middle of the pack going thru turn one this time.  I took it easy and maintained my position, then started working on the bikes in front of me after the second lap.  It was a great time!  I managed to pass one or two bikes every lap.

I rediscovered the brakes during the trophy dash.  I usually made up 2-3 bike lengths just braking for turn 3 on the bikes in front of me.  It was so nice to see the distance shorten so fast, but not feel like I was out of control or late braking.  I have to give the Performance Friction pads and PFM cast iron rotors two thumbs up!  I just can't thank Fred from www.yoyodyneti.com enough for suggesting these pads when I got tired of the EBC's dragging on my rotors. 

On the 7th lap, I was closing in on Brad Frey on a stock GSXR750.  Coming down the front straight, I noticed Brad's bike let out a good puff of smoke just before turn one, so I quickly got off his line.  He didn't smoke in thru turn 1, so I hoped for both our sakes that it was a freak occurrence.  I was wrong.  :(  Just after he pitched it into turn two, the bike blew something big time.  130MPH while leaned over is NOT the place to blow a motor!  I had backed off a few more bike lengths so when the "Days of Thunder" super smoke cloud started I was able to avoid his oil slick and stay out of the smoke.  It was still rather distracting to see the oil on the track, tons of smoke and Brad sliding off the track. 

The CRA Red Flagged the race and after coming back into the pits and thinking about it some, I decided to skip the restart.  I had too much fun in the first part of the race passing everyone and I didn't want to deal with the oil problem in turn 2.  I figured I had worked my way up to at least 3rd place before the red flag...so I patted myself on the back and ended the day happy.  I also had managed to run a 1:52.2 during the race.  One more second taken off my lap time.  :)

May 7th:

It was a little colder on Sunday.  Highs in the upper 70's and mostly cloudy.  Still good racing weather!  I felt pretty good in practice and during one session I managed to get passed by two bright red F4's. "Wait a second!  I passed them during the trophy dash!  Can't let them think they can beat me!"  :)  I followed them around for the rest of the lap then going down the front straight I did a nice draft pass on both of them...was sooo nice seeing them go backwards just before turn 1!  I kept them 5-6 bike lengths behind me for the remaining lap or two before practice ended.  Fun times!

Trying to avoid the big grids again, I registered for the two Heavyweight ( Superbike and GP ) sprint races.  I'm thinking I shouldn't race that class anymore.  It was pretty depressing to not be able to gain on anyone.  I did manage to pass one or two bikes, so I didn't finish last, but it sure was a downer.  Only good thing was the fact that I had managed to turn a 1:51.3 in the first race.  I guess I'm still getting faster, so all is not lost.  The Heavyweight GP race was a repeat of the 1st race, but I have no idea what my lap times looked like. All in all...it was a successful weekend.  None of the Evil Pumpkins crashed and even more important...we all had fun!

I'd also like to thank Steve Guynes for all the work he did to my bike over the winter and JR from headhunter-racing.com for stopping by and supporting the team.

Darn it…I’m faster then you…stay behind me! ( June 23-25 )

Friday morning was nice and sunny with high’s in the mid 70’s. Perfect riding weather! I had a lot of things to try this weekend. I had just installed my "new" Lindeman rebuilt stock shock and I wanted to work on my riding technique. The usual easy to say, but hard to do thing called "relaxing". How can someone really relax while doing 160MPH through a corner is rather difficult, but that’s what everyone seems to tell me, "Just relax".

All the fast people I’ve talked to, all the books I’ve read and the one real school I went to all say that the key to going fast is learning how to relax. However; but they never come out and tell you what to do to "relax". I finally figured it out though, mostly. It took a lot of seat time, reading "Twist of the Wrist I & II" and finally Jason Pridmore’s STAR school to remind me of a few things. Even then, the final "secret" of relaxing didn’t just jump out at me. You would think that the books or Jason’s school would have a simple formula that all could read and understand. Sorry, it doesn’t seem to work that way. L However; I did find the one thing that helps me more then any other thing to "relax" while going faster than your co-workers think is sane.

(Looking further ahead to reduce your perception of Speed) = RELAX ( for me )

That’s the major key to figuring out how to relax on a motorcycle while your knee is dragging the ground at +130MPHin turn 2 at BIR. Heck, the same "trick" will work for all the turns at BIR and every track that has a corner.

That being said, I will have to warn you that, it’s not easy to trick your survival instincts. I’m still working on it and I noticed a good improvement in a troublesome corner today. More on that later.

Friday practice saw me burning up the rest of the rear tire I had put on at the start of the first race weekend ( those Pilots last a LONG time ). Working on that looking ahead and relaxing thing I mentioned earlier. Experimenting with different lines, braking markers and pumping my ego by helping out a few new riders with their riding.

First the tires, we had a little problem this weekend with tires. It seems that some other club decided to have a race during our weekend and they stole our regular tire guy. After turning my rear Pilot into a slick on the right side Friday evening, I went over to the Michelin trailer and found no tires in stock and none expected. Not a good feeling. Okay…how about a rear slick? They had one, but can only sell in sets for a total of $400. Double what I had budgeted for tires this weekend. As a result of no new tires, a good thing that happened ( budget wise ) an almost new race take off Pilot was found in the trailer. So, instead of spending the $200, then $400 that I was looking at, I ended up spending only $75 for a rear. I’ve been hearing some good things about the Bridgestone’s and new Pirelli’s. I guess if the same scenario happens next race weekend might see me trying those new Bridgestone’s. Oh well.

Saturday was a repeat ( weather wise ) with Friday. Nice and sunny, but not too hot. My first practice session I scrubbed in the "new" rear Pilot. I worked on staying more to the rear of the bike when hanging off in the corners instead of riding the front so hard. I had about half success and didn’t really learn too much. The first sprint race would be my learning experience for the weekend.

I then went out and followed a new rider Cary Tankson (#711) on his bright green ZX-7 to try and help him with his lines and maybe spot some things he could work on improving. I managed to actually notice some problems he was having and was even able to make some suggestions. He managed to drop 2 seconds off his lap times by the end of the weekend and he thanked me for helping him do it. All I did was tell him he was turning too early and not looking far enough ahead on the track. Something that I still seem to need to work on doing better too.

Middleweight SuperSport Expert Race saw me gridded on the 4th row and I got my usually crappy start. It was so bad that even Kurt #4 on a bleeping SV-650 beat me to turn 1! Grrr. I showed him when I made the easy pass down the front straight. Then he had the nerve to pass me again in turn 4. I think those darn SV’s are too over powered and Suzuki needs to limit them like they did the ‘busa. It was amazing to see Kurt weaving thru all the slower riders going thru the corners. That guy has no fear.

I eventually managed to put him behind me using the slightly higher horsepower of the GSX-R600. My big "learning experience" comes thanks to David Wappler (#250) on a bright orange F4. I could draft pass him down the front and I was faster then him in all the corners, but he would always get past me under braking into turn 3. Very irritating to know you’re faster then someone and have that someone always in front of you. He was very good under brakes while I was semi-good under brakes trying for the slow in and fast out of the corner technique. That just didn’t work with him there to show me how much he wanted 15th place. The problem was even more apparent when I checked the results and noticed that my fast lap was a full second faster then his. I had managed to turn a 1:50.1 while his best was a 1:51.something.

What the hell can I do to keep this "slower" rider behind me where he belongs?!

I’m pretty darn confident that my brakes are better then his with the Performance Friction pads and PFM rotors. Hmmm…Brake later and harder? I didn’t really remember what I was using for my brake marker into 3 either. That tells me that I was obviously doing something majorly wrong. You should be able to remember something so important. As a result of not knowing exactly where I was on the track, maybe I was braking too early?

I’m not really a good sprint racer. My starts are definitely a handicap for sprint races. I also hate going through turn 1 and 2 with bikes weaving all over the place looking for a hole at +150MPH. The 17 lap trophy dash was coming up later in the day. Maybe I can use the 17 laps to pay more attention to what I’m doing and re-establish my braking plan for turn 3.

Unfortunately, the 63 bike grid for the Middleweight SuperSport Novice race had other ideas about my race plans. I think there were 2 red flags, maybe 3 in that 6 lap race. The ambulance crews ended up being too busy and all our ambulances had to leave the track to transport injured riders. All racing stopped until at least one ambulance could return. Now for the other bad news, the 17 lap trophy dash that I was looking forward to ended up getting shorting to 10 laps. 10 friggin laps! That’s NOT the race I paid $35 for 17 laps. 10 Laps is slightly long sprint race that costs $25. We ended up with a total of 8 red flags for the day and my race being completely scrubbed until Sunday.

During the break between my "learning experience" and Sunday morning, I ended up asking myself not, "Am I braking too soon?", but the big "secret" question of, "where am I looking?". From the way I remember it, I’m going into turn three, looking for my brake maker, hitting it, then looking for my turn point. That turn point seems to come up awful fast. Too fast. Hmmm…maybe I should trying taking some of that advice I’ve been giving others and looking further ahead.

During practice I come into turn 3, hit my brake marker then look to the start of the apex of turn 3 instead of to my turn point. The end result is that I end up using less brakes and come into the corner faster while still keeping my corner speed up and my exit speed the just as good. I also ended up feeling more relaxed going into turn 3 then I can ever remember feeling. I didn’t really notice a definite improvement until the sprint/trophy dash at 10:30.

After my first practice session, I checked my wallet and I looked up at the dark clouds and decided that registering for any of the sprint races today would be a very risky thing. The Sprint/Trophy Dash would have to be my final answer this weekend.

I got an okay start this time and I found my self only 2/3rd’s of the way back this time. Lot’s of work to do. Good thing I’ve got an extra 3 laps for this sprint race! The first lap had me a little nervous as all the novice 750’s in wiggled around fighting me for position. I found that I could pretty much pass them at will on the entrance to a corner or the exit by getting a better drive out. They just made me nervous due to not being sure what line they would take. I even managed to make a few braking passes going into turn 3. I was rather amazed at how slow the riders in front of me were. One guy I passed under braking waited to brake marker 5, while I hit 6 and started my braking, yet I still flew past him under braking at marker 3! I just kept my focus on where I wanted to end up 3-400 feet from then and it all seemed to be so easy. I caught Dave, my braking "teacher" on the 5th or 6th lap. He was following Chuck (#50) and looked to be giving him some problems. I managed to sniff their draft down the front straight then watched the gap disappear going into turn 2. I found that I was carrying much more speed thru turn 2 and I used that speed to pass them both on the exit of turn 2. I have no idea what happened next, but I got the easy pass, hit my brake point going down the middle of the track ( protecting my line ) was looking to the apex. Things seemed to be going nice and slow, everything on track, but for some reason I missed my turn point and went way wide on the corner. How Chuck and Dave didn’t manage to re-pass me there or between 3 and 4, I have no idea.

I did manage to keep the position and by the time turn 10 came around I had even ended up with a good gap. I was closing in on Dave’s team-mate, Benjamin George (#97) and the guy that Ben was battling with, but ran out of laps. I think if I had a few more laps, I might have been able to pass them too. Oh well. That will have to wait until next weekend I guess. J I also hope they don’t read this and learn anything! hehe

My weather guess proved accurate and the rain came down 5 minutes after my race ended. Talk about luck! I get the only dry race of the day, I beat the slower rider that beat me yesterday and I manage to save money! Win/Win/Win situation. My unofficial lap times had my at a steady 1:50, but I’m hoping that the stop watch had a little error in the eyeball to finger interface and I squeaked out some 1:49’s or something.
