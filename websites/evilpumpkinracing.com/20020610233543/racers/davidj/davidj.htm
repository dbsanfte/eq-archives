Smooth Track, Bumpy Rotors

MidAmerica Motorplex

April 12-14, 2002

I was worried that our first outing at the newly constructed MidAmerica Motorplex would be a replay of last year’s debacle at Heartland Park (see: That which doesn't kill us...still bites) I need not have worried. The track there is flat and smooth as glass-weirdly smooth, in fact. It is made from some exotic new asphalt polymer that is as shiny as Jesse Ventura’s head. Despite that, it seems as sticky as anything I have been on (I haven’t been on Jesse’s head), and it is very easy on tires. I couldn’t even scuff up my knee pucks! The track layout is great, with all sorts of weird fishhooks, tight flip-flop esses, double apex toenail burners, and screaming-in-your-helmet carousels. Take a look at the track map at http://www.midamericamotorplex.com/. The penalty for going off the track is rather high at the moment, because the track is essentially floating on a giant, semi-solidified mud pie. Failure to negotiate a turn results in a sudden stop followed by a SPLORK sound as you auger in. Crashed bikes had to be CARRIED away, which was made even more difficult by the large percentage of the earth’s crust still clinging to them. Downed riders became hideous swampthings, frightening pets and small children as they shambled back to the pits. This is a transitory problem however, that should be solved when the grass grows and the land forgets that it was a cornfield. This place will be glorious once the amenities are finished. What do you need running water for anyway? Oh yeah. The mud.

The actual racing was, as is too often the case, fraught with bizarre parts failures of the sort that only come with racing. The first to go was my GS 500’s head gasket. That ungrateful hunk of metal and goo only lasted 1.3 race weekends. I was very fortunate to have a spare in the depths of Pumpkin Clint’s junk box. After standing around thinking the obligatory “I don’t wanna do THAT”, we yanked the head off and made the swap. Back in business! It ran flawlessly after that. The SV 650 was not so fortunate. After a practice session on the first day, I noticed it had blown a fork seal BIGTIME. Usually a blown seal gets you some detectable munge on the fork tube, but this thing was slobbering like Pavlov’s own bulldog. Another lap and I would have gotten fork slime on the brakes or tires and gone kersplut. I was lucky, I guess,but the SV was done. The big new Suzuki dealership in Council Bluffs didn’t stock fork seals “cuz them things never go bad”. The real winners of this weekend’s mechanical ruinage award were the GS brake rotors. These things went from stone-flat to satellite dish in one 8-lap race. We had three GS 500s running, and all three managed to warp their brake rotors. My rotor had only 1 race weekend on it before this. These are the $175 EBC cast iron “race” rotors. Apparently they are expendable like tires on the GS. No, wait – my tires actually outlasted this rotor. Maybe we can use some friendlier pads or sacrifice some chickens to the lords of thermodynamics. Or maybe we should just use the brakes less. We’d be faster, until we became mudskippers.

Summary:

  1. MidAmerica Motorplex is a wonderful track with lots of potential. Once they get the run off areas converted from cornfield-quicksand to lonesome prairie it will be among the best tracks I've seen.
  2. It is hard to predict what part of a race bike will fail, which is why I bring two. Even so, the perversity of the universe and its accursed laws of physics can strand you. The wily racer brings all the spares he/she can muster and cultivates good relations with (i.e. sucks up to) anyone who might help out with parts later.
  3. There is something up with these EBC brake rotors. I know the GS is hard on rotors because the brake pads are horrid broad flat things that aren't conducive to cooling, and there is only the ONE rotor to slow this careening cow down. Still, the stainless stock rotor lasted me 2.5 YEARS. A solution is needed.
