A little about Eric

  • Age: 30
  • Profession: Space Shuttle Flight Controller
  • Race Org: CMRA #343 - 2nd year novice
  • Bikes: 1990 GS500E/1999 SV650
  • Classes entered:  (will be) Lightweight Sportsman (GS), Lightweight Twins (SV)

I started riding a Yamaha 80 way back when I was 6.  I did that for about 5 years and then was bikeless until 1991 when I bought a FZ600.  Frome there I've owned the following bikes: 1989 Ninja 600R, 1993 CBR900RR, 1997 Bandit 1200, 1997 VFR750, 1998 CBR1100XX, 1999 CBR600F4.  I still have the CBR1100 (the F4 was too slow).  THe 900RR is owned by my best friend Tim and he did a lot of work on it and it puts out around 140 brake hp like my XX does.  I am basically a dragracer turning roadracer.  I have been dragracing since I was 15.  An coincidently, I always manage to pass the first couple of rows on the start from the grid.  I spent all of last year endurance racing on a team that I started, Team Bad Company (#43) which consisted of myself along with 2 other first year novices and one expert after we got the SV. 

We did the first half of the season on my GS500 and it got us to 5th in the lightweight superbike class. The SV got us to 4th in class and we would have gotten to 3rd in class but we missed the last race at Hartland Park, KS.  Oh well, there's next year!  I didn't  sprint race at all but I plan on it this coming season.  I'm easy to find in the pits during the races due to the fact I always wear a black cowboy hat.  I'm not really a Redneck since I don't listen to a whole lot of country music (more heavy metal) and I still have all my teeth.  But I'm from Texas so you can call me a hick.  I do work at NASA here at the Johnson Space Center in Houston.  It's a blast and a very interesting job. 

I'll start my bench racing yarns this year and unfortunately it's all about a practice I had at Henderson (Oak Hill).  Tim was the only other team member that came with me.  We towed the trailer from the Clear Lake area in Houston up to the track and set up around 9am.  We didn't know it at the time but Steve Guynes and Derrick pitted right next to us.  Sorry guys, didn't snap to who you were.  Anyhow I jumped on the GS and Tim on the SV and we started some laps.  I managed to run a 1:47 on the GS and Tim was lapping some 1:39s on the SV.  A word on the GS: it has some engine mods with Race Teched forks and that's about it.  The Metzler rear I had on it was bad enough and I had to really be careful with it.  It was spinning and sliding without me trying too hard.  I run a Michelin TX-15 up front and it does great.  Word from the other Pumpkin racers is that the Bridgeston BT-96's are the way to go on this bike.  Thanks guys, I got a set on the way!  later on I hopped on the SV and man does that bike run.  I managed some 1:37s on it.  After that we let another racer try the bike out - we invited him to run the bike since we are looking for some faster riders for this upcoming endurance season.  He ran some 1:36s.  I got back on and matched a few 1:36s and Tim ran some 1:37s.  Tim is usually only a second or two behind me on all the tracks.  So with that, we felt pretty good since this other racer will be an expert next year.  I did some more laps on the GS but 1:47s were all I was brave enough to push it to.  We are hoping to head back out in December for more testing (an advantage to living where it rarely snows) and I will have the Bridgestones on the GS.  We also will be dialing in the SV's suspension.  We recently added a Fox shock to it and now the front end needs readusting.  Until next time...

Oak Hill practice again 12/11.

This is all about tires.  I ordered a set of BT-96s from JB per the feedback of the other GS riding EPR guys.  Man.  Thanks for the tip.  They shaved 6 seconds off my lap times at this track!  A bit about the tires of others who NEED to be running them.  I got a set that consisted of a 110 up front and a 140 out back.  Previously I was running a 110 Michelin TX-15 up front and a Metzler MEZ1 140 in the rear.  I'd have to say the front is as good or better than the Michelin and the rear is worlds better than the Metzler.  In fact there is no comparison.  I could feel the chassis squirming from the amount of traction the tires were giving me. 

Another thing that was amazing is that the track itself was a lot colder and the humidity was very high. It even drizzled for a while and then we went back out.  Then it poured and just to see how the tires did I put on a raincoat over the leathers and rode through that.  It channels water really well in a straight line, a lot better than the other set up I had.  Even with the bike slipping leaned over it was somewhat predictable and didn't wash out suddenly.  I managed a string of 1:41s on the track when it was dry compared to the 1:47s that were my previous best so I think I know what tires I will be running next year.

It's to the point that the GS grinds the pegs really bad despite hanging off to try and reduce the lean angle.  Now I gotta dial in the Race Tech stuff in the front and ditch the stock shock on the rear (it really sucks).  Maybe some rear sets are in order now too.  One caveat to the tires is that the tread depth isn't that much.  After half a day of riding around on them along with some other guys from my endurance team they are about half cooked.  One more practice day and they will be finished.  But once I quit running them as long as an endurance race I'm sure they should last about 3 or 4 race weekends for sprints.  But it's time for me to tear down the engine and see if everything's OK down there.  If there is anything that needs repair I'll be sure to send an update.

GS Update 2/7/00

I figured I'd share a little insight on the mods I have done to my GS since I seem to have a pretty heavily modified one.Prior to rebuilding, the engine had 26 brake hp.  All hp was measured on a Factory Eddy current dyno.  So the numbers here will be less than the standard Dynojet dyno.  I'm told if you add 15% to the eddy current numbers you get pretty close to the Dynojet numbers.  Anyway, we did a compression test on the bike and it had 45psi in one cylinder and 90 in the other.  Not real good.  This bike made it through half the endurance season for my team last year, including a 8 hour race!  The bike had a Yosh race exhaust, Factorypro config 10 jet kit and a K&N stock box replacement air filter. The bike also has Race Tech vlvs in the forks and Progressive springs (leftover from another project).  Tires were a Michelin TX-15 up front and a Metzler MEZ1 racing tire in the rear.  No rearsets and stock shock.

For mods I decided to fix everything that would lead to loss of compression. In went a set of 0.5mm oversize pistons from Suzuki.  The pistons were fly cut for bigger intake vlvs and for a bigger cam at a later date.  The head was treated to a lot of milling to boost compression and while it was apart I decided to get a serdi radius valve job and put in 1mm oversize intake vlvs.  I had a set of GSXR cam sprockets laying around and they were the same as the GS sprockets so I put them in and degreed the stock cams.  Also, a set of Nology spark plug wires went on.  Let me say this, the wires were worth 3 brake hp on this bike!  Nice gain for $90.  After all of that the bike went to 38 brake hp.  The thing about the bike is that it ran to 6000rpm on the dyno and then flatlined at about 36hp.  At about 9000 rpm it would jump to 38 hp.  We think the carbs were now a restriction.  It didn't matter if we had an air box or not so leaning it out and removing that high rpm restriction didn't help.  Due to $$, I had to race only endurance and we managed to pick up a sponsor and we got a SV650 - what a bike!  So no race stories with the GS for sprints.  No suspension changes.  Seemed to us that the bike would run very well in the corners and just get beat in the straights - mainly (the GS).

Now, I have just added some other stuff to the bike for the upcoming season. The bad thing is I built it for the CMRA sportsman class which only allows 500cc.  So the pistons I chose kept it under that.  Now CMRA is PACE affiliated and the Lightweight Sportsman class allows 700cc for the GS.  Had I known this was coming..... New mods for the bike are a set of 35mm CR flatslides and a reground intake cam from Megacycle.  One snag here was when we put the vlv springs in for the cam (I decided to replace all 4 springs for higher revs), they didn't fit the stock retainers and spring seats in the head.  Since I am running KZ1000 intake vlvs we simply put in a set of KZ1000 seats and retainers. However, since they were laying around in the shop, I decided to run a set of aluminum retainers at the top of the springs.  They fit the springs they sent perfectly.  You already read about the BT-96 tires I will be running this season.  The only other changes are that I have drilled over 220 holes in the frame (rear subframe), stock rear sets, and cut off a bunch of extra mounts and the passenger footpeg brackets.  I left one tube on the right side to mount the pipe.  Believe it or not I kept weighing the metal shavings and all the other stuff I cut off and it came to 14 pounds off the bike.  I didn't go into the front of the bike or the frame in front of the swing arm.  Also, the unessential wiring was removed (lights and horn).  I need to get a scanner so you can see this.  It was pretty time consuming overall but I did it a little here and there over the off season. 

I am also replacing the stock brake rotor with an EBC unit.  I haven't ridden it yet to know if that helps any.  Seems that getting a stupid head gasket in is becoming a nightmare.  So no dyno numbers yet.  I had to wind up buying the SV650 from our sponsor since he bowed-out this season.  So my hopes of adding Woodcraft rearsets and the Works shock are on hold.  Bummer.  I'll be sure to report dyno numbers hopefully later this week.  I'm basically going for bust with it.  Had I known I would be running under different rules and now own a SV650 I probably wouldn't have spent the $$ on the GS like I did.  But since it's done I'll at least have to run it one season.  No sense in losing $$ without flogging it a season!

I plan on changing the gearing too but haven't bought the sprockets yet.

GS Update 2/15/00

I did put the GS together with all the parts and there were a few things that went wrong.  The biggest thing was that the cam has too much lift for the pistons.  When I try to degree it where it belongs, the valves hit the pistons.  Consequently I have had to degree the intake cam (the only one I replaced) to a 117 degree lobe center.  The cam card recommends 105 and my tuner recommends 100.  So I'm way off for the perfromance on that.  Also, before we even started the bike on the dyno we were checking the needle position on the CR flatslides I have on it and John (tuner) said that the carbs would help but felt that they came with the wrong slides for the best power. 

Sure enough once we got going there was a flat spot in power that never went away.  Prior to adding the cams and carbs the bike put out 33 brake hp.  I think I reported 38 or something like that but we looked at the old records and that's what it did last time.  It peaked at 9100rpm.  Now the bike puts out 43 brake hp at 10,000rpm.  The flat spot actually moved down to below 7000rpm now where there was one at 8000rpm with the stock carbs.  So even with the cams mis-degreed the bike still made a 10hp improvement at the rear wheel or roughly a 30%hp gain.  I'm happy.

We went to the track on Feb.12 and the GS ran very well.  I have some friends that are going to endurance a Honda Hawk and it has a pipe on it.  I had no problems running it down on the straights.  The bike definitely revved quicker and keeping it out of the dead spot was a lot easier.  I think I only hit it a couple of times all day.  For some reason the rear didn't feel right and eventually we figured out why.  I was on the SV650 and Doug (friend) was on the GS.  I was showing him some lines on the track.  We made turn 2, a sharp left-hander, and I suddenly saw the rear wheel on the GS shudder side to side badly.  Doug stopped the bike and could barely push the bike in neutral.  We looked at the wheel and noticed the spacer was screwed up.  We loaded the bike up into the trailer (on the track) and it was done for the day.  Once we got it home there were only 3 balls remaining from the bearing.  Last time I went out to practice the other bearing went
out.  I guess I should've replaced them both, huh?  Lesson learned. 

I didn't get any lap times for the bike but it did feel a good deal more powerful.  I also put on a EBC brake rotor with new pads.  It took a while for the pads to bed in.  It feels like the rotor doesn't stop as well as a stock one.  Maybe after more track time it will grip better but as of this moment I don't li,ke the 'upgrade'.  Gonna bleed the brakes and see if that helps.  But other than that I'm ready to race next weekend after I replace the bearing. 

1st Race of the Season: 02/18 - 02/20/2000

This is for those of you that didn't make the races this past weekend.  It's more of a personal account for me than the usual 'team' thing since we are sprinting now. The only race Saturday was the GT Lights race which is a 30 minute sprint in which experts and amateurs are scored separately.  Of note there were a bunch of 125GP bikes there and I had no idea that those things only weigh a tad over 150 pounds. 

Yeah, um....  Anyhow when the flag dropped, Tim and myself along with a guy on a hawk rode off on the holeshot and I was in third place for a lap.  Then the GP bikes started huffing along and the field spread out.  I eventually finished 5th in the Amateur ranks and I think 10th overall out of 19 entries.  Tim won the amateur end on the last lap, wearing out his opponent and using every bit of power the SV could give him.  Man those GPs handle the corners fast!  I knew I wouldn't win this one but I just wanted to see how the GS would do against hawks, Svs, and other bigger bikes.  A technical note here is that I had to remove the carbs right after the last practice session and clean them - twice - to fix a stuck needle that was overflowing gas onto the engine.  It took all of us about an hour and a half to figure out what to do after the first attempt failed.  It also fouled the plugs so I had to clean those the best I could for the race. The culprit was traced to the gas tank and the amount of rust in it.  There were little particles like fine sand all over the gas lines and the filter didn't really stop them.  Looks like I will have to Kreem the tank before Texas World Speedway in two weeks.

Sunday was a pretty busy day.  I got to start off with Super Twins.  This class is an unlimited twins class and I had to run against all kinds of big bore twins.  I entered just to see how I could do.  I ran this one on the SV650.  Well I got the holeshot again on the novices and never looked back. I was in third (again) after the flag dropped.  A couple of experts, one on aTL1000S and one VTR, got past me within the first couple of laps.  On about lap 3 an amateur on a 996 showed me the front wheel as we entered turn 6 and I just kept into it until the last second to make the turn.  After that I started riding the SV for all it was worth, knowing that the 996 would have a distinct advantage in the straights.  No one got times on me for this race but I'm sure I was running mid 1:3s.  Next thing I know I crossed the line with a checkered flag and won amateur division, 5th overall.  They ran two other classes on this race, Middleweight Sportsman and another one that escapes my memory so there was a fair amount of traffic.

Next was Lightweight Superbike.  Again, I was on the SV650.  I just watched Tim lose to a provisional amatuer (yellow shirt) on a SV650 and that guy got 2nd overall.  Something tells me he's raced before.   He was there about two rows in front of me on the grid.  First the experts took off and on the second wave launch I managed to get behind him as we entered turn one.  He launched really good.  Better than any provisional rider I have ever seen, that's for sure.  I followed him closely, watching his lines as the tires heated up.  We both were sliding here and there trying to run fast but the tires were cold.  We caught the experts on the first lap and started making our way through traffic.  As we did so, the guy made passes with almost no room to spare.  Again, very uncharacteristic for a provisional...(If it sounds like I'm whining/bitching - I AM!)  Eventually I couldn't keep up with him as the traffic was no so kind to me and I didn't want to quite take the risk of crashing.  On lap 7 I was coming downhill to turn 5, a right-hander when I downshifted and the bike found a false neutral.  I tried to shift again but it was too late.  I put the brakes on hard and let off as I left the track into the grass.  I kept it up and turned back towards the track, watching about 3 bikes repass me.  Got back on the track and finished the race.  I managed second amateur and 7th overall due to the off track excursion.  I'll take it.  Dunno who the new guy is but he's either raced before (my belief) or he's possibly another phenom like the Haydens or something.   imanaged some 1:35s in this race.  My previous best in practice was 1:37s

Next race was Lightweight Sportsman.  Last year the class was scored separately between novice (amateur) and expert.  This year they are scored overall.  I guess I forgot to read that part.  This was a pretty fun race as I got to battle with a SRX single and some twin with EX500 fairings (I thought it was a EX500 but none entered the race that was an amateur - so I'm not sure what it was).  We battled for the 4th position after 4 laps and I finally got both of them on the same lap.  I wasn't sure what place I was in as I was in the rear of the grid and didn't know who was really in my class as they also ran Heavyweight Sportsman with us.  After I passed the two bikes, I just kept going until the checker and it was pretty uneventful as I didn't catch anyone and didn't have anyone trying to catch me.  I got 4th in this one.  The 3 bikes in front of me were all experts so I was a little upset with the fact that we are all scored together.  I lost to a Yamaha (who's size wasn't listed), a Laverda 668 (which in my opinion has no business in this class with 70hp stock - email to the CCS was sent prior to the race weekend to try and get this bike added to the list of non-allowable bikes), and a MuZ single.  All of those bikes were maxed out and here I was on the ol' GS with a stock shock...boo hoo.  I was actually happy with the finish on this one since the bike did better than I thought it would.  I ran some 1:42s on this one.  The tires were starting to slip a little.

Last race of the day for me was Classic.  The GS was built in 1989 despite being a 1990 model and the bikes have to be 10 years old so I made it. Steve (endurance rider) talked me into this one.  Again, I was unaware that the amateurs were combined with the experts and I was hosed.  Of note I did manage to beat two older GSXR750s out there.  Man they were fast on the straights but in the corners the riders would just get on the brakes too soon and left wide openings so me and the GS took advantage.  This was another good race despite no podium since they didn't separate any size for the bikes this year either.  Heaviweights and experts, oh my!  One more 4th place finish here.  I was shocked.  There was a lot of traffic in this race and I tired not to get into the way of the GP Singles that were running with us.  By this race the Bridgestones were quickly dying so I did my best not to try and slide the bike around too much.  In turn 2 which is a sharp lefthander right after a long straight, the bike would sometimes slip so bad I swear it was crashing.  But it would just dig the stock pegs into the ground and the back end would dance around under full throttle.  Still managed to run 1:42s on this race but it was kinda scary.

Well that was all for me this week.  I think the best 'victory' had to be the Super Twins win to oust the likes of a 996 and other big twins.  The SV650 is one hell of a bike and the Michelin Race Pilots we run on it stick like glue.  It is a blast to ride and is a very serious machine on tight tracks like Oak Hill.  things will be different on the 2.9 mile curcuit at TWS.  The GS needs more stuff to run well.  I could barely keep up with the 125s in a straight line, which bothers me, and would just get killed in the corners.  The EBC rotor I stuck on it finally started grabbing better as the day wore on.  But the SV with two rotors was a lot better.  The fact that I had to be scored with experts for the podium finishes is really weighing pretty heavy on me at the moment on whether or not I will try to sell the bike since there is no way I can beat them on this bike (the GS).  Still, I was the first amateur in the LW sportsman race even though it didn't matter. But any advantage the bike would have had will disappear at Texas World since it favors hp in a major way.  I'm thinking I will stay with it since I will make all the races this year so if anyone misses one it would hurt their points considerably.  I dunno.  I hate quitting.  I will stop running the GT Lights as it's an expensive race and I won't do well in that one by the end of the year due to the bikes I have to race against.  In case you are wondering I share ownership with Tim on the SV and we split the races up so that's why I can't run it in all the classes I could.  Anyway, it's 2 weeks until TX World so I need to get some tires on the way.  Until then...

A busy weekend to say the least

One 6 hour endurance and 4 sprints.

This was all fun since Friday night I managed to cut the palm of my left hand a good inch on a bracket I was fabbing for the number plate on the GS. It bled for about an hour but it stopped and didn't re-open. Saturday was endurance day. We ran two teams out of our pit. Team Bad Company was the title we used last year and Dan and Steve are using the name this year since they are planning on racing all season and Tim and I aren't sure yet. So we ran under the name TBC2 - Lug Head. Lug being Tim's nickname on the team and The Head is mine. We were gridded on the 27th row in wave 3. Grid positions were based on entry time. We entered that morning so all the pre-entry teams were way up front. I started and managed to catch the slower guys in turn 3 of the second wave. It was the ususal start from the rear, you gotta spend the first 3 or 4 laps getting through the heavy traffic. Not long after we got started I saw Steve Breen and his SV650 in the hay bales in the right hander coming out of the backfield. Same corner where Ryan was killed last year. I knew he was chasing Faltless, the reigning LW endurance team so I was thinking that was good for us to finish strong (especially since he was already up and walking around - good to know a friend didn't get hurt). I decided to actually move my rump to the edge of the seat this race so that I would stop grinding the pegs.

After a few laps, sure enough, the knee sliders were skipping the tarmac. A first for me. I didn't really know what to make of it. A lot of racers told me that it was a way of gauging how far over the bike was. Well, that's great and all but I wasn't really sure how far over the bike was. The first few laps of that had me treating it like a peg scraping - I'd lean the bike up a bit. After a while it sank in that I didn't have to do that since the bike itself was OK. Yeah, yeah - big deal, right? For those of us not used to it, it's a bit confusing the first time what to do with it. After a while I could feel it in my legs from the moving around. I came in when the fuel light came on the instrument cluster which was 29 laps. We are still running Sharkskinz fairings on the SV and it does a pretty good job but at the end of the straight I could feel the wind pressing against my helmet. It needs a taller windscreen. The speedo was reading an indicated 158mph and it was cutting out a little as the rev limiter was trying to work in 6th gear as I came off the banking. We're also running a shift light and it would glow (at 9400rpm) shortly after I'd pass the start/finish line about halfway down the straight in 6th gear. We're still running the stock chain and gearing. Tim didn't have this problem but he also weighs about 35 pounds more than me. Also, we couldn't repair the dented fuel tank from the last time despite several attempts to pull it out.

This was costly as the bike needed to come in for fuel after 50 minutes as opposed to about an hour and 10 minutes without the ding at TWS from last year. We went lap for lap with another team and the dent was costing us due to more frequent pit stops. Tim and I were both averaging 2:02s and on a couple of laps we hit some high 1:59s. At the end of the race Tim was about 8 seconds behind the other team when they came in. I could see them and figured they'd refuel since there was about 30 minutes. I had my stopwatch ready and as the guy got off, the other rider got on. Crap. I knew when Tim came in we'd have to refuel. Sure enough, he signaled that the bike was dry and he came in. We just splashed about a gallon and a half in it and I took off. I ran an average of 2:01s trying to make up the ground but it wasn't enough and we got 3rd for the class. 19th out of 62 teams overall. Needless to say a new tank is on the way prior to the next endurance at Cresson. My hand didn't bother me a whole lot like I thought it would. I knew the cut was there but I padded it with big guaze squares and taped it to my hand.

Sprints: fun and fire

As we started getting the bikes ready for the day, I looked over and saw none other than Steve Guynes and the other EPR gang. Cool, pitted right next to them. We talked for a bit and then the fun began. The GS has been a real pain in the butt to start. My engine tuner thinks part of the problem is due to my intake cam being out of time from piston clearance problems. So as the battery was slowly draining down and choking it no help I reached for the starting fluid and sprayed a little in there. A little burble of life. More spray. Another burble. More spray and POW! Fire in the hole! Some of the excess spray had dripped down and a small backfire lit the poor GS up like and old Hibachi grille. Tim ran for an extinguisher in the trailer and Derek Delpero was yanking his out of a box. I was vainly trying to snuff it out with a rag and Tim let it rip. After the smoke literally cleared, I spent the next 10 minutes wiping the extinguisher stuff off everything including the velocity stacks...Rather than keep fighting it the hard way we pulled up the SV (the GS was undamaged - I think it's truly indestructable) and we jumped off the GS and it started right up. I barely made it out for the first practice. The BT-96s once again amazed me with the traction and they were scrubbed in after a single lap. The Michelin Pilots usually take about 3 laps to scrub in when new on the SV.

Racetime

The flag dropped and I got a great start just to have several experts come blowing by me on turn 3 with an amateur on a 996 Duc. Oh well. On the second lap a guy named Reagan (really cool guy) on his 916 (amateur) passed me on the back straight. Crap. 3rd place now. I was trying to pass an expert on a VTR but I just didn't have the power to get enough of the bike in to out brake him into some turns. So the VTR and the 916 would just pull away on the front straight. I'd catch them in the turns after the back straight and I managed to pass the 916 in the chicane just before coming onto the front straight. Only problem is every lap he'd beat me to the start/finish line. Not good. This went on and I saw the white flag waving - last lap. I figured it was now or never. I kept the throttle on way into turn one and swung really wide. I was all over the 916 and I think he bobbled a little and I managed to squeak past him. With that I caught the expert before the back straight and actually got a little bit of a draft on him. We came to the corners and I sat back just a hair and then let it rip through the 'kink' just past the horseshoe and managed to carry enough speed to finally out brake the guy in the chicane before coming onto the straight. The VTR came blowing by. The 916 didn't. 2nd place. Man, I will defintitely take it. I looked back and the 916 was a good 8 lengths behind me. I have to say this was the best, most exciting race I have ever had. Next up was LW sportsman on the GS. We lined up and I noticed one of the guys from last race wasn't gridded. I was thinking 3rd place for sure unless a new bike showed up this weekend. We ran with the LW Supersport bikes and they went first. I had a lousy start but no one passed me. I watched the MZ and Yamaha rocket away from me - both bikes have about twice the hp the GS does. And expert riders on them doesn't hurt their chances either. I ran the race and passed a couple of amateur LWSS bikes. No drama here. I came in and figured 3rd place. The results got posted and I got 4th. I wondered how that could be. The guy who was gridded in front of me that was an apparent no-show also ran the LWSS race and used the grid position up there. A big no-no. Just as I was figuring this out the guy (Mike Soutter) went to the officials and did the honorable thing. You're supposed to grid in your worse spot when you run more than one class at a time at the same time. He didn't. But he took his lumps and admitted it. I don't think he was trying to get away with anything. But after that they reposted the results and I got 3rd. Mixed feelings about getting it, though since he still would've beaten me. His laverda makes pretty good power. Anyway the gang got some 2:10s on me on that race.

This is a 11 second improvement from the last time I was there. Tires did most of it but the bike did run a little stronger from the carbs. I need to regear this bike badly. I thought I was coming off the banking at 10,200 rpm in 6th gear but it wound-up being 5th gear. That gets fixed quick. I will try going up 3 teeth on the rear and see where that leads as TWS is the fastest track the GS has to run on. LW Superbike. Mr. Brad Champion returns. And I got whomped in to second place again. I was right behind him for about 4 laps out of the 6 and then I had some trouble negotiating traffic. It sucks watching his prov nov shirt flapping in the wind as he extends a lead. Oh well. Classic was the last race and Steve (from endurance) got even with me. I got 6th in this one as the 750GSXRs were unstoppable. Hardly an even match at TWS.
