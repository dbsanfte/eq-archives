Gabrelle
 
 

"Gabrelle!"

The tiny, young halfling looked up from the row of turnips she was weeding.

"Yes?" she called out.

"Your father wishes to speak to you!" her friend, Sabba, ducked her head out from the small cottage.

Gabrelle sighed and stood up. She dusted off her hands by brushing them on her skirt. Weeding was about number one on her list of "Times I wish I was elsewhere." She picked up her hoe and headed into her father’s cottage.

[i]Things have never been the same since Mama died.[i] She thought to herself.

She unlatched the front door and entered the house. It had never looked like how Mama had kept it. A bright, fire burned in the stove with a large pot of stew hanging over it. The stew never tasted like how Mama made it. She had tried so hard to be like her mother. She could never sew like she did, bake like she did, or even hoe like she did.

"Gabrelle, come here girl."

Gabrelle jumped when she heard her father’s voice calling from the back room. She quickly entered the room and looked at her father.

Gorbin Stormhaven sat in his small wicker chair. Well, it was small to most other races but most halflings needed a light running start to climb into it. He rarely left his chair. He sat there and stared out at the small waterfall running through the Vale. He puffed on his corncob pipe, smoking a mixture of dried turnip greens and jumjum stalks. His hair, well what was left on his head, was a silvery gray striped with black. He turned his head slightly as she entered.

"Come child." He waved his hand towards her and she walked over and sat down next to his chair. He lovingly placed his hand on her light brown hair, as he always did.

"The weeds do not cease their war on the turnips I hear." He chuckled with a puff of smoke.

"No, Papa, but the turnips have sent for reinforcements!" Gabrelle smiled up at her father.

He chuckled louder and tapped his pipe on his chair.

"Wit such as your mama’s." he said with a broad smile.

"I miss her so, Papa." She laid her head against his chair.

"As do I, dear. As do I."

"Tell me of her again, when she was young." She looked up at him.

He stared off at the waterfall. "Aye," he waited a moment, "She was like that waterfall." He pointed his pipe at the streaming water.

"The falls?" she asked.

"Aye, see the waters never cease, my buttercup. They continue year and year." The pipe returned to his mouth.

She stared at the falls and tried to understand the old man.

"But that is not why I called you!" he said, clearing his throat. "Gabrelle, it is time."

"Time to water the jumjum stalks?" she sat up a bit.

"No, no, no." he said shaking his head.

"Time to fix ol’ Shakey’s stuffing again?" she cocked her head to one side.

"No, no!" he rolled his eyes.

"Oh, I know! You need more fire beetles eyes!" she stood up clasping her hands together.

"No! Child! Sit!" he coughed.

She sat down in front of him on the floor.

"You are almost 150 years old daughter."

She was accually 160 but she didn’t want to tell him.

"And you have never been past the wall!" he waved his hands towards the direction of the Thicket.

She had been past the wall once, well, really on top of it throwing rocks at the passing goblin and then hiding before they could see her.

"It is time," Gorbin sat up proudly, "to see the world."

She blinked several times, not really sure she had heard him right.

"S-s-see.."

"Yes! You must go beyond the Thicket. You must run across Karana’s land. You must challenge the orcs of the Commonlands. You must see the treetops of Kelethin from the highest platform. You must spit from the side of the boat in the Ocean of Tears." His eyes lit with excitement remember the days of his youth. "Aye, Gabrelle, you must see these lands the gods have graced us with."

Her jaw was now wide open and her eyes continued to blink.

"But what of you… and the garden…and…"

"Oh, may the weeds vanquish the turnips and sell them as slaves!" With the word "vanquish" a small cloud of smoke puffed into the evening air. "Now, go and fetch me stick."

She got up and tripped over the floor rug and stumbled into the front room. She grabbed her father’s walking stick and brought it back to him.

"Aye," he said grasping the stick almost reverently.

"Do you know why this stick is important to me, girl?"

"It was Mama’s," she replied looking down at it.

"Yes, but it was more than Mama’s. You see these small notches here?" he pointed at the small markings all along the length of the staff.

"Yes, what are they?" she leaned in closer and eyed the notches that she had never really noticed before.

"Each notch represents a giant your mother killed."

Gabrelle gasped and looked up at her father’s twinkling eyes.

"Your mother was not just a gardener, Gabrelle. She kept the art of Karana’s craft as did I." He held the staff out to her. "Here."

She backed away from it as if I had a disease. "No, I cann…"

"Take it, child, I will not ask ye twice!" His eyebrows furrowed down over his eyes.

She took it and gently at first as if her hands could hurt it.

"It is now yours." Gorbin leaned back in his chair and puffed another cloud of smoke. "Now, go! Train your magic as I have taught you. Send a rain of thunderstorms over the Clan Runnyeye!"

She stood up and held the staff with one hand. Slowly she turned the staff and softly touched the bottom of the staff to the floor of her fathers house.

It was time.
