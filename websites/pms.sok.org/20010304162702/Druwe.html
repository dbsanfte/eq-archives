Druwe Firewolf
Wood Elf Druid

I was born and raised several days run from the Wood Elf city of Kelethin.  My father, mother and I lived in our treetop home amongst a large grove of ancient oaks in peace and harmony.  The grove was also home to many animals that sought shelter under the canopy of leaves and branches.  There was a beautiful stream that fed a deep pool of cool water nearby.  Everyday we foraged for our meals and Tunare always provided for us.

Of necessity we were self reliant, my father was skilled in many things including smithing, and my mother was a fair hand with needle and thread.  I spent many a day helping my father with the bellows of his forge, digging for the ores to create metal and carving out the molds he would use to make all manner of items.  He was a patient and skilled teacher and I grew to love the sound of the hammer on the anvil and the smell of the fire as it heated the metal.  I still remember the thrill of making my first cooking pot and the look of pride in my father’s eye as I pulled it from the quencher.  For my mother I would gather silk which she would sew together to make things for our home.  Occasionally I would find the remains of some poor beast and would take its hide for tanning as Tunare taught us to do.  My hand was never as skilled as my mothers but she taught me well and we would spend the evenings piecing together materials into clothing and other things.

As far back as I can remember I was in the woods playing with the animals.  Being an only child and some distance from our nearest neighbor they became my friends and I learned much from them.  The bears taught me strength and patience.  The lions taught me the thrill of the hunt.  From the wolves I learned speed and cooperation.  Treants showed me wisdom and love.

My destiny was set one early evening when I was still quite young.  I was many leagues from my home, having run with a pack of wolves that were old friends of my family, including my dearest friend.  Her name is difficult to give as wolf names do not translate well into common tongue, but the closest would be Sky Runner.  We were just preparing to find a cozy cave to spend the night when suddenly we were set upon by a group of evil beings I later learned were called Orcs.  Their red eyes and guttural shouts filled the night as they tried to kill the members of the pack.  A group of three Orcs chased me into a narrow canyon where I was good and truly trapped.  Thinking that my end was certainly near, I closed my eyes and said a prayer for my family to Tunare.  When I opened my eyes again to face my death I was shocked to find that I was back at my home looking into the startled face of my father.  Tunare had saved me through a miracle.  I quickly told my father what had happened and we left at once for the wolf pack.  Several hours later we arrived to find that the Orcs had been vanquished.  Some few members of the pack had been hurt including Sky Runner.  I bent down to examine her injury and that is when the second miracle occurred.  As I touched Sky Runner’s fur the air was suddenly filled with a bright blue light and I felt a kind of energy leaving me and going into her.  As soon as this subsided she leaped to her paws and to my amazement showed no sign of a wound.  After my initial shock wore off the other injured wolves came to me and I healed all of them.  After I rested my father and I went to the canyon where I had been trapped and we saw the bodies of the three Orcs that had chased me.  Each one looked as though they had been set a fire by a sudden blaze, yet the earth around them remained uncharred.  The next day my father and I returned to our home where he and my mother spent the evening talking quietly about what had happened.

A few weeks later my father and I set out for the tree city of Kelethin.  I was very excited having never before seen the great city or any city for that matter.  Even before we arrived we met other travelers on the way and I was amazed at all of the different types of people.  High Elves from the east, Dwarves from the west, Gnomes from the south and others from places I couldn’t even image.  The city was so alive and different from what I had known that I cannot properly describe how it made me feel.  After exploring some of the sites, my father and I spent the night in a small inn.

The next morning we met with Heartwood Master a Priest of Tunare (much later I learned that the world frequently uses the term Druid).  Father described to him the miracles that had occurred and Heartwood Master began to ask me many questions and had me describe to him the feeling I had when the healings took place.  Eventually he explained that Tunare had granted me a great gift.  I had been given the ability to channel my life’s energy, which he called mana, and use it to perform miracles.  He told my father that with training I could eventually become a very powerful priestess for Tunare.  What’s more, that there was a danger in not being properly trained, as I needed to learn to control my abilities to avoid unintentionally harming others.  After much discussion my father and I agreed that it would be best for me to stay in Kelethin and take instruction from Heartwood Master.

Over the next several years I learned quickly and well how to control Tunare’s gift.  I was taught how to focus my energy and travel to far distant lands in the blink of an eye, how to heal others of all races, how to blend into the scenery as if I wasn’t there and I was also taught how to fight and defend myself.  Heartwood Master explained that this training was necessary, as Tunare required us to protect the land and innocent people from evil.

I have had many adventures and fought in many a battle in order to do as Tunare would have me do and prove worthy of the gifts she has given me.  I return home from time to time to tell my parents about my adventures and the places that I have been.  In many ways my life seems like a dream that one day I will wake up from.  But until that time I will continue to do the good works of Tunare and follow the path she has shown me.
