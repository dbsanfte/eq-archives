Amideh

You are sitting at a table in the Tall Tale Ale drinking a well-deserved kalish. The door quietly creeps open, and a pale figure slips inside. She could be sixteen, could be twenty - it is hard for you to tell because the girl is so slight, almost more elvish than human (though you know from her face that she is, in fact, Human). She gnaws nervously at her lower lip, then sidles quickly along the wall and darts behind the bar into the back room. You might have forgotten about her immediately but for the bard to your left, who has been regaling you all night with tales of heroism (most of which you have heard many times before). "The new barmaid," he says, "And late again. Say, want to hear a little about her?" Having nothing better to do, you agree. He begins to chant in a low cadence:

"The story you're about to hear is infinitely grim
It deals with little Amideh, she's tiny and she's slim."

You groan at this pathetic effort, and he sheepishly admits that he just started writing the song and it might need more work. "Anyway," he goes on, "as I said, her name is Amideh. She has no last name, no family, and no home. However, but a year ago she had a home, and oh, what a home. She lived in splendid comfort in the palace of the Sultan of South Ro. No, no, do not be misled. She is not a princess. Many travel the roads claiming noble or even royal status in order to deceive the gullible. You have met these pretenders yourself, in nearly every inn in the land. But Amideh is not one of them. She was but a slave, as she freely admits. Yet she felt honor, for she was neither a scullery wench nor a dairy maid, but rather personal handmaiden to the Sultan's daughter, the lovely Princess Aleea. Ahh, I see that is a name you recognize.

"Now, the Sultanate was a civilized place, and though a slave, Amideh was taught to read and to write, and had learned of the Gods. She knew that our destinies are woven by hands from above, and that every being born to this world has their rightful place in it. It was not for her to question the reason the Gods had granted the Princess her exalted status, nor to question why she herself had been chosen to serve. To her, it just was as it was, and was as it should be. And Amideh loved the Princess with all of her heart, as she knew she should. She adored her mistress' beauty and her elegance, and marveled at her fine breeding, and envied her intelligence and her playful wit. And she never doubted that the Princess loved her too in her own way, though truth be told, the Princess was mercurial and had a bit of a wicked streak (as is not uncommon for those in her position), and her playfulness sometimes turned darker, though never for long. And so the days turned into weeks, and the weeks to months, and Amideh was content.

"That is, until evil boiled up out of the swamp. You have heard of this sad event? Then you know that the Sultan was caught unawares by the horde of Trolls and Ogres, and his army was overwhelmed, and his realm sacked. I have visited the ruins on the southern edge of the desert. They are inhabited today only by groaning mummies and zombies, and a few standing stones are all that is left of a once beautiful palace. But I digress.

"As the battle turned against the Sultan's small army, Amideh dressed the Princess and herself in long dark cloaks and they attempted to flee into the desert. But they were spotted, and cornered by a squadron of Ogres at the gates of the palace. Escape was impossible. Amideh bravely (if foolishly) sought to defend her mistress as best she could. Of course, an Ogre is hardly intimidated by a slip of a girl armed with a makeshift club. She was brushed aside by the back of one huge hand, slammed against the wall, and everything went black. When she came to, the Princess was gone, undoubtedly to the same horrible fate that befell her father, the Sultan.

"The Ogres stripped Amideh of her cloak, hooked a chain through the ring in her collar, and dragged her from the rubble of the palace. She was packed into a crude wagon with a handful of other survivors of the Sultan's household. The army then began its march back deep into the swamp towards the dark and filthy cities of Grobb and Ogguk. You have never visited these places? You are fortunate indeed. A lucky few of the captives were to be consigned to the slave pits, while the cleaver and the larder awaited the rest. Yes, it is true. Trolls and Ogres do eat people, and do worse things besides. Fortunately for Amideh there is little honor among such creatures.  Deep in the swamp the Ogres and Trolls began to quarrel about the division of their ill-gotten booty,and soon they came to blows. The wagon into which Amideh had been thrown was knocked over in the melee, and she was able to slip through a small gap and escape into the darkness. She wept as she thought of the fate of those left behind, but she knew that there was nothing she could do. For several days she wandered lost in the swamp, miraculously avoiding both Troll and Froglok, until she emerged at the desert's edge. She beheld the still smoking ruins of her home, and collapsed in despair.

You must admit that up until this point, the story has interested you. Sadly, the silly bard suddenly breaks into his unfinished piece, singing:

"She roamed the desert hoping there was something there to eat
What did she find? A scarab bug. She ate it? No indeed.
For lack of nourishment she grew as slender as a thread
By evening time so weak she was she lay there looking dead
While back in Grobb and Ogguk they all found their loot enchanting
Beneath the harvest moon above the wicked Trolls were dancing..."

He is a truly terrible, terrible singer. No wonder he has no audience but you. You cut him off, and beg him to just finish the story. He seems miffed, but then goes on, speaking hurredly.

"Well, there is not much more to tell. Amideh eventually found her way to our fair city. But as you know, Freeport can be a cold and intimidating town, and Amideh was terribly poor and hungry and alone. She tried to find honest work, but she had no trade skills to offer. She tried to beg, but beggars are many these days, and she met with little success. She even tried to steal, but she was not cut out to be a rogue, and she failed miserably. In desperation, she went to the seedy bars that lie alongside the docks of East Freeport, planning to sell her body for a few silver pieces in order to survive.

"This is where she was approached by a cleric of Erollisi Marr, who offered her food and shelter in exchange for menial chores at the Temple. As Amideh scrubbed the shining marble halls day after day, she listened to the lessons of the Goddess, and she found wisdom and comfort there. Unfortunately, the stern Mother Superior seemed long ago to have lost track of the kindness that is the essence of the teachings of the Goddess of Love, and the cruel initiates gossiped about her checkered past and mocked the collar she wore as her sole memento of happier days gone by. More and more, Amideh found herself sneaking out in aimless nighttime wanderings, only to incur the wrath of Mother Superior upon her return to her unfinished chores. That is how she found this tavern, and I don't think she goes back to the Temple much any more.

"They teased and mocked her every day for no special reason
They made her sleep outdoors, yes, even in the rainy season
They never let her sing or dance, they gave her no new clothes
She knew no jokes, she drank no ale, they disapproved of those
A disconsolate sight was she in silks and leather fetter
They gave her only scraps to eat, the Temple dogs ate better
They never even let her go outside to roam, the fact is
Twelve hours scrubbing every day was just her normal practice
I cannot envy Amideh, she never had a chance
To think that they could be so cruel, now everybody dance!"

As the fool launches into an off-key mandolin solo to accompany his pathetic crooning, you lose control. He is abominable. Your tolerance is gone. You stand up slowly and draw out several inches of steel. The idiot finally notices your expression, gulps, then leaves quickly muttering something about a forgotten engagement at the Rusty Dagger. You revel in the sudden peace and quiet.

"Pardon me, but may I get you something to drink?" You look up and two large eyes meet your gaze for just a split second before dropping modestly.

"Brand new here?" you ask.

A smile lights up her face. "Why yes, I began just  last week, if it pleases you."

 It does please you. You order another drink, and for once, you make sure to leave a good tip.
