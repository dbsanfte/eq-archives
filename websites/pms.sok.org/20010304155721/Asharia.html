Asharia Oakenheart
Wood Elf Druid

Sitting at a table in the inn, the young wood elf takes pen to parchment to begin a retelling of her past – where she came from and how she came to be here at this time and place.  A telling of the events that led up to her wishing to join this guild of strong-willed women.

I was born in Kelethin, the magnificent city of the wood elves, towering high in the trees of the Greater Faydark.  From the moment I was old enough to know right from left, I knew what I wanted to be.  I could feel the pull towards nature every time I ventured down from the city into the surrounding woods.

Animals that were normally fearful or aggressive towards my people would neither run from me nor attack me.  I would spend my free days as a child tussling with wolf pups or simply admiring the grandeur of the forest around me.  Touching the magnificent trees that towered far above even the highest platform of the city, I could almost sense their sluggish thoughts.  I could lose myself in their perception of their world; lose myself in the timelessness of centuries of immovable existence.

I can still vividly recall when I first felt the pull to my true calling.  I was alone, wandering through the forest far from the city, lost in thoughts about my purpose here, why I felt this strong attachment to nature, and why I felt so uncomfortable around my own kind.  The elders seemed so distant from the world, so arrogant, almost like our tall, pale cousins who lived in the finely built city on the edge of the forest.  They did not seem to understand how to live with nature fully, for while they lived in the forest and let it grow up around them, the guards still fought off the wolves just as earnestly as they killed the occasional orc that was foolish enough to wander too near the guard posts.  They claimed to revere nature while they sharpened their axes and forged their swords.
Lost in these thoughts, I had wandered deeper into the forest than ever before.  Here, far from the lights and noise of the city, there was a deep sense of peace and, just beyond the range of normal perception, a deep sense of wisdom and vast knowledge.  It seemed to me that I could almost hear whispers in the wind, as if there were some conversation or something that I cannot even begin to describe taking place, somewhere just beyond my ability to grasp.  I could almost hear the whispers; almost understand the words, as if the forest was trying to tell me something.

I knelt on the mossy ground, and dug my fingers deep into the soil, trying with all my might to understand what I was being told.  My mind was racing frantically, my shoulders were tensed, my body rigid.  For hours I sat thus, failing time and again to grasp what seemed almost within my reach.  Finally, in exhaustion, I collapsed on forest floor, tears of frustration seeping from my eyes.  I lay there, weeping silently, completely exhausted, alone, and far from home.  I did not know where I was, nor did I know how I was to return home.  Frightened, hungry, I crawled to the nearest tree and curled up between the exposed roots, and slept.

It was dark; I must have slept for hours, for it was in the deepest hours of the night that something woke me, leaving me with a  sense of urgency.  I still sensed the whispers, but they were stronger now, and seemed to be not many voices, but one voice, a voice that no mortal ears could truly understand.  I knew now why I had thought it too be many voices, for the voice could be hear in the rustling of the branches overhead, in the sighing of the breeze, in the buzzing of the insects, and in the lonely howling of a lone wolf; all distinct in and of themselves, and yet, at the same time, all one, working together in a magnificent harmony of nature.  I relaxed as the whisper began to coalesce into something I could understand.

I knew then what I needed to do, what I was to become.  I was one of those gifted with an affinity towards nature and her needs.  I could sense the Mother’s hand in all that was surrounding me, and I could sense Her hand guiding me forward toward my destiny.

Suddenly, as if a path had been laid before my feet, I knew what to do; I even knew how to get home.  I was no longer lost in this giant forest, I could never be lost here, for the very trees pointed the way for me, the wolves gathered to guild me home.  As I neared the city, my entourage passed through the druid ring, and halted.  They would go no farther, for they did not like the fires, and, though they did not fear death, they did not care to throw their lives away on the careless blades of the guards surrounding the city.

I walked on, my hand trailing across one of the nearest stones and made my way towards the light and sound of my home.  Blinking at the brightness, I made my way up into the city.  I felt as if Someone else were guiding my path, for my feet took me to a part of Kelethin that I had never before visited.  I halted as I reached a massive building with a sign over the door proclaiming it the meeting hall of the Soldiers of Tunare.  This was where I was to train, where I was to learn my purpose and hone my affinity with nature.  I was to become a Druid.  I now understood my pull towards nature, and why I felt uncomfortable in the confines of the city, I was one destined to travel, to protect nature in all its forms.  Squaring my shoulders, I pushed open the door and entered.

“I am here to see the Heartwood Master,” I proclaimed as heads turned towards me.  There was one who stood out from the others, not so much in appearance, as in the wisdom and power that seemed to radiate out from him.
It was he who answered, “Yes, child, we know.  Tunare has guided you to us.  We have watched you since childhood, and She has shown us what you would become.  You are ready to begin your training.”

And thus it began.  Thus was my path set, and my future proclaimed.  I spent many years among them, learning, growing, and training.  I spent my time, as all young wood elves must, fighting the invasion of the Crushbone Orcs that was a continuous threat to our borders.  I used that time to grow stronger, to develop the powers that the masters were teaching me.  Finally, though, I knew it was time to leave this place, to venture out.
I was given the names and locations of various other sects of druids who would be willing to help me.  And I set out to travel the world.  I journeyed through the mighty Butcher Block Mountains, home of our allies, the steadfast dwarves, to the edge of the Ocean of Tears.  I took the boat west, to the city of Freeport, and began my exploration of that area.

It was in the area bordering Freeport, known as the commons, that I first made contact with a member of the Sisters.  I had been battling the minions of the undead near a ruined circle of stones and was grievously wounded.  I was dying.  I had not the strength to call upon the healing powers of Tunare to aid myself.  It was at the moment when I was about to give up hope when the cold Teir’Dal woman came to my aid.

I was frightened of her, of her powers of necromancy, of her heritage, for it was whispered back home that these dark skinned cousins were of the deepest evil, a poison in the land.  It was said that they hated everyone not of their ilk, and everything not of their making or in their control.  I feared that she would slay me, that she would tear my soul from my body, and torture me into oblivion.  Needless to say, I was shocked when instead of delivering my soul to Innoruuk; she gave some of her own life force to me, to mend my wounds.  Her only wish to me was to train and grow stronger.  She left me with only her name and her affiliation with a guild that I had thought only legend.  She was Laezra Belladonna of the Dark Order of Knights.

I began to train harder, working nonstop to grow and strengthen myself.  At the same time, I began to hear rumors of troubles in the land, rumors of a dark order who was stirring up evil, and causing to end of trouble.  I began to hear of a dark necromancer who was attacking those she had been previously allied with.  Each description of her was closer than the last to my matching my memories of the woman who had saved me.  Fear blossomed took seed in my breast and began to grow.  I feared for when she came to collect the debt that I owed her.  I feared what the ultimate price would be.  So when the summons came to join her in the Southern desert, I hesitated.

My fear of being branded a liar and an oath breaker finally overcame my other fears, and I journeyed south to meet her.  When I arrived however, she was not there.  Somehow, I had known that she would not be.  So I traveled back to the Faydark and took up arms again against the legions of orcs on our borders.  I fought harder than ever before.  It was then that I became affiliated with the young bard, Celestral.  It was after fighting along side her that I came to know more of the sisters.  I learned from her who Laezra truly was, and the nature of the war being fought.

It was after a long day of fighting orcs that I came to witness the frightful conclusion to that war, and came to know the power and love of the sisters.  When Celestral was thrown from my side by the raging demon that had been Laezra, one of her sisters immediately rushed to her aid.  When everything settled, and I found Laezra’s still form lying on the hill, it was a sister who relieved me and continued to heal her when my own strength had failed me.  It was then that I found myself drawn to this organization of women whose ties to each other overcame the blind hatred and evil of the dark order, and looked beyond the betrayals to the true ties of love and friendship.
Still overwhelmed by events though, I traveled away from my homeland to the distant and newly discovered land of Kunark to try to gather my thoughts and make some sense of events.  I finally decided what I needed to do, and that was how I found myself that the door of a tavern, the Tall Tale Ale, submitting my application the darkly clad woman who was their leader.

And so here I, Asharia Oakenheart, sit, penning this, wondering why I feel the need to write down the events that brought me here, and waiting to see what is to come of my affiliation with this mysterious order, known as the Pretentious Malevolent Sisters.
