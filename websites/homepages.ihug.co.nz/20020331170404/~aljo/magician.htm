Magician

Magicians are the summoners of the arcane arts, and wield the power to call forth everything from a loaf of bread to a huge Fire Elemental to fight for them. While not as adept at offensive spells as the Wizards, Magicians still wield considerable power in this area as well.

 

Player Guide: Magician  By Katarina and Alexian

 


Overview:
Considered the summoners of the arcane arts. Some regard magicians to be the most mysterious of the pure spellcasters. They have the unique ability to summon food, weapons and elementals to use in battle. These advantages alone makes the Magician one of the most powerful, albeit underestimated classes in Everquest.

Positives/Negatives:
The general knack against Magicians is that they seem to be a "middle of the road" class; certainly not as offensive spells minded as a wizard, and lacking the healing skills of a cleric, and the melee skills of a fighter! So what’s so good about a Magician? It is the ability to rely solely on yourself for a good portion of the time. A Magician has the ability to summon food, water, bandages, and weapons when needed. All these are essential items usually bought and traded in town. Initially, food and water can be quite expensive and annoying to travel back and forth to town to replenish. However Magicians can conjure food in a moment’s notice and are for the most part self-sufficient.

Of course, the biggest ability of a Magician is the ability to summon powerful elementals, which will enable you to stand toe to toe with powerful creatures and gain experience faster than any other race in the game (with the possible exception of Necromancer). Imagine your elemental taking all the damage, while you sit back out of harms way casting at will. This is why the Magician is one of the best (and one of the only classes) that give you the ability to play alone, without grouping.

There are negatives as well. As mentioned before magicians do not have the offensive spellcasting ability as a Wizard. Also being a pure spellcaster Magicians have very few hitpoints, and cannot stay in melee combat very long. Magicians can not wear anything more than cloth armor, and thus their Armor Class tends to be very low. However, you will have spells to offset this. Their melee damage is low but is prevented by the weapon they are limited to, which is the staff. The staff does decent damage early on, but as you advance you will use more and more of your spells and soon see you melee skills fall by the wayside.

Elementals:
Ah, now we have reached the pinnacle of the Magician’s ability! The Elemental. Elementals can be cast after reaching level 4. There are four types of elementals available: earth, air, fire, and water. Each elemental has distinct advantages against certain foes than others. They are considered to be "pets" in the game and are directed as such. Some commands include "/pet attack", "/pet back off", and "/pet guard". They attack in melee style, and some higher level elementals are known to use magic on targets. Elementals have the ability to equip summoned weapons for use in battle (by simple holding the weapon and giving it to the elemental). They tend to be loyal above all else, and will protect you instinctively. They only disappear when they are killed, when you are killed, or when you change zones. They also automatically regenerate hitpoints.

Elementals also tend to be very different every time you cast them. I have had elementals who was really brawny (high in HP) but tended not to hit for high damage, and I have had just the opposite. I even had an elemental that attacked every monster in site. But, it never fails when I find the one I like that’s strong and hits for high damage, I either die, or have to zone and consequently the elemental disappears! The only drawback of using the elemental in battle is that it shares a portion of the experience from each kill. GM’s have said that it doesn’t use this experience to gain HP, or attack advantages, but I tend to believe they get better the longer they have been fighting.

Starting Strategy:
Starting a character as a magic user can be tough, but not impossible. First of all expect to die a lot early, this is normal. You lose very minimal experience when you die early in the game so its really only a minor inconvenience. I suggest buying yourself a staff as early as you can, because the initial weapon you begin with (dagger) will increase your piercing skill. This is not the skill you want to be proficient with. Magic users can equip good staffs that can hit for a lot more points then daggers and this uses the 2 handed bashing skill. Take on simple targets early, such as bats or wasps, and the like. Also try not to team up until level 4. Chances are you are not much use to anyone this early in the game, and you do have to share experience so early in the game.

When you register with your guild, you will get the guild tunic and 5 training points per level. I have several strategies here.

1. You can use your points to train your melee skill (2 handed bashing).
2. You can pick an area of magic to focus your training on such as evocation or channeling (see below).
3. You can save them for later when you really know what area of magic suits you best.

I prefer option 3: even though you struggle early in the game you can put your points on at anytime in the game. Later if you have trouble with some area of magic such as summoning elementals you can dump all your training points into conjuration. I also would not spread your points around, they are more effective if concentrated onto one skill. I feel that is a more planned strategy that will help you get the most out of your training points.

Levels 1-3
The first levels for any spellcaster are a fairly difficult affair. Magicians start out with two spells: Minor Shielding and Burst of Flame. These are going to be your bread and butter for a while, so get to know and love them. Equip that dagger you started with, pull that guild tunic over your head, cast a minor shield and you're ready to go!
The first rule of Norrath should be "/con everything". Learn what you can kill and what can kill you in your area. Don't be afraid of "an even fight" at 1st level, just stay near the guards and be ready to run if something just won't die. If you get killed, don't worry, you didn't loose anything except your guild tunic (which you can't get back if you die). The Magician's life at levels 1-3 consists of killing small creatures, sitting while your mana regenerates and killing more small creatures. When you're not killing, use your sense heading skill to start learning the lay of the land, wherever that may be.
Once you scrape a pile of gold together, go and buy the summon food and drink spells. These are a godsend, because you'll never be without sustenance again! You'll hear every member of your parties saying, "uh-oh, I've got to pick up some more food", and you'll giggle ;) Put a practice point or two into conjuration, just to jump start it.
Experiment with parties when you get to level 3. Aside from the fun of fighting with friends, they'll let you get more experience faster, especially when the bats and wasps aren't paying off like they used to. Partying below level 3 is not really needed, but you certainly are welcome to do it. I've found that you'll learn more about you character if you stay out on your own for a while.
Finally, you're at level 4!

Level 4-7
When you make this level, go running for your guild master and spend one point of training in meditation. You only need to spend one, because you'll be practicing it on your own quite a bit, trust me. Now get ready to spend that pile of money you've been saving! I don't want to be a spoiler, so suffice to say that all elementalkins are not the same. Each one has it's own strengths and weaknesses, so either ask around or try them all yourself, but whatever you do, get one! You can also get a better version of BoF now called Burn.
Try to cast your elementalkin before you leave town. You'll need a reagent to succeed, so find out what you need and put it on your shopping list.
A note about armor: don't worry about it. I don't recommend buying any, because you'll probably be able to find cloth armor on creatures, and as a Magi, you can't wear any better than cloth anyways. Also, the difference between a full set of cloth and nothing is pretty insignificant in the scheme of things. Your shielding spells are your main armor, so always have one active when you're outside.
Now you're ready to take your new elementalkin out for a spin. Head outside to a location that is pretty full of things that you want to kill, cast the elemental and sit and meditate to regain the staggering amount of mana you just lost (don't worry, it'll get better). Your elementalkin is essentially a level 4 warrior that you can control. While the control is not as precise as you'd like it to be, with practice you can figure out how to control your pet. When you run around searching for prey, always make sure your pet is following you. If you turned sharply around a tree, make sure it didn't get lost. If you loose it, go back to where you were recently and start looking around for it. It should eventually come back, but this is a big hassle, so just try to make sure you don't loose it in the first place.
A note about elementals: "Pets", as they are called colloquially, do not have any duration. They're with you until one of the following happens: You die; It dies; You change zones; You turn yourself (or the pet) invisible; You cast Reclaim Energy on it; You log off. They also take quite a while to cast, so don't expect to summon one while you're in battle.
Another note about pets: Yes, they do take a share of experience and no, they don't level up at all, but don't let that worry you. Your pet is your own personal warrior, and it's not like it's just standing there sucking up experience. More often than not, it's the one doing all the killing while you're standing there sucking up experience! With your pet, you can attack things on your own that no other spellcaster could ever dream of defeating alone. Those higher level monsters give more experience and better loot, so even though you split the experience with your pet, you get all the loot. If you get into too much trouble you can run away safely and leave the pet as a sacrificial lamb for the monster (you can always summon another pet).
Once you have a good idea of how to control your pet, you're ready to be a productive member of a party!

Level 8-11
This is an interesting time for the Magician. Obtaining a level 8 elemental’ing is priority number one, and by now you probably have developed a taste for a certain kind of elemental, so you don't have to buy all four spells. This puts you in an interesting position: You now are a pretty strong party all by yourself, and you'll be tempted to go solo to get more experience. This is okay, in some circumstances, but on the whole, the monsters on your level tend to appear in groups, and you likely can't handle groups yet since Magi's don't get any area-effect spells until level 12. Just be careful.

Miscellaneous:

  • One of the move valuable skills early is your channeling skill. This skill will help your casting in several different ways. First, it helps you block out interference when casting (such as thunder, others casting, being hit while casting). One of the biggest annoyances early is not being able to cast a spell because some small creature is hitting you. Second, it allows you to cast for greater distances, and moving targets. This is very helpful as well because most NPC’s will run away when they are almost dead. Third, it will allow you to cast when moving. (at higher levels)
  • Grouping is another way to gain experience quickly while having some support around you. You can group several people at once, even with elementals. (although your are now sharing exp. with your ele, and other people). But remember a well-balanced group is most effective. I.e. Try to find a good melee fighter, a cleric or shaman for healing and yourself for offensive spellcasting. If you need more melee attack, feel free to cast an elemental.
  • One other notable skill, which is available at level 5, is Meditation. This enables you to recover mana more quickly, as well as memorizing spells more quickly as well. The only way to learn this spell is to train the basics of it early at the guild. Then whenever your mana gets low, or you need to memorize spells just sit down and meditate. The drawback to meditation is that you have no ability to cast spells, walk around or even see past your own spell book. Meditate is a totally inactive state.

Overall Tips:
Try to save your mana for the second half of a battle. That is: once the creature you and your pet are fighting is down to about half of its health, then start casting on it to bring it down the rest of the way quickly. There are, of course, exceptions to this, but generally the idea is to save your mana for when you'll really need it. Have some faith in the warriors in your party and in your pet, they might be able to take the monster down with a minimum of fuss. But on the other hand, if they start to get low on health, open up with whatever spells you can.

Using a staff versus a dagger is entirely up to you. A staff does relatively more damage in the early levels, but you're not going to do much damage with either, and when you reach the higher levels, you probably won't even bother trying to get any hits in on the larger monsters anyway.

Think before you attack that camp. Look around for any monsters that are going to come running when you start attacking their friends. Finally, make a party whenever you can, that's the most fun part of the game!

Conclusion:
I hope this give you a good idea of some of the major points of being a Magician. There are too many advantages to just disregard magery when you are selecting your class. I feel that mages tend to advance significantly faster than other classes. Also the magic isn’t just cast and boom, it take strategy and practice to use these abilities to your advantage. In final, I hope I have enlightened you to our class and hope to see you fighting along my side someday! FARE YE WELL!
