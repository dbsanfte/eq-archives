Enchanter

The primary focus of the Enchanter’s art can be summed up by the name of the arcane order to which he belongs: Enchanter. Their spells are crafted to enchant people, places, and things. This art allows them to charm beings and make them fight for their new master, or magically enhance the capabilities of a sword. They also have some skill in offensive spells, but are never as skilled as a Wizard

 

Player Guide: Enchanter By Kastur

 


Overview
Enchanters are the one of the classes least understood. People in the land of Norrath think we are only good for making their equipment magical, when what we do is enchant the minds of our enemies in ways that are unique and enjoyable to behold.

Positives & Negatives
Enchanters are a very subtle class. Of the four true spellcasting classes, our power is not immediately obvious. The Necromancers and mages get pets and the wizards get massive firepower. We get spells that are best suited for grouping and adventuring. When grouped our power lies in making the group more effective and powering down the creatures we fight so that we can take on much harder ones than the group could without an enchanter. When solo we must be strategists to plan our attacks in such a way as to make us come out on the winning side.

Enchanters have intelligence as their primary stat. Since we have high intelligence it also means we advance our skills very easy and have no trouble keeping all our skills maxed.

For adventuring, we get invisibility before all the other classes and can go to places alone or take our group to places that others can not even attempt till they are much stronger.

Since we are a pure casting class we are very limited in both armor and weapons available to us.

Creating your Character
First you must select a race depending on which you would prefer to play. Your choices include: Dark Elf, Erudite, Gnome, High Elf, and Human. Make your choice based on which race you will find most fun to play, not by which has the best numerical stats. The Dark Elves are for those who like to play the side of evil. The choice between the others could be based on other race advantages/disadvantages. The humans and Erudites have no night vision but enchanters do get the spell Serpent Site at Level 12 which gives you Infravision.

Stat allocation is your next step. Intelligence and Charisma are the two primary stats for enchanters. I would recommend putting most into Intelligence. It is not yet know if charisma has any effect for an enchanter other than merchant prices. If buying prices is all that is affected, the other points could be put into Wisdom to cut down on fizzles, or Agility to avoid being hit and interrupted in combat.

Starting Out
You start out in your home city with two spells, Minor Shielding and Shallow Breath. Get these spells into your spell book and memorize them right away so you can start improving them. But refrain from using the shallow breath spell while in town unless you want to experience death at an early age. Cast minor shielding on yourself and then head off in search of the enchanter guild to start your training.

When you find your guild leader he/she will give you a fancy new robe to wear and let you spend up to 5 practice points in any skill. Enchanters increase skill very quickly but still need a boost at the beginning. Enchanters start out with a dagger so all practice points can be used to increase piercing skill for the first few levels. Then set off to find the newbie killing grounds.

ALWAYS have your shielding spell active. I always start an attack with the Shallow Breath spell and then resort to melee for the remainder of the battle. Until you have more spells and have gained a few levels this is how most battles will be fought. /Con everything before you fight to be sure you have a chance.

The weapon skills we can use are piercing, one hand blunt and 2hnd blunt. We are intelligent enough to keep several at the max level. You start out with a dagger but the staffs give higher damage. Most casting types prefer to start learning the 2hnd blunt skill as soon as they can afford a staff.

The only armor we can wear is cloth, which is quite cheap. Later on you will start replacing it with magic items. But most all of the money you collect will be spent on spells instead of armor and weapons.

Spells
Enchanters definitely get the best buffing and debuffing spells in the game. But we also get many that allow us to adventure and fight.

Spells like Lull / soothe / calm are great once you gain a few levels and are now attacking higher level creatures that tend to be in groups themselves. Without lull, if you attack one, they all attack and you find yourself running like a newbie back to the nearest guard all the while yelling "TRAIN". If instead you cast lull on all the ones you don't want to fight, and then attack the one you want, it will come solo and leave its friends behind. This allows your group to attack mobs that are otherwise untouchable till a much higher level.

For buffing up our party we get spells that make our melee fighters stronger and fight faster. We get spells that shield our friends from attack with Haze and Rune. When we are in a group our fighters can take on stronger foes and take less damage, which means less healing time between fights.

When our group finds something to attack, the enchanter can make it weaker, lower its stamina, lower its armor class, stun it, hold it in place, make it unable to fight back at all. We can turn a red tombstone type monster into a blue pussycat. If we start to get overwhelmed with too many at once the enchanter can hold the unwanted with memorized spells while the fighters concentrate on one at a time. Or he can make them run away if he so chooses.

Our direct damage spells come in two varieties. The shallow breath, suffocating shear and choke spells are damage over time spells that don't do a lot at first but as the battle wears on they keep damaging the creature. The other series is chaotic feedback and sanity warp. These do 45 and 88 damage respectively and go up as you gain in levels. They are not as mana efficient as a wizard but when something is about to kill your friend, a fast 88 points of damage can save the day.

For adventuring and travel we also shine as no other class except maybe the bard. We can make all in the group invisible, breath under water, see at night, levitate over dangerous areas, or bind them in cities. We can also change the minds of npc's that don't like us so they welcome us, and will sell to us. If a npc proves to tough or too angry, just make him your friend instead.

Soloing Strategy
For all the great party and adventure spells we have, we are not the greatest at solo, but we can do it well as long as we plan out our attacks.

We can be extremely successful if we group our spells in certain ways. One of the hardest thing for any caster is, once a npc is in our face and hitting us, it is very hard to cast in-between attacks. If you cast languid pace to slow it down first it becomes much easier.

We can cast choke and then mesmerize which holds it in place while we do a quick meditation to regain some mana while it takes damage. Other strategies could be to cast DoT (damage over time) spells then cast invisibility and meditate while it is taking damage again. One bit of warning here is that some npc's can see invisible. We can also change the mind of an npc so he no longer hates us and goes back to what he was doing while we heal and meditate for round two of the fight.

One of our other advantages is the charm spell. If you see 2 npc's you can charm one to attack the other. Charm does wear off but the creature will most likely be hurt from its fight and be easy for you to finish off. The end result is you just got the experience and loot from 2 npc's that others could not have fought alone. Charm also takes a regent so it tends to be used sparingly at early levels where the cost of the regent is high compared to the loot gained.

Group Strategy
When grouped your first responsibility is to buff up your fighters as needed. Once a fight starts your mana is best spent by first casting some dot spells and a couple debuff spells on the npc being attacked. After that depends on the size of the group:

1) If it is a large group I then meditate while the fighters beat it into a pulp. If the npc starts to run I can either cast a direct damage spell to finish it off or a holding spell like root and whirl to let the fighters finish it off.

2) If the group is small I usually add a direct damage spell or two and then join in the melee. Always try to save enough mana for one final damage blast or holding spell incase someone is getting close to death.

If a party member is trying to run away and is already slowed because his health is too low, being able to make the npc stop either by a holding spell or changing its attitude has saved many a friend.

The Future
The enchanter spells are still much under development and many are not yet working while others are still being added. There are spells that enchant items and a whole series of animation and illusion spells that have yet to be put into place. The enchanter is already a viable class and can only get better.

Conclusion
The most common thing I hear when I group in EverQuest is "Never been with an Enchanter before", but almost everytime afterwards I am sure they will always welcome one into their group.

The enchanter has been viewed as a weak class and so has been chosen by very few people. This is because the enchanters skill is not immediately apparent. For those who look at a spell list or some stat pages and don't see the enchanter as a strong character class, RedEye has made the Ogre warrior which is perfect for you. For those people who enjoy a bit of strategy and challenge, the enchanter will let you do things that no other class can come close to.
