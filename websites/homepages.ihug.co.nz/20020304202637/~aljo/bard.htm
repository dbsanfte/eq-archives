Bard

The Bard’s stock-in-trade is his ability to play songs. His songs are actually spells of a sort, and their magical affects last as long as the Bard continues to sing and play the correct instrument. They have some passing knowledge of the Warrior ways (mainly for self-defense), but their main focus is always their art, and so they will never be as skillful as Warriors

 

Player Guide: Bard By Jyzanthemum

 

Being a Bard is like being a left-handed pitcher. There are plenty of you around, but not enough to really call you commonplace, and every good team needs at least one.

Bards have been called the quot;jack of all trades" in Everquest, and with good reason. No other class is as good at combat AND as good at spells. Unfortunately, the full saying is "Jack of all trades, master of none", and that holds true for the bard as well. You will never be as good a fighter as a Warrior, and you’ll never be as good a magic user as a magic using class, but nobody else does both like you do. Bards also receive more Rogue-like abilities than any other class (except the Rogue, obviously), and so really do have a wide variety of skills to choose from.

Bards are very popular, because their songs affect an entire party (with a few exceptions), all the time, with no mana. You will usually have no trouble finding a party to join. Soloing with a bard is challenging, but not the impossible cause many make it out to be. You are still much better off in a party though, and if you decide to play a bard, get ready for a social life.

Starting Out

Race
When creating your bard, the first choice you have to make is the race you are going to play. Bards are very limited in the races they can choose, so there isn’t a lot of thinking to do here. Your choices are limited to Human, Half-Elf, and Wood Elf. All three are excellent choices and are roughly equal to each other. Humans have the worst base statistics of the three, but there are no experience penalties for playing a human. Wood elves have infravision and get the forage skill right from the start, a useful ability for finding food and water. Half-elves are a mix of the two, receiving no special abilities other than infravision, but receiving better base statistics than a human or even a wood elf. Don’t worry about the statistics though, you can be a successful bard no matter your race. Decide which race you like the best based on looks, type of race and starting location. Even if you’re not a dedicated roleplayer, other players and NPCs will treat you differently based on your race and that is far more important than a few statistic points here and there.

An additional consideration is starting cities. Humans can only start out in Freeport or Qeynos. Wood elves can only start out in Kelethin. Half-Elves can start in all three. Half-Elves can usually start in Felwithe as well, but since city does not have a Bard Guild, you won’t have that choice as a bard. This doesn’t make Half-Elves the superior choice, it just makes it an important consideration. As far as what the best starting location is, that’s really a matter of taste that I won’t go into here.

 

Statistics
The most important statistics for a bard are Strength, Dexterity, and Charisma. Some sources also say that Wisdom is important for the bard, but how it affects your bard is uncertain. Strength plays into the warrior aspect of the bard, namely how much you can carry and how hard you hit things. Dexterity afffects your armour class and Rogue-like skills. Charisma affects your influence, both to NPCs and presumably the influence your songs have, though the precise effect of this is undetermined at this time.

So, where do you put your extra stats? It’s up to you, and largely depends on your race. A wood elf will probably want to pump up their strength. A human could use a little more dexterity. All the statistics are useful to you though, so spread them around how you want to play your character.

 

Entering the Game
Once you enter the game, you have a starting weapon, a PK note, and a quest letter. Your first job should be to find your Bard Guild and complete the quest on your quest note. You’ll gain some experience and get to practice your new skills. The Bard Guild is usually pretty easy to find. Listen for the music coming from the building. Right now, combat is going to be the most important skill you have, so put your skills into your weapon type (probably one handed slashing) and get yourself out to the local newbie killing zone. At this point, your life is not much different from a starting warrior. You don’t start with a spell/song, like the spellcasting classes do, so you need to make some money!

Once you’re out there on the killing fields, get to work killing those newbie monsters. As always, watch your health and be prepared to run for the guards when you need to.

Once you have accumulated a couple of gold, it’s time to truly become a bard - go and buy a song!

Buying Songs
Every Bard Guild has an NPC that sells bard songs. Check the list out and start looking forward to the high level songs. Only buy the songs that you can actually sing though, because you can’t scribe them until you’re of the appropriate level, and if you die and a song is in your inventory, it will disappear!

To scribe your song, use the same procedure used to scribe a regular spell. Open your spellbook by clicking on it, then drop your song into a blank box in the spellbook. The progress indicator will start to tick down as the song is scribed.

Once the song is in your spellbook, you can memorize it by clicking on it, then clicking on one of the ovals to the top right of your display. Again, the progress indicator will keep you informed of how long the memorization will take. You can only memorize 8 songs, but this won’t become a problem for quite some time.

Once you have your first level song, start singing it by clicking on the oval with the icon in it. Don’t ever stop singing! It costs you nothing in terms of mana, and if you aren’t at your skill cap for your level, you’ll always be improving your skill. Plus, it looks pretty. Your first song is Chant of Battle, and it’s a pretty good one. Go out and do some more killing on the newbie fields and enjoy your new "pumped up" status. You’ll be surprised how much better you fight. Try joining a group, and enjoy the compliments of your compatriots as you pump them up as well.

 

Bard Songs
I’ll do a short description of the first 10 songs that bards receive. After that, you’ll be on your own, but help is always around the corner. Politely asking a higher level bard will usually get you the answers you seek. Word of warning, this is still beta, and many of the songs don’t work as they are supposed to, or at all. Assume that these songs could change at any time.

  1. Chant of Battle - (Singing skill) Adds to your AC and strength. A weak effect, but you’ll mostly be singing this song at a low level, and it will work well for you. This song affects everyone in your party.
  2. Chords of Dissonance - (Stringed Instruments) Damages everything within a certain area from the person singing/playing the song. A very useful spell for damaging monsters, especially those that cannot be harmed by normal weapons and/or a gang of monsters all in a group. This spell is VERY dangerous though, as everything affected by it will NOT be happy with you. In dungeons for example, you will find yourself attacked by monsters that were ignoring you up until you started playing this song. In inns and cities, this song will get guards and merchants attacking YOU very quickly. I don’t know a single bard who hasn’t been killed because they played this song too close to a guard or merchant. When you use it, get in the habit of turning it off IMMEDIATELY after you kill the monster you are fighting.
  3. Jaxan`s Jig o` Vigor - (Singing skill) This song increases your stamina as you sing it. Stamina is very important when you run long distances, or in long fights. This song affects everyone in your party.
  4. Lyssa`s Locating Lyric - (Singing skill) If you have nothing targeted, this song finds the nearest corpse. Not too useful. The strength of this song lies in its targeted ability. Select the person whose corpse you wish to locate (such as yourself), and start singing. Soon your body will jerk around in the direction of the corpse, and the corpse is selected. You will make many friends with this song, locating long lost bodies, an all too common problem in EQ.
  5. Selo`s Accelerando - (Percussion skill) One of the best songs a bard has, this song enables the bard to run like the wind, particularly if the person is using a drum and is skilled in its use. This song affects everyone in the party, and so is very popular for groups making a long distance trip. The song also decreases the stamina loss caused by running for long periods of time. Whether it is getting from A to B faster or outrunning an angry swarm of monsters, this is one of the most handy songs a bard will ever sing.
  6. Hymn of Restoration - (Stringed Instruments) This is the crème de la crème of bard songs, at least in the first 10 levels. Singing this song provides a constant regeneration of health for as long as the song is played. Healing rates vary with levels, but singing the song alone up to level 11 heals 3 hit points per "pulse", which roughly equates to one round of combat. Playing this song with a stringed instrument increases the effect to 5 per pulse. This song effects everyone in your party and will really make you popular, both among your fellow adventurers and among the monsters you are fighting, who will often stop beating on the beefy fighters in the group to get at the pesky bard healing everyone. In a party that switches off the person who is taking damage, this song is amazing, because it allows players to take damage, heal while someone else takes the damage, and then switch off again, vastly increasing the durability of the party during a fight. After the fight is over, get our your lute and start playing. Soon, even the most grievously wounded fighter in the party will be ready to go again, all without mana expenditure.
  7. Jonthan's Whistling Warsong - (Singing skill) This song is essentially a beefed up "Chant of Battle" that only effects the singer. It has little use in a party, but is useful when soloing. Some say the healing song is better, but you don’t need to heal if they can’t hit you.
  8. Kelin`s Lugubrious Lament - (Singing skill) This song used to make monster "sad" and stop attacking you. Now it just makes them "sad". Nobody seems to know what that means. The song is probably not functioning properly at this point.
  9. Elemental Rhythms - (Percussion Instruments) This song vastly increases the party’s resistance to fire, cold and magic, particularly when played with a drum. Useful for fighting spellcasting monsters and the like, but the situations are pretty specialized.
  10. Anthem de Arms - (Singing skill) This song increases the party’s strength and more importantly - attack speed! The increase in speed is hard to notice at times, but it is there, and you will REALLY notice it in parties with a lot of "hitters". If you are in a party that is fighting a lot of monsters and not taking a lot of damage, you will find this song even better than the mighty Hymn of Restoration, and that is saying A LOT.

 

Instruments
There are four kinds of instruments, stringed (lutes and harps), winds (flutes), percussion (drums), and brass (horns). Each is useful for different song types. A song will note the type of instrument needed on the song’s description. Even if a song needs an instrument, like Hymn of Restoration, it will work if you are NOT playing that instrument. Which is handy, because a) Sometimes you will get a song before you get the skill needed to play the instrument and b) sometimes you don’t want to tie up your hands holding an instrument when you could be holding a weapon!

Equipping Yourself
Generally speaking, bards equip themselves like fighters, with the same armor and weaponry for the most part. One disadvantage that bards have is that they have to spend a lot of their money on songs, while a fighter can spend it all on equipment. Get yourself some good armor, a good weapon and get out there and make some more money, because you’re going to need it! At around level 6 or so, you are going to have to decide whether you’re going to be a bard-fighter, or a fighter-bard. What I mean by this is that there are two schools of thought on how to play a bard in combat, with instrument, or without. Chords of Dissonance and Hymn of Restoration are much more effective if you have your lute in your hand, but if you’re holding your lute, you cannot hold a weapon. The question is, do you want to take the reduced damage that hand to hand does and compensate by being a more effective healer/damager? Or do you want to be a less effective healer/damager bard and deal more damage with weapons. Most bards choose the latter route, but you will see many that choose the former route in your travels. If you are going to be a lute-wielding bard, make sure to spend some time working on your hand to hand combat skills, as they will become very important to you.

Multiple bards in a group
There is no limitation to the number of bards that a group can have, but there is a limitation, at least currently, of one instance of a song per party, so if there ARE multiple bards in the group, they cannot all be singing the same song. Generally, one bard will sing the healing song and the other the Anthem, or some other song. Personal preference rules the day, though.

Bard Tactics - Or a Day in the Life of a Bard
There are many ways to play a bard, so tactics could be a long discussion. Let us instead show one way that a bard can use his or her songs over the course of a few hours of adventuring.

I will play the central character in this little tale. Jyzanthemum, for purposes of the tale, is a level 10 bard, with all the songs except Kellin’s Lugubrious Lament and Elemental Rhythms memorized.

Starting out my day, I wake up in the Steamfont Mountains. Looking about, I see a potential victim nearby, a Gnome Skeleton. Starting up my level 7 song, Jonthan’s Whistling Warsong, I wait until I see the "Strength" icon appear on my screen (so I know the song has "kicked in") and Iattack! My axe bites into the skeleton again and again, my blows strengthened by my song. Every few seconds I see the message "You begin to whistle an ancient warsong" on my screen, indicating that the song is doing its work on me. After a while into the fight, I see that the skeleton is damaging me, despite missing me much more than normal due to the song, so I decide it is time to change songs to the level 6 song, Hymn of Restoration. As I change to that song, the "Strength" icon disappears, replaced by the "Healing" icon. Soon my blows have slightly less bite, but I see the message "Your wounds begin to heal" over and over. Whenever the gnome misses me, I see my health inch upwards instead of remaining the same, and I know the fight will be mine.

Soon the fight is over, and I loot the corpse. I am still rather hurt, so I keep my Hymn active while I look for a new victim. Although I am soon healed, I have been running around looking for a new victim and I notice my stamina is almost depleted, partly from the fight, partly from the running. I stop for a moment, sit down, and start playing Jaxon’s Jig of Vigor. Soon I see the little "boot" icon telling me the song is working and I see the words "The jig sends energy zinging throughout your body" on the screen every few seconds. My stamina rises very quickly and soon I am ready to fight again.

I am in search of prey when I hear someone shout, "Can anyone help me? I can’t find my corpse!" Always wanting to aid a fellow adventurer, I volunteer my services, only to discover they are halfway across the zone. Little matter to a bard though, as I get out my drum (which strangely, looks like a flute - it is a beta after all), equip it, and then start playing Selo’s Accelerando. Soon I have a new "boot" icon on the screen and the words "You begin to move faster" appear on the screen. I start running toward the entrance of Ak’Anon, where the recently-killed adventurer awaits. The terrain flies by under me, and I easily pass other adventurers moving down the path at a full run, much to their amazement. I even occasionally take damage as I fly off the tops of hills and crash down to the ground in my haste. The damage is inconsequential though in my thirst for speed. Soon I am standing before the entrance of Ak’Anon and locate the person I have been seeking. Selecting them, and putting my drum away I start singing Lyssa’s Locating Lyric. I wait a few moments and suddenly, the words "The tune pervades your senses and beckon you" appear on the screen and I am physically jerked in a new direction. I know where the corpse is now.

With a cry of "Follow me!" I lead the adventurer over some hills to his corpse, carefully not starting the Accelerando, lest I leave him far behind. Soon the adventurer is looting his body and offering me money for finding the body for him. Of course, I do not accept, as the task was simple for me and I was glad to help.

As I part ways with my new friend, I run into several adventurers who are forming a party to go to Crushbone, a settlement of the orcs. Seeing me running down the path with my song leaving a trail of magic behind me, they quickly ask if I will join them. Eager to fight the orcs, I agree. Accepting their offer into the group, suddenly all the members of the party glow with the affect of my song, and we are off! Crushbone is three zones away, but we cover the distance in a remarkably short period of time.

Once in the zone, we are beset on all sides by orcs. I switch to the healing song and ready my axe. Soon most of the orcs are dead, except one tough Legionnaire. Seeing that the "tank" of the party is not very hurt, I switch to the level 10 song, Anthem de Arms, so that we may finish him off more quickly. Soon the words "A burst of strength surges through your body" appear on the screen and everyone’s attacks increase in speed. The orc is in trouble, and soon is close to death. He turns and begins to run away. Everyone chases him, but it is hard to hit a fleeing foe, so I quickly switch to my level 2 song, Chords of Dissonance. Soon the flares of light around my party cease, but the orc finds the song affecting him! The words, "Orc Legionnaire winces" appear on my screen and see that his health decreases with each pulse. He can run, but not far enough away from my song’s effects, and soon he keels over in death.

My song has attracted the attention of other orcs, though, who were damaged when I ran past them singing. Soon I am beset on all sides and separated from my party. I keep singing my damaging song, as there are far too many of these lesser orcs for me to hit at once with my axe. Soon one orc falls to my axe, then another. I notice as I switch from orc to orc that each one is already damaged before I even target them with my weapon. The Chords of Dissonance have been working! By the time I am fighting the final two orcs, the smaller of the pair, an orc pawn, falls over without ever being hit, and his brother does soon after. I have won, but at a cost. I am seriously wounded. Equipping my lute, I begin playing my healing song and healing at an increases rate due to the lutes effects as I loot the corpses and return to my party for more adventuring.

As you can see, the life of the bard is never dull.
