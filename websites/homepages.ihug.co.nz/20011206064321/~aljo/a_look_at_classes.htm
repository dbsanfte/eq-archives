Classes by Mahrin Skel


Unlike some previous games, EverQuest is completely class-based, which means you choose a Class, and that determines what skills, spells, and (along with race) abilities you will have. There are no Tank-Mages in EverQuest, no Dual or Multi Classing, you pick your class and you play it.

That, combined with the high time investment required to get a class to even medium levels, means that you're going to want to consider the class of your primary character very carefully. And you're going to want to go further than just finding what class is "best", because of EQ's very Group-Oriented nature. Warriors may be easiest to play, and needed for every balanced party, but they are *much* more effective with Clerics in their party. Wizards are the lords of "Nuking", but without a Tank to stand up front and take the hits, they don't do well. In general, I suggest you pick a class because you *like* it, not because it seems the most powerful. EQ is much more balanced then many games, "Best" is as much a matter of player knowledge as of inherent strengths of the class.

That being said you need to know a bit more about the classes, to make a judgement based on what they are actually like to play. They fall into two basic groups, the Tanks and the Spellcasters ("Hybrids" are much more Tank than caster).

MELEE CLASSES

Warrior:

989 says that "Warriors are the masters of armed combat, in all its many forms. They are at home on the battlefield, and are trained to take punishment as much as dole it out, so their Hit Points are the highest of any class."

I say that Warriors are just about the easiest class to play. It's straightforward, you walk up to something and start hitting it until either it falls down or you have to run away. If you play with a spellcaster in your group (highly recommended you have a magi and a clerical caster), you use Taunt and try to keep all of the mobs focused on you. You get a wide selection of toys, can wield almost any weapon and wear nearly any piece of armor.

Warriors are built to dish out and take punishent in melee battles, and they do it well. However, if you don't have a Cleric along, you spend *outrageous* amounts of time sitting on your tail between fights, regaining your health. If you don't have a Magi caster along, you wind up chasing down "runners", praying that they die before they reach reinforcements.

Paladin:

989: "Paladins are the very picture of a holy knight, fighting for the cause of good in all aspects of his life. They share some of the power of a Cleric, and a good deal of the fighting ability of a Warrior, but have a few powers all their own."

I say that Paladins are about 75% Warrior and 25% Cleric. As "Emergency Healers" they are great, the guy who keeps everyone alive when the Cleric is out of mana, especially since Lay On Hands heals someone *fully* (but they can only use it once per EQ day, or an hour and a half realtime).

As Tanks they aren't bad at all, and supposedly the best magic weapons in the game will be Paladin-only. They do take a sizable XP penalty, however.

Ranger:

989: "The Ranger is a meld between a Warrior and a Druid, sharing some of the skills of both. They are at home in the outdoors."

Just what the package says. Rangers are very much in the AD&D mold, with Tracking, and melee combat skills second only to the Warrior. Their magical talents are similar to the Paladin, except that Rangers get some of the Druid's better "Utility" spells. Similar XP penalties to the Paladin.

Shadow Knight:

989: "The evil twin to the Paladin class, Shadow Knights derive all of their powers from the evil gods whom they serve. They are a meld of Warrior and Necromancer, and share some of their abilities and spells."

In actual play, these guys can be awesome, especially the infamous TSK (Troll Shadow Knight). Harm Touch is the reverse form of Lay On Hands, doing half of a creature's HP in damage, with no saving throw. Melee is very much that of a Paladin, spellcasting is more damage-oriented than healing, without a doubt the TSK is the easiest to "Solo".

Monk:

989: "Monks are dedicated to the pursuit of shaping their bodies into a pure weapon, and because of this they nearly shun all weapons and most forms of armor. They are very religious as well, and the gods are known to bless them, not by prayers and spells, but by magically enhancing their attacks."

Monks have one advantage over every other class: Equipment is entirely disposable. Although there are things that it is nice for the Monk to have, and are worth getting, a Monk can function barehanded and naked, no other melee class can.

They share a few "Roguish" skills like Sneak, Instill Doubt and Safe Fall, and a couple all their own: Mend (self-heal 25% of HP every 6 minutes or so) and Feign Death (fall down and play dead. If it works, what was fighting you loses interest).

Bard:

989: "The Bard’s stock-in-trade is his ability to play songs. His songs are actually spells of a sort, and their magical affects last as long as the Bard continues to sing and play the correct instrument. They have some passing knowledge of the Warrior ways (mainly for self-defense), but their main focus is always their art, and so they will never be as skillful as Warriors."

Bards are an interesting case, not quite like any other class. Their songs require no mana, can be played indefinitely, and can affect either all members of a party, or all mobs in the vicinity. Two bards in a party can be awesome, even one can fantastically improve party performance. On the other hand, Bards don't solo well. If you're naturally social and like to group, as a Bard you'll never have trouble finding a party that wants you along.

They get some of the Rogue skills, most especially the only non-Rogues that can Pick Locks.

Rogue:

989: "They are the secretive, mysterious class of the world, who sneak about in the dark. Some say they are assassins, and some that they are nothing but common thieves, but the truth lies somewhere in the middle, and they are capable of both. They are quite skilled with many weapons, but they cannot stand toe to toe with a Warrior and prefer to make the kill by a sudden attack from the rear, at which they are extremely skilled. They would much prefer to not be discovered at all, or if they are, to not be there for their opponent’s blow to land. The darker skills of picking locks and stealing are also their forte."

Many people blow off EQ's version of the Rogue, assuming that since they can't steal from -pvp players they aren't good for much. Big mistake, the highest level character in Beta was a Rogue named Kendrick. Rogues are very good in melee, have skills that are unique to them such as Backstab, and in general have been far underestimated.

Rogues cover the gamut from the "Mercenary" to the "Footpad". A Barbarian Rogue, with high Strength and the Slam racial skill to stun an opponent and get behind it for an extra Backstab is able to hold it's own with the Halfling Rogue, who is very nimble and quick, but not so hard-hitting.

SPELLCASTERS

Clerics:

989: "The Cleric is a holy man endowed by his patron deity with the power to heal, and, to a lesser extent, call upon the wrath of his deity to smite his foes. Prayer is how the Cleric gains his ability."

Very much the traditional Cleric. Can wear just about any armor, can only wield blunt weapons, has the best Healing spells, good "Buffing" spells, and a few so-so direct damage spells. Clerical casters are *essential* to a balanced group.

As soloists, the Cleric can do well against Undead, as members of a party, they should keep an eye on the healthbars of the entire group and Heal as needed, while fighting.

Druids:

989: "A Druid is completely at home in the outdoors, and is a friend to all animals. Because of this, no animal will ever attack him unless first attacked by the Druid. They are spellcasters whose focus is all things natural, allowing them to call upon nature for aid. They share some woodland skills with their Ranger brothers.

Druids are the Jacks of all Trades of the spellcasters. Melee roughly equivalent to a Cleric at lower levels (but restricted in armor the Cleric would get at higher ones), offensive spellcasting far better than any other Clerical caster, the best "Damage Shield" type buffs in the game.

Where Druids really excel is as the master travelers of Norrath. No other class gets both an invisibility spell (Camoflage at 5th) and a "speed-up" spell (Spirit of the Wolf at 14th). In addition to this, at 19th and beyond, they get some Teleport traveling spells, and "group" versions of those at high levels. However, don't take that "no animal will ever attack" seriously, for game balance and technical reasons most animals are as aggressive to Druids as anyone else.

Shaman:

989: "They are similar to the Cleric, but are more of a tribal witch doctor and are typically found amongst the more primitive races. Their primary focus is largely healing and other Clerical type spells, but they also have a good deal of offensive spells."

Where Shamans really excel is in Party situations. Only the Enchanter has buffing spells as good as the Shaman, and the Enchanter can't Heal. However, the Shaman is also decent solo, where the Enchanter typically isn't. This is mostly due to Shamans being limited to the "Big" races, and having better melee skills.

Necromancer:

989: "These are the dark brotherhood of the arcane arts, dabbling in death. Through evil spiritually based spells, they are able to animate dead bodies and skeletons to do their bidding, as well as having spells which leach life from their victims."

Necros are just plain cool. Who else can cast spells that take points away from your target and gives them to you, making them a simultaneous Heal and Direct Damage? They also have the ability to Summon pets, and the *best* "Poisoning" type spells in the game, bar none.

On the other hand, NPC's tend to dislike Necros. Finding someone to do business with can be difficult, and you have to spend time actively cultivating better Faction standings in order not to get whacked by guards. Still, Necros are probably the *best* spellcasting solo class in the game.

Wizards:

989: "Wizards are the master seekers of knowledge, and their talents and spells are focused on this goal. They use powerful spells to not only see things from afar, but to transport them there as well. This thirst for arcane knowledge has also made them the true masters of the strongest form of defense: offensive spells of pure destruction. In their ability to do magical damage, they have no rivals."

Pretty much what they say, Wizards are second to none at the Flash-Bang business. Nobody can do damage as fast as a Wizard, no spellcaster can do as much. However, they are very much all offense and no defense, if the fight isn't over when you run out of Mana, and you don't have a Tank along, you might as well start running, if you can.

Magicians:

989: "Magicians are the summoners of the arcane arts, and wield the power to call forth everything from a loaf of bread to a huge Fire Elemental to fight for them. While not as adept at offensive spells as the Wizards, Magicians still wield considerable power in this area as well."

In actual play, these guys are the "good" version of a Necromancer. You have pets, you have damaging spells, you're not a Tank-Mage, but you are a Tank *and* a Mage. Easy soloing, decent Fire Support in parties, a good class all around.

Enchanter:

989: "The primary focus of the Enchanter’s art can be summed up by the name of the arcane order to which he belongs: Enchanter. Their spells are crafted to enchant people, places, and things. This art allows them to charm beings and make them fight for their new master, or magically enhance the capabilities of a sword. They also have some skill in offensive spells, but are never as skilled as a Wizard."

Enchanters are the most group-oriented of the Magi classes, and are a *very* powerful aid to a party. With the best "buffing" spells to power up the party, and "de-buffs" far better than anyone else in the game, Enchanters can easily double or triple the effectiveness of a party. Anyone who writes off Enchanters has never seen what they do for a party.

All in all, there are no "Broken" classes in EverQuest, and all of the classes are nicely unique with their own strengths and weaknesses (some of them don't differentiate completely until around 20th level, however). And when I say that one class or another is easier to "Solo", it's important to note that soloing is the hardest way to play the game, those who play in balanced groups will far outrun soloists.

--Mahrin Skel
