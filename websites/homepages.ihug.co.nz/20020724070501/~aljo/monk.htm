Monk

Monks are dedicated to the pursuit of shaping their bodies into a pure weapon, and because of this they nearly shun all weapons and most forms of armor. They are very religious as well, and the gods are known to bless them, not by prayers and spells, but by magically enhancing their attacks.

 

Player Guide: Monk  By Ripley


OVERVIEW
With all due respect to Warriors, in the long run, Monks are the most effective pure melee combat professionals in Norrath. They attack quickly, avoid hits well, and require little in the way of material aid. And let's face it: They've got the best moves.

CREATING A CHARACTER
Monks must be human. When you distribute your extra Stat points, I'd recommend putting them in Strength and Agility. The former adds to the damage you do in an attack, the latter helps your Armor Class.

As with all characters, your first quest will be to find your guild, either in Qeynos or Freeport, since the Monk class is restricted to humans only. Frankly, I'm not familiar with the guild in Freeport, but in any case, I have been told that the guildquests for the Freeport monks aren't working yet, so at least for now, I'd suggest you start in Qeynos.

GETTING STARTED
Unfortunately, monks don't have much in the way of moves at the start, just your basic punch and kick. You start out with your standard food, water, guild note and PK note. The PvP choice is up to you, but at the start, your monk won't be good at it, even compared to other beginning classes, so if you are interested in that aspect of Everquest, I suggest you stash the note and at least wait until you are high level (30th or above) to engage in it. Once you do reach that level, you'll be a combat machine that very few characters can stand up to on a one-on-one basis (assuming you're fighting someone of the same level).

The Qeynos monk organization is known as the Brotherhood of the Silent Fist. It's located in North Qeynos, across the pond from Crow's Tavern. If you're entering Qeynos through the main gate, go through the initial tunnel leading into the city, then turn right, go past and around the corner of Sneed Galliway's Trading post, and go straight across to the Brotherhood. The guild master is LuSun; he's located in the rear of the compound, so you go through the building, and around to the back. Give him your note, get your tunic, then go and find trainer Phin Esrinap.

Phin is usually in one of three places: in the main building, sitting just outside of it, or standing on the grass near Sneed's. Any trainer, of course, can teach you skills, but only Phin can give you beginning quests, so go to him first.

When you find Phin, first use three of your five beginning practices. Use one practice to learn the basics of Mend. Use a second to learn the basics of Dodge. Use a third to learn the basics of Safe Fall. Save the other two practices for later.

A Tip: From here on in, save all the practices you accumulate until 17th level, save for learning the bare basics of new skills as you advance. More on that later.

After you train with Phin, say 'Hail Phin Esrinap' to learn about your first quest. I won't spoil it, other than to say that to find out more information from Phin (or many other NPCs, for that matter), type 'What KEYWORD', with the KEYWORD being one of the Capitalized Words in Phin's dialogue.

Once you've found Phin, go out and start bashing. Basically, go left around the corner of Sneed's, and go through the entry tunnel, out the main gate and into the land.

BEGINNING ADVENTURING TIPS
The pastures right outside of Qeynos and Freeport are filled with beginning creatures. It's not really necessary to team up at these low levels, it'll just hinder your experience gain. Just attack anything that moves, but do note that before you actually hit something, target it, then do a /c to Consider it. Anything that's Yellow or Red, I'd avoid for now, unless you just enjoy dying for no reason. On the other hand, dying at any level below 5th is just not a big deal -- you don't lose any experience, no one can loot your corpse, so if you do die in the Qeynos or Freeport pasture, when you resurrect in front of the city gates, just go back out, get your things from your corpse, and jump back into the fray.

I won't go into detail about monsters, or geographic areas, since tips along with those lines apply to all classes. But in terms of Monk-specific guidance, be aware of a few things:

As you travel or fight, at least in the beginning, hit 'Mend' as often as you can. It takes awhile to recycle, but trust me, just hit it every time you can, even if you're not wounded. It'll fail most of the time in the beginning, but as you build it up, it becomes quite effective. Around the time it hits 50, you can stop hitting it out of habit, and just use it in combat situations -- it'll still fail a bit, but more than half the time, it will work, and there's nothing like watching your health go from half to full in one shot. To be honest with you, most Monks spend all their practices on Mend, to get it up quickly. I think you can accomplish virtually the same thing by just habitually hitting a Hot Key, but certainly, given the usefulness of the skill, you can do a lot worse things with the Guild Training that you earn as you gain in levels. It's up to you.

  • Also practice 'Safe Fall' often -- it's another great skill to have, as it lets you survive steep drops (a nice ability to have in certain places, such as the Wood Elf city in Greater Faydark, or in Everfrost, if you like climbing the glaciers there). In the beginning, you have to be careful about where you practice it -- once you hit 5th level or so, you can practice it in Qeynos from the Grounds of Fate balcony where Captain Tillin stands; you'll have enough hit points to survive the fall there, even if Safe Fall fails. Obviously, practicing this skill can get VERY tedious, but if you have patience, it'll help in the long run.
  • As you get practices from training, it's not necessary to waste them on combat abilities beyond learnings the Basics of a given skill. Your combat abilities will go up nicely during the course of adventuring; instead save your practices for a the critical non-combat skills, one in particular, which we'll mention soon.

    COMBAT SKILLS
    As far as combat skills go, just focus on hand-to-hand, don't worry about weaponry. In the long run, your hand-to-hand skills will far outshine any blunt weapon. Here are some (though not all) fighting skills you'll want to work on:

    Hand-to-Hand: This is your basic punching. It gets better as you go along, just turn it on in combat and let it happen. It doesn't do as much damage as many weapons, but it is fast, and as you work on it, it becomes rather potent because you connect so often (Monks are one of the few, if not the only, class that can vastly improve its basic hand-to-hand). And I'm told that at 17th level, your punches become magical, which not only adds a bit of damage, but also lets you take on Magic Weapon-only monsters such as Ice Bone Skeletons and Scarecrows.

    Kick: You'll only use this skill until fifth level when you can get...

    Round Kick: This is the first of your Special Attacks, each of which counts as a Magical Attack. By themselves, Special Attacks won't take down the Magic Weapon Only Monsters, but they can be good, and at advanced levels, devastating supplements to your punches. The animation for it is nifty, and it does about as much damage than punches, although it is slower. Basically, use this in combat until 10th level, when you can get...

    Tiger Claw: A low striking punch, it does almost as much damage as Round Kick, but recycles faster.

    Dodge: It functions automatically in combat once you've trained in the basics, so you don't need to activate it or anything like that. Basically, if it succeeds, the monster misses. Same thing is true for...

    Block: Which is available to you once you hit 12th level. Also automatically activates in combat.

    Dragon Punch, Eagle Strike: Other advanced attacks.

    Flying Kick: This is trainable once you hit 30th level. Can be devastating, as much as 70 HP per attack.

    Monks can also use objects for Throwing, but that skill is mostly useful just for getting the attention of a monster, as opposed to doing any real damage.

    Feign Death
    Perhaps the most useful skill you'll learn, at least for yourself if not for other adventurers, is Feign Death, which you can pick up at 17th level. Remember how you saved all those practices over the levels? Here's where you slap them all, in one shot.

    Feign Death lets you do just that -- pretend you're dead. If you successfully use the skill, you flop on the ground and it looks like you're dead. Why is this important? Suffice it to say that there will be situations in which you are suddenly overmatched, i.e., a train nails you from behind, or a Hill Giant suddenly spawns next to you, etc. In those cases, you can Feign Death, and the monster(s) will think you're dead and leave you alone. You can lie there as long as you want, even long enough to recover all your Health if you want.

    A real Life Saver of a skill, and worth saving up for.

    MISCELLANEOUS
    As with any other class, of course, Monks do have their weaknesses -- they can't cast spells and can't use most weapons. Monks are very limited in their choice of armor: leather and cloth, which you buy in NPC shops; Patchwork, which is created by players who use Tailoring; and a few esoteric types, such as Mesh and Snakeskin, which are available only from monsters in certain dungeons.

    On the other hand, your AC naturally increases as your Defense improves with experience, so that makes up somewhat for the inability to wear heavier armor. And although you don't have quite as much health as a Warrior, you have more than any other class. As a result, whenever you're in a party, your main function will be as the 'Tank,' meaning you're the one who wades in first and becomes the target for the monster(s), while your fellow adventurers either cast spells from behind or jump into the fray later. Monsters generally go after whoever hits them the most, and in most cases, that will be you, so if you're in a party, make sure there's a healer, or else you personally could be in trouble.

    In any case, do remember of course that Everquest is designed for Parties. If you want to go solo, sooner or later you will have problems and you will find yourself overmatched. Solo is fine for short stints, but for a monk to be optimally effective, the character works with spellcaster back-up.

    Now go and use your skills wisely. Bring pride upon our class.
