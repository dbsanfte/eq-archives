Our Process Model

At Rahvalor Interactive we constantly test and evaluate our methods so that we can best fulfill the needs of our customers. So far, experience has been our best teacher. We bring to your service years of trial and error that have yielded a basic development process that has proven to us and our customers to be straightforward and effective.

Whether this is your first foray into marketing or you have a specific need, our team will work with you through a series of carefully constructed steps so that the end result will add value to your business.

Discovery – Establishing goals and expectations

During this stage we learn about your company, your products and services and your expectations. We help you to:

  • Determine long and short term goals
  • Define your target audience
  • Determine the appropriate technology to deliver your message

We suggest you break out the coffee and donuts because this stage requires a lot of talking, planning and hashing out the details. It may take a while. In fact, it may be the longest stage of the process and you might wonder when we’re going to get started on your project. Don’t worry, we will. But the preparation that goes on before development will build the foundation for everything to come. If your foundation is shaky, your house is eventually going to collapse. Better to put the time and effort into planning the proper steps up front.

During this phase we will develop a complete proposal and budget estimate so that you can answer the two most often-asked questions: "What do I get?" and "How much is it?"

Pre-production – Making sense of the Discovery phase

During this stage we organize everything we learned during the previous stage and acquire any materials or content that may be required. We’ll collect photography, organize or write copy, gather preexisting materials, outline the stages to come and make sure we have all of the elements that need to go into your project.

Prototype – Getting to work

The prototype is otherwise known as the “first draft”. Depending on your project, we may present you with...

  • A digital mockup
  • A test site
  • A storyboard
  • A print proof

This first draft allows you to make revisions, add features, or just change your mind about the details without getting so far into a project that making changes will cause a production (and budget) nightmare. We will present you with as simple and comprehensive a version as possible for your comments and review.

The first version of a web site in particular might raise a few eyebrows. It won’t be pretty, and we mean that literally. It will be a text-only site, with text-only links for navigation. The content and structure will be there but the pretty pictures will not. We do this for a very important reason. We want the site to work.

With only text to contend with, we can easily rearrange navigation, add pages, change layout and modify content without worrying about working within the confines of a specific graphical look. Imagine that your site’s graphics are designed to accommodate four main navigation links. What happens when you realize that you need five? Often, when graphics are designed first, you are locked into a pattern or layout that is not so easily changed without adding to the time and cost of the project.

Design – The pretty pictures

Aesthetics are subjective. This is where we listen entirely to you, your likes and dislikes, your preferences. Our goal is not to love your graphics – but to be sure that you do. Of course, we will do that within the boundaries of functionality, usability and overall good marketing presentation. We won’t sacrifice quality, but we aren’t going to debate shades of blue or photo left vs. photo right. We will present our ideas, make judgment calls, offer our expertise and hope that you agree – but in the end, you must be satisfied with what you see.

Production – Putting the pieces together

This is when everything comes together - design, content and functionality. By the end of this stage, you will be able to see your completed project in all its glory.

Testing and Debugging – Making sure everything works

This is the techie stuff that we do to make sure that anything electronic does what it should. It means we see that your forms are programmed and functioning properly, your site links are working, your database is functioning, or your CD is running. It’s important during this stage to test on multiple browsers, multiple platforms and multiple computer systems. Some very different things may happen to a web site, CD or presentation on a Mac versus a PC, in Netscape versus Internet Explorer or on one laptop versus another desktop. We make sure that however customers access your information, it will be running at 100%.

Evaluation and Approval – Getting your feedback

Ultimately we want your stamp of approval. By the end of a project we will review it with you to ensure that we have met the project requirements.

Launch – Making it public

This should, and generally does, only happen after a project is completed. We’re not fans of “Coming Soon” pages on web sites or incomplete “interim” content. It’s better to take an extra week, even month, making your site, ad or presentation right, than to make a bad first impression on a customer.

Maintenance – Keeping it fresh

Also known as “support”, this is the point where you often find yourself asking, “What now?” The “what now” is that we follow up either on a predetermined schedule or in an ongoing capacity to ensure the success of your endeavor. If you have a web site, this may mean keeping news articles current, adding additional pages for products or content or embarking on a search engine marketing campaign. If you have a content or customer management system, this may mean tweaking features as you use it and find that your needs have changed, or providing additional training for you or your staff.

The important thing for you to know is that whenever you have a “what now” question, you can call or e-mail and we will gladly spend time with you to find the answer.
