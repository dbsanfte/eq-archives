Saethorne's guide for Human Shadow Knights.
 This guide is intended to help someone who is considering a Shadow Knight. While it is titled as a
 guide to Human Shadow Knights, most of the information is generic to all Shadow Knights.



1.) Levels 1-9.
	a. Why a human?
		1. Humans gain experiance faster.
		When I started Saethorne, I also had an Ogre SK. Even though the ogre had a 2 lvl advantage Saethorne
		hit level 9 while the ogre was stuck at level 7.

		2. Humans have better faction.
		Saethorne can safely shop and bank in Qeynos and Freeport. I have banked in Steamfont, Kelethin and the High Elf city.
		Its a lot easier to get around if I dont have to worry about guards and vendors killing me.

		3. Humans have well rounded stats.
		While my strength may be low compared to an ogre or troll, its better then a dark elve or erudite.
		My int is lower then a Dark elve or Erudite, but much higher then either a troll or Ogre.
		There are some disadvantages to the human but mostly their attributes are average or better. Its better in my
		opinion to present no real weakness then it is to have a clear advantage in one area at the expense
		of a deficiency in another.

		4. Starting areas
		Both Qeynos and Freeport are near very good zones that will keep you busy untill level 15 or so.


	b. Creation.
	Its important to say that there are no hard and fast rules here. Any combination of skill
	point distribution will make a successful SK.However I have learned a few things from my
	own that may help you.
	Int
 	Human SKs are pretty bright right out of the box, at least as far as melee types go. They arent
 	meant to be casters and dont really need as much int as a pure caster would. Trolls and ogres benefit
 	from spending a lot of points here, but thats because they're stupid in the first place.
 	My recomendation is to leave this as is. or at least only put a few points in this field.
 	If you think 80 points for int is bad, ask a troll shadow knight what he has for int some time.
 	Strength
 	I have found strength to be a problem for my Human, He's at 90 uneqquiped and it makes it tough for him to wear bronze armor
 	and be able to carry any signifigant amount of loot. This problem is eased by the use of the siphon strength spell and some
 	equipment gives strength bonus, so its not really all that bad. It certainly hasnt affected my enjoyment of the charecter.
 	It is simply one more thing to consider as I go about my adventures.
	Dexterity, agility, stamina.
	I consider these to be second line attributes, they will affect how you play and fight. But not as much as you would think.
	Any short comings here are easily rectified by the use of magic equipment down the road.

	c. Strategy.
	Basically at this point in your career, you are a warrior. You have no spells, You wont get bash till level 6, and you
	never get kick. You do get one advantage and that is your harm touch. Ill cover harm touch in more detail later on, for now
	lets just say its a once a day (mostly) unresistable direct damge spell that casts instantly.

	d. Equipment.
	Up to level 5 you shouldnt worry about equipment at all. Save every dime you get. If you loot some cloth armor or a rusty weapon
	use it if you like but its just as good to sell it. A nice rusty two hand sword or axe is nice to have right now.
	The reason I say dont worry about it till level 5 is corpse decay. Your corpse is only good for about 45 minutes after you die.
	If your new to the game or unfamiliar with your zone this is plenty of time to lose it and everything you have bought. If you want
	to be safe, bank your money and buy your gear after level 5.
	If youve been busy, you shoulld have enough money to buy most of a set of leather armor (skip patchwork armor entirely if you can) and
	at least a rusty weapon or two. You may have better, you probably shouldnt have worse if you can manage it.
		1. Begging.
		Since this is a section on equipment I thought it would be a good place to address this issue.
		Your a Knight, the shining embodiment of evil and war. Begging is beneath you. DONT DO IT!
		People hate it. Anyone inclined to give away equipment is not going to give it to the whiner in the zone
		who has been begging all night. People can and do give gear away. If your a decent person to be around, you will eventually find
		someone who is willing to help you out. So dont be a pain in the butt. Its more fun to get it on your own anyway.
	e. Skills.
	You should put one training point into each of your combat skills to get them started. From taht point you should save them untill
	level 6 for bash then level 9 when you finally get to cast your spells
	f. Tips.



2.) Levels 9 - 15.
	a. Spells.
	This only covers the first circle of spells you get at level 9. Combine these spells with your pet, harm touch and tanking
	abilties and you have a very effective killing machine :).
		1. Disease Cloud
		Great spell, Hits for a few points of damage, then blocks the mobs regeneration. I use it
		to start every fight (if I initiate the fight anyway). Its also useful as a taunt.

		2. Invisibility to Undead
		Very handy to have. Helps in traveling or sneakiing around in befallen. It only lasts till you
		cast a spell, or attack somone. If you cast this while you have a pet, your pet will die.

		3. Leering Corpse
		Summons your pet. Requires two bone chips. Pet is the same as a first level necros pet.

		4. Life Tap
		You will use this spell for every fight. This spell takes HP from your target and gives it to you.
		Contrary to popular belief, you cant use this spell in place of a heal. The amount of HP is relatively
		small and anything you could use it on will probably take at least the same amount of hp when it attacks
		you in retaliation. Think of this as a way to stay in the fight longer and ease the burden of whomever
		is healiing for your party.

		5. Locate Corpse
		Very useful spell. Target yourself or another player and cast this spell. It will turn you to point in the
		direction of that players nearest corpse (only if its in the same zone). Some people charge for it. I never have.

		6. Sense the Dead
		Turns you to point in the direction of the nearest undead. Good for finding pet components, plus you can hunt
		bone chips till you have a stack (20) then sell them to other players.

		7. Siphon Strength
		SImilar to lifetap only it steals strength from the mob and gives it to you.Not very useful offensively, but as a
		human SK you will use it constantly to keep from becoming encumbered.

	b. Pets.
	Pets are a considerable advantage to the shadow Knight. They can help you take on mobs that you could never touch alone, they can taunt
	the baddie off you so you can make a getaway and avoid a death. They can also get you killed if you don't use them properly.
	1. Commands
		 /pet stay
		 Causes pet to stay.

		 /pet sit
		 Causes pet to sit. Just like a player a wounded pet will regenerate faster if it sits.

		 /pet stand
		 Causes pet to stand. You cant attack from a sitting position, neither can your pet.

		 /pet follow me
		 Causes pet to follow you, this is the preferred command for traveling with your pet. Pet will be somewhat less aggressive towards
		 other mobs compared to a pet in gaurd mode.

		 /pet guard me
		 Pet will guard you and attack anything that comes into aggro range.

		 /pet guard here
		 Pet will guard a spot with the same results as above. Pet will not move unless following a mob that it has engaged. Very useful for pulling.

		 /pet kill
		 Pet will attack targetted mob.

		 /pet back off
		 Pet will stop attacking mob (if it can).

		 /pet report health
		 Pet will tell you what percentage of its hitpoints it has.



	c. Combat.
	Im going to describe the technique I use in combat solo,with a pet. There are other styles that Im sure are just as effective.
	The first thing you need to do is to set up your hotkeys so that you can control your pet. You arent going to have time
	to type the commands manually.
	Your going to want, at the least, the following commands.You should try to put them all on your main hotkey menu.
		/pet follow me
		/pet kill
		/pet back off
		/pet report health
		/pet no taunt
		/pet taunt
		Your also going to want a few non-pet commands.
		taunt
		bash
		ranged weapon.

	As soon as you have summoned your pet, use the pet taunt key to turn off your pets taunting. The command is a toggle, so
	hitting it again will turn taunting back on.
	There is a very good reason for this, your pet is basically a level 4 pet, while your fighting mobs closer o levels 9 - 15.
	If your pet manages to successfully taunt your opponent, hes not going to last long.

	Now then, your pet is summoned, youve turned taunting off, youve told the pet to FOLLOW you, NOT Guard you. If you have an extra
	weapon, give it to your pet. Nothin valuable. You wont get it back.
	Your all set.
		1. Pick out a nice and juicy mob that cons blue to you.
		2. Get close enough to it to cast on it.
		3. Cast poisen cloud on it. This is a first level spell you should have. It basically hits the mob for a few points
		   of damage and as a bonus it stops the mobs HP regeneration for a time. It also makes the mob really angry at you.
		4. Now hit your auto attack key and run TOWARDS the mob. Run right through him untill you are behind it.
		   Turn around and start whacking it with all you got. At this point the mob will turn around to face you and your pet
		   will also engage the mob from behind.
		5. As long as your fighting, you want to bash and taunt as often as you can. The idea is to keep the mob so mad at you
		   that it doesnt turn around and go after your pet. Bash, taunt and lifetap (another spell) as often as you can untill
		   the mob (or you) goes down. The idea here is that your pet is doing a steady amount of damage to your opponent while
		   the mob is focusing its attention on you.
		6. Keep checking your pets health with the hotkey you made. If you see your pets starting to lose hp, its time to decide what
		   you want to do. If your winning, stick it out. If it doesnt look like your gonna make it. Turn your pets taunting on and
		   stop attacking, no bashing, taunting or casting. When the mob turns on your pet - run for it. This MIGHT give you
		   enough of a head-start to make it to a zone or gaurd. When the mob is done with your pet, it will remember you and come
		   after you with a vengeance. Of course you might try out your harm touch instead of running for it, but thats your call.
		7. Its best not to think of your pet as an extra party member who will help you solo. It can't do that. It doesnt have the
		   hitpoints or the skill to last long on its own, and it certainly cant tank like a necro pet. Think of it more as a long
		   lasting DOT (damage over time) spell.
		8. Following these steps, I usually win most of my fights and have the pet left over to boot. This not only saves money in
		   bone chips and rusty weapons, but the pet gains exp on the kills also.
		9. Heal up, rinse, repeat.




 3. Thats it. Now you know what you need to survive as a Shadow Knight. remember the class doesnt become really effective for a long time.
    You just have to stick it out and play aggressively untill then. The first 9 levels arent much fun, but once you break thru itll be
    worth it.
