  • GENERAL
    • DEFINITIONS
    1. "Antlerless deer" means those deer having no antlers or antlers less than three (3) inches in length.

    2. "Fluorescent Orange" means clothing having a dominant wave length between 595 and 606 nanometers, an excitation purity of not less than 85 percent, and a lumination factor not less than 40 percent.

    3. "Driving Deer" means to pursue, drive, chase, or otherwise frighten or cause a deer to move in a direction toward a person or persons known to be waiting for a deer.

    4. "Paraplegic" means an individual afflicted with paralysis of the lower half of the body with involvement of both legs.

    5. "Bearded Turkey" means male turkey which carries a visible beard (a tuft of stiff filament-like feathers projecting outward and downward from the center of the chest) and exhibits other typical physical characteristics of a male turkey including, but not limited to, bright red, white, and blue head coloration, visible spurs, and black iridescent body coloration.

    6. "Conibear-type Trap" means "conibear" model traps and similar body-gripping traps and devices, whether or not enclosed in or comprising part of a box, tube, or other enclosing device.

    7. "Box Trap" means any trigger operated device intended to restrain an animal by confinement in a box or cage.

    8. "Jacklighting" means the use (by an individual or in conjunction with others) of an artificial light at any time to illuminate, jack light, locate, attempt to locate, or show up wild birds or mammals or any other vertebrates when that person or persons have in their actual possession, in the passenger compartment of their vehicle, or in the storage area of a trunk or van, unless locked in a case, a crossbow, long bow (straight limb, reflex, recurve, and compound bow), rifle, gun or pistol, except in accordance with these rules and regulations.

    9. Editors Note: Some NEW or CHANGED regulations are highlighted in RED.
    1. DEER - GENERAL DEER HUNTING REGULATIONS
      2.
      1. All deer must be tagged in a conspicuous manner by the hunter killing same immediately after taking, with the tag provided, with the permit for shotgun, archery, muzzleloader and/or paraplegic/double amputee hunter deer season. This tag must be separated from the permit, with the appropriate month, day, and time of kill or other information completed as required, and signed by the hunter. The tag must remained attached to the deer until the animal is processed for consumption or taxidermy. It will be considered prima-facie evidence of hunting without a valid deer permit if any portion of the deer permit/tag is altered or separated in any manner.

      2. Except as provided in 2.1, 2.7, 3.2, 4.6, and 6.6 no individual shall purchase more than one deer permit/tag for each deer season -- one each, muzzleloader, shotgun, archery.

      3. Hunting, pursuing, or molesting deer below mean high tidal water, or while deer are swimming in any waters of the state is prohibited.

      4. The construction and/or use of permanent tree stands, or the use of nails, spikes, bolts, or climbing devices which may damage trees, is prohibited on state property; and on private property without the permission of the landowner.

      5. The use of electronic deer calls is prohibited at all times.

      6. The use of natural or artifical scents, mouth, or hand powered deer calls to attract deer is permitted.

      7. The use of decoys to attract deer is prohibited.

      8. Written permission is required to deer hunt on privately owned land (20-15-1 d). Such written permission shall be carried at all times while deer hunting, and failure to have such written permission shall be prima-facie evidence of not having written permission to hunt such land. Written permission must be shown to any authorized person upon demand, and must be presented when checking the deer. (see also 12.1).

      9. Only one firearm or bow may be possessed in the field per individual hunter, while hunting deer.
    2. DEER - MUZZLE-LOADING DEER SEASON
      1. Muzzleloading Season: November 6, 1999 - November 28, 1999
        Permit Required
        1. First muzzleloader deer permit - Statewide, Either Sex
        2. Second muzzleloader deer permit - Either Sex, Private Land (May only be purchased AFTER checking the first 'statewide, either sex' deer)

      The total bag limit during the entire muzzle loading season is two (2) deer.

      Some cities and towns have more restrictive ordinances, check local ordinances prior to hunting. All other deer hunting regulations shall apply (see Part 1)

    3. A current muzzle-loader deer permit is required for the muzzle-loading season. All deer must be conspicuously tagged by the hunter killing same immediately after killing or taking as provided, and taken by that same hunter to an Environmental Police Officer, or other Department designee for checking, within 24 hours.

    4. During the muzzle-loading deer season, all hunters except those hunting raccoon at night, or archers in tree stands, or waterfowl hunters as provided, must wear a minimum of 200 square inches of solid daylight orange material, contained in outer garment and worn above the waist, at least a portion of which must be included in a head covering visible from all directions.

    5. Firearms for muzzle-loading deer season are limited to .45 caliber or larger using percussion caplock, flintlock, or older ignition systems and in-line ignition systems. Only single or double barrel firearms are allowed. All conversion kits which use a replacement barrel to convert a typical modern shotgun into a muzzle-loading rifle are prohibited. See Part 15.10.

    6. Ammunition for muzzle-loading deer season is limited to a single lead projectile, including round ball, or maxi ball type projectiles. Sabot rounds for muzzle loader firearms either lead or jacketed are limited to .43 caliber or larger. Powder is limited to black powder or equivalent substitute. Possession of modern shotgun shotshells while hunting is prohibited. A muzzle-loader is considered unloaded when the percussion cap or pan powder is removed.

    7. The limit for the muzzle-loading deer season shall be one deer per permit as provided under parts 2.1 and 2.7.

    8. After checking as provided in Part 2.2, the muzzle-loading deer hunter will be entitled to purchase an additional deer permit to take a deer on private land only. Only specified vendors will be permitted to check deer. The list of approved vendors will be available prior to the season.

  • DEER - SHOTGUN DEER SEASON
    1. The shotgun season for white-tailed deer shall be:
      • December 4, 1999 though December 12, 1999 inclusive, private and management lands,
      • December 13 - December 17, private land only
      in the Counties of Providence, Kent, and Washington except for New Shoreham (Block Island). Some cities and towns have more restrictive ordinances, check local towns prior to hunting. A current shotgun deer permit/tag is required for the shotgun season.

    2. Shotgun deer hunters may purchase permits to take two deer. The first shotgun deer permit shall allow for the taking of one deer of either sex during either portion of the shotgun deer season. The second shotgun deer permit shall allow for the taking of one either sex deer on private land, during either portion of the shotgun deer season. Permits may be used in any sequence. Second permit must be purchased prior to opening day.

    3. No person shall take a deer with a shotgun, capable of holding more than five shells.

    4. Ammunition permitted for shotgun deer season includes rifled slugs or sabot round. Buckshot limited to double 00 or larger, buckshot prohibited in 20 gauge. Shotguns of 10, 12, 16, or 20 gauge are allowed.

    5. All deer must be conspicuously tagged by the hunter killing same immediately after killing or taking as provided, and taken by that same hunter to a state-operated or sanctioned checking station for checking within 24 hours.

    6. Deer killed or taken on the last day may be checked through a check station no later than 6:00 PM on that date, or by an Environmental Police Officer within 24 hours of taking.

    7. During the shotgun season for deer, all hunters, except waterfowl hunters as provided, must wear a minimum of 500 square inches of solid daylight fluorescent orange material, contained in an outer garment and worn above the waist, a portion of which must include a head covering visible from all directions.

    8. Waterfowl hunting from a boat or a blind, over water or fields, when done in conjunction with decoys, is permitted during the shotgun deer season*. All other hunting, except otherwise provided under 9.2, 9.3, and 11.10, shall be prohibited during that period December 4, 1999 through December 17, 1999.
      *Subject to Federal Waterfowl framework not established at time of filing.
  • DEER - ARCHERY DEER SEASON
    1. The bow and arrow hunting season for white-tailed deer shall be October 1,1999 through January 31, 2000, inclusive, statewide except for New Shoreham (Block Island). On Prudence and Patience Islands in Narragansett Bay, October 30, 1999 through December 28, 1999, Friday, Saturday, Sunday, Monday only. Some cities and towns have more restrictive ordinances, check local towns prior to hunting.

    2. The limit shall be one deer of either sex for the current archery deer permit, except as provided under 4.6

    3. No bow used in hunting deer shall be equipped with any mechanical device for drawing or releasing the arrow (20-15-4). A bow string release device whereby the bow is hand held, hand drawn, and finger released is permitted.

    4. All Broad heads used for deer hunting must be of the non-expandable, fixed cutting edge type. cutting blades must be made out of metal. Broadheads designed to rotate around the axis of the arrow when in flight are permitted. For additional information, see RIGL § 20-15-4.

    5. All deer must be immediately tagged by the hunter after killing or taking as provided, and taken by that same hunter to an Environmental Police Officer, or other Department designee for checking within 24 hours.

    6. After checking as provided, the archer will be entitled to purchase an additional mainland archery deer permit to take a deer of either sex by bow and arrow. Mainland shall mean all areas of Rhode Island open to bow and arrow hunting except Prudence and Patience Islands. Only specified vendors will be permitted to check deer and sell additional permits. The list of approved vendors will be available prior to the season.

    7. All archers when travelling to and from a tree stand, during the deer muzzle-loading season, must wear, above the waist, a minimum of 200 square inches of daylight fluorescent orange.

    Patience and Prudence Islands
    1. Archery season dates shall be October 29, 1999 through December 27, 1999 Friday, Saturday, Sunday, and Monday only, and as provided in 4.1 The only legal method of deer hunting on Prudence and Patience Islands is by archery, except as provided by 5.20 - 5.29 of these regulations.

      • October 29 - November 22 - hunting restricted to deer with antlers 3" or greater.
      • November 26 - December 27 - either sex deer hunting.
        Total season bag limit is one (1) deer.

    3. A Prudence/Patience Island Archery Deer Permit (deer tag) is required to hunt on Prudence and Patience Islands.

    4. While archery deer hunting, no possession of firearms permitted, except during the special paraplegic hunter deer season.

    5. Reservations to hunt management areas are required by lottery. Reservations will be required on the south and north and Barre Units of Prudence for the first three weeks, Friday through Monday. Reservations will be required on Patience Island for the first two weeks, Friday through Monday.

    6. A lottery system will assign hunters on Prudence Island and Patience Island during the reservation period. Lottery applications can only be obtained by mailing a request with a self-addressed stamped envelope (9 1/2 x 4 inch envelope) to:
      DEER LOTTERY,
      Division of Fish and Wildlife,
      PO Box 218,
      West Kingston, RI 02892
      Application dates will be July 15 through August 15, 1999. Two individuals may apply on one application, however, an individual may only apply once in the lottery system. Duplicate, illegible, incorrect, or incomplete forms will be rejected. The lottery reservation is non-transferable and non-negotiable.

    7. On the south and north ends of Prudence Island 30 hunters respectively, per day may be assigned. On Patience Island and the Barre Unit on Prudence Island, 10 hunters respectively, per day may be assigned.

    8. A quota of 60 deer may be taken, of which, no more than 20 deer shall be antlerless. Hunting on the Islands will end when quota is reached on or before December 27.

    9. All deer taken on Prudence Island shall be immediately tagged by the hunter and then checked by that same hunter through the deer check station designated by the Department of Environmental Management and a leg seal must be attached to the deer prior to leaving Prudence Island. The leg seal must remain attached to the deer until processed for consumption.

    10. All deer taken on Patience Island shall be immediately tagged by the hunter and then checked by that same hunter through the deer check station on Prudence Island, or on the mainland by contacting an Environmental Police Officer or other Department designee within 24 hours.

    11. The Division of Fish and Wildlife retains the right to alter the number of hunters, number of days hunted, and total deer harvested within the above season, if conditions warrant.

    12. Proficiency testing is required of all archery hunters, hunting deer on Patience and Prudence, private and state owned lands. The proficiency card is valid two years from the date of issue and must be carried while hunting.

    13. For private lands, written permission of the land owner must be obtained, and as required by local ordinance, must be countersigned by the Chief of Police or his representative on the Island.

    14. The driving of deer while archery hunting or participating in deer driving for archery hunters is prohibited at all times on Patience and Prudence Islands.

    15. On Prudence Island, during the period October 1, 1999 through December 31, 1999, inclusive, the casting of rays of artifical lights, including spotlights, headlights, or other artifical light, at any time, on any highway or roadway, whether public or private, or any field, woodland, residential property, or forest, any building or farm animal, for the purpose of spotting, locating, or attempting to spot or locate any wild animal or wild bird is prohibited.

    16. On Patience Island, there shall be no hunting of any species of wildlife, except archery hunting for deer, as provided. The possession of any firearm at any time on Patience Island, except for archery equipment as prescribed during the established archery season for deer, shall be prima-facie evidence of illegal hunting, (except as provided in 5.20).

    17. Waterfowl hunting Prudence and Patience Islands - - The possession of firearms is permitted while enroute to, or during waterfowl hunting; waterfowl hunters must possess all of the following: A minimum of six (6) decoys, steel shot as specified in Rhode Island or Federal waterfowl regulations, and both the Rhode Island and Federal waterfowl stamps. No ball ammunition may be possessed. Possession of a loaded firearm more than (10) ten feet above mean high water is prohibited, except as provided in (Parts 10.2; 14).

  • WILD TURKEY
    1. There shall be one (1) season for hunting wild turkey: Thursday April 27, to Tuesday May 23, 2000.

    2. One thousand (1,000) permits will be issued. If more than 1,000 applications are received, permits will be issued by a random selection process.

    3. Area open to hunting restricted to: That portion of Providence County west of the Blackstone River, Kent County, Washington County, except New Shoreham (Block Island).

    4. Hunting hours shall be from one half hour before sunrise to 1:00 PM. Eastern Daylight Time, daily, provided that all hunters shall leave the woods no later than 1:00 PM.

    5. The bag and possession limits shall be one (1) bearded male turkey per license year.

    6. Notwithstanding the provisions of 20-13-13, the methods of taking shall be limited to shotguns, gauges 10, 12, 16, and 20 only, with shot sizes 2, 4, 5, 6, and 7 1/2 magnum loads only; and by archery equipment consisting of a long bow (straight limb, reflex, recurve, and compound bow) of not less than forty (40) pounds of pull, and shall use broadhead arrows, with at least two cutting edges seven eighths inches (7/8") or greater at the widest point. A bow string release device where the bow is hand held, hand drawn and finger released is allowed.

    7. The use of any other firearm, live decoy(s), baiting, dog(s), cooperative drive(s), electric calling devices, or any other devices, to capture or ensnare turkeys is prohibited.

    8. The shooting of turkeys from the roost is prohibited.

    9. Stalking or attempting to sneak up on a turkey to get a shot is prohibited.

    10. Hunters must carry a turkey calling device (non-electric) while hunting. Calling is the only legal method for hunting wild turkey gobblers.

    11. A person shall not hunt, kill, take, or attempt to take, or possess any wild turkey unless they possess on their person a permit to do so. Provided, however, that if an individual has a valid Rhode Island hunting license, that individual may call call turkeys for individuals with a valid turkey permit.

    12. A person receiving a turkey permit is required to sign, in ink, on the face of the turkey tag prior to hunting. A person killing a wild turkey shall immediately, before transporting or removing the carcass in any manner from where it was killed, complete and attach to the leg in a conspicuous manner the "turkey tag" provided with the permit following instructions printed on the tag. Said "turkey tag" shall remain attached to the turkey until the turkey is presented to a duly authorized check station for checking; where such tag shall be removed and a metal tag attached. The metal tag must remain attached until the carcass is prepared for consumption or taxidermy. The hunting of wild turkey when not in possession of a valid, signed unused "turkey tag" shall be prima-facie evidence of hunting without a valid turkey hunting permit.

    13. Within the same calender day, but no later than 4:00 PM Eastern Daylight Time, after killing a wild turkey, the entire fully feathered intact carcass of the bird must be presented by the person killing same to a duly authorized check station for the purpose of being recorded, examined, and sealed.

      Open Season - - The open season for raccoon shall be 6:00 PM October 1, 1999 through February 29, 2000, inclusive; excluding the period of December 4, 1999 through December 17, 1999, inclusive, in Providence, Kent and Washington Counties. In the Town of Portsmouth, Prudence Island, season shall be limited to January 15, 2000 through February 29, 2000, inclusive, with written permission of the landowner and special permit issued by the DEM representative on Prudence Island. That portion of Providence County east of the Blackstone River shall remain open to raccoon hunting during the period December 4, 1999 through December 17, 1999. Provided, however, that bow hunters with a valid deer archery permit may take raccoon on Prudence Island during legal deer hunting hours, October 29, 1999 through December 27, 1999. Special permit is not required for bow hunters under this later provision.

      Bag Limits - - There is no season limit on raccoon. There are no limits to the taking of raccoon by bow hunters as provided under 10.2, or under special permit on Prudence and Patience Islands. There is no bag limit on fox.

    3. The trapping season for mink, muskrat, skunk, raccoon, fox, opossum, and weasel shall be November 1, 1999 through January 31, 2000, inclusive. Traps may not be set, staked, or placed prior to 8:00 AM on opening day, November 1, annually.

      1. The trapping season for beaver shall be December 1, 1999 through February 29, 2000, inclusive. Traps may not be set, staked, or placed prior to 8:00 AM on opening day, December 1, 1999.

      2. All trappers participating in beaver trapping must first obtain a Special Permit (no fee) issued by the Division of Fish and Wildlife as well as a current RI Trapping License

      3. Individual trappers may be limited to the number of beaver harvested as established by the Division of Fish and Wildlife.

      4. The number of beaver to be harvested within each watershed shall be determined by the Division of Fish and Wildlife, and the Division reserves the right to alter the number at any time.

      5. Special beaver permits/tags will issued for selected watersheds, or specific sites within watershed. Special permits/tags are valid only for the assigned trap sites.

      6. Harvest reports and.or unused permits shall be returned to the Division of Fish and Wildlife within 14 days after the expiration of the permit; failure to return these records or permits within the specified time period may result in no future permit
      7. All permittees may be subject to administrative penalties of up to $1,000.00 for violation of any of these regulations pursuant to RIGL § 42-17-6.

    4. The use of "conibear" type traps in sizes 110 or 120 (or equivalent, not to exceed 6 1/2" jaw spread) shall be permitted on land or in water; size 220 (or equivalent, not to exceed 8" jaw spread) shall be permitted only if such trap is completely sub
      1. For the taking of beaver, the use of 220 and 330 "Conibear" type traps shall be permitted only if such traps are completely submerged underwater and in a vertical position. Trap jaw spread not to exceed 10 inch opening when set.

      2. Setting traps within 10 feet of a beaver lodge/bank is prohibited, unless allowed under nuisance complaint request.

    5. Permits to trap state land are required, and are obtainable from the Division of Fish and Wildlife Field Headquarters, PO BOX 218, West Kingston, RI, 02892. "Conibear" type traps only - - water and tree sets only, 110 or 120 or equivalent only, except by special permit.

    6. All "box" traps must have the name, address, and license number if applicable, of trapper attached. All steel traps must have the valid license number of the trapper embedded or cut into the trap, or a metal tag attached to the trap which, as a minimum, bears the trapper's license number as designated in 20-16-7.

    7. The taking of fisher or bobcat by any method, is prohibited, except as provided under the Protected Nuisance Furbearer Policy (20-16-2).

      1. All protected furbearer trapping or hunting will be conducted by licensed trappers with special permits only.

      2. Special permits for taking protected furbearers will be issued only by the Department of Environmental Management's Division of Fish and Wildlife, and Division of Enforcement.

      3. Persons with problems will be given the name of trappers and will then make arrangements to trap the nuisance animal. The complainant will then request a special permit to be issued to the selected trapper. All complaints must be checked by a biologist and/or Environmental Police Officer before a special permit will be issued.

      4. All decisions involving protected nuisance furbearers will rest with the DEM Division of Fish and Wildlife and Division of Enforcement.

    8. Possession, at any time, of a pelt or carcass of an otter, fisher, bobcat or beaver that has not been tagged by an approved state or federal agency shall be prima-facie evidence of illegal taking. The Division of Fish and Wildlife may require that the skinned carcass be turned over for analysis. Otter may only be possessed under 20-16-2 and 20-16-8. The pelt or carcass of the above named species must be presented for tagging within 10 days after taking or before selling the pelt or carcass, whichever comes first, except beaver, otter, and bobcat, which must be tagged within 72 hours after taking.

    9. There is no closed season for red squirrel, chipmunk, woodchuck, or coyote (coyote pelts must tagged by Division of Fish and Wildlife prior to sale). During the shotgun season for deer December 4 - 17, 1999, all coyote hunters must possess a valid shotgun deer permit. Legal hunting hours for the above species is sunrise to sunset, see 9.1.
  • REPTILES AND AMPHIBIANS
    1. The removal from the wild, for commercial purposes, of any reptile or amphibian is prohibited except by special permit. The taking of snapping turtles at any time shall be limited to the following methods: turtle traps, snagging, jugging, bow and arrow, or while legally fishing. Turtle traps must have a metal tag attached which, as a minimum, bears the trapper's name and address. Jugs must be marked indelibly with name and address of trapper.

    2. The following species of reptiles and amphibians are protected and possession without permit is prohibited at all times: Timber rattlesnake, Crotalus horridus; northern diamondback terrapin, Malaclemys terrapin; wood turtle, Clemmys insculpta; eastern box turtle, Terrapene carolina; spotted turtle, Clemmys guttata; and bog turtle, Clemmys muhlenbergi.

  • MANAGEMENT AREAS
    1. The possession of valid Rhode Island hunting, or combination hunting and fishing license, shall serve as a permit to hunt State Management Areas and Tidal Lands pursuant to Title 20, Chapter 18, §20-18-2 and 20-18-3 of the General Laws.

    2. Permit shall be valid only from the first day of dove season in September through February 28, except when check stations are in operation for upland game at the Great Swamp, Carolina, Durfee Hill, and Arcadia; for turkey hunting; when special permits are required for deer hunting; or for waterfowl hunting on state lands. (see current abstract for check station dates). Bow hunters and muzzle-loading hunters hunting deer on state management areas do not need to check in or out.

    3. The carrying of firearms on state management areas at any other time is prohibited except as provided in Management Area Regulations.

    4. Individuals must check in, and out of state management hunter check stations each day they are open for upland season. Check station dates will be listed in the current Hunting Season Abstract. Management areas are closed to small game hunting from 1:00 PM to sunset on Wednesday, November 24 and Fridays from October 22, 1999 - November 19, 1999.

    5. Dog training and all hunting, except for bow hunting for deer, and waterfowl hunting, as provided, is prohibited on state land during the period of October 11, 1999 through October 17, 1999, unless otherwise provided.

    6. From the period April 1 through August 15, annually, there shall be no trespassing, disturbing, or molesting of any species of nesting birds on Hope Island in the West Passage; Gould Island in the Sakonnet River, East Island and West Island off Sakonnet Point, or Gould Island in the East Passage, except by special permit. All cats, dogs, and other domestic animals are prohibited at all times upon these islands, except that hunting dogs may be used during that period set aside by the Department for hunting of game birds and mammals on Hope Island only.

    7. All users of state Management Areas, during the period October 16, 1999 through February 29, 2000 (exceptions includes: boat launching sites, raccoon hunters, archers in tree stands, or waterfowl hunters as provided) must wear a minimum of 200 square inches of solid daylight fluorescent orange material in a head covering visible from all directions.

    8. The possession and/or use of paint guns on state management areas is prohibited. See RIGL §32-2-4.

    9. A hunting license is required when training dogs on wild game on state management areas, except when participating in state-sanctioned field trials.

    10. Complete dressing of game birds and mammals on state property is prohibited. Field dressing (removal of entrails only) is permitted. Head, skin, feathers, wings, tails, etc. shall remain attached to the carcass.

    11. Dogs on state management areas allowed only under the following rules:
      1. No dogs shall be trained, run, or exercised or be permitted to run at large on state management areas from March 15 to August 15, inclusive, without written permission of the Division of Fish and Wildlife except as otherwise provided in these regulations.

      2. Walking of dogs on leash of not more than 25 feet in length shall be permitted on roads and trails only.

      3. All dogs must be wearing a collar with the name and address of the owner or handler and license tag.

    12. Dog Training Areas - Dog training shall be allowed on designated areas only, between January 1 and September 30. During this period, dogs may run freely provided they are under the command of the trainer by either voice, training collar, or check cord.
      1. Areas designated for training dogs are the Black Farm Management Area, Nicholas Farm Management Area north of the Trestle Trail, and the Arcadia Midway Trail Area confined to the developed field complex east of Brook Trail, south of Austin Farm Road and west of the established field edges comprising the Midway fields area. Field trial areas are as depicted on maps drawn by the Division of Fish and Wildlife.
      2. Training hours shall be sunrise to sunset.
      3. All persons using areas for training shall possess a hunting license.
      4. All dogs using areas shall be licensed and have proof of rabies vaccinations.
      5. Training with live birds (pheasant/quail) is permitted provided the bird is released unharmed and all necessary permits are obtained.
      6. Shooting of live birds for training purposes is prohibited.

      7. Sanctioned field trial events may be held at the following locations by requesting a special permit from the Division of Fish and Wildlife: Arcadia, Carolina, Durfee Hill Marsh, and Burlingame (Durfee) Pond, and Nicholas Farm.
    13. John L. Curran State Park/Management Area
      1. All hunting shall be within the inner boundaries of the buffer zones marked as posted by the Department.

      2. Only shotguns and bows and arrows may be possessed or used while hunting.

      3. The hunting of and/or possession of Mourning Dove or Wild Pigeons (Rock Dove) is prohibited.

    14. SIMMONS MILL POND MANAGEMENT AREA - Only archery equipment, bow and arrows, may be possessed or used while hunting white-tailed deer within the Simmons Mill Pond Management Area, Little Compton.

  • DEER DAMAGE
    1. Application - - To obtain a deer damage permit in accordance with RIGL §20-15-3 the landowner shall make a request in writing for a deer damage permit application to:
      Deer Damage Permit,
      RI Division of Fish and Wildlife (DFW),
      Oliver Stedman Government Center
      4808 Tower Hill Road,
      Wakefield, RI 02879

    2. Applicant will provide:
      1. Name, address, telephone number, date of birth, and hunting license number;
      2. Location of property, plat and lot number maps;
      3. A list of employees who may take deer per specific property including their hunting license numbers, addresses, and dates of birth.
      4. Safety zone permission signatures, countersigned as required, see 14.5 and 14.6
    3. The deer damage must be verified by a DFW biologist. If deer damage is verified and all applicable regulations are met, a permit will be issued upon receipt of a $50 permit fee per landowner/per parcel as set forth in RIGL §20-1-22. No refunds will be made.

    4. The applicant shall demonstrate to the satisfaction of the DFW the use of deer damage deterrents and that no practical alternative to shooting the deer is available, as set forth in RIGL 20-15-3.

    5. The property must qualify for safe hunting under RIGL §20-13-7. Written permission to hunt within 500 feet of occupied buildings must be obtained and provided with application prior to issuance of a permit. If required by a town or city governing authority all permits shall be countersigned by the local chief of police or designee before permits are valid for use.

    6. The permittee must comply with all local, town, and city laws and ordinances as they relate to hunting and firearms.

    7. Any town from which a deer damage permit is requested shall be open to a regulated deer hunting season, and such season shall be sufficient in length and type to serve as a deterrent to property damage caused by deer.

    8. Landowners will issued a permit to take a specified number of deer as determined by the DFW. Permittee or subpermittee shall notify the Department, or its official designee, within four (4) hours of the killing of any deer. On the mainland reports will be called to the Division of Enforcement (222-3070), permittees shall give their name, address, and permit numbers. On Block Island, deer must be tagged at the designated check station. As soon as available, an Environmental Police Officer or an official designee, shall inspect and tag the deer with a metal seal.

    9. The DFW reserves the right to limit the number of employees that may be issued subpermits under the landowner's deer damage permit. No more than five (5) persons may hunt in unison as specified in RIGL § 20-13-11.

    10. Harvest records and/or unused permits and subpermits shall be returned to the DFW within fourteen (14) days after the expiration date of the permit; failure to return these records or permits within the specified time period will result in no future permits being issued to that permittee or subpermittee.

    11. Permits will be issued for up to 365 days. The number of days for which the permit shall be issued will be based upon a DFW biologist's recommendation. The DFW retains the right to alter, at any time: the number of hunters/permit holders; the number of and specific days on which hunting may occur; and the number of deer that may be taken by any permittee or subpermittee in any town or any landowner's property.

    12. Reissuance of permits for perennial vegetation may be permitted without reinspection at the discretion of the Division of Fish and Wildlife.

    13. All permittees, while hunting, must possess a valid Deer Damage Permit for that specific property and a valid RI Hunting license.

    14. Damage permits will not be issued during legal deer seasons except as otherwise determined necessary by the DFW.

    15. No permit shall be issued to anyone whose rights to hunt in the State of Rhode Island have been revoked.

    16. All deer hunting regulations promulgated under RIGL Title 20 shall apply.

    17. All permittees and subpermittees may be subject to administrative penalties of up to $1,000.00 for violation of any of these regulations pursuant to RIGL § 42-17-6.

    18. In the event that deer damage is particularly severe on listed farms larger than five (5) acres, with annual gross income exceeding $2,500, with the exception of start-up operations at the discretion of the director or designee, and when all other reasonable means of controlling crop damage, including current daylight damage permits and hunting during the regulated season have been ineffective, a DEM designee may recommend the issuance of a jacklight permit with the approval by the Chief of the Division of Fish and Wildlife or his/her designee. Exemptions for smaller parcels may be granted if safety considerations permit.

    19. Any request for jacklight permit must initiate through the Division of Fish and Wildlife. Applicants must complete a Jacklighting Deer Damage Permit Application, to include level of effort to alleviate the problem. The Division of Enforcement will be forwarded a copy of the permit upon approval.

    20. All applicants will be checked against a current hunting violation conviction list by the Division of Enforcement. Permits will not be issued to any person who has been convicted for any hunting violation under RIGL Title 20, within three years preceding the date of application. Permits may be revoked by the Division of Fish and Wildlife at any time for violation of any hunting provision under RIGL Title 20.

    21. The number of jacklighting permits issued at any one time to a single applicant cannot exceed two (one permit to the landowner or primary lessee or spouse or lineal descendent of such, and one permit to designated employee). Permits are valid for fourteen (14) days. Extensions for additional 14-day periods may be granted during the permitting year upon review of existing application by the Division.

    22. Jacklighting permits may be issued after the closing date of the the shotgun deer season until April 15, and from September 1 to the start of the muzzle loading deer season. Exemptions may be granted for severe damage to annual or early-harvest crops upon approval. Kill records and/or unused permits and subpermits shall be returned to the Division of Fish and Wildlife within fourteen (14) days from the expiration of the permit; failure to return these records or permits within the specified time period will result in no future permits being issued to that permittee or subpermittee.

    23. The permittee is required and shall be instructed to notify the Division of Enforcement and the local Police Department immediately prior to use of the permit, indicating their name, permit number, location, and time during which shooting is to take place. This requirement will be printed on the permit and becomes a condition for validating and authorizing its use. Permittee must additionally maintain a record of the jacklighting activity, to include time, and made available for inspection by Environmental Police Officers. Firearms shall be limited to shotgun only pursuant to Section 3.4 in the deer shotgun regulations. Method shall be limited to illumination by artifical light, for the purpose of safety.

    24. Permittee or subpermittee shall notify the Division of Enforcement (1-800-498-1336) within four (4) hours of the killing of any deer. A limit of two (2) deer may be taken per night. Permittees shall give their name, address, and permit number, and as soon as available, an Environmental Police Officer or designee shall inspect and tag the the deer with a metal seal. All deer taken under this permit shall be turned over to the Environmental Police Officer or designee for dispersal within the DEM's approved surplus deer program. The Director or designee may determine that other use of deer may occur if the surplus deer program cannot utilize the deer harvested in this section.

    25. Liability of any and all damage or injury incurred while exercising use of this permit is the responsibility of the permit holder.

    26. As a condition of the permit, the owner of the farm must permit hunting during the regulated season, allowing sufficient numbers of hunters to abate the deer damage problem, consistent with the size of the property and any safe constraints. Proof of hunting activity is the responsibility of the landowner.

    27. All permittees and subpermittees may be subject to administrative penalties of up to $1,000.00 for violation of any of these regulations pursuant to RIGL Chapter 42-17-6.

  • GENERAL
    1. Taking of moose or bear by any method is prohibited except as provided under special permit issued by the Director of the Department of Environmental Management.

    2. The importation, exportation, possession, liberation, or sale, offer for sale, barter, or offer to barter of mute swans and their eggs is prohibited, except by special permit issued by the Director of the Department of Environmental Management or his designee.

    3. There is no closed season for monk parakeets or rock doves (wild pigeons).

    4. During the period October 16, 1999 through February 29, 2000, all hunters, unless bow hunting for deer, hunting raccoon or fox at night, or waterfowl hunting from a blind or boat, must wear a minimum of 200 square inches of solid daylight fluorescent orange material, contained in outer garments and worn above the waist, and visible in all directions. This may be a hat and/or vest. (see exceptions: muzzle-loading deer season - Part 2.3; Shotgun deer season - Part 3.7).

    5. All dogs utilized in hunting activities must be wearing a collar with a valid license tag, and the name and address of the owner or handler thereon.

    6. Waterfowl hunting is permitted no more than 10 feet above mean high water on Prudence Island, except in that period from November 1 to November 30, and during the period October 12 to October 15 by paraplegic and double amputee deer hunters with special permit, and as otherwise provided.

    7. Shooting at any animal at any time, while such animal is on a telephone line, pole, or tower, or electric line, pole, or tower, or any communication device is prohibited.

    8. A muzzle-loading rifle, not to exceed .40 calibre, with round ball and patch only, may be used to small mammals during the established seasons for these species. The use of solid ball in a muzzle-loading shotgun is prohibited at all times, except when deer hunting as provided.

    9. The use or possession of laser sights or night vision equipment while hunting is prohibited.

    10. No person shall import or possess gamebirds for the purpose of liberation or stocking of said gamebirds into the state of Rhode Island without first having received a permit to do so from the Division of Fish and Wildlife. Persons holding a commercial game license or shooting preserve license may liberate gamebirds or mammals in connection with field trials and shooting preserves. See RIGL 20-2-16.1, 20-2-29, and 20-17-1.
