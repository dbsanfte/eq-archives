DIVISION OF FISH AND WILDLIFE
RHODE ISLAND FISHING REGULATIONS FOR THE 1999 - 2000 SEASON
 RULE 1 PURPOSE
 The purpose of these rules is to regulate freshwater fishing seasons and bag limits annually.

RULE 2 AUTHORITY
 These rule and regulations are promulgated pursuant to Chapter 42-17.1, Environmental Management, and Sections 20-1-12; 20-1-13 in accordance with Chapter 42-35, Administrative procedures, of Rhode Island General Laws of 1956, as amended.

RULE 3 APPLICATION
 The terms and provisions of these Rules and Regulations shall be liberally construed to permit the Department to effectuate the purpose of state laws, goals and policies.

RULE 4 SEVERABILITY
 If any provision of these Rules and Regulations, or the application thereof to any person or circumstances, is held invalid by a court of competent jurisdiction, the validity of the remainder of the Rules and Regulations shall not be affected thereby.

RULE 5 SUPERSEDED RULES AND REGULATIONS
 On the effective date of these Rules and Regulations, all previous Rules and Regulations, and any policies regarding the administration and enforcement of Chapter 42-35 and Sections 20-1-12, and 20-1-13 shall be superseded. However any enforcement action taken by, or application submitted to the Department prior to the effective date of these Rules and Regulations shall be governed by the Rules and Regulations in effect at the time the enforcement action was taken, or application filed.

RULE 6 REGULATIONS As follows:

PART I

FRESHWATER FISHERIES REGULATIONS

  1. The open season on all species of trout, salmon, and charr shall close on 28 February 1999, at midnight and shall commence at 0600 hrs. on 10 April 1999. From 10 April through 30 November 1999, the daily creel and possession limits for trout shall be six (6) fish; and from 1 December 1999, through 29 February 2000, the daily creel and possession limits for trout shall be two (2) fish. All streams and designated trout ponds shall be closed to all fishing from 28 February 1999, at midnight to 0600 hrs. on 10 April 1999, unless otherwise designated. Possession of trout, salmon, or charr during the closure shall be prohibited.

    Free Fishing Days: A freshwater fishing license shall not be required by either residents or non-residents on the 5th and 6th of June 1999.

  2. The daily creel and possession limits for northern pike shall be two (2) fish, and the minimum size limit shall be twenty-four (24) inches (measured from the tip of the snout to the end of the tail). The daily creel and possession limits for chain pickerel shall be five (5) fish, and the minimum size limit for chain pickerel shall be fourteen (14) inches (measured from the tip of the snout to the end of the tail). No person shall possess any northern pike less than 24 inches in total length or chain pickerel less than 14 inches in total length.

  3. The daily creel and possession limits for black bass (largemouth and smallmouth bass) shall be five (5) fish, either singularly or in aggregate, and the minimum size limit shall be twelve (12) inches (measured from the tip of the snout to the end of the tail). No person shall possess any black bass less than 12 inches in total length.

  4. The creel and possession limit for domestic or landlocked Atlantic salmon (Salmo salar) in the fresh waters of Rhode Island shall be two (2) fish per day and shall be included in the daily limit for trout, salmon, or charr. The minimum size for Atlantic salmon shall be fifteen (15) inches total length. No person shall take any Atlantic salmon from the Pawcatuck River downstream of the Potter Hill dam.

  5. The minimum size limit for American eel Anguilla rostrata shall be six (6) inches (measured from the tip of the snout to the end of the tail).

  6. The creel and possession limits for trout, or charr taken in the Wood River between Route 165 and Arcadia Road from 15 May 1999, through --29 February 2000, shall be two (2) fish.

  7. The following waters shall be restricted to the use of artificial flies, a conventional fly rod, and a single-action reel: Deep Pond, Exeter; Mowry Meadow Pond, Glocester; A. L. Mowry Pond, Smithfield; and Upper Rochambeau Pond, Lincoln.

  8. The following waters shall be restricted to fishing by persons fourteen years of age and younger: Frosty Hollow Pond, Exeter; Geneva Pond & Brook, North Providence; Lapham Pond, Burrillville; Sidel's Pond, Cranston; Silvy's Pond, Cumberland; Biscuit City Pond, West Kingston; Lloyd Kenney Pond, Hopkinton; and Cass Pond, Woonsocket, and Slater Park Pond, Pawtucket. (Cass Pond and Slater Park Pond shall be restricted only for the first two days of the season, nd 12 April 1998 10 and 11 April, 1999).

  9. The following activities shall be PROHIBITED:
    a. Using corn for bait in any designated trout waters.
    b. Chumming in any designated trout waters.
    c. Snagging fish in any fresh waters.
    d. Tagging, branding, marking, or otherwise willfully mutilating any fish which is to be released alive into any fresh waters of the state except by special permit issued by the Division.

  10. With the exception of designated catch-and-release areas, any legally-caught trout salmon, or charr taken from the fresh waters of Rhode Island shall be immediately released alive into the waters from which it was taken or shall be killed at once and become part of the daily bag limit.

  11. Beach Pond, Exeter shall have the following special regulations: The season for trout shall open on 17 April 1999, at 0600 hrs. and close at midnight on 29 February 2000. The daily creel and possession limits for trout (all species in aggregate) shall be five (5) fish. The daily creel and possession limits for black bass shall be five (5) fish, and the minimum size limit shall be twelve (12) inches (measured from the tip of the snout to the end of the tail). The daily creel and possession limits for chain pickerel shall be five (5) fish, and the minimum size limit shall be fourteen (14) inches (measured from the tip of the snout to the end of the tail). Six (6) lines with a single hook each may be used while ice fishing.

  12. Killingly Pond and Hazard Pond shall have the following special regulations: The daily creel and possession limits for black bass shall be five (5) fish, and the minimum size limit shall be twelve (12) inches (measured from the tip of the snout to the end of the tail). The daily creel and possession limits for chain pickerel shall be five (5) fish, and the minimum size limit shall be fourteen (14) inches (measured from the tip of the snout to the end of the tail). Six (6) lines with a single hook each may be used while ice fishing.

  13. That portion of the Falls River from the bridge at Austin Farm Road downstream approximately two miles to the bridge off Brook Trail shall be designated as a "no-kill" or "catch-and-release" area. Fishing shall be permitted with artificial lures equipped with a single barbless hook or single barbed hook which has been crimped only, and all fish caught shall be returned to the water immediately. The possession of any trout, salmon, or charr while fishing in this section of the river shall be prima facie evidence that said trout, salmon, or charr was taken in violation of this regulation.

PART II

ANADROMOUS FISHERIES REGULATIONS

  1. No person shall take more than twenty-four (24) alewives (Alosa pseudoharengus) or blueback herring Alosa aestivalis either singularly or in aggregate from the fresh-water streams or rivers of the state in any 24-hour period. No person shall take any alewives or blueback herring from the fresh waters of the state without a valid freshwater fishing license. No person shall take any alewives or blueback herring from the fresh waters of the state on any Sunday, Monday, or Tuesday of any week.

  2. No person shall take any alewives or blueback herring by any means other than by a hand-held and hand-operated dip net with a diameter no greater than two and one-half feet (2 1/2') or by a seine not greater than four feet (4') deep by eight feet (8') long.

  3. No person shall erect any artificial obstruction to fish passage in any stream or in any way alter the natural stream bottom to hinder the passage of fish.

  4. No person shall place a seine or netting more than half way across any stream or mouth or outflow of any stream.

  5. Any person taking an alewife or blueback herring must remove it from the pond or stream and keep it in his or her possession.

  6. No person shall take any American shad (Alosa sapidissima) from the fresh waters of the state.

  7. No person shall take any smelt (Osmerus mordax) by any means from any stream or river in the state with the exception of the Pawcatuck River.

  8. Fish Ladders
    1. Trespass within or obstruction of the entrance or exit of any fish ladder in the state is prohibited.
    2. For the purposes of regulating anadromous fishes, all fish ladders owned and operated by the state will be set aside as fish cultivation facilities pursuant to chapters 20-12-1 and 20-12-5 of the General Laws of Rhode Island.
    3. The areas below each fish ladder where fishing will be permitted shall be designated with an official boundary marker or informational sign.
