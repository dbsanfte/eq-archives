Becoming an Outdoors-Woman coming to Rhode Island

Ladies! You say you have always wanted to try backpacking (or shotgunning or fly fishing), but never had the opportunity to learn?

Your husband (boyfriend, brothers, father), you explain, just might not be the ideal teacher (remember learning to drive from Dad?).

You are not alone. Research by Dr. Christine Thomas, Associate Dean of the College of Natural Resources at the University of Wisconsin-Stevens Point, into barriers to women in outdoors pursuits found that many more women would take up outdoor recreation if they had quality instructors that taught in a non-threatening manner on a beginning level.

And that is why Dr. Thomas started Becoming an Outdoors-Woman in 1990.

Since its inception, the non-profit, educational organization has conducted outdoor education seminars for women in 46 states and nine Canadian provinces and now reaches some 20,000 women a year. Anyone 18 or over is invited to learn such outdoor pursuits as canoeing, fishing, fly fishing, archery, cooking wild game, camping, shooting, backpacking, turkey hunting, map and compass, birding, outdoor survival, pursuit of white-tails and more.

And now, for the first time, Becoming an Outdoors-Woman is coming to Rhode Island under the co-sponsorship of the Rhode Island Department of Environmental Management's Division of Fish and Wildlife.

Grab a friend or Mom or sister or all of the above and head to the University of Rhode Island's W. Alton Jones Campus in West Greenwich June 1-3 for a weekend of fun and relaxation. Register for four of your favorite three-hour outdoor sessions, sleep in heated cabins with indoor plumbing, eat in the dining hall and enjoy a campfire in the evening.

But hurry. Reservations are open on a first-come, first-served basis. The weekend fee is $200. Phone Jan McDowell, coordinator, at (973) 948-4001 or e-mail to: jmcdowell_owa@yahoo.com for information and registration forms.
