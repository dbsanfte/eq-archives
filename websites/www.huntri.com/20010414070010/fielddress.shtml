Field Dressing Whitetail Deer

There are several schools of thought on the dressing of deer. It is a simple task that is not too difficult and must be done. If done correctly, you will enjoy excellent table fare and the satisfaction of having seen to the meat yourself. Field Dressing or "Paunching", is the minimum that should be done in the field. "Hog Dressing" which includes the removal of the heart and lungs of the deer, can be done; but doing so leaves a larger opening in the deer and increases the chance of dirt or bacteria entering the body cavity while transporting the carcass back to your vehicle and the check station.

Before beginning, you're deer tag must be properly attached to the animal.

Some hunters have been told that they must "bleed" the animal after it's death. This is absolutely false. This is not a domestic animal and is not killed in the same way a domestic animal is, so there is absolutely no reason to cut the animal's throat at this point in time.

Try to find a spot where you can position the deer laying on its back, with the animal's head and chest is raised slightly.

1. Starting with the anus, the intestine lies inside a channel within the pelvis and this must be removed. First cut a circle around the anus, skin deep. Once this is done, pinch the end and cut deeper into the channel, keeping your knife against the inside walls of the channel. You are freeing up the intestine for later removal. A good, long but not too thick-bladed knife works best here. Once you have cut all round the intestine as far as you can go, leave it for now and go on to step 2.

2. The sexual organs, for a male deer, grab them close to the body and cut them off. What is left is a small circle of flesh showing. For a female, locate the udder and cut around it removing the udder. Here too you will be left with a small circle of exposed flesh just bigger than the udder was.

3. At the top of the circle of exposed flesh from the removal of the genitals, run your fingers up under the hide and place the blade of your knife (Hook-bladed knifes such as the Wyoming knife work quite well here) between your index and middle fingers. Cut straight up to the sternum, guiding the blade with your fingers and taking care not to puncture the paunch. Once the hide is split to the sternum, there is a membrane and a layer of stomach muscles to cut through.

4. Return to the genital area and use a drawing motion with just the tip of your blade and cut through the membrane. With a sharp knife, this is easy to make about a 2 inch cut, just enough to slip two fingers inside and now guide your knife the same way you did cutting the hide, all the way to the sternum. The layer of stomach muscles are now the only thing between your knife and the paunch and great care must be taken to ensure you don't puncture the paunch. Repeat the same procedure as when making the membrane cut. Make sure your fingers keep the blade away from the paunch as you make your cut up to the sternum. The organs in the paunch will start to roll out now, but it is easier if you try to keep them in the body cavity a little while longer.

5. Now cut the remaining membrane and stomach muscles in the lower part of the circle to the pelvis, so you should have it all open from the pelvis to the sternum.

6. Pull out as much of the paunch as possible. Find the diaphragm that separates the heart and lungs from the paunch. If you reach around behind the stomach, in-between the diaphragm and stomach, you can locate where the esophagus enters the stomach. This can be very carefully cut here and any remaining membranes holding the paunch in from under the spine. The paunch will now roll out.

6a. If you wish to Hog dress, instead of cutting the esophagus where it enters the stomach in step 6, cut out the diaphragm and reach up inside cutting off the esophagus as high up as you can. Then remove the heart and lungs at the same time as the paunch.

7. Moving to the still-attached intestines and bladder, locate where the intestines and bladder enter the pelvis channel from inside. It is not necessary to split the hams to remove the intestine (this only exposes more meat, that must be trimmed and disposed of later), but that may be easier for those new to this procedure. A pair of forceps can be used to pinch off the bladder and keep urine from leaking. You may just pinch it and cut it off, or remove it with the intestine at the same time.

8. Work your blade in the same way that you did around the anus, only from the inside, freeing the intestine from the surrounding connective tissue. Cut and pull until the intestine and bladder come lose and can be pulled out.

9. Now roll the animal over onto its stomach and let any blood drain out.

After checking the deer as provided by law, hang the animal up in a suitable place. We hang our deer by the back legs, and split the chest open on any animal that will not be mounted. Cut down the sternum with a good sturdy knife or a meat saw. You may also continue the cut to the lower jaw and remove the rest of the esophagus. Once done, place a splitter in between the ribs to open up the chest and help cooling.

Tarsal glands do not need to be removed from the animal like some people like to believe. If you wish to use them for future hunts as a lure then by all means cut them off and save them; but take care not to touch them with the knife you'll use while processing your meat, or they will taint it.
