Jesus Wasn't A Vegetarian ©
by Keith D. Bailey

As a member of HUNTRI who is also a Christian, several people, including Mr. Quinn, have asked me to voice my opinion on the recent "Jesus is a Vegetarian" campaign which is currently being run by an infamous animal rights group.

I have been extremely reluctant to do so, as this group is not interested in serious debate or defense of this position, but only in scandalous publicity. This group has a poor track record in the area of actually helping animals, but excels in controversy. For instance, Ducks Unlimited has made huge inroads into the preservation of waterfowl and related habitats, yet gets little coverage in the press due to it's pro-hunting attitudes.

This group (which I refuse to acknowledge by naming) of animal rights extremists however, has become the darling of the public due to it's promotion by Hollywood and the media through headline grabbing campaigns such as posters of nude starlets, and telling college students that they should drink beer instead of milk because they believe dairy cows are mistreated. Clearly they realize that this last statement is pure irresponsible folly, but of course their goal was to get the name of their organization back into the forefront of the news, a goal they achieved in spades.

Now they have resorted to out and out blasphemy. A strong word to be sure, but it fits the situation. The flippant use of the son of God is indicative of the fact that many today consider Jesus to be a leftover icon from the sixties, a flower pushing hippie peacenik, eating wheat germ and organic honey, ignoring the fact that millions of good people consider him to be the savior of mankind (this writer included).

The willingness to place the worth of animal lives over and above the lives of their brother humans reflects the almost paganistic lifestyle led by many today. The "New Age" beliefs of the modern world promote "just being good" and that's all you have to do. "Being good" today, means being liberal. I won't mention other attitudes for the sake of this article, but hunting and even the eating of meat are definitely not considered good karma by these people, and they have the cooperation of the media to promote these ideas. Now in the march to political correctness, they have actually twisted the Gospel to fit their philosophy. In this they have done a dangerous thing because the word of God is alone sufficient to defeat these outrageous lies and is right there in the Bible for any who choose to look.

First and foremost, there is nothing in the Bible to show Jesus was a vegetarian. Nothing.

Secondly, Jesus chose 12 disciples over all mankind to preach his Gospel and 5 of them, Simon Peter, Andrew, James, John, and Philip, were fisherman.

Furthermore, one of Jesus' most public miracles was the feeding of 5000 followers, using 5 barley loaves and 2 fish. Not the act of a vegan.

The main thrust of the Jesus is a Vegetarian campaign would be valid only if animals were on the same spiritual plane as human beings. Despite the beliefs of the liberal left this is just not so.

Genesis 1:26: Then God said, "And now we will make human beings; they will be like us and resemble us. They will have power over the fish, the birds, and all animals, domestic and wild, large and small." So God created human beings, making them to be like himself. He created them male and female, blessed them and said, "Have many children, so that your descendants will live all over the earth and bring it under their control. I am putting you in charge of the fish, the birds, and all the wild animals. I have provided all kinds of grain and all kinds of fruit for you to eat; but for all the wild animals and for all the birds I have provided grass and leafy plants for food." and it was done.

The point has been made, and it is valid, that in Paradise (Eden) there was no death and therefore no eating of animals by Adam and Eve, or by the animals themselves. The painful truth is, that we are no longer living in Paradise, and after the fall of mankind into sin, God himself killed the first animals.

Genesis 3:21: And the Lord made clothes out of animal skins for Adam and his wife, and he clothed them.

God had decreed that the wages of sin were death, and after the fall from grace man was instructed to sacrifice animals to cover his sins, animal life to substitute for the human in order to cover the debt that was owed. Jesus Christ was offered up as the perfect sacrifice 2000 years ago, negating the need for further sacrifices. The animal rights crowd should be thankful to Jesus for the sparing of millions of lambs, bulls and doves, if for nothing else.

So what does the Bible say about the eating of meat?

In Genesis 9:2-3, God tells Noah that all the animals are good to eat.

In Leviticus chapter 11, we are given the Jewish food laws, which decreed restrictions on the types of animals to eat and the methods of preparation. Nowhere does the law ban the eating of all meat.

One well known Biblical character that happened to be a vegetarian was Daniel. Daniel became a vegetarian while under the rule of King Nebuchadnezzar, but only to keep from violating the law of God. Meat for the royal Babylonian court, of which Daniel was a member, came from animals sacrificed to pagan gods, and as a Jew he was forbidden from eating such foods. The faithful Israelite and his three companions solved this dilemma by simply requesting raw vegetables to eat. This was done to obey the word of God, not out of some misplaced belief in animal rights.

Going back too far? The Old Testament not relevant in today's world, you say? Let's look to the New Testament and see what it says about the eating of animals. It has already been mentioned that Jesus ate fish, fed fish to thousands of people and picked 5 fishermen to be his apostles, but a most interesting event is found in Acts 10.

The Apostle Peter was the guest of a tanner while evangelizing in Joppa. While praying at noontime, Peter receives a strange vision from God. He sees what we would today call a large picnic blanket. On this blanket are all the animals that are not kosher. God speaks to him saying, "Get up Peter, kill and eat." Shocked, Peter replies, "Certainly not, Lord! For I have never eaten anything ritually unclean or defiled." God then says, "Do not call anything unclean that I have declared clean". While Paul was contemplating the significance of this visitation three servants of the Roman Officer Cornelius arrived. Cornelius had been praying to God, asking how he could find salvation. He received a vision of his own telling him to seek out Peter. Normally Peter, a faithful Jew (yes, the Apostles still considered themselves Jewish while following Christ), would have had nothing to do with a Roman because of the unclean foods they ate. Reassured by his vision, Peter teaches the word of Christ to Cornelius and later to other Gentiles (non Hebrews).

The most important message of this passage is that the Gospel is for all peoples, but interestingly it also says many things about the eating of meat.

Firstly, Peter is a fisherman.

Secondly, he is staying with a tanner in Joppa. A tanner processes animal hides into furs and leather.

Thirdly, Peter receives a message from God, that not only are some animals all right to eat, but now all of them are.

After reading this chapter alone it should be evident that we are not required, or even encouraged to be vegetarians. The eating of meat is covered by what are referred to as "Christian Liberties". Issues that are a matter of choice in your following of Christ. Today's terminology would call these things "non-issues". There were all sorts of rituals and traditions being practiced at the time of the Apostles by other religions. Some were benign, like vegetarianism, others were intolerable, such as sex with temple prostitutes. Because of the crossing of huge cultural borders by the spread of the Word, new converts to Christianity had many questions, and problems of tolerance, that came up often. Ones much like Peter faced with Cornelius.

The Apostle Paul's quote from Romans 14:13: " So then, let us stop judging one another. Instead, you should decide never to do anything that would make your brother stumble or fall into sin. My union with the Lord Jesus makes me certain that no food is of itself ritually unclean; but if a person believes that some food is unclean, then it becomes unclean for him. If you hurt your brother because of something you eat, then you are no longer acting from love. Do not let the food that you eat ruin the person for whom Christ died! Do not let what you regard as good get a bad name. For God's Kingdom is not a matter of eat and drinking, but of righteousness, peace and joy which the Holy Spirit gives."

We can see that vegetarianism is not the issue here, but to not so culturally offend the person/ persons, you are preaching to that they leave the church. If having pork chops for your dinner is something you do, then fine, nothing prevents you from doing so. However, if you are eating with someone who would be offended by pork, do not eat it in their presence.

Romans 14:20: All foods maybe eaten, but it is wrong to eat anything that will cause someone else to fall into sin. The right thing to do then is to keep from eating meat, drinking wine, or doing anything else that will make your brother fall. Keep what you believe about this matter, then, between yourself and God. Happy is the person who does something he judges right.

The smaller cultural differences were considered so trivial compared to the importance of fellowship that the answer given was not to argue and debate them, but to just simply not do them while in their presence. Jesus and the Apostles did not come to us in person and then in print to speak of the virtues of vegetarianism, but of God.

What else does the Bible say about animals? Is it all just about sacrifices and meat? Not at all.

In Deuteronomy 25:4, Moses states that it is cruel to muzzle an ox to keep it from eating some of the grain while using it to tread on the wheat, separating it from the chaff.

In Numbers 22:22, Balaam is whipping a donkey because it will not travel down a path. He doesn't know that an angel is blocking the way because he is disobeying God and traveling in another direction from the one he was told to. Instead of simply making himself visible to Balaam and telling him to turn around, the angel speaks through the donkey and asks why he is beating him after such faithful service?

Exodus 23:4 says to show kindness to a donkey that has fallen under its load. "Don't pass it by, help it to its feet."

A man's character is reflected in his actions. Cruelty to animals reflects an inner sickness of a person's soul. Anti-hunters would quickly jump on this and say that hunting and eating of animals would be the ultimate in cruelty, but it's just not so. Skilled hunters are praised in the Bible, starting with Nimrod the "Mighty Hunter" in Genesis 10:8-9. No self-respecting hunter would purposefully cause an animal unnecessary pain with a bad shot, either with a gun or bow. Hunters I know also follow the trail of a wounded animal for hours, and are distressed if the animal gets away and suffers needlessly.

Jesus was not a vegetarian, and even if he were it would have no impact on how a Christian should live today. Christ did not cut his hair. No group today professes that uncut hair is necessary to be a good Christian. He was never married, but marriage is not a sin by any means. To concentrate on one facet of Jesus Christ's life just to further promote a personal cause is a dangerous practice. Surely these people feel safe using of Jesus because, to them, it must be like using Paul Bunyan or some other fairy tale character. I wouldn't want to be in their shoes come judgment day.

About the author:

Keith Bailey is a sometime contributor to HUNTRI and is also its alternate delegate at the Federated Rhode Island Sportsman's Clubs. Currently employed as a welder in North Kingstown, he is a longtime Christian and currently attends two Baptist churches in Rhode Island. Banzai (an old nickname from high school) was also a vegetarian for 11 years. Throughout that time Bailey never promoted the idea that eating of meat was wrong, stating that it was purely a personal choice. His wife and later, his son, never joined him in his eating habits and were never asked to. Some years ago he was invited on a hunting trip, at which time he chose to give up the veggie life after much thought. An avid shooter and sometime hunter, he hasn't looked back since.
