Ahhh warcraft 3. The sequel to the game that convinced me Blizzard was the KING of video game companies. Better believe I was hyped for this particular title. To the left, is the beta CD itself, scanned, for your viewing pleasure. A LOVELY orc graces it's cover.... Vaguely reminiscent of apes from certain Charlton Heston movies - no? But in all seriousness, installing this game seemed to take an eternity. (Only because I was so thrilled that I would be playing what will be the highest selling game in computer history). As soon as I connected to battle.net, I had to download a patch. Standard fair for beta testing, during the LOD beta, patching was an almost daily occurrence.
So after installing the patch, I logged into a very cool opening image of a human castle, complete with portcullis, firing cannons, and sword wielding knights. I Clicked the "battle.net icon" and a large oaken door closed over the screen. When it slid open, I found myself in blizzard chat. Quite a change from the diablo format, and there are many new options available. Friends groups have been given a button, and the overall handling of the chat rooms have been greatly enhanced. Notice how the characters have been replaced by the icons - similar to of Warcraft, Starcraft, and Classic Diablo. This helps the issues people were having seeing everyone in a particular channel.

So, I decide to fire up a game. The first thing I noticed when joining - Warcraft 3 now features a way to stop ladder abuse - ladder games are anonymous Your grouped by your rank, and have little control over who your opponent is. This will stop the rampant ladder padding that has plagues Starcraft and Warcraft 2 since the inception of the ladder ranking system... hopefully....  I selected the night elves as my race, and a beginner map from the list. Once I created the game, it gave me the loading screen, displayed to the right. That little blue bar seemed to take an eternity to fill....
The night elves are incredible. All their buildings are living trees, which can uproot, move.... and even FIGHT. The first unit I constructed was a "wisp." These small creatures, which not only harvest wood, can detonate, removing magical protections from an area. The central tree-ent that serves as your main base, can root itself near a gold mine and it harvests gold FOR YOU. No need for peons or workers here. From he start, you are allowed to summon a "hero of the realm." Essentially a miniature Diablo character, he can wear equipment, use scrolls to permanently raise stats, gain levels, carry potions to recover mana and life, he can even carry around the familiar "town portal" scrolls to escape to safety. QUITE Awsome. You can summon THREE heroes over the course of a game, one for each upgrade your "main" town hall has acquired.
Click To Enlarge

Yet another intriguing feature are non-allied buildings. You can "Hire" mercenary units there, everything from ogre's, to troll axe throwers, to goblin sappers. You will be squaring off against these creatures regularly as well, in the form of "creeps" - non-allied units that guard resource spots and wander the battle field. They are very dangerous, however, they usually drop equipment and other magical items, which your hero can equip. The treasure aspect is VERY cool. It's like having a small scale diablo 2 within the larger game.

After a few moments of playing with the units, and testing build orders, I began constructing a fighting force. Along with my hero, who I had leveled up to 5 roaming monsters, I fielded a group of archers, and panther riding melee units. Using my owl scouts, I located my enemies base, and launched an immediate raid. He met me with a large force of grunts and other assorted orcish units. My opponents hero, a Minotaur, began rampaging through my lines, slaying a great many of my panther riders. In retaliation, I focused all my archers fire on the hero, while my melee units and my hero held off the swarm of grunts. (Reminding me of a protoss v.s. zerg battle.) Back at home, I continued to produce more archers and melee troops to replace the fallen. By the time the battle ended, my foes forces lay routed, and I had a substantial number of troops and my hero (demon slayer) still among the living. Once reinforced it was easy to crush what few units he could field, as I swarmed over his base.
After a good battle, my opponent and I exchanged "gg" and he left. I stayed in the game and razed his entire base, for old times sake. Half the fun of winning is the pillaging that occurs at the end in my humble opinion =). To the right you can see the new stat screen shown after a battle. Those that are familiar with blizzard games should be accustomed to the format. Notice the new category that ranks your hero's points.

All I can say is... wow. This game is marvelous. I fired up humans a few times, and frustrated an opponent with a choke of doom - photo to the left. Notice the slope of the hill - yup, this game is 3-D. Your units scale and shrink as they run up and down hills, through streams, and over ridges. Also introduced is a day and night cycle, which has a powerful impact on the game - some forces are stronger at night than in the day, and vice versa. For example, at night, the Dark elven woman can become invisible, effectively cloaked. I am amazed, how blizzard can take this tried and true concept, and add so many new wonderful things. Awesome game. And worth every second of the wait!
