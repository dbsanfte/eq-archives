Here you can find a large compelation of genral DAoC information.
Stat caps per level (Str, Dex, etc...):

A) 1.5x your level from items (weapons, armor, jewelry, etc...)
B) 1x your level from single-stat buffs (aka baseline buffs, like +Str or +Dex from a Healer or Shaman)
C) 1.5x your level from double-stat buffs (aka spec line buffs, like +Str/Con or +Dex/Qui from a Shaman)
D) Unknown as of yet if there is any caps from Realm Skill stats.

- A, B, C and D all stack together.
- By level 50, A+B+C is capped to a maximum of 350. And D goes on top of that.
- At level 50, your stats are capped at +75 from items

Resistance Caps:
- Items give a maximum of +26% on any resists at lvl 50 (1 + (your level/2))
- Race Gives:

- Kobolds: 5% crush, 5% energy
- Norsemen: 3% slash, 2% crush, 5% cold
- Trolls: 3% slash, 2% thrust, 5% matter
- Dwarves: 2% slash, 3% thrust, 5% body
- Buffs has no known cap.
- Realm abilities has no known cap.

Skill Caps:

A) +11 from items at lvl 50 (1+lvl/5) (weapons, armor, jewelry, etc...)
B) +10 from Realm Rank

- For a maximum of +21 to a spec (like weapon or shield)

Benifits of + to stats:

- 50-200= 1% gain, per 2 points
- 200-300= 1% gain, per 4 points
- 300-350= 1% gain, per 5 points

- Still not known the actual benefits of Realm Skill +stat gains.

Things that effect your chance to hit something:

- Enchanted bonus on your weapon (and bonus also increases damage contrary to what is said from Sanya)

- Your level vs. their level (the higher their are than you, the harder they'll be to hit)

- The number of people fighting the same opponent - this isn't group dependant.

- Weapons Styles with bonus to-hit

- Str, Dex, Qui...NONE of the base stats have any effect on your chance to-hit (I disagree with the Dex portion of this statement from my own experience)

Things that effect your chance to NOT get hit by something:

- Enchanted bonus on your armor

- Weapon styles with bonus to defense

- Dex affects your chances to Block & Parry

- Dex & Qui affects your chances to Evade

- Your level vs. their level (the lower the level they are to you, the harder it is for them to hit you)

- Armor Factor on your Armor affects the chance to negate a blow (seen as a miss)

- Absorb % on your armor affects how much dmg taken when you do get hit

- AF also decreases the damage taken

The effectiveness of shields and shield spec:

A) Shield spec has a base 5% chance to block +0.5% per spec point

B) Enchanted bonus on Shields also affects chance to block (Unsure in what way, maybe A+B, or A+(AxB))

- Small shields: (supposedly) can block only 1 target, and has the base chance to block
- Medium shields: (supposedly) can block up to 2 targets, and has an extra 5% chance to block vs. Small
- Large shields: (supposedly) can block up to 3 targets, and has an extra 5% chance to block vs. medium and 10% vs. small

- Shield Sizes:
- Round = Small
- Kite = Medium
- Tower/Heater = Large

Effectiveness of weapon spec:

- 1 to 2/3rds of level = 25-100% dmg
- 2/3rds to 100% of level = 75-125% dmg
- 100%+ = 100-150% dmg

That scale is linear.

- Your damage continues to grow if your weapons spec is above your level, but slower.
- I believe each weapon spec point above your level adds about 0.66% to your damage.

How stats affect weapons:

- Axes = 100% Str
- Swords = 100% Str
- Hammers = 100% Str
- Spears = 50/50 Str/Dex
- Staffs = 100% Dex
- Hand to Hand = 50/50 Str/Dex


Damage Calculation:

- Damage = WeapDmg (which is a possibly capped weapon DPS) * Delay * Quality * Condition * (WeapSkill / Target's AF)

Also, there is an interesting formula for the effective AF of a piece of armor:
- Effective AF = Listed AF * Quality * (1 + (absorb * quality))

- Notice the quality double-dipping which makes it extra important for armor.

Siege Weapons:
- BUILDING THE SIEGE

Firstly, the siege can be made of elm, oak, or ironwood. The more expensive the wood, the better the siege. All the other parts are standard for the weapon category. The full parts list can be found by right-clicking on the recipe. All recipes require possession of the Wood Plane, Smith Hammer, and Sewing Kit. There is no forge/lathe requirement.

- Current Wood and parts weight:

Ram - 200
Ballista - 167
Catapult - 199
(With wood and minus ammo.)

Ammo is 25s a pop for ballista bolts and catapult stones, and may be purchased from one of the supply keeps (Bledmeer Faste, Dun Crauchon, Caer Benowyc) or any portal ticket vendor (they are on page 2). The special ammo is Ball of Ice, Bullet and Grapeshot and costs 50s from the portal ticket merchants. The ticket vendors are in the border forts and portal keeps. They can also all be crafted with Siegecrafting.

Ammo weighs 1 per 8 and stacks to 20 (2.5 per stack). It must be unstacked before loading.

Position is crucial for all the siege as they can not be moved after construction. Give all the parts to the person making the item and they can move into position. A high level tank will be able to move slowly with a trebuchet unbuffed, and if buffed they are soloable by all classes. Lifter II is usually sufficient to always have a ram and be unbuffed.

Controller/Builder with Siegecraft 1+ uses the hotbar to craft siege like any other craft. Alchemists and Spellcrafters can recently been granted the ability to make and use siege but they are very limited in skilling (1% of primary trade).

Takes less than 10 seconds per attempt to build. A craft check is made like the other crafts. Failure rate is the same as yellow crafting attempts (later blue, green and eventually grey). Skill ups are about the same as other crafts. Almost automatic at first and then decreasing in frequency. Once your skill is 50 over the difficulty the craft time is halved.

- IT'S BUILT, NOW WHAT

Once the siege is on the ground, it's time to command the weapon. You can type the commands as you go, /arm, /aim, /fire or you can use the pop-up control box. I recommend using macros for /arm and /fire. The box will automatically pop-up when you build and disappear when you leave control range. To get the control box up later 1) target the siege, 2) hotkey macro or type '/control'. If you are already controlling a different siege hotkey macro or type '/control release' first. There is no timer or delay; you can do it very fast. Once the siege is up anyone with a craftskill can target uncontrolled siege and use /control to get the control box to pop up. If you die you automatically release control.

The control box has 4 clickable boxes, a timer bar, and an inventory box (where all ammo clicked is shown).

- The 4 boxes, in order, are:

ARM
LOAD (not used for rams)
AIM
FIRE

Using the button or a macro (/fire, /aim, /arm) work the same.

(Loading and aiming can be done in either order. Once aimed you can skip the aim step when attacking the same target.)

Click on ARM first - You will see the siege crank back into firing position. Rams take 5 seconds, ballistas 10 seconds, and catapults 15 seconds. Trying to fire early doesn't reset anything on ARMing. It's just a wasted keystroke and will reset the timer bar.

The firer/controller should drag unstacked ammo onto the siege to load it. The window holds 7 per page for page 1&2 and 4 on the third page although using more than 2 on the last page tends to bug it. Since arming takes 10 seconds for ballista and 15 seconds for a catapult there is plenty of time to unstack and load ammo while arming. Siege is easily a one person operation. Dropping an ammo on the siege will restart the visual timer. Regardless a text message will tell you when the weapon is armed.

The operator can both run the siege and repair it. You can not give commands to the siege during a repair although it can be arming. Previously repairs were faster, but now they are now about 20s and it is helpful to have another person /repair.

Click LOAD next - if you have multiple ammo in the siege, you should select one by clicking on its name (it'll turn yellow). Wait for timer. 5 seconds.

"Invalid Ammo Slot": this message occurs when you do not have an ammo selected or the only available ammo is in slots 17+ (page 3). I recommend not using page 3 (load only 14 at a time). Just ignore the jammed ammo.

- Targetting:

For ballista choose target normally. If there is a wall in the way click where it would be - it should come up in target with a message about not being in view. It'll still work, but only for other siege or inanimate objects. /assist will not work for targetting but no LoS is needed so camera adjustment can work as well for direct targetting (leave your camera somewhere with a view).

For catapults, use ground targetting. Ground targetting can be set manually, with /groundset [####] (1500 minimum), or with /groundassist [player]. The ground target looks different for each client. The ToA client leaves the GT visible until moved. The vertical position can be set using Page Up and Page Down while holding the Ground Target key (F5 default).

Once you have the crosshairs and triangle at your desired position, you may aim at that spot. If the target is out of range it will give a message.

For F5 targetting you may also leave the siege go acquire a target and return to the siege. A quick /control siege targetted will give you control back and ready to aim (you can assemble, arm, and load the siege before going out and getting a target.)

Now press AIM . This rotates the siege towards the target. Rams do this almost instantly while catapults seem to take a few seconds. Rams have a high probability of jack-knifing in the gate area (avoid this by placing the ram 100-150 units from the gate). No effect other than looking strange. Skip this step once target has been aimed at once.

FIRE - this launches the ammo at your specified target. If it isn't ready to be fired it will give you a message. Try again in a few seconds. Hit arm again immediately after firing.

- REPAIRING SIEGE

Repair requires no wood. It takes approximately 20s and is not always successful.

Rams last 12 minutes without repair while catapults and ballistas last 24 minutes. Rams last 44 minutes maximum and others last 88 minutes. Siege damaged by enemies last significantly less. Especially if damaged by ballistas. Siege items naturally lose hits every second. Rams lose 10 hits/second and ballistas/catapults lose 5 hits/second.

Repair is based on Woodworking skill. You may repair siege with Woodworking as low as 200 with failures.

- RANGE FINDING

For rams, the range is about 200. Macro /groundset 150 will give you a good range from the gate. Basically within a step or two from door out to about half a ram length. If you are confident you can place the ram at max range and it will not go through the door, making it harder to target.

For ballistas and catapults if you are within spell range (1500) you are probably too close. The hill crest is usually too close for the first gate. Use /groundset 1500 to see your minimum range before placement. Inside a keep the front of the tower (Hib/Mid) to the inner gate is about 1500.

Far range: Far range varies by siege type and according to the archery code where shooting up lowers range and shooting from height increases range. Shooting from the Lord Tower adds about 700 depending and shooting up at the Lord Tower loses almost that. On level ground, long range is 2500 for onagers and scorpions, 3500 for catapults and ballistas, and 4500 for trebuchets and palintones. This is beyond what you can ground target from the siege visually. The trebuchet can go about 5200 from a keep to a much lower target and can be as short as 3800 shooting up.

- OVERALL ASSESSMENT:

- Small ram: Caps at 450 to level 1 gate. No tarp.
- Ram: Caps at 570 against grade 1 doors, still 300-500 a shot vs. grade 6 doors. Has a tarp.
- Siege ram: Caps at 750 to level 1 gate. Moderate damage to level 10 gates. Has a tarp.

- Scorpion, Ballista, and Palintone: decent speed (15s+ per shot) but do minor damage to players. Damage to siege weapons is about 1000 vs. same wood siege. A fully repaired siege has 7200 hits so this is significant especially considering normal decay. Damage to gates is 15-25 (this may have been removed). Although the damage can be repaired it shortens the life of the target siege. A siege can only take about 24,000 hits before it decays in spite of repairs.

- Onager, catapult: Good for practicing range-finding and controls. Do minimal damage to opposing rams (20ish) and they probably no longer damage gates (not personally confirmed).

- Trebuchet: For the serious keep assault with defenders or relic keeps these are the way to go. Some damage vs. guards/PCs but slow. Need 3+ firing together to kill PCs. Expensive but the only siege capable of doing more than scaring defending PCs. Best used for defense where attackers can be expected at a very specific location (the front door).

- Salvage, or what to do when the job is done.

Rams salvage for 500 of the appropriate wood. Timer is 500 seconds (8:20). Repair right before salvage and you'll make it (decay timer is 12:00).

Ballistas salvage for 700 of the appropriate wood. Timer is 700 seconds (11:40). Repair right before salvaging just to be safe. The decay timer is 24 minutes.

Catapults salvage for 1000 of the appropriate wood (it will say salvage for 1100). Timer is 1100 seconds (18:20). Decay time is 24:00 so slap a repair before starting. The ironwood versions of these actually salvage for more or equal in wood than their crafted cost.

These weapons naturally decay to half their salvage value in wood.

This information is based on testing and some information directly from the former Tradeskill Dev. Some of the numbers may be off slightly as they are the result of tests rather than direct information.

For costs and parts check the merchants and your tradeskill recipes.
