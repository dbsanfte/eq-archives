Here is a listing of different tactics that will help keep you, (well, MOST of you), from becoming Venom Lord kibbel. Read well, and maybe you can play, live - and die - better.

Tactic# 1: When beginning any low level magic user, such as the necromancer or sorceress, the first half of act I can be made incalculably easier by equipping a bow. Not only does it keep you at a distance, it allows for a mana-free way to dish out some dmg. One can use a bow to "crutch-up" a character till later in the game when they can rely totally on their magical arts to mash besties.
Tactic# 2: You are under no obligations to spend your skill points immediately. In fact, some classes, such as the sorceress, nearly REQUIRE that you stock skill points in order to be successful the long term. Save the points you earn at lower levels, and then use them to buy multiple high level abilities at once. Continue to "multi-upgrade" your top end skills to see drastic upgrades in your character's power.
Tactic# 3: The hordric cube is a powerful device, but it can bite you in the ass as easily as help you. Be certain to remove all unnecessary components from the cube before transmuting, or they will be consumed to no effect. Additionally, while three of the same class of gem will transform into a higher class, 9 of the same gem, all transmuted at once, will ONLY yield one gem of the next higher quality. So tread cautiously when using the cube, 9 times out of ten, the results of a transmuting will NOT be to your liking. Check the arsenal for a complete listing of the Horodric Cube formulas.
Tactic# 4: Item imbuing is a powerful feature of the game, and when used at a grossly appropriate time, will yield the most powerful items attainable. One thing to remember, the normal and nightmare mode imbues DO carry over into hell mode, so don't worry about having to use them. Secondly, the quality, (according to Blizzard), of the item post imbue has NO bearing on the weapon quality after imbuing, however, It is strongly recommended that the finest type of item be used when imbuing - why take chances? Secondly, as you advance in level, the number of powers, and the ranges on those powers, (i.e. the min/max), increases drastically. So, saving an imbue as long as possible increases the ODDS of acquiring a great item. (As with all things in life, nothing is guaranteed).
Tactic# 5: Armor is an important factor of any character's survival, but in some cases, it is possible to overdo it. While melee characters, such as the barbarian, paladin, and the pikeazon basically require the expenditure of 170 strength to wear ornate plate, this kind of point placement is foolish for a bowazon, necromancer, or sorceress. Those classes will function quite well with 125 or LESS strength, and even WITH ornate plate they would not last long when mobbed anyways. So, understand the strengths and weaknesses of your class before spending heavily in strength.
Tactic# 6: One often overlooked factor in Diablo 2 is the role character level plays in armor rating. A lvl 65 sorceress with 25 armor is harder for a lvl 30 creature to hit than a lvl 10 paladin with a 2000 armor. The majority of player armor value comes from character level, and, as many ppl have discovered, creatures ten or more levels higher than them will CONSISTENTLY land blows, regardless of base armor class. Thus, it is very important, (especially if playing a heavily armor dependent character like the paladin), that one battles monsters at or below his character level.
Tactic# 7: When playing diablo 2, survival is as much tactics as it is personal power. In order do avoid becoming demon chow, a player will need to develop proper "mob management" skills. The first rule, is never run into somewhere you can't run out of - and you WILL be running away - alot. Getting caught in a dead end with a pack of champions on your butt is a great way to die. Rule two - if at all possible, fight from a door. This keeps you from becoming totally surrounded, and keeps the bad guys both lined up, and coming at you one or two at a time. (Optimal for both spell castors and melee players). Rule three, try to split groups of mixed creatures up - makes them easier to manage. Often, a person can get the melee units to follow them around a corner, without the support of the missile attackers. Finally, play off the stupidity of the computer A.I. We all KNOW what happens in warcraft and starcraft when a unit gets stuck in a "U" or "C" shape. Guess what - Diablo 2 uses an almost identical pathing engine - abuse it's stupidity, think of it as your payback for all the Acron that mindlessly ran in circles while a lone hydra picked them off. (Anyone with a bow, 750 arrows, and half a brain can kill mephesto without taking a scratch - emphasis is on the half brain point).
Tactic# 8: Unique items - they can be among the most powerful items in the game, but, only if used appropriately. Through normal mode, unique items remain viable for all classes. But after act 4 normal, unique items loose most of their combat effectiveness, with a few notable exceptions. The fighting classes are better served by high end rares, as only those will have the armor-damage potential to cope with the progressively harder monsters. But at the same time, classes like the necromancer and the sorceress, who do not need heavy armor and destructive weapons, can hunt for these uniques, as they grant some of the most powerful "bonus" abilities available in the game. The uniques to be on the look-out for are: Goldskin, Silks of the Victor, Iceblink, Heavenly Plate, Ume's Lament, Torch of Iro, Wormskull, Tarnhelm, The Stone of Jordan, The Irion Jong Bong, Nightsmoke, Goldwrap, Eye of Etlich, Culwin's Point, Chance Guards, Frostburns, Treads of Cthon, and Tearhaunches. Even if you do not wish to equip them, they will command TOP prices in the trading channels.
Tactic# 9: Beware of trading scams. There are a great many slight of hand artists that can rob you blind, even WITH the trading window's security. Be on the look-out or you could end up loosing something really nice. The best advice that you can have is NEVER click the green check if they have not done so. Also, double check the item before you accept - people have gotten very good at swapping items with he same pallet, and you may be trading for a jordan and end up with a +1 to dex ring. Always insist that all gear to be traded be placed in the upper left hand corner of the trade window - this will make slight of hand routines signifigantly harder. Another popular trick is to claim that the trading window is screwed up, and then demand that you trade old-school style. These people are of course playing sorceress's and will use telekinesis to snatch up your gear. Just be shrewed, and you will not get taken for a ride.
Tactic# 10: A well refills half a player's hit points, stamina, and mana points, plus, refreshes very fast. Try to stage most of your battles near a well, and in mid fight, simply gulp back your lost health. This is a great way to save those valuable rejuvenation potions for bosses/uniques. It is worth the time investment to lure a few enemies back a ways so you can fight near an unlimited mana/life pool.
Tactic# 11: When designing your character, it is best to do a "trial run," that is, create a hacked open character, and grant yourself lvl 20 abilities. Then test them all out, deciding which to place skill points in. After you have made a "road map," create your realm or hard core character and follow your recipe. The results will be FAR superior to a hit-and-miss skill point system. (And how else would you figure out how suicidal it is to place more than 4 points into zeal or strafe?).
Tactic# 12: Take it easy on the power leveling. There is actually an exp penalty for being in a place too difficult for your level. For every five levels the monsters are over you, you recieve a 5% exp penalty. A lvl 30 character in hell/hell is suffereing a 50% expirence penalty. A 10,000 point monster is onyl worth 5,000 at that penalty. You need to kill twice as much, and it is twice as hard. On the average, a lone player should stay in normal mode till 30-35, depending on equipment. One should NOT leave nightmare mode until lvl 50, as the base creatures in Act 1 hell are just as hard as the monsters in Act 4 nightmare. Quite often, people mistake characters for being weak classes, when in fact, the player is merely playing monster much too high a level for him.
Tactic# 13: Be careful when allocating skill points to abilities. Most skills "level off" after the placement of a few skill points, and continued point allocation is of little benefit. A good example of this is energy shield. After level 8, the percentage of damage absorbed increases in single digit incriminates, offering MINUSCULE improvement. At this point, the practical limit on that skill's power has been reached. Instead of wasting 10 skill points to get an extra 10% damage absorbed, it would be wiser to invest the points in a skill with better "top end" growth. Iron Skin as an example of an ability that grows consistently, even at high levels. If in doubt - remember - MOST of the level 30 Skills are worth every point you can place in them. Gathering a few +1 to all items is preferable to adding points to "topped" off skills.
Tactic# 14: Never, ever, ever take a way point unless you are sure it has not been mobbed. You appear at the destination instantly, but it may take up to THIRTY SECONDS for your computer to load the area. The result of this is 30 seconds of you standing there, immobile, helpless. Often, you will load the area, only to find your corpse lying on the ground. Waiting pks or monsters will be able to do as they please, while you load the map, making way point hopping a dangerous endeavor indeed. Some jerks even think it's cute to lure a massive force of baddies to a way point and then go else where, leaving a death trap for the next person to enter. Beware.
Tactic# 15: Potion management is a critical skill for the fighters, particularly the barbarian and the paladin. Quaffing a few "pots" immediately at the beginning of a fight can give you a "buffer", as the "interest" on the potion is effectively adding life as you continue to take blows. When the damage begins to outstrip the speed of recovery, it is time to chugg a rejuvenation potion, which recovers life almost instantly. Combining this with a vampire ring/ammulet, you can yo-yo your way out of deadly situations.
Tactic# 16: What skill is better to max - Bone Spear or Bone Spirit? Bone Spear will past through multiple enemies in a line. Bone Spear is very good for getting rid of a lot of enemies. At higher levels Bone Spirit does more damage than Bone Spear but Bone Spirit can only hit one monster at a time. Bone Spirit is easier to aim and hit enemies because it will track a target. Bone Spirit will, however, only hit a monster that is targeted when the spell is fired, so you can't just fire Bone Spirits in an area off screen where you think monsters are coming from like you can do with Bone Spear. If you want to clear large areas if monsters, use Bone Spear. If you want to add additional damage to help out your Minions and Golems use Bone Spirit. You can use both spells but should you decide to specialize in just one, note the advantages of both spells and pick one that best fits your playing style.
-Bone Spear:
* Hits multiple targets
* Doesn't need to be targeted on an enemy, you can fire in their direction
-Bone Spirit:
* More Damage
* Easier to hit Monsters
* Better player v.s. player
Tactic# 16: Unique item generation - this has been a thorn in many players sides, and they did not even realize it. Did you know, that a unique item will NOT generate in an area where a person already HAS that particular unique item? So, if you are in act IV, and gambling for, say a wormskull, it WILL not generate, drop, or fall out of a chest if someone else in that act is walking around with one. Hence the name "unique." It is this reason that a person that has one Stone of Jordan will have to trade or mule to find a second. So, before wasting hundreds of thousands gambling, make sure no-one else in the game has the item you are hunting for.
