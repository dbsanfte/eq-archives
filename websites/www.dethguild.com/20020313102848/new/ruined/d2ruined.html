Diablo II: Lord of Character Destruction - Reduction Pack (Editorial)
By SilverDeth
Little did we know, the horrors that awaited us there....
-Marius-

My first thoughts when Mike called me, announcing that our internet gaming group had nailed down 3 copies of the beta was something like this:
"YEAHHHHAHAAAAAAAAAAAA!!!!!! OHMYFUCKINGGODYESSSSSS!!!!!!! KIIIICCCKKKASSSSSSSS!!!!!!!"
(Or something along those lines). The fact that all our finest characters and gear resided on USEast seemed trivial at the time, we know plenty of people, it would be no problem getting dozens of different builds to play, and play we did, swapping the beta(s) between us to test this or that tactic.

That pretty pink slip was quavering in my hands as I waited for the CD to spin up and install what had to be the highlight of my gaming year. Little did I know... as Marius echoed in classic Diablo 2... the horrors that awaited me there...

For many weeks prior to the beta test, I tried to voice my concerns, speaking out at places like D2S&T, the diabloii.net forums, (and the web site that is not worthy of my spit). I knew some of these sites were given tons of merit by the designers at Blizzard Entertainment, (the makers of the Diablo II expansion pack), so I always spoke my mind, trying to argue, civilly, and considerately, to let people play as they wished. Any playing style should be accommodated, and reasonable latitude should be given to account for this. I had to make a visible, yet mature stand for the way a very significant number of people enjoyed playing the game, and I managed this for well over 3 months. After tolerating scores upon scores of infantile replies and flames from totalitarian players, (who are too insecure to stand the thought of letting others play as they liked), I severed all ties with this community of individuals, in a rather nasty post, which started my "mission" - I have not returned, nor called this site by name since that day. I left knowing for sure that these extremists would not be heeded, surely blizzard could not be so daft, that they would give these ranting, moaning people the time of day... let alone cave to their demands. (Yes, those who argue against me once sounded very much like me, crying about this or that imbalance - notice how civilly they treat those who disagree with them now. Complaints and criticisms are fine, as long as you agree with these types of "people".... if you don't step in line with their ideals... well..... be prepared to be drug through the mud.)

Yet, to my abject horror, this is exactly what happened. On installing the expansion pack, we discovered many, if not most, of the characters we had access to were utterly... crucified. The zealot paladin, who depended so heavily on his shield, found his blocking percentage reduced to 21% - with a increased blocking pavaise and maxed holy shield mind you.... the iceblink on the polearm barb all but non-functional, the twinker sorceress.... and the bowazon, with her vaunted 153 SIAS bow almost entirely ineffective.

However, this was not enough to dampen our enthusiasm. We went into this knowing that we would need to find better gear, after all, what is the point of converting if there was not godly weapons and gear to aspire for. The sorceress still concerned us, but we shelved her for the time being, instead focusing on the barb, who we figured we could easily modify with better gear. After all, even a level 87 barb with a 203 IAS Grim Scythe should be effective right?

Boy, were we wrong. Even with 23% life steal, and 22% mana leech, we had difficulty sustaining whirwind. Unleechable undead, and physical immunes, or ghostly version of classic monsters made life literally... hell. Fortunately, Mike, always the consumate power gamer, quickly solved most of these issues. We purchased a smoking Bec De Corbin of Quickness at the vendor, for an astounding 387,000 gold pieces, (little did we know....), and went to work on the physical immunes. "Ok, we can cope with this," I was thinking. Concentrate seems to be a good way to fight when out of mana, and the Barb's 831 Ornate turned out to be a very good investment. As were points in warcry. Confident we had this thing beat, we blazed away, swapping concentrate, berserk, ww, and weapons as the situation mandated. Then our gear started falling apart.....

No problem! Time to use that handy new "repair all" feature...... WWHAAAAAT!!!!! 2.5 MILLION to repair all this stuff??? Not enough CASH?? (Almost 1/3 of this was his peechy new helm we received as a quest reward...). What the hell...... The sickening realization soon hit us that we were unable to afford repairs... even when equipped mostly with exceptional gear... let alone the elite items we were hungering for... We settled on repairing the bec and the grim scythe, and returned to the bloody foothills to continue treasure hunting, hoping we would see a few of those mage plate and sharktooth armors to help fill our coffers. Soon however, almost all of our gear was smashed. The few paltry drops we had received were not even enough to cover the cost of repairing ONE of the polearms, and all the barb's gear, except his ornate, (which was hovering at 12 durability), was in shambles. Thus, with almost all our gear highlighted in red, we were forced to make numerous trips though normal mode, selling off Gothic and field plates from act 4 until we slowly, over the process of 3 hours, had acquired enough cash to repair everything... well almost everything.... we gave up on the helm.... it's price tag didn't justify it's skill meager bonuses. After all our equipment lay in tatters once more from another trek in act 5 hell, (and Baal Dead at our feet), we decided that this barb was no longer fun to play. We wanted to kick some demon butt, to be heroes. Instead, we became the slaves of our own gear.

Well, back to the sorceress. Maybe we can dredge up a cool bow for the amazon.... er.... wait... are those flesh spawners... OMFG..... immune..... monsters... everywhere.... even FALLEN, spear women..... Ok-ok-ok.... this is fine, she has a few points to blow... we will simply spend a few here, and a few there, spread out... perhaps dangerously so, but it's the only way to handle these things. I thought, odd, she seems to be... casting slower.... SHE IS CASTING SLOWER.... A full tweaker rig, this level 81 sorceress was casting about 3 steps slower than usual. Uuggghhh. Orb tactics... largely massacred. Investment in firewall was also a mistake, as the skill appears bugged, and not only suffered a huge duration reduction, also does not display accurately as to where it is. The range reduction in static was actually a blessing, as at level 20, this sorceress had a tendency to draw way to many monsters at once, hooraay there. Then we looked at her shield new blocking details in the character screen.... cool they show your... blocking.... rate.... 5%... WHAT THE FUCK, we have a 40% wall of the eyeless on.... it was then we realized how badly they had nerfed the shield blocking. We were no truly offended till we actually received a rare clasped orb. 5% life steal, enhanced damage, 12 to life, IAS, +2 to firebolt, +1 to telekinesis. Wait a minute, I thought they would weight these things.... for spell castors.... and the realization hit us... (this is becoming a theme here), that with well over 1500 total prefixes and suffixes, the odds of getting extremely good mods on a wand, staff or orb just sank from the toilet and into the sewer. HA! We will beat this.... off to... the .... the.... vendor... we'll just... buy.... guess what... they don't sell orbs. But they will sell you a staff with +2 to firebolt and +3 to lightning bolt for 500,000 gold pieces. And then we learned, that you cannot gamble uniques.... how the hell are we supposed to equip a sorceress NOW, I asked. Rares have been pretty stale, then, the now impossible to acquire uniques are the best equipment we could ask for. Oh well, glad we have a stockpile of stone, goldskins, frostburns, and other such gear, to anyone that lacks these things.... good luck....

The final slap in the face of our sorceress demo.... the pk. An amazon joined our game, and we chuckled as she went hostile. (Mike looked at me and smiled - "She is soooo fuckin' dead... hahaha..."). This is one sturdy, duel centered sorceress, and the -100 resistance was going to put that zon's twitch wearing ass in a sling - or so we thought. I camped the flayer jungle way point, frozen orb and static field at the ready. After the PK's many comparisons to myself and a part of the female anatomy, we clued him in to our location. She [the amazon] appeared not 30 seconds later, with an oddly colored, glittering bow in her hands. "I got you!" I thought, as I unloaded my... single.... orb..... but still, it should have been fatal... <wink> she vanished... wahhhh!? Did she just.... tele.... <death cry> as 2 arrows destroyed a level 81 sorceress. Yes, she had a teleporting item. After telling me how I was "owned," (I thought blizzard screened these people out of the beta grrrrrr), the player killer helped herself to our hard won cash, and left the game. How the hell... I looked over at Mike again.... "Fuck her." he said with a shrug. So we left the sorceress behind, as many people most certainly will once they see what was done to her because blizzard screwed up - remember spell timers we done due to blizzard's screw ups, not balance concerns. They got too artsy, now all of us pay the tab.

Totally disgusted, with this line of thought, (for now), I can talk about some of the other classes. The amazon is little changed. All she needs is - a back up, obscene elemental damage bow, and a powerful knockback imbued high end weapon, with a little crushing blow added for kicks, and she hammers swarms like nobodies business. Resistances are now important however, and can no longer be ignored, and large bosses are harder than ever. The assasin really, really, really sucks it up v.s. groups, although her single opponent skills are rather impressive. Cool, but really lacking in hell level crowd control. (Her best trap is similar to CE, and we know how good that is multiplayer...). The necromancer is almost totally unaffected by the expansion pack. But rest assured his poison attacks are still weak enough, that even with SERIOUS investment, they are no match for the minions of hell mode as a stand alone skill. The nerfs applied to spear and spirit do nothing to improve the state of artillery, and he is reduced to a plodding minion crawl. The druid you ask? Essentially GOD. All three of his trees are exceptional, and he can specialize in either and expect to do VERY well. I have seen were-bear druids with well over 6,500 hit points, and inflicting barbarian like damage. The druid rivals the amazon for pure power in the expansion pack. Interesting mix... totally unfair... lets TRY the paladin shall we, had several to work with....

The Paladin... an interesting change. Zeal has been improved, and conviction... was blown through the roof. (They fixed it in 1.45). While a paladin STILL remains rather lack luster alone, he has been turned into the ultimate tool - his very presence can assure anyone who parties with him ultimate success. Some combos, (particularly conviction + static field), are so powerful they will UNDOUBTEDLY be changed before, or soon after release. (I was right, they did). Blessed hammer got.... well... the hammer, so to speak. While this was "the" premier way to make a power house solo paladin, I have very mixed feelings about this skill. It was after all, a bug. Blessed Hammer + Concentration was never, never intended to work - Con is supposed to improve physical attacks. Many arguments over what exactly Blizzard's intentions with this game were, but the game counting blessed hammer's damage as physical CLEARLY was not one of them. So, I cannot say I blame blizzard for nerfing the hell out of this skill. However, the paladin's other valid solo skill - conversion, was also reduced - to a paltry 15 seconds in duration. Despite the improvements made to zeal, conviction, holy shock/fire fanaticism, and fist of heavens, the paladin is now, more than ever, reliant on other classes to do much of his killing. While he may make them succeed beyond their wildest dreams, his own ability to battle evil has been greatly reduced. The lowered monster AR makes defense a much more practical alternative for the paladin, however, this does not save him against the various magical assaults, and his low vitality still shines through in places with a lot of this kind of damage. It's hard to say what my thoughts are on the paladin. The class specific shields are nice, as resistance seems to be an inherant property, but this in no way makes up for the loss of blocking, and even with armor that is SOMEWHAT effective, the badly, badly curtailed block rate is always glaring back at you with a snarl.

As you can see, most of our beta time was spent playing peoples old characters, while not as utterly optimized as our own behemoths, they still featured solid builds with great gear - the duped stuff, (which we avoid like herpes on our own characters), that some of these characters had even surpassed our own rig. What conclusion did we draw from our time in hell? Your old characters are toast people. All those hours you spent in classic D2 - waisted. Vanilla Diablo II was designed, (and this was stated many, many times by Blizzard), to force a character to specialize in one specific area. They hoped this would breed diversity, filling the realms with a plethora of different and very unique characters. However, this blew up in their face like a claymore, due to the fact that many high level skills (lvl 24+) were utterly inequal in power. It would thus, stand to reason, that when given a choice between two skills, the majority of players will pick the most powerful of the two... or three. The result? What elitist players dubbed "cookie cutter" characters. People min maxed till they achieved the ultimate character build, getting the most power out of a synergy of perfectly balanced and maxed skills, which, when combined with the finest gear, resulted in a devastating killing machine, who could blast through the minions of hell like a panzer column through Norway. These builds were publicized, and everyone copied them, in what became a sort of contest to see who had the best stuff. All of these "uber-models" are based around 3-4 extremely high level skill specializations, combined with powerful weapons and carefully defined areas of play, (ares where those skills are most effective). The sheer power of a players mastery + gear + area thus overcame all of the intended weaknesses, such as physical or magical resistance. And this very specialization is what kills you in the expansion pack.

Blizzard decided to change it's philosophy - from one of specialization to one of diversity. They implemented many new types of monsters - some of which feature physical, magical, or elemental resistances so incredible that a highly specialized character is going to be utterly unable to cope with them. I can't say I see this as a totally bad thing. Challenge is good - and I am always looking to fight the "better mousetrap" as it will teach me how to build a better mouse. BUT, there are four HUGE problems with these changes, that the designers, who maybe, MAYBE take a character to nightmare difficulty, totally missed.

1: They [Blizzard] can change the system at will, Alter the rules. Alter the gaming environment. They can alter everything - but what about the players? Our skills are embossed in granite. (Our tombstones?). We cannot change our characters to meet the new challenges. We spend hundreds of hours on a warrior, perfecting him to the environment, then, suddenly, the rules are changed - what was once a successful strategy, is now, no longer effective. We are left with hundreds of hours lost, and a character that can now barely fight his way through areas once cleared with ease. It's down right insulting to have your work stolen from you like this. And it is work. And time - little bits of your life, that could have been spent doing other things, stripped away from you. I have no problem with changing the rules to correct imbalances, which, BLATANTLY exist, but if Blizzard can change their minds, we should have the opportunity to change ours as well. If they would let us re-distribute skill points, I would shut up and vanish. That would be fair.  Instead, they punish us for making wise choices on skill and character point allowcations, which is exactly what is happening in the Lord of Destruction Expansion Pack, all under the moniker of re-balancing. Call it what it really is - nuking everyone's old characters.

2: The illusion of more powerful objects. This is an example of Blizzard nullifying advantages through hindrances. If you notice, with the changes implemented Lord of Destruction, they have carefully, and masterfully negated each and every bonus - higher defensive rating and lowered monster AR is countered by more powerful and damaging monsters, resistance adding gear is carefully picked apart by -100% resistance reductions in hell mode. Massive elemental damage adding prefixes, such as "smoking," are negated by a myriad of elemental immune monsters. Crafted fast cast gear - don't worry, they fixed that with casting timers and increased animation during spell casting. Weapons like "Cruel war pikes of Evisceration" are all but helpless before physical immunes and the unrealistic repair costs necessary to maintain them. Blizzard added a ton of new powers and abilities, then, very methodically, countered every single one of them. And, thus, by the time everything is all said and done, the characters are NOT one witt more powerful than they were in the original game, and, in cases like the sorceress, are far, far, far less mighty.

3: This is supposed to be an expansion setting. Where we take our old heroes through new environments, and face new enemies. However, your current fighters, the "old guard" so to speak, are almost powerless here. The game was supposedly gauged for elite characters, players with insanely high level characters and way too much spare time. Then, why can they not use most of these new pieces of gear, or excel in the new gaming environment? It's geared for high level characters, but none of the existing high level characters. No, this is an entirely new game. If your looking to pull your Classic Diablo 2 character into the fold, and begin destroying monsters, you will not like the taste of this dish, the expansion pack. It's a new game, with new rules. They should market it as such. It is not an expansion of your old character - it's a reduction. Only a new character, designed to make up for the new shortcomings and hindrances will excel here. Some people do play for the challenge of the game. And this is fine. But those of you interested in many of the things that makes Classic Diablo 2 popular - the monty haul aspects, the success of crushing foes, RELATIVELY balanced player v.s. player bouts, and of course, the loot, loot, loot, I can advise you - don't buy the expansion pack. You will be horribly disappointed by what you get, and dearly, dearly wish you had not taken your level 87 soon-to-be-mule-lance barb into LOD.

4: Probably the most upsetting of all is the inequity of what the expansion pack has to offer the various classes. Some classes enter the expansion pack, and a few small changes in gear and skill points will have them ready to romp, at a reduced level mind you, however, they will still remain relatively effective fighters. However, one class, the sorceress in particular, suffers a drastic drop in personal power in the expansion pack. They cannot purchase an elemental damage hydra bow at the vendor, or place a few points into berserk, no, they need a top to bottom redesign - based on broad generalization which means a devastating change in intensity. It's fact - almost every change instituted in the expansion pack impacts the sorceress negatively, and even worse, more so than the other classes. She suffers from the -100% resistances more than other classes, who do not have to agonize over trading fast cast, (such as it is), for resistance. Shield blocking nerfs go a long ways toward negating the usefulness of her class specific item. (I'll get back to it later). Casting animation for the sorceress has been extended - greatly. And don't forget the glaring feather in the cap of this travesty - Spell timers - which render most tweaking strategies null. (You thought static field was over-used in the past? Well now its a REQUIREMENT. As is cold mastery to beat immunes. Nice job - what little diversity that existed in the sorceress has now been KILLED.) But, the insults do not even stop here, oh no, far from it. With the massive increase in prefixes and suffixes, the chances of acquiring the coveted "+2 fastest cast resistance bahamut's with skills" orb just took a tumble.  ("I've fallen, and I can't get up.") This would not be so bad... if you could actually buy or gamble an orb, thus getting the same odds of acquiring a reasonable weapon right along with the paladin, druid, amazon, barbarian and assasin. But, of course, you can't. And on the subject of gambling - notice how the melee characters now can gamble elite and exceptional weapons and armor, while the spell castors, are totally unable to gamble the unique items, which they DEPEND ON - they must beg, trade, and scavenge for rarely found unique items. And then, (not thought yet....more slaps to sorceress players....), they [Blizzard] allows several lynch pin sorceress spells to be found on magical items. With static field and teleport rings available to other classes, one must seriously question why even play a sorceress? I am sorry, while I was asleep at the wheel, did the sorceresses take over the ladder? Are they rampaging up and down the chaos sancutary solo, thus making the bowazons and lance barbs cringe fearfully at their might? Come on man, this is tatamount to giving Saddamn Hussain a nobel peace prize while repremanding Jimmy Carter for crimes against humanity. Quite simply, whether intentional or not, every "fix" hits the sorceress in the kidneys. They have basically been reduced to walking static machines, who weaken monsters so their Barbarian hireling can kill them. How heroic, please, allow me a moment to retch.

As WarpField always says: "A once a mighty sorceress, now reduced to lowly quill rat fodder."

Basically, the entire solo experience is dead, we will get that clear right now. The advantages of playing in a group so clearly outweigh the suffering that soloing in multi player games has become, you would need to be a Sirian-calibur masochist to attempt this feat. It's possible, but not worth the trouble, as even a 2 man team can devastate all who oppose them. Exp penalties, and drop reductions - that's the reward for a player that is made and equipped to solo. This little social engineering stunt by Blizzard is not appreciated. Don't mistake me, I play in a party - a close nit group of friends, and I can trust them to play smart, not gobble up every magical item that drops, and know they will risk their lives to help me. (A favor I return). Such is not the case for all the other people out there. Frankly, a bad, (or selfish), player is a greater threat to you than a multi-shot lightning enchanted boss. (Combine the two and you have almost certain character death). When not with my comrades, I do not particularly enjoy teaming with the masses. With my buddies, when a rare bow drops, I can be sure my amazon will get it. And my friend can be assured in knowing he will get any wands that fall for his necro. Not with the rabble though. As soon a that yellow hits the ground, they will drop everything they are doing - even if it leaves you in a compromising situation, to rush up and snatch it. Even if your "ally" is a necromancer, and it was a chu-ko-no that fell, you can count on him promptly leaving the game as soon as he fits it in his inventory. To hell with you, they don't care. Frankly, I would sooner not be forced to join the ranks of such people. And certain individuals pathetic rebuttals to solo play are all the identical - as they sputter the same, tired old drawl - <say it in a whiney, 37 year old D&D dungeon master's voice> - "If you want to play solo, play single player." Blah blah blah. Listen, even when you are "soloing" the river of flame, your are still being part of a group. People are in the game. Maybe you will meet one. Maybe you will get attacked by one. Maybe you will ally for a while to defeat a "stone skin magic resistant teleporting" Hephasto... the human interaction is still there, your not sequestered in a glass bubble like single player. You simply want to be far enough away from other people that the objects of value, the items you EARNED, are not snatched up by some random passer by, with no consideration for the effort you expanded in finding it. Selfish? No, just wanting to keep what we rightfully EARNED. But, solo play has been made so difficult and unrewarding, that many, many, of these players will be moving to other games. Hopefully, I can save them 40 bucks in their search.

And another, perhaps unrelated note, I have noticed many sub-humans, particularly on the web site that is not fit to spit on,( but several other forums, such as D2S&T, and diablo ii.net as well), who have no tolerance for dissenting opinions. These, ironically enough, where the "divine inspirations to us all" that so flagrantly stood upon their own soap boxes and touted how "multi-arrow" and "whirlwind" were broken, and must be fixed, lest the earth split asunder, and we will all die of aids. Now, when others DARE to complain about changes that were made to appease these... squeaky cogs, these same protestors descend like a pack of street mutts, and try to tear those disseminating a different idea apart. While I frequently shred these mongrels limb from limb, most others lack my rabid ideals and wicked ability to cut ignorant people down verbally. And thus, they are beaten into submission by these howling animals. "Shut up, quit crying - whiner, don't buy the expansion pack if you don't like it - I'll enjoy using your bandwidth..." ahhhh how I enjoy the wails of these gutter pups, as I stand there, kicking their asses, time and time again, refuting their every point, parrying their every pathetic linguistic blow, and leaving them languishing in an ally, choking on a puddle of their own blood. You people disgust me. Your the most intolerant bunch of louts I have ever seen. You seek to control the way other live, play, and think. I find it amusing that blizzard listened to you, and I intent to illustrate how waisted your attempts at nerfs were. For, while the game may not be... much fun anymore, it can still be abused, at least by the true "cry-babies" rabid standards. And I intend to prove it. To all of you out there who speak your mind when you see something you disagree with - KEEP FIGHTING! Ignore those who are too blind to see the truth. Keep talking, keep playing, and keep battling - the fact that the... bilge rats.... squawked until they got their way only proves that if we raise enough stink, actions will be taken on our behalf. Fight till they kill you. Only then, can you really quit.

So, in closing, I feel the expansion pack was a betrayal - a betrayal to the players - who faithfully trusted blizzard not to obliterate their characters, a betrayal of the masses upon masses of people - who enjoyed a particular style of play, and most of all, a betrayal of the fans - who expected something great, a heroic final chapter in a great game.

What they got, was something else.
