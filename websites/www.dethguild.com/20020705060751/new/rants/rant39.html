This is sort of a eulogy for my mother. I want to express it so people can see who she was and understand the impact it has in my life.

My mother was primarily a housewife. She was proud of this and made no bones about it. It was an occupation. She raised six headstrong and independent children. And she was proud of each and every one of us. She believed that her children should have the best education possible. She met with the PTA, school board, and many school workgroups to ensure each one of us was doing our utmost to learn. We each were taught the basics of cooking, cleaning, sewing, and first aid so that we may have all the necessary tools to live in the world. Mom’s goal was to see each one of us with college educations and making a living wage. And of course, stay out of jail. Well, she achieved the last two, but one daughter married without college and the eldest son went into security. She encouraged us to seek and fulfill our interests, no matter how varied.

Mom raised us with an open mind about many things. She let each one of us live life to what our needs were. This included experiments in drinking, and in one case, drugs. Her belief was if you are going to do it, be safe. We all were told don’t drive if you drink. The understanding was if we got caught, we stayed in jail overnight until we learned our lessons. Each one of us learned that there were duties and obligations. These were to be honored. Whatever we did, it had consequences. We were taught to accept those consequences to our actions and live with the results. We each also were taught that family was very important. It didn’t matter if you were mad at each other. That dropped in a family emergency. I remember when my niece’s mom abandoned her. We all got together and assumed all legal fees to make sure my brother got custody. Now my niece is a straight A student, teaching herself Linux, and three foreign languages. Not bad for a sixteen year old…

My mom was a self-taught genealogist. She became the official family historian for the Corey Family Society of America. FYI, they are a Mayflower/Revolutionary War society. She was internationally known and wrote one book on family history. She had two more books in the works. Sadly, they will probably not be completed.

Mom had a sense of irony too. The day she died, she was at a cardiac rehab class. She walked out after working with the heart monitors, went to her car, and had a fatal heart attack. She always claimed the doctors really don’t know much. I suspect that this was her way of proving it.
