This section is all about survival. Those of you new to Baldur's Gate will find it more strategically balanced than diablo. The enemies are just as deadly, if not MORE lethal than you. Your wits and strategy is what makes the difference between alive and dead. Read carefully here and you may yet live. Ignore all good advice at your own peril.

Tactic# 1: Keep with the party. If a character wanders too far afield he will be mobbed. Even a group of weaklings will bring a bad ass down, so stick close. One should choose a formation with melee fighters in the front, spell castors in the middle, missile attackers in the rear. By moving ina formation like this, players reduce exposure of their low hit point wizards to the claws and fangs of the beasties.
Tactic# 2: Don't start a fight with Drizzt. He is a 17th level ranger with 92 hit points, 95% magic resistance, a -7 armor class, and he's packing a +5 defender and a frostbrand scimitar. Even your best fighters will need critical to hit him, and magic rolls off the drow like water. Better to just help him than start a fracas. I have never seen anyone beat him, although i hear it is possible. But I doubt it....
Tactic# 3: One of the first things you will notice is that it's possible to go on killing sprees in Baldur's Gate. Town is no longer neutral ground, and anything goes. Don't like the prices a merchant is charging? Well, kill him for his obstinance. However, two things to remember before striking down the locals - once you kill a store owner, he is DEAD, you can't bring him back to life, so by killing the rare arms merchant, you effectively remove him from the game - along with all his wares. Also, when you kill wantonly, your reputation drops, and after it reaches a certain point, you will become hunted by bounty hunters, mercenaries, and the flaming fist paladins. Additionally, stores will charge 10x cost, good NPC's will attack you, and clerics will refuse to heal your wounds. However, this does not mean you have to be a lil goodie two shoes either. You can tithe money to the church to increase your reputation. I have found keeping your rep at about 10 is cost effective, and will keep the mercs off your back. Another point, if no one sees you do it, it didn't happen. Thieves are the masters of moving silently, stealing, hiding in shadows, and backstabbing. If you can sneak up on a victim in alone, or in a dark ally, knife him in the back, and loot the corpse, no one will be the wiser. Same thing with stealing. If you break in at night and nobody sees it, you can get away with all the loot scot free. You can still play an evil character, but you need to be TACTICALLY evil, or you will be ground to dust by an overpowering flood of the goodie goodies.
Tactic# 4: Always identify a magic item before using it. I am certain nobody appreciates donning a girdle of masculinity/feminity and sprouting boobs. Cursed magic items, poisoned potions, and hexed scrolls abound, and using a magic item before a wizard or cleric can identify it is not an investment in a long life. Watch out for cursed berserking weapons, when they are equipped they cause the person using it to fly into a frenzied attack against the party.
Tactic# 5: Always keep your magic users out of melee, that is, if you ever want them to be able to cast spells. Spell casting takes time, and if they are struck before the incantation is finished, a wizard's spell is interrupted. Keep them behind the biggest hulking fighter you can find, with a missile attacker or thief behind them to watch for backstabbers.
Tactic# 6: Never underestimate the power of thieves! You can pickpocket just about anyone, snatching items you would have otherwise missed, steal expensive goods from stores, rid yourself if nuisances QUIETLY, and detect traps. The latter is VERY important, as several areas are so shrewn with traps as to be suicidal for a thiefless party to navigate. They may not have the melee muscle in a strait up fight, but thieves can solve just about any other situation that might arise, so always ALWAYS keep a good thief about.
Tactic# 7: Dual classing is a special ability humans have. It allows a human character to stop advancing in one class and start fresh in a new one, while retaining the abilities of the previous class. This can be very powerful when used properly - for example, progress a few levels as a fighter, earning a good store of hit points. Now, dual class to a wizard, and you will have a spell chucker who can wear armor, use a melee weapon reasonably well, AND in a flash, rip off the armor and start casting spells. Be careful though, you can also screw yourself because of the experience cap, so do careful calculations before you ditch a class. (Before you can access the powers of your old class, you must reach an equal level in your new profession). You should dual class all spell castors as a fighter for two to three levels before changing over to a wizard or specialist. Unless you LIKE playing characters with 23 hit points that is...
Tactic# 8: A note on females, they manual says they stand equal to men, and they do, but in their own way. Females tend to have a higher intellect and wisdom than men, but they cannot receive the exceptional strength bonus that male fighters are eligible for. The result of this is female spell castors are smarter, while men tend to be better at crushing things with their bare hands. So, if you want a big mutton head with an axe, your best bet is to go male, but if you want to hurl spells with the best of them, pick a chick.
Tactic# 9: The most powerful spells in the game are not the raw evocations as many of you might suspect. The blast radius of a fireball or reflecting arcs of a lightning bolt have a nasty tendency of being as harmful to your allies as they are to your enemies. Conjurations such as animate dead or the monster/animal summoning spells can instantly create allies, doubling or even tripling the ranks of your force. At very least, you are placing a group of cannon fodder in between you and your foes, allowing you to pick them off with ranged attacks. Wands of monster summoning are especially powerful items, and if you can acquire one, your wizard will become a particular menace.
Tactic# 10: Remember, fighter mages can use wands even when wearing armor. You can use a wand based fireball to soften up a beast before you dive into melee with it. This also works great with magical items such as the wand of polymorphing, which requires that you close into melee range to use, never a smart idea for a wizard.
Tactic# 11: Do not underestimate the power of missile attacks. A bow specialist may lack the pure damage of a melee warrior, but the ability to strike from long distance more than makes up for this. With an archer, you can hit and fade, using terrain to massacre your enemies. Missile specialists become especially deadly once they acquire magical arrows. Always keep a good sword or mace as one of your quick weapons however, in case a foe closes into melee with you.
Tactic# 12: Clerics and paladins can turn undead, an ability that can save your entire party. Most of the undead you will face in Baldur's gate can be easily turned, or even blasted out of existence by one of these holy warriors. Evil clerics or anti-paladins can CONTROL undead instead of turning them. These evil characters can enslave the creatures to do their bidding. A powerful ability, but be sure to dismiss them before entering a town, as most people do not react well to a mass of skeletons or ghouls wandering about their village.
Tactic# 13: When choosing missile weapons, there is a staggering selection available. Daggers and darts have a advantage in the number that can be thrown, but they are severely limited in range. Sling stones are rather weak, but are capable of harming creatures like skeletons that normally shrug off damage from piercing weapons. Bows are a good middle rage weapons, with respectable range an damage. Crossbows have a greater range and do more damage than any other missile weapon, but are slower to fire than bows. Magical projectiles abound for each weapon type, so the choice depends on playing style and preference.
Tactic# 14: Don't be scared to run away. Often times you will simply be overmatched beyond any hope of victory. The game uses a true random encounter table, so it could very well pit you against a creature you have no hope of defeating. If the fight looks hopeless, flee.
Tactic# 15: Set traps! Certain spells like glyph of warding and skull trap explode when an enemy touches them. Cast a few and have the party wait behind them a several yards. Creep out with your thief and find the bad guys. Backstab one of them to get the stone rolling, then haul balls back to the wards and your waiting party. Pursuing the thief, the monsters will blunder stupidly into the traps, WHILE your party opens up with a can of their nastiest long ranged whoop ass. Otherwise powerful opponents will get hammered by this tactic. Be careful, your enemies set traps too....
