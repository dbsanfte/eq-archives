Brief Synopsis for Presentation

 

          

            A new technology entering the electronic market today is the introduction of Online Billing Systems.  These systems allow for the collection of all or most of your personal bills to be collected in one central location.  This location is your own personalised database of all your bills. There are two major front runners in this new venture, E-route and Canada Post’s Electronic Post office(EPO).  Both ventures have pros and cons as well as security and privacy issues that must be dealt with before Canadians accept Online billing as a viable alternative to paper billing.  It is interesting to note that the privacy issues seem to be more important to the older generations than it is to the young.  This fact may hold the key to whether or not either one of these billing systems survives.

            The purpose of these systems is to allow the Canadian public to pay their bills online in on central location.  These two new systems also eliminate the need for a paper bill to be sent to your home, instead everything is sent directly form the biller via the Internet.  This will help reduce waste as well as make bills easier to pay online.  E-route and Canada Post are also working in conjunction with some of the major telephone and cable companies across Canada in order to have them giving their customers the option of paying directly online.

E-route and Canada Post both have some major backers that may propel them ahead of the other.  The E-route system is supported by six of Canada’s leading financial institutions comprised of Canada Trust, Canadian Imperial Bank of Commerce, Mouvement des Caisses Desjardins, National Bank of Canada and TD Financial Group and Royal Bank of Canada.  At the same time, Canada Post’s system is backed by the National Bank of Canada and the Bank of Montreal.  This provides both systems with a solid base of potential customers for future growth.

As stated before, one of the major problems that Online Billing Systems are facing, is the effort to be accepted as a safe and secure system, where people can feel confident about exchanging their personal information.  This is such a major issue that many people today do not feel safe doing transactions online.  Typically, however, this feeling of insecurity is divided between generations.  The results of a survey we performed showed that the younger generation feels a great deal more secure about exchanging personal information on the internet, than the older generations.  

The Canadian Government, however, is currently working on a new bill that will hopefully subdue many of the worries that society has concerning this issue.  Bill C-6 addresses the rights of privacy an individual has when he/she allows her personal information to be collected.  The bill will eventually apply to all businesses that do this sort of information collection, and will ensure that the individual's consent is obtained at all times when this information is being handled.  Thereby, a company can only use the information it gathers for the sole purpose it originally stated and that which was agreed upon by the individual.

As we have just seen, both of these new electronic billing systems have their respective advantages and disadvantages.   Security and privacy are a very big issue when it comes to any information being sent over the Internet, and in the case of the billing systems, the issue is still up the air. However, new laws such as Bill C-6, and an increased awareness among the younger generations, seems to point towards the acceptance of Online Billing Systems, and it would not be surprising to see their base of customers grow substantially in the near future.
