Hail fellow Truthseekers,

I have recently discovered some interesting documents concerning the history of our guild, which I would like to share with the rest of you out there. I do not know how many of you know our guild’s past, but I had very little knowledge of it, until now. This is an account of what happened to me a couple of days ago while wandering around Norrath.

As I heard the first roar of thunder in the distance, I knew it was time for me to seek shelter. I looked up to find that the once so sunny and beautiful sky was almost entirely covered with dark, heavy clouds. I knew then that I was too far away from Ak’anon to get back before the rain would leave me soaked.

Damn. I quickly gathered my belongings that were spread all over the ground and decided to seek shelter in the abandoned mines which I had visited a couple of weeks ago. Hmmm, that was odd, I thought to myself as I approached the entrance to the mines. A faint trail of smoke emanated from the hidden chimneys on the mountainside. Curious, I approached the entrance with caution, as I knew that no mining had been going on for the last decade or so. When I got nearer to the entrance, I could see a faint glow emerging from inside, but only for a brief moment. Feeling the first drops of rain on me, I threw all caution to the wind and ran towards the mines. I barely got there in time before the full effect of the storm that had been brewing hit the mountains.

As the storm grew in intensity, darkness slowly descended upon the Steamfont Mountains. Exhausted, I dropped to the ground, happy that I had made it to the mines before the downpour really set in. Occasionally a flash of lightning would penetrate the pitch-black darkness that had enveloped the mountains. Content with my dry place to rest, I just sat there, enjoying the constant roar of thunder mixed with the steady rhythm of the rain beating on the ground. I do not know how long I sat there, listening to the storm, but it must have been quite a while.

I began to feel hungry and thought it best if I moved further inside the mine, to a place where one of the chimneys would channel the smoke from my cooking outside. That was when I remembered that there had already been smoke coming out of the chimneys before I got to the mines. Maybe other travellers had sought shelter within these solid rock walls.

Using the constant flashes of light from the lightning, I located my backpack and found my trusty Stone of Light. I cannot remember how many times it had saved me from stumbling into deep chasms while I explored the underground. The stone had been a gift from a very friendly ranger I had helped save some animals from a roaming band of gnolls, but that is another story.

As soon as my eyes had adjusted to the increase of light I started out in search of the other travellers. The first fireplace I reached had not been in use for some time. In fact, not much in these deep tunnels showed any sign of being frequented by any sort of living thing. Everything was covered in a thick blanket of dust with months old cobwebs hanging everywhere. The second and third fireplaces seemed as desolated as the first one and I began to wonder if indeed I had seen smoke coming from the chimneys. I knew of one more place that was close by which I would ] check out before heading out for the surface again.

Rounding a corner while brushing away the overhanging cobwebs, I came to a small cavern I had not seen before. Before me were five passageways evenly spread, giving the impression of standing upon a giant hand. Peculiar, I thought to myself. I had not missed any tunnels or paths on the way down here. Upon further examination of the tunnels, I noticed that two of them had recently been chiselled at. I was not aware that the gnomes had begun mining again, so who could have made these new tunnels, I asked myself.

While contemplating which path to take I heard a faint noise coming from the left ‘finger’. Suddenly, I realised that I was completely lost deep within the mines, alone, and without a clue as to who was down here with me.

As I approached the source of the noises, all sorts of tales started to drift through my mind. Tales of old speaking of horned beasts, gigantic floating eyes so evil that you did not dare think of them and numerous other dreadful tales. Sweat started to trickle down my brow. Be it from fear of the tales or the intense heat I do not know. Judging from the noise I was getting close and as I rounded a corner I could see what had caused it.

Shocked, I stood at the mouth of a large cave deep within the mountains with various fabled monsters. I gasped for air as I gawked at the creatures. Never in my many travels had I ever seen such dreadful monstrosities. To this day I still lack the words to describe them. What they were doing I do not think I will fully comprehend ever, but my best guess would be that they were looking for something in the Underground or mining, and the way they were doing it was incredible.

I do not know how long I stood there staring at them before I collected my wits and just in time. A couple of the workers in the cave had noticed me and started yelling. Scrambling back through the tunnels as fast as I could, I lost all my equipment, my weapons, my Stone of Light, everything, but I did not care. All I could think of was getting as far away as possible from those abominations.

I still shudder at the thought of what they might have done to me had they caught me. Running as fast as I could with no light I managed to elude the monsters somehow, but my joy was short as I stumbled upon a chasm. As I plunged towards the bottom, all I could think of was not to scream as it would alert the monsters to my whereabouts. The impact with the cold hard stone floor was not a pleasant one. I was knocked out cold.

Waking up from what must have been an eternity judging from the craving from my stomach and my parched mouth, I slowly got on my feet again. My entire body ached but luckily I had not suffered any serious injuries apart from a couple of broken fingers. There I was, sitting in the dark in the Underground, completely lost, with no equipment, no light, no food, and pondering how I could get out of there.

After contemplating the situation for some time, I started to see how steep the wall was and whether or not it was possible to climb up again. If I had not broken my fingers on the one hand I might have been able to get back up again, but I dared not risk another fall if I failed.

Searching for another way out I stumbled upon what appeared to be a halfling corpse. It must have been down here for eons as nothing but the bones were left and its backpack. I quickly searched the backpack for a torch or some other useful things. Two torches, a tinderbox, a rusty dagger, a locked box, some rolled up parchments and a ring was all I found in the backpack.

Lighting a torch, I saw that at the far end of the chasm was a staircase chiselled out in the rock. It would seem that my departure from the Underground might not be so difficult as I had feared. My mind put somewhat at ease by that fact made me ponder who this halfling was. You might think this was reckless of me, but I have always had an inquisitive mind and I could not help myself. So instead of hurrying out of the Underground, I sat down and started to read some of the pieces of parchments.

I never learned the name of the halfling, but what was revealed to me in those hastily scribed letters still astounds me. I will not go into detail what they said, suffice it to say that they concerned themselves with an ancient order called the Truthkeepers. That order possessed knowledge of the rise and fall of the Barbarian empire, the Human legacy and the Erudite rebellion. It was all connected with what has later become known as the Combine Empire. What their knowledge consists of I do not know, but I do believe that the Truthseekers are a direct descendant or offspring of that order.

How that knowledge was lost I can only guess at.

This concludes my tale I wish to share with my fellow guildmembers. Exactly how I got out is another one I might tell sometime.
