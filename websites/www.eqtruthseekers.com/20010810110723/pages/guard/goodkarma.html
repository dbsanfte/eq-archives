My Interpretation (Goodkarma)

I sit here typing this with the Charter printed out in front of me and my heart on my sleeve. Sator's request for comments that are in line with our Charter has prompted this. Unfortunately, I cannot simply do a literal interpretation of the issue at hand without also voicing my personal views, but I will try to keep them separate. What follows is my interpretation of the issue at hand. I have used Webster's Ninth Collegiate Dictionary as my reference. There are three parts to this post: the first is my attempt at a literal interpretation of the issue of guard killing as it relates specifically to the Charter; the second are my personal views on the issue; and lastly are my final thoughts on this game in general. For purposes of this post, I am assuming that guard killing and guard camping are one and the same; that is, the killing of a guard.

Literal Interpretation

The Charter is very clear on several issues; leaving only the interpretation of the words open. "The Guild is bound to help citizens in need of assistance." If guard killing were allowed, how can we say that we are not breaking this code? Whether we want to admit it or not, guards do provide assistance to those in need; and not just the newbies. I myself have had to run to the guards in NK with a cyclops or GrimFeather hot on my trail and eager to taste my blood. By killing a guard, we are effectively taking away a form of assistance for others in this land. A literal read of this code then would suggest that, albeit not a direct infraction, literal interpretation could be said to indicate that by killing a guard, we have effectively caused a citizen of Norrath to lose assistance. No matter how much we would try to replace the guard we have killed, we simply cannot replace that guard once it has been slain. Trains are often long and, even if the Truthseeker is a very high level, it is easy to become overwhelmed by a train of 15-20 creatures, even if they are green, we could be low on health and/or mana after killing the guard, thereby not being able to render the assistance the guard would have, etc. If we are to stay true to our Charter and help all citizens in need, can we then justify allowing guard killing knowing that by doing so, we are NOT rendering assistance to those in need and, in fact, are putting them in harm's way?

"Giving due respect to all people of Norrath regardless of experience." Due respect regardless of experience - we have with this statement acknowledged that all people have different needs from our own and have promised to respect those needs; the need of a level 26 magician with GrimFeather hot on her trail is to have the guards at the stone bridge in NK ready to help her; the needs of a level 26 magician with an angry hill giant on her trail is to have the guards at the toll booth in WK ready to help her; the list goes on. One of the definitions found in the dictionary for respect is: "to refrain from interfering with". By killing guards we are interfering with other citizens and their needs for the guards. Another definition found in the dictionary for respect is: "an act of giving particular attention". By setting forth in the Charter we do not condone guard killing, we are giving particular attention to the needs and beliefs of all citizens in Norrath above our own selfish needs and thereby, in my opinion, the Charter would be consistent in terms of the above statement regarding rendering aid to all already contained in the Charter when coupled with a statement of no guard killing.

"Through good works the Guild should promote a reputation of honor." Honor - probably the most difficult concept to interpret as it is tainted by each individual's personal thoughts, opinions and heart. But as this is the literal comparison of my post I will do my best: honor, as one definition in the dictionary states is "one whose worth brings respect". How can we hope, as a Guild, to have people acknowledge us for our worth if we do not do that which our Charter dictates? As I have interpreted above, we are bound to be good and render assistance on Norrath. How can we earn respect from those around us if we do not hold ourselves to our Charter? Another definition of honor: "one's word given as a guarantee of performance." Our word should be our bond. We should pride ourselves on the fact that we have, in our Charter, aligned ourselves with good. If we do not stand by that word, what guarantee does anyone (including our fellow guildmates) have that we will follow through on anything we say? Can we truly say our word is our bond if our Charter is inconsistent and allows something in it contrary to it's base and core values? Who would want to join us in our adventures knowing that, when a Truthseeker says "you have my word" it doesn't mean all that it should? Interestingly enough, the dictionary lists a synonym of honor as reverence and makes this statement "implies profound respect mingled with love, devotion and awe." Stop and think a moment. What better testament to a Guild as a whole and us as individuals to have those around us not only respect us, but honor us, love us and feel devotion to us based on our beliefs and our actions. A truly lofty goal, perhaps...but a goal definitely worth attempting to achieve.

"Stealing from any player in the game will not be tolerated..." I initially was not going to include this statement, but I came across an intriguing little definition in the dictionary. One definition of stealing: to take the property of another wrongfully and especially AS AN HABITUAL OR REGULAR PRACTICE. I think my emphasis says it all. As I stated, I have assumed guard killing and guard camping are one and the same for this interpretation. Camping is nothing more than the habitual and regular practice of killing the same thing over and over and over again. Therefore, by literal definition camping/killing guards is literally stealing (and I know I am opening a can of worms here but this statement is made in relation to guard killing/camping ONLY, but it does sort of tug at the conscious, doesn't it?).

"No involvement in Guild War." Okay, you're going to have to bear with me on this one a bit...it is a stretch but I think it's an intriguing thought. If we are to assume the definition of a "guild" as being "a group of those who are like minded, bonded together with common goals and traits", are not guards their own "guild?" Do they not all act the same (with the exception of the corrupt guards which are really not that dissimilar to any member of a guild who strays from the path)? Do they not have their own Charter (to protect)? Now, assuming a definition of "war" as being "a pronouncement of violence by one group against another group", by adding language to the Charter that guard killing is allowed, have we not just declared war on the guild of guards? Again, it may be a big stretch, but it is worth a moment or two of your thought.

Personal Views
Now I open myself up to some potentially painful criticism, but onward I go. I do not believe in guard killing nor do I want to sanction it. I have a very difficult time killing anything that resembles a player of any race or class. I cannot, in my heart, kill a guard just for the sake of killing. I don't have it in me. Just as I cannot bear to be in the same zone when someone I know is in a duel, I cannot bear to cause harm to anything that resembles another player...be it an NPC, guard, etc. Although I strongly condone the right to defend oneself if one is attacked based on your race or class, I must admit to not being quite so adamant about it if you have killed NPCs or other like things and lost your faction with guards. You knew what you were doing when you were taking the faction hits. You acknowledged the trade off with your faction to whatever it was that you were seeking when you took the faction hits. Therefore, if a guard attacks you based on your faction hits, I don't think you have the same right to defend yourself without justification as, say, a dark elf does who is attacked simply based on his/her race. Although the Charter cannot be so specific as to state reasons for the attacks, if guard killing/camping not allowed, I do believe that a statement needs to be added to the Charter that states if a guildmember is attacked WITHOUT PROVOCATION, that guildmember has the right to defend himself no matter who or what is attacking him. But I personally think that by adding a statement to the Charter that says "Guard killing/guard camping is allowed" we have reduced ourselves and our ideals. Stop and think about how that looks to someone (especially ourselves) reading our Charter. We have made it a point to state in our Charter that we condone the killing of a specific group.

Final Thoughts

As I read through the posts on this issue, I am again reminded of something. There is no "end" to EverQuest; there is no way to "win" the game. This is an ongoing, fantasy based adventure. The comments about not having an easy way to obtain a circle, obtain money, obtain items, overcrowding, etc. all point to the fact that a lot of Truthseekers are trying to "win." We have, as humans, a need to bring a certain amount of closure to things (a definable end to a game, if you will) as well as a need to compare ourselves and our acquisitions to those around us (thus explaining why so many people inspect me). However, in my opinion, the true meaning of EverQuest is being lost in all of the above. I was in Greater and Lesser Fay last night - my first time in the areas. I was luckily traveling with an old friend of mine who knows me well and is very tolerant of me and my idiosyncracies. He told me of the giant chessboard...I had never seen it. He took me to look at it and I was in awe. How many of you have seen it? Have you ever stopped to wonder why certain pieces are lying on their sides on the ground? Were perhaps the Gods playing and one got angry when he lost and pushed them aside? I saw my first Emerald Drake last night. Have you ever wondered why their tail flick back and forth as they fly? Is it for balance? And how on Norrath can they fly with that big tummy they have? I saw my first fae drake hatchling (I think that was the name..I was so excited I may have it confused). It is like someone took a rainbow and put wings on it. A very good friend of mine told me recently that to look at the world as a child, for all it's wonderment and for all it's beauty is to know that it is a good day. When was the last time you had a good day on Norrath? When was the last time you stopped to look at the way the moon rises, the way the water ripples and reflects on the OOT, or the way certain markings on trees resemble animals? EverQuest is an adventure. It is about more than killing, gaining levels and obtaining items. It is about seeing things you've never seen before, appreciating all the beautiful and wonderous things that surround you, traveling to new places, and meeting new friends. Settle it in your hearts that you cannot "win". You will be much happier. I know I take a lot of grief for my "chatting" and how easily I am distracted by conversations and sites, but most of the time, when I log off from the game, I am happy and excited. Can you say the same?

Since this post is as much a part of me as of my character, I will sign this as it should be:

Goodkarma and Lynn
