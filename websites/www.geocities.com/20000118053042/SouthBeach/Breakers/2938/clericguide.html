Tily's Guide To Playing A Human Cleric
~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~





    Have you ever had to sit for ages to wait for your hit points to regenerate? Have you contracted rabies and gotten stressed waiting for it to wear off? Have you ever killed a beastie, only to die seconds later from poison damage? Have you ever wanted to help people in trouble without actually taking the thrill of the kill from them? Well if this sounds like you, and you want to play a character on Everquest that doesn't have to suffer from these complaints, then the life of a Cleric could be for you.
    But before you jump off and create a man or woman of the Gods, you should read a my warnings. The life of a cleric is not an easy one. It is true that you get to wear most of the armor in the game, but that stuff costs a lot of money, as do spells. So if you are planning on being a healer be prepared to save every copper you get, don't leave those snake fangs because they are only worth 1 copper each, stack them till you have 20 and sell them for 2 silver. Collect everything and sell everything that you don't need. To begin with it may seem a chore, but when you are ready to buy banded armor, keep in mind that at that point, your spells will cost well over 5 platinum each! Also you may want to buy a good weapon too ( although Clerics are restricted to blunt weapons ) which will be yet another expense. And there are a lot of spells that you will need to survive, but I will discuss those later. Also, remember that a cleric is not a warrior with spells. It is true that at the lower levels you will be able to tank quite well, casting strike at creatures and buffing yourself to amazing stats, but as the levels gain, you will become weaker and weaker in melee combat, and will have to drop back and prepare to heal your group as you attack monsters. A cleric is a healer not a fighter! Remember that.
    So you ask why be a cleric if it is so much hard work? Well there are a great many advantages, most of which have not been listed above. The main advantage being the high demand for clerics. Since there are so few, and their purpose so important, you will never have a problem finding a group. In fact it can get annoying at times with the amount of people asking me to join their groups, so that they can fight safely and not have to spend ages waiting to heal. No group is complete without a healer of some sort. You will learn this when you see warriors shouting, no pleading, for a kindly cleric to cure their poison before they die. You can also make a tidy profit accepting donations for healing people from disease, as well as poison and also for buffing them.
(although I never accept donations for helping others.)

    So you have read my warnings. Weighed the pros and cons. Are you ready to become one of Norrath's clerics? Let me start by saying that there are many different approaches to the creating a good cleric. Dwarves are believed to be the best race to choose because of their naturally high strength and wisdom. Personally I prefer to play a human for many reasons. Firstly they have higher charisma which will affect your money income. Secondly they are accepted in most other cities, and thirdly a dwarven female is not that aesthetic to look at! Keep in mind this guide has been written assuming that the reader will have at least some background knowledge of playing Everquest. For meanings of words and phrases that are game related the check out Allakhazam'a Magical Realm for definitions of game terminology

    Which ever class you choose, the most important stats remain strength and wisdom. Wisdom is directly related to the amount of mana you have and how many spells you can cast before you need to sit down a rest. It's no use being able to stand toe to toe with an asp, without having enough mana after the fight to cure your poison! Strength is also very important for a cleric. Not only for being able to dish out more damage in melee, you will also be able to carry more. What is the point in being able to wear full plate mail if you are not strong enough to carry it? Since this page is about playing as a human cleric, I would recommend you share your stats between the two, and maybe add a few to dexterity or agility to help from getting hit so much. But really you don't need to add that much, as clerics have very high AC thanks to their buffing spells. But it's better to not get hit at all right?

    Religion is very important to a Cleric as they derive their power from the Gods themselves. Choose the deity that best suits you, or that has a temple in the city you wish to begin in. I will write this guide for a human starting in Freeport, so there are two religions that you can choose from. But even if you do not wish to begin there, this guide will help you out as the first 5 levels are basically the same for everyone, no matter their class, race or starting city.

    So you've created your character and now you are ready to begin. Enter the world of Norrath and appear on the streets of  Freeport. Sit yourself down and memorize your two starting spells first. They are minor healing and courage, both of which you will be using A LOT over the next few days of your life. Minor healing is as you would expect a healing spell, the first in a long line of them, which will restore a lost hit points of your target. Courage on the other hand is your first buffing spell. Casting this on yourself ( or another player ) will add AC and HP. Meaning that you will be better armored and have higher health. Once the spells are memorized close your spell book and stand up. Wasn't that tedious? Get used to it as you will be staring at the inside of that book quite a lot until you reach level 8 and can learn the meditate spell. Now it is time for you to find your guild and hand in your guild letter. Look in your inventory and right click the letter to read it. Both possible cleric guilds are in north Freeport, so zone there and find your guild master. Your guild will be either The Hall Of Truth, or the Temple Of Marr. Both of these locations are clearly marked on the map inside your game manual, so go to the appropriate guild hall and find your master. Hand him or her the note and receive your tunic. Put it one and equip your club.

    As for training this is up to you. Personally I don't like to waste the points building up skills that will naturally increase the more I use them. I may die more often, but since at the beginning levels you don't loose experience and get food and drink, it doesn't really bother me. But if you are not as laid back as me, and would prefer being safer then feel free to spend some practice points. Personally I would advise you to build up your one handed blunt skill. You can of course build up your 2 handed skill but this would be pointless at this point as your club is one handed, and also because at later levels you are able to summon weapons which are one handed. Also I believe that one handed weapons are better than 2 handed ones. This is for several reasons again. Firstly, although you may be able to hit for more damage with 2 handed weapons, the delay time is longer meaning you don't get on so many hits. Also you will be able to equip a shield if you are carry a one handed weapon which will add to your armor value. And since clerics aren't fighters, having better AC if a nasty monster decides to attack you instead of your team members then you are much more likely to live.

    Now you are ready to take on the monsters that roam the "newbie" zone of your starting city. If you are in Freeport, head back to where you started and go out the west gate. Out here you will find rats, bats and snakes. For the time being stick to them. Avoid the orc pawns that roam this area as they could be very dangerous for you at the moment. I would advise that you decide on a certain vermin and stick to it. This way all loot you find will be stackable and you will not run out of space so quickly. Buff yourself with your Courage spell and get out there and start killing things. When you see your life is low after a fight, heal yourself and carry on. Keep buffing yourself when needed and watch out for the orc pawns and occasional wolf. You will find that you are staying out fighting longer than other players as they will need to sit down and rest, while you can just keep healing yourself. Sell your loot and carry on fighting. Get to know your way around this zone as you will be spending the next 3 levels here. As soon as you have 2 gold in your inventory head off to your Temple of Marr and buy yourself the Strike spell. ( there is no person selling it in the Hall Of Truth ) This is your first offensive spell and packs quite a nice wallop on those rats! By this point you will be ready to take on those annoying Orc Pawns and Fire Beetles. Keep in mind that if you attack an orc pawn, a fire beetle will help it. Be prepared to be double even triple teamed on, so know where the guards are if you have to run! You will start getting some better loot now. rusty weapons and cloth armor are worth a few gold. Fire beetle eyes are a better source of light than that silly candle, so equip one and destroy your waxy friend. Also keep an eye out for decaying Skeletons as they provide good loot. They also sometimes carry cracked staves which you can use as a one handed blunt weapon or sell for over a plat.

Keep fighting in this area until you are at least level 3. I stayed there till I was level 4, but at level 3 you will be able to survive in the East Common lands. By this point you should have quite a bit of loot and hopefully a backpack or some sort of bag ( you can carry more loot )...Buy yourself some more spells. Cure Poison is a good spell to have, but for the time being you will not need it, so unless you can afford to buy it I would wait. Instead, make sure you have the Divine Aura spell. This spell is amazing! It will grant you temporary invulnerability, and will save your tail many times! Unfortunately it has a very long recast time, so most of the time it will appear as a gray oval on your spell list. Don't waste it. as you never know when you will be pounced on by an irate critter.

Also if you have enough money, start investing in some cloth armor. It may not seem much, but it does help.

    Now you are ready to head out into the wilderness, I bet by now you are jumping for joy that you won't have to kill another rat again! Anyway, head out through the west gate and keep going until you zone. You will enter onto a sandy area. keep heading west until you reach the grass. Follow the edge of the grass to your left until you can see a path heading west across the greenery. Follow the path. You should pass three huts, keep going until you reach an Inn ( the FIRST Inn, there are 4 in this region ). Now prepare yourself for battle. Buff yourself and hunt around this area, killing spiderlings, firebeetles, moss snakes, black wolves, decaying skeletons, maybe black bears, and the ever annoying orc pawns. Make sure you con EVERYTHING before you attack. If you get into trouble head back to the Inn, where there is a guard and Inn Keepers to help you. Be careful though as this zone has a lot of high level critters that would chew you up in a matter of seconds. Beasties to look out for are Young Kodiaks, Lions, Lionesses, and the ominous Griffon ( if you see one shout where you saw it and run...these things use guards as toothpicks, so imagine what they'd do to you! ). There are other beasties too but they are non-aggresive and will leave you alone as long as you don't antagonize them. Keep killing and looting. You can sell your stuff at the Inn, so don't worry about having to trek back to Freeport. Stick at it until you reach level 5. Now you have a whole new set of spells to buy. By this point you should have the cure poison spell and full cloth armor. In my opinion the rest of the 1st level spells are a waste of money, but if you bought them don't worry, they might come in handy. The 5th level spells you will want to buy are Light Healing, Furor, Ward Undead, Holy Armor, Gate, Summon Drink, and Cure Disease (preferably in that order, but keep in mind all these spells are important.). Ward Undead is the spell that sets clerics apart from other spell casters. You will be able to devastate undeads with it. The decaying skeletons will crumble with only one blast, and you should be able to kill normal skeletons with ease. Holy Armor is a new buffing spell which will add to you or another players AC value. Furor is an upgraded version of your strike spell which will do twice the damage and make life a little easier for you. And Gate is a very handy spell to know. It will teleport you to your starting position, and will save time, and can also be used to escape nasty beasties. Also at this level you will be able to learn the channeling skill. This will please you greatly as once the skill matures, you will be able to regain concentration when interupted at casting and your spell will cast! So don't forget to spend at least one practice point learning the basics of this skill.

    By the time I reached level 5 I was bored with the way my character looked, and decided that it was time to start upgrading my armor. It is time to get some patchwork armor. You can either buy it from other players who are tailors ( it will cost you between 2 and 4 gold a piece ) or make it yourself. If you followed my advice of not wasting practice points, now would be a good time to use them to increase your tailoring skill. You should be able to practice it up to level 21 in one go and save a lot of time and money. Trade skills are a personal preferance, so don't worry if you don't want to use this one. You can always buy stuff from other players. My character has at least a skill of 21 in tailoring, jewel crafting, and smithing. It is very easy to do if you follow my advise and save practice points. But keep in mind you should really only choose one trade as they can be expensive to learn, so don't expect to become an expert in all fields, but at least you will be able to make basic equiptment. There are many guides to tailoring out there, so I won't go into detail, but for those of you who don't know the next paragraph will explain the basics.
 

    In order to make patchwork armor you will need three things. A small sewing kit, a pattern ( both of which can be bought at the Leather and Hide shop in east Freeport ), and a ruined pelt. The type of pelt determines the size of the armor you will make. A ruined wolf pelt will make small armor, a ruined cat pelt will make medium armor, and a ruined bear pelt will make large armor. MAKE SURE THAT THE PELT IS RUINED otherwise you will lose both the pattern and the pelt. Since your character is human you will need a ruined CAT pelt. You can get these from Pumas, Lions, and Lionesses, all of which roam the East Common lands. You might be able to solo a puma at level 5 but it is dangerous, so I would either team up with people, or use the auction command and see if people have the pelts for sale. You should be able to buy them for at most 2 silvers. Personally, I give all ruined pelts to any budding tailors I come across, and I'm sure other higher levels do the same so just ask. Once you have all the componants, open the sewing kit, and put one pelt and one pattern inside. Cross your fingers and press combine. With a skill level of 21 you should suceed 2 times out of 3. If it worked you should have a nice piece of armor, if not, then you will loose both the pattern and the pelt. Keep trying and you should have yourself a nice set of armor and a new look. You can also sell your wares for at least 2 gold each, so you can use this skill to make some money. You decide if this appeals to you.
 

    Okay....So now you have a new set of spells, some new armour and a basically feeling cocky right? Well be cautioned, you are now going to start taking on some new beasties. You should be able to solo black bears, large spiders, and possibly pumas and rattlesnakes. If you are more of a social person find yourself a tank character of around the same level and hit the dunes behind the Inn. Kill pumas and rattlers, but always con them first and be careful. At level 6 you can either stick to the dunes, or head through the cave up on the hill ( you'll see it ) and head to the North Desert of Ro. In a group you should be able to kill Sand Scarabs, Desert Tarantulas, and Jackels.
Watch out for the roaming mad men, shrivelled mummies and most of all the Ghouls ( I've seen one wipe out 10 people in a few minutes. And yes. I was one of those people! lol ). Have fun. Make friends. Heal people you see in trouble, and cure them if they need it. You'll become very popular and will not have problems grouping or finding help when you need it.
 

    Once you reach Level 8 put at least one point in your meditate skill. You'll finally be able to learn spells quickly and regain mana in minutes. At level 9 you will have some wonderful new buffing and healing spells, but I won't ruin it for you by telling you anymore.
 

    So there you go. You can become a great healer! Use your skills wisely and be warned that if you heal someone who if fighting a really nasty beastie, it may decide that you are a threat and attack you! Apart from that don't be selfish with your skills, help others, and the reward will be much better than money....
