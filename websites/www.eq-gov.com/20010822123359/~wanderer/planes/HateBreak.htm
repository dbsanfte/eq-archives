This information should prove invaluable to players inexperienced on Hate raids and would like to know what is going on. All decisions made by a raid leader should be obeyed even in direction contradiction to any information contained herein. For inexperienced raid leaders, this documentation may provide valuable insight into what goes on behind the scenes in running a smooth raid.


Preparation in South Ro

As is the case with most large party raids, the first stumbling block is gathering the right people in the right quantity at the staging area. When planning a Hate raid, it is important to ensure that a sufficient number of people are prepared for the raid well in advance and make the appropriate preparations. I would suggest that this include purchasing the necessary gemstones ahead of time, having non-porting players try to camp in South Ro ahead of time.

As people begin to arrive, the leader should have a fairly good idea of the composition of the main groups. At this point I will assume that the leader has had sufficient general raid experience to form sensible groups. As a guide, I will make the following suggestions. The make-up of all secondary groups is unimportant insofar as their roles are secondary and so long as they have healers, tanks, etc in roughly good combinations. Group 1 is the designated "pull group". Allow me to give an example of this group's make-up. This group should include a rogue to scout the area and a monk/shadow knight to feign death pull. One of the senior clerics should be in this group, as well as the senior enchanter. The other two members should contain a wizard to port up and one other. The sixth man could be a bard or a necromancer to ensure the enchanter and cleric both have mana. Group 2 should be constructed in a similar manner to group one. Any groups without a full time cleric should form into groups of 5 with one space left open for the primary wizard to teleport the group up. The "6 man" from each group should all group together to form five person groups of their own to be ported up afterwards.

Once general buffing and preparation has begun (and ideally is nearly complete) the wizard and rogue will port up alone. The wizard drops the rogue off and heads back down to rejoin the main group. The rogue's job is to watch the "zone" patrol and keep the war-leader informed as to when they have passed the zone in point. Once the rogue calls clear the real transport can begin.

The South West Break

The groups ferried up by the wizard should arrive on group at a time and invisible. The rogue should wait for them and guide them as they arrive to the main camp. Once a group arrives invisible and "safe" in Hate, they should exit the house and turn immediately right. Upon reaching the wall, the parties should make another right and travel along the wall. The idea is to stay as high on the wall as possible to avoid agro below and to remain invisible, of course. If Innoruuk is up, then people need to be careful that they do not walk too high up the wall as it is possible for him to agro from above during his merry march around the upper floor. Once on the wall, people will quickly reach the southeast corner. They should make yet another right and continue along the south wall. It is customary for people to stop at approximately the midpoint of the south wall to check for any agro. From here proceed to the southwest corner and once again stop to check agro. Turn right again and head north to a point just passed the house that forms the southwest corner alcove. This is the main camp area. If anybody receives any agro, becomes visible, or just generally irriates any Hate mobs they should stop and die like big boys and girls. Presence of mind is important here for newer initiates, as their instincts may tell them to help a comrade who has become visible or to drag that "one agro mob" to the camp, but the best thing to do is to slide down the wall to ground and just die where you stand. This clears up the run path for groups that are following along behind you and it keeps the number of deaths to a minimum. The rogue or another member of your group can drag your corpse to the main staging area where you can "safely" receive a ressurection.

An Alternative

Some war-masters like to cut out some of the random elements in their raids. If a number of wizards are available and this is your style then by all means send up several groups to kill the southern patrol route thereby making the zone in point that much safer.


Hate Once Inside

At this point, the wizard(s) has transported up all the groups and everyone has arrived at the main camp by some means or another. At this point the rogue-feign death puller team should begin scouting around. The following is a brief discussion of what this team is likely to find.

Zone Patrol

The first monsters you are likely to encounter on porting up is the zone-patrol. They will spawn on the east wall just north of the portal house and proceed south initially. Occasionally, one will stop next to a house to buff the mobs inside and then cut at an angle due SW, which could lead them into the port room itself, but they do not stay there long. However, since they are zone-patrol, they will usually be right beside the port room, so porting up could be a problem. Zone-patrol can be Cleric, Spite, Ire, or Rat, and in that order of appearance. These monsters are on a normal Hate timer (eight hours). They will "depop" if they reach the zone boundary and respawn again approximately thirty minutes later. They have a less than normal drop rate.

Normal Roamers

There are two patrol routes in Hate, the North and South. On both of these routes, there can be a Loathing Lich, a Revultant Rat, or an Ire Ghast. These patrols are on thirty-minute timers, so be extremely careful and pay attention to time. The route can run clockwise or counterclockwise, and the mobs can stop mid route and head back. On the southern route they like to stop on the south wall near the house adjacent to the portal house, on the northern end of the route near the center of the zone, and near the Totem (a pillar of faces with flames in their mouths). These patrols should be handled with caution and only pulled from a clear area. If they are pulled near a house that has not been checked and cleared the can (and often do) agro the static spawns inside. All the roamers have low drop rates.

Southwest Corner

The southwest corner is the other favorite pull spot (other than the zone-in room). There are three semi-static spawns there, and they are two Clerics and a monk. They are on a thirty-minute timer and have very low drop rates. They are here for one reason only... because it was a great pull spot away from most of the roamer path. By being a 30 minute spawn, it makes camping here much more difficult. I still prefer the zone-in room, even though you have to fight with more of the roamer path.

Static Spawns

The ones in the houses are static spawns, and even they come in two flavors. Some of the houses are huge, so they have fixed spawn points and roamers within the houses. Static Spawns have the highest drop rates and are on about an eight-hour timer.

I would suggest that the pull team clear some of the static spawns surrounding the main camp while being aware of the location of the southern patrol. Clearing the immediate surrounding area provides an element of safety to the main party and gives the puller room to pull the patrol without fear of "agro'ing" static spawns in the nearby houses. It was initially possible to pull to the intersection just north of the main camp point, but some time ago Verant modified the pathing in this area. Pulls to this location are no longer completely safe and some ugly pulls can result (two spitestone golems, for example). I would suggest that the southwest corner is cleared and the main party move to this location. A single, reliable individual should be designated to time these spawns as they are on a thirty-minute timer. Several minutes before they respawn, normal pulls should cease and the party should prepare for the repop. Individual pullers have their own tactics and pull order and thus will not address this in this documentation. However, the pullers need to pull quickly and thoroughly in a controlled manner. The idea is to find a rhythm and pull at the maximum rate that the main group can handle. This is important since many people cannot afford to sit in Hate for long hours. Raid leaders need to be aware of this. A not uncommon complaint amongst enchanters (for example) is that Hate takes too long to clear and that the Forsaken Revenants on the north side of the zone are never reached in a feasible time.


The Maestro of Rancorr in brief

Once a clear path is made to the Maestro's room, the main group should be moved to the front entrance to his house. This is a large house with an entrance near the center of the zone looking out at the ramp to the upper level. There are several monsters inside this house, which should be cleared before staging an attack on the Maestro himself. The main group should move far enough inside this house to avoid any exterior agro yet far enough away from the organ to avoid agro'ing the Maestro himself. A renewed buffing time should be set and every effort made to thoroughly buff up in preparation. The general strategy for Maestro is to send in the tanks in an initial assault and keep the casters far enough away to avoid the life drain. When Maestro's health gets low the casters should move closer into casting range preferably all in one tight group. It is annoying and deadly to have the casters spread out or within his life tap range early on. If the casters do not stay together then Maestro will agro them individually and fly around the hall erradatically. This makes it impossible for the tanks to continue the fight and is generally not welcomed. The casters should obviously nuke all out for the kill at this point. A more detailed look at Maestro is beyond the scope and intent of this documentation, so please refer to further information regarding him as needed.

Furthermore, a discussion of pulling order, techniques and the tactics for assaulting Innoruuk and the upper level is also beyond the scope of this documentation.
