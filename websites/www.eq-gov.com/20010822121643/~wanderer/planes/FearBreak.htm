This information should prove invaluable to players inexperienced on Fear raids and would like to know what is going on. All decisions made by a raid leader should be obeyed even in direction contradiction to any information contained herein. For inexperienced raid leaders, this documentation may provide valuable insight into what goes on behind the scenes in running a smooth raid.

Preparation at the Portal Staging Area


As is the case with most large party raids, the first stumbling block is gathering the right people in the right quantity at the staging area. When planning a Fear raid, it is important to ensure that a sufficient number of people are prepared for the raid well in advance and make the appropriate preparations. I would suggest that this include purchasing the necessary gemstones ahead of time, having non-porting players try to camp at the portal ahead of the meeting time, and finalizing the break time well in advance.

As people begin to arrive, the leader should have a fairly good idea of the composition of the main groups. At this point I will assume that the leader has had sufficient general raid experience to form sensible groups. As a guide, I will make the following suggestions. The make-up of all secondary groups is unimportant insofar as their roles are secondary and so long as they have healers, tanks, etc in roughly good combinations. Group 1 is the designated "pull group". Allow me to give an example of this group's make-up. There should be two tanks, preferably one ranger and the other is ideally a warrior. One of the senior clerics should be in this group, as well as the senior enchanter. The other two members should contain an evac'er and one other. The sixth man could be a bard or a necromancer, to ensure the enchanter and cleric both have mana. Group 2 should be constructed in a similar manner to group one.

Once the majority of the party is gathered at the portal, the buffing needs to begin with the clear understanding that the break will commence once this is complete. The choice of buffs is fairly obvious. Skin, stat, regenerative buffs (mana and hp), and ac buffs are a given. Magic and disease are the only resist buffs that have any business being cast. Those circle buffs that druids cast out of habit are completely unnecessary and on a thoroughly buffed tank will take up valuable space. Ideally everyone should have Dead Man Floating as the levitation component reduces z-axis agro, which is most important for the break in.

At this point the raid leader should designate a feign death character to enter Fear. For the break this will character's judgment will be crucial, but is a final way of checking that no one is already inside. The raid leader should be paying attention to time at this point. Everyone needs to be fully buffed and the casters need to have time to restore full mana but the deadline is set so that buffs do not begin to wear off. Groups 1 through 3 should be fully buffed, including dmf (or levitate and see invis) and ready. These groups should move to the ramp in front of the portal. When an "all clear" call is made by the feign death character inside, these three groups should all "invis" up and prepare to zone in.


The Initial Break In and Common Outcomes

On the leader's call, group 1 should zone in. After 30s pass from when the last person in group 1 zoned, all of groups 2 and 3 should also zone in. The outcome of this event is complicated and as such there are no hard and fast rules for how to proceed. I will summarize the common outcomes and discuss some guidelines for how to continue given more common circumstances.

The Blessed Outcome

The "ideal" case is that all three groups arrive safely at the portal inside and no immediate agro occurs. In this case each group should wait for all members of their group to arrive and then proceed down the hill to the west wall. This is the initial base camp. No other groups should zone in at this time! These three groups have sufficient numbers to break Fear and in the result of anything going wrong the people outside will fill the fallen ranks in the first three groups to continue the break. It is important to keep these other groups informed and interested, as this is a very frustrating time for those waiting at the portal. The puller should begin a very close survey of the immediate area surrounding the camp. Once the party (groups 1 - 3) lower invisibility, there will be some mobs which agro and attack shortly thereafter. The enchanters should be ready. For these fights there is no need to fight all the mobs! If large numbers "add" in the initial fights, kill the one currently being attacked (and perhaps one or two more afterwards) then evacuate. The idea is not to make a stand and fight until the bitter end. Kill what you can and evacuate if necessary. The raid leader should be the only one calling evacuation unless he is dead (make a suitable chain of command).

More Common Scenarios

A more common outcome is that one or two people become visible. If this person is not the evac'er, the cleric, or the enchanter of that group then that person is left to die. The group proceeds down to the west wall and drags the fallen corpse with them. This outcome closely resembles the most favorable outcome and may proceed in a similar manner.

If a group's cleric, enchanter, or evac'er becomes visible while zoning in, then that group should immediately attack to defend their comrade. The enchanter does crowd control while the tanks get on a single target. The druid or wizard in the group should avoid agro and be ready to evacuate at any time. This group should aim to kill at least one mob and then teleport out.

The more desperate scenario is that groups 2 and 3 arrive in Fear to find a mess of mez'ed mobs at the portal and group 1 in heated combat. In this case, all three groups should assist and try to kill what they can before teleporting out.

Summary

There is no harm in teleporting out of Fear on the break. The only aim is to kill as many mobs as possible before escaping and minimize the loss of life. Once the groups reform at the portal the process may be repeated as many times as necessary to break in fully, and fallen members of groups 1 through 3 can be temporarily filled using the full dressed battle ready members of the other groups waiting. If you evacuate on the initial break, have dead people inside, and haven't killed anything, then you are not doing well and should prepare for a long night.

The aim is to get three groups to the west wall and clear all wandering mobs from the portal hill and anything that is wandering immediately to the south of the hill. Fear is considered broken when there are no mobs that wander on the hill surrounding the portal and you can safely zone in at any time unless a pull is in progress. The mobs here include turmoil toads, scarelings, fetid fiends, spinechiller spiders, and shiverback apes.


Fear Once Broken

The break is now complete and it is safe for the remaining party members waiting outside to zone in and join the three groups waiting at the west wall. The main puller should wait by the portal and keep that immediate area clear so that these people can zone in safely. Fear is considered broken at this point, and once the reinforcements arrive the raid should proceed smoothly.

Once the wandering portal area mobs are cleared, the puller should be clearing things north of the camp but staying clear of the main temple (well clear). Clear the Fetid Fiend house, the spinechiller spiders, the Amygadallian warriors, etc that wander between the camp and the north wall. Next clear the Tentacle Tormentors and any Graveyard mobs on the west side. Next look south and east toward scareling tower. An Amy warrior has a long path that comes close to the tower, so wait until he is clear of the area and pull scarelings. Gradually work your way east but stay well south of the temple!

As the puller moves east and more of the western side is clear, the main party has the option of moving east to shorten the pull distance. One option is to move the southwest corner and sit up on the ledge there, as it is a moderately "safe" point within the zone. With a more experienced group you can follow the puller east and first stop at scareling tower, then move on to the next tower east of there. I would suggest moving the main party as little as possible as it eats into the time taken to clear the zone.

Fear mostly clear

Eventually, a good raid will have cleared a majority of the mobs in Fear, specifically those that reside south of the main temple save a few mobs in the southeast. This includes Fright Fingers, Gorgons, GlareLlords, Amy Knights and Warriors, Fright and Dreads' temples, Fetid Fiends, Tentacles Tormentors, Scarelings, Shiverback Apes, Spinechiller Spiders, Phantasms, Samhains, Turmoil Toads, Worry Wraiths, and Boogey Men. A few Glare Lords, and other miscellaneous stray mobs may remain, but the zone is looking largely empty now.

The main party should return to the west wall camp at this point and the puller should scout the path north along the west wall. Assuming this is clear, the party should move north to the north wall camp. From this location, the puller has a shorter "pull distance" to grab Phoboplasms, Nightmares, and those remaining stray mobs. This is also the location to begin a staging for an attack on Cazic Thule. It is largely a matter of preference for the puller, but the temple area can be cleared from here or cleared from the west wall camp as the party moves back in that direction. If the temple is cleared from here, the puller should pull to Fetid House and let the "tagger" move south to tag one mob. The rest can safely be kited around the portal hill without Fear of agroing the main camp far to the north. Additionally should the puller make a bad pull, his corpse will be in an easily accessible location to retrieve.

This document is largely presented from the perspective of main party and does not address some tactics and information that is crucial to successful pulling. This is beyond the scope and intent of this document; novice pullers who don't have a tutor should review further resources.
