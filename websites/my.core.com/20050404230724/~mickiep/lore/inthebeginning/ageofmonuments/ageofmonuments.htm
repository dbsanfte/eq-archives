The Age of Monuments

Return of Venril Sathir
Over the next 500 years, the other races began to build new cities and cultures and intercontinental trade began once again. The goblins dug in the northwest, multiplying and raiding the giants and remaining Iksar. They established mines and armies, claiming the land for themselves. The giants returned to bickering among themselves, with alliances constantly shifting so as to keep the balance of power between them. The Froglok returned to their home in the Great Swamp, to their city of Krup. The Sokokar forgot their training and domestication, and became the foremost hunters of the Iksar in the Emerald Jungle. The dragons retreated to secrecy and seclusion, to once again pull the invisible strings of fate from afar. The Sarnak hid in Trakanon’s Teeth, and begin to slowly and secretly build cities and mines, and to train for the day when they would take the place of the Iksar as masters of the continent. Smashed and broken, the remaining Iksar fell back into a tribal existence, scraping out a life of hardship and torment in the Emerald Jungle and the forests.

A sledge explorer arose in search of the ancient wreckage of the fleet which sank at sea under the leadership of Rile long ago. He discovered the corpse of Rile draped in jewelry and took it whole as a trophy.

The corpse of Rile was then sold to a goblin king, Kaboktok, who placed it in his throne room for all to see. He claimed it was his goblin ancestor who slew Emperor Rile at sea.

The mountain giant king, Farslik, descendant of Warslik, descended upon a rich goblin city. The mountain giants, with the aid of frost giants, captured the city-mine. Farslik took the bones of Rile as a souvenir.

For the first time, the Sarnak ventured out into the ruins of the Iksar cities. Few returned, but those who did possess priceless scrolls dealing in dark rituals and forbidden magic. The Sarnak began to explore dark magic themselves, finding they have an uncanny knack for its use.

The goblins mounted an all out offensive on Konikor, sacking it for all its worth. Part of the bounty were the bones of Rile, which were sold to an Iksar merchant who then traveled east. The mountain giants retook Konikor that same year.

The merchant (whose name was Vlistis) sold the bones of Rile to the descendants of the Cult of the Arisen. They took the bones to Charasis and began to search for the Amulet of Sathir in the wreckage, hoping that by bringing the soul of the father into the body of the son they may perhaps bring back the first king of Sebilis.

Fifty years of searching finally revealed the Amulet and dark rites were performed upon it. The bones of Rile. Nothing happened however and the cult members believed they had failed utterly. They left the bones of Rile on an altar beneath the ruins of Charasis.

The Sarnak, hungry for more dark knowledge, journeyed into Charasis to loot its ancient tombs. A priest in the group found the scrolls the cult was using and in the presence of the bones of Rile unwittingly completed the ritual. Venril Sathir was snuked from his place of torment and after 1600 years was joined to the body of his backstabbing son. He rose from his bed-like altar and slew all the Sarnak. One of the Sarnak was spared, one that would loyally do his every bidding.

For one hundred years, Venril Sathir traveled among the ruined cities of the Iksar, collecting what remnants of power he could, and building about him an army of the dead. He traveled west and seduced goblin and giants lords alike with promises of immortality and untold power.

Venril established himself in the Dreadlands and began to rebuild the shattered ruins of the Karnor. His minions raided caravans for weapons and supplies, and he began to build expeditionary forces. He magically perverted some of the mortals to make a legion of lycanthropes, led by Thuuga, a wizard Iksar serpent changer.

The giants met in the council and decided to unite against an unknown enemy in Karnor, whose horrid legions were pillaging their cities and caravans. The goblins were asked to join to stomp out this new presence in the Dreadlands, and they agreed to do so for a price. They would receive Konikor, the richest mine in the world as their own. The giants agreed and an assault was planned. The dragons were the ones to leak information to both sides that this threat in the Dreadlands must be stopped, fearing themselves that this unseen force may be an Iksar wizard of reborn power. The dragons also convinced Thuuga that Sathir believed the lycanthropes were expendable and untrustworthy, and that he would eliminate them soon. Thuuga secretly joined the Alliance of the Stone against the undead.

The Alliance of Stone (giants and goblins) descended unwittingly upon the Karnor. Venril was alerted to the attack before it occured, however, by an agent of the Ring of Scale. The battle lasted for an entire year, and in the end, Sathir’s forces were crushed and driven back, most decisively by the betrayal of his lycanthrope troops. He took refuge in the Bile, at an outpost he named Varis.

Agents of the dragon council convinced the goblins that the giants had no intention of giving up Konikor (which was untrue). They counciled the goblins to sneak attack and take it by force while it was weak. At the same time, agents of the council advised the giants that the goblins intended to take Konikor in battle and other mines as well. The dragons’ plans worked, and war ensued.

A thirty year war was fought between the giants and the goblins, called the War of Lies (in that each side believes the other to be liars). It ended in a stalemate, with very heavy casualties on all sides. The giants retained control of Konikor.

The Sarnak established outposts in the Frontier Mountains and captured Sokokar young to train them for use in battle.

One of the members of the Ring of Scale disappeared over Trakanon’s Teeth. Trakanon was implicated, however no evidence could be found. In fact, it had been the Sarnak testing their prowess in combat against their dragon ancestors.

The Sarnak invaded and conquered small goblin mines in the Frontier Mountains. The giants took once again to squabbling amongst themselves. The Sarnak began to breed Goblin and Froglok slaves.

As the dust settled, the last of the gods came to Norrath. Mithaniel Marr, God of Valor, and Erollisi Marr, Goddess of Love, created the Barbarians, a hardy race who settled the cold and rugged northlands, near the ruins of the Giant empire. Being the youngest race, they were generally unwashed and rugged, possessing very few social graces. And while they too had a warlike culture, there were those amongst them who began to believe in something more.

By this time the other civilizations of Norrath had either long since declined, or were well on their way, and this small minority of Barbarians saw an opportunity to triumph where the others had failed. Perhaps this was a seed of wisdom planted by the Marr Twins, or perhaps it was only by chance, but as the Barbarians spread out across the lands, warring with both each other and any other race encountered, this tiny movement continued to grow. And so even amidst desolation and war, there was hope.
