New Racebike for 1999 Season!

We'll I just set my 1999 race plans and beyond. I'm now the proud owner of my second GS500. It's an 89 model with full Airtech fairing/solo seat, Progressive springs, Fox shock, K&N filter/no airbox & Supertrapp exhaust. The motor is basically stock inside with the charging system still working and the starter motor. I think I made a good deal on the bike, price? $1,050 US dollars.

So now I've got two GS's to take to the track. Gee just like the factory teams with an A & B bike :-)

I hope to have both bike dyno'd before the season starts so I'll keep the list informed.

It's great now I can put all those thoughts out of my mind about riding a SV650 or MuZ "Skorpion" and just concentrate on riding & racing GS500's.

Pickup 1989 GS500 from Joe

On Saturday January 16th I was finally able to make arrangements to pick up the GS500 I paid for in December. The previous owner lives in Birmingham, AL but agreed to meet me in Atlanta, GA. So Saturday morning I jumped in the truck about 6am, arrived in Atlanta about 12:30pm, and returned home about 8:00pm.

The bike was in essentially the same shape as the October AHRMA race in Talladega, AL when I raced against it. The tires look good enough for at least one or two more weekends but the owner mentioned the right fork leg was leaking. I was surprised by how dirty and greasy the bike looked, but then I wasn’t looking that close in October because I had no idea I’d be buying the bike. The nice surprise was the bike came with a rear stand, a Clymer shop manual, Suzuki factory shop manual, extra exhaust gaskets, extra oil filter gaskets, and extra spark plugs.

Sunday January 17th was a nice warm day in the 60’s so I decided to wash the bike and get a closer look at my new race bike. The first item I inspected was the chain, which I decided needed to be cleaned. So I used WD-40, a rag, and a toothbrush to removed a thick layer of old chain lube and dirt. The chain looked to be in decent shape once on the gunk was removed so I wiped off the excess WD-40 and applied some chain lube. WHITE WHEELS! I hope my next bike has black wheels, these white wheels on my two GS’s are a pain to keep clean. While cleaning the front wheel I noticed what I first thought was a brake fluid leak from the brake caliper. On closer inspection it turns out to be fork oil running down the fork leg and onto the caliper. I need to get the fork seals fixed before the first race and I’ll install new brake pads while I’m at it just in case they’ve been saturated with oil. I decided to call it a day after washing down the Airtech fairing and solo seat. I almost forgot I spent some time remove the corrosion on the battery terminals with water & baking soda. So this 3 hour wash/inspection has turned up only the fork seals and brake pads that need to be fixed before the first race of the year.

I noticed a few miscellaneous items that I want to change before the first race. The fiberglass body work needs a few places reinforced due to cracks and rips. The gel-coat has a lot of surface cracks but I can live with the cracks, it’s a race bike. The clubman handlebars are in a awkward position because they can’t be rotated down into position before hitting the top triple clamp. I’m using these bars on my 91 GS but I had a set of risers made to fix this problem. However a set of risers won’t work on this GS because the Airtech fairing mounts are very close to the bars. Also I need to build up the steering lock tab, that little piece of metal that mounts to the steering stem, because my hands smash into the gas tank before the ears on the bottom triple clamp hits the tab. Expert racers have already warned me that you need to build up this tab or risk having mashed-flat thumbs when you wreck.

On Tuesday January 19th I ordered some new parts from JB Cycle (also the eastern US Bridgestone race tire distributor). I picked up two sets of kevlar pads he had in stock. After we blew the dust of the packages he sold me these two sets of pads for the price of one new set. What can I say, I try to save a few dollars whenever possible. I’m staying away from the sintered pads because of so many other GS racers having problems with warped rotors. Also on order are new fork seals and a set of Vortex clipons. I hope to have JB install the new fork seals and set up the front end before my first race at the end of February.

On Saturday January 23rd I invited myself over to my friend and fellow racers garage to borrow his arc welder. With a little of his guidance I was able to weld a little more metal to the stock steering tab and decrease the bikes steering radius, thus no more mashed hands into the gas tank or fairing when I turn the handlebars. I spent Sunday removing the remainder of the decals and stickers with a hair dryer.

I hope to pester another friend before the first race, who has experience working with fiberglass, to help me repair the cracks in the fairing and solo seat. Then if needed I'll repaint the fairing with the gas tank so all the body parts are a nice clean white.

1989 GS500 - Part II

During the weeknights of January 25-29 I spent 2-4 hours every evening (except Wed) working on the bike. I replaced all of fasteners on the fairing so everything is a standard size and I also installed 3 quick disconnect pins in place of nuts & bolts. On a warm evening with the fairing off I degreased the engine. I drilled a 4 or 5 more bolts to accept safety wire. I probably wire more than I need to for a tech sticker, but I'd rather error on the side of being over prepared than risk a critical bolt backing out. Removed one rounded off bolt that holds on the front sprocket cover by drilling the bolt out, oh what fun. (Now I can finally figure out what gearing I have) This doesn't sound like a lot of time, and I'm sure I left out a few misc. items, but by the time you add in the drive to and from parts store your evenings become very short and sleep becomes a luxury item.

Saturday January 29th, I drove over to a friend's house who just happens to be building a fiberglass airplane, and harassed him to help me fix my fiberglass bodywork. The bodywork has some cracks/rips and a lot of stress cracks in the upper portion. We took the body panels into his garage and he started inspecting the work that needed to be done. His first reaction was this stuff sure is a cheap piece of junk. I informed him it maybe flimsy but it cost about $600 new from Airtech, so it's far from being cheap. He said the parts looked to be made of two layers of bi-directional fiberglass cloth which goes around corners & curves easily but doesn't give a lot of strength. We would be using triaxial cloth for the repairs. After placing the required respirator over my face we mixed some epoxy to spread on the triaxial cloth. The solo seat & lowers just needed a few cracks repaired, then we started on the fairing upper. My friend decided the upper was not structurally sound so we reglassed almost the entire piece with the triaxial cloth.

Weeknights Feb. 1-5. The fiberglass parts were cured and ready to be picked up on Tuesday. My friend did a great job filling and sanding all the parts smooth, so they're almost ready for a coat of paint when the weather gets a little warmer. The fairing upper is so much stronger now it's hard to believe it's the same piece of bodywork.

Sometime during this week I found RUST in the tank and not just a little pitting, it sounded like small rocks in the tank when I swished it around. Great, now I need to find another tank or "Kreem" this tank.

Saturday February 6th. Visited by my local Suzuki dealer and picked up all new bolts for the front sprocket cover and extra oil filter cover O-rings. When I tried to replace one of the old non-OEM sprocket cover bolts(10mm head) with a new one(8mm head) it wouldn't tighten up. When I checked the old bolt against the new one, the old bolt was slightly longer. OK now take one of the new longer bolts, cut it the length of the old bolt, file the end smooth, and presto it works. Now instead of the stock 3 different length bolts for the sprocket cover I have four different sizes, but at least they have the same 8mm size head. This is just an example of what you get with a older race bike, there's a lot of parts that don't fit like they did when it left the factory.

My daughter and I finally stuck my numbers on the bike, and a few lightening bolt decals just to cover up some of the fiberglass patches. I know there's not time to paint the tank and fairing before the first race so it will just have to look like a "mongrel".

Sunday February 7th. Today I decided to just switch the good 91 model gas tank between both bikes for this first race since I don't have time(plus the temperature needs to be at least 70 degrees for curing) to "Kreem" the 89 tank. For some strange reason I decided to take the rusted stock exhaust system off the 91 model so it could be sanded & painted. Well the bike must have been pissed because of all the time I've been spending with the other bike because one of the exhaust header bolts broke, damn! I messed around with the broken bolt for awhile then I decided to just leave it until I get some expert help before making things worse than they already are. Since the exhaust was already off I did sand off the rust and paint the thing.

On Saturday February 13th I plan to have JB @ JB Cycle replace the forks seals, install the clipons, and change the brake fluid on the 89. The 91 needs a rear tire and hopefully JB can extract the broken header bolt. I'm still pissing & moaning about breaking this bolt. Oh well I should have one fully functional bike before the race and that's the reason I bought two bikes in the first place. This is about all that needs fixed before the first race, I hope.

DeLand-AHRMA Race February 24th-26th 1999

Wednesday February 24 - Pulled out of the driveway about 6pm with a full tank of gas in the truck and caffeine drinks in the cooler. About 10 hours later and 5 cokes I pulled into DeLand to catch a few hours sleep before the gate opened at 7:30am

Thursday February 25 - The gate opened at 6:30am instead of 7:30am, so 3 hours of sleep will have to do. My first impression of the track is lots & lots of paved area. The DeLand event is held at an active municipal airport so the track is made up of runways and taxiways. The first practice session I went out on the 91 GS since this is the bike I raced last year to get a feel for the track. Before the next practice session I switched the gas tank off the 91 and onto the 89 (I still need to fix the rust problem with the 89 tank before it's used). This would be the first time I would ride the 89 bike at speed since I purchased it in December. My first impression is it pickups up revs much quicker and handles better than the 91. The 91 sat for the rest of the weekend and I only rode the 89 because it handles much better and has a much stronger motor. Gee,  I'm suddenly very happy with my decision to buy the bike even with all the little aggravating things that had to be fixed before this race.

Funny thing happened during the day, the one other GS pilot came over to introduce himself and he normally races with the CRA @ Brainerd, MN. It's a small world because after talking with John Ryti for a few minutes we realized we both know Rich Nelson. John was a great source of info about mods to the GS and he even helped my friend diagnose an electrical problem with his MZ Skorpion.

Friday February 26 - Race day and there is only one practice session before the races begin! I'm sure glad that I made the decision to make the practice day to get in some track time. During the riders meeting before the races some of the riders are complaining about the bumpy surface of the track, especially where a runway meets a taxiway. The starter proceeds to tell us the historical significance of racing at an airport because right after World War II motorcycle roadraces were held at airports or closed public roads. There just weren't that many race course facilities in existence at the time. He also mentioned that the riders from New Zealand said a lot of their race tracks are much rougher that the airport were going to race on. I thought it was interesting that I saw or meet racers from New Zealand, England, Ireland, Japan, France, & Germany, all trying to get some racing in before the big race at Daytona on March 1 & 2. It was mentioned during the rider's meeting that during the morning practice session that the fastest bike clocked on the speed gun was a Ducati "Supermono" @ 126mph.

My first race of the day is BOT F-2 which is open to 4-stroke twins 750cc dohc. Also racing at the same time would be the supermono class. I got a good start and after everyone else is up to speed I was passed by a bike or two on the first 3 laps of the race. On the next lap a Ducati "supermono" passes me on the straight and amazingly I caught back up on the brakes and by carrying more corner speed through the turn. Well I spend the remainder of the race dicing with a Supermono! I even passed the Ducati on the brakes and led for a few corners. It was tough to watch the bike pull away on the straights but it was well, "very cool" to catch the bike up on the brakes and in the corners. I'm laughing in my helmet during the race thinking about my $1,500 POS racebike passing a $30k Supermono. I finished about 5-7 bike lengths behind the supermono at the finished line and 5th in my class (BOT F-2).

Race 11 is my final race of the day, BOT F-3 (open to 4-stroke twins 650cc dohc). I got another good start and was the first or second BOT F-3 bike into the first corner. Two F-3 bikes came by the end of the second lap, on the straights of course. (I wonder what it would be like to pass a bike on the straights, probably won't find out racing a GS) On the third lap I heard a audible roar of a bike close behind me and a few corners later John Ryti came by on his GS500. We stayed within a few bike lengths for the remainder of the race and I tried to make a pass two corners from the finished line but came up a little short. I finished 4th in class.

This first race of the year was a was a lot of fun (high grin factor). I found out I actually can have fun riding a GS that handles well. Now I have to figure out why the 89 handles so much better than the 91. My guess is the tires. I can't believe I actually beat the 89 bike at the October 98 Talladega race when Joe owned and raced it. The bike not only handles better than my 91 it actually has more motor. That's it for now. If you have any questions or comments email me @ tbb10785@hotmail.com

WERA - Roebling Road, GA ( Mar 26-28th 1999 )

Friday March 26 - Friday morning was wet and cool so I decided wait to see if the sun was going to come up before paying for practice. I practiced last fall in the rain and the track has good traction except for the sealant patches. The problem is some of the turns have so many patches that they all can't be avoided which causes the tires to break loose as you ride over them.. With this knowledge in hand and sunny weather forecasted for Saturday & Sunday I decided I wasn't going to practice in the rain on Friday.

About lunch time the track was still wet so I decided to find a welding shop to get my steering tab welded back unto the steering stem on the 91 GS. When I got back to the track about 2pm the track was dry but the skies were still overcast. I wanted to get in a little practice but the officials wouldn't prorate the price of practice so I decided two practice sessions wasn't worth the $75 fee. I did make good use of my time because I found a vendor at the track who repairs leather and they sewed up a few loose seams on my leathers & also added a WERA patch.

Saturday March 27 - The two morning practice sessions went well and the 89 bike was handling great with the new Bridgestone tires. After lunch I was entered in the mini endurance, lightweight Solo 20 race. I got a good start from my back of the grid starting position and just continued to pass bikes until the end of the race. The bike ran great but continues to get passed on the straights by just about everything except the small vintage bikes. I finished 4th of 13 novice riders at the finish, just one place out of the prize money, however I was the first GS500 novice to cross the line. Since I traveled to the track alone, this is the only race I had someone to clock lap times. Another rider's pit crew got a few times on me and my laps were in the 1:29's.

Sunday March 28 - For the first practice session I decided to run the 91 GS which had the wheels and tires from the 89 bike that I raced on at DeLand. This was an experiment to determine if the handling problem I had last year was the tires. Well after a few laps I increased my pace and I can positively say that the handling gremlin I had last year was the tires. For me the 80 series Metzeler Comp K's really slows down the steering and makes the bike feel very heavy. Both the Dunlop & Bridgestone radial fronts make the bike handle well for me.

I switched the gas tank back to the 89 model for the next practice session. That's right I still haven't had warm enough weather to Kreem the original 89 tank which is full of rust.

First race after lunch is Clubman. I'm was gridded in the back because I don't have any season points but I did get a good start, so good that I thought I might get a meatball flag but I just timed it well. Going into turn one I moved all the way across the track to set up an outside pass and it worked because I passed at least 3 bikes. I passed a few more bikes during the next 3 laps and worked my way up to 5th place. For the remainder of the race I could keep the 4th place bike in sight but not make up any time. I finished 5th of 14 novice riders, first GS500.

My final race of the weekend was Lightweight Twins which is open to up to 750cc twins, excluding Ducati 748's. This race was a lot of fun because I was constantly passing other riders for the first 4 laps from my back of the grid starting position. On lap 5 I started catching a yellow bike in 2nd place and by lap 6 had made a pass on the brakes only to be repassed on the front straight. The bike was a older Ducati 750 F1 and he was pulling out 10 -15 bike lengths every time we hit the front straight, I couldn't even stay in his draft. On the last lap I made a pass in turn 3 and tried to put as much distance as I could before we came onto the straight for the checkered flag. In the last turn I was coming up to lap a slower rider and decided to pass on the outside. As I got beside of him he started moving over until I was almost off the track. We touched as I went past, his left handlebar touched the right side of my leathers. As I headed toward the checkered flag I was just waiting for the Ducati to come past and he did about 20 yards before the flag. It was a good race and I finished 3rd of 14 novice riders, and again 1st GS500.

As a side note WERA allowed Yamaha TZR250's and Aprilia RS250's to be Clubman legal this weekend. Let's just say this ended in a protest being filed and had quite a few rider's upset about the future of the EX500/GS500 based class. Send WERA a note at wera@wera.com if you have any suggestions or comments on the matter.

If you have any questions or comments for me send them to tbb10785@hotmail.com

Long Days, Going Faster, First in Class, & Baby Sitting

Friday May 14, 1999 The weekend started off by driving 450 miles to the track, we arrived about 4pm. I was busy until 11pm setting up our pit (actually my wife set the tent up), installing 3 new tires, and finally installing new clutch plates in the 89 bike. For this weekend I bought the new Bridgestone BT-45 140/70-17 rear along with the BT-56 110/70R-17. Yes I was tired but circumstances just happened where I ended up working on the bike at the track instead of being ready days ahead of time. I would recommend doing all your repairs and maintenance before loading the bikes for the track.

Saturday May 15, 1999 This was a combined WERA Sportsman and Vintage weekend so I decided to race the 91 bike in Vintage 5 since it meets the letter of the rules being superstock legal for a GS. I also signed up for the mini-endurance Lightweight Solo 20 race.

I practiced on both bikes before lunch and my best lap times were in the 1:07.3's. I funny I can do the same lap times on the stock 91 bike (with race tech fork springs & emulators, Penske rear shock) as the 89 bike with Airtech fairing, clip-ons, Supertrapp exhaust, Fox shock, and K&N with no airbox. On Sunday I posted my best time of the weekend of 1:07.03 which was 2 seconds faster than when I raced at Talladega during the October 98 AHRMA event (best time of 1:09.04). Even though I didn't finish on the podium in four races over the weekend I had to remind myself that I did drop 2 seconds off my lap times from my last visit to the track. Also I'm getting more comfortable with sliding the rear and not freaking out every time the rear tire breaks loose.

First race after lunch is LW Solo 20 I got a good start and passed a few riders going into turn one. I scared myself during the middle part of the race by running off the track twice but didn't lose a position. I was pushing too hard and wasn't riding smooth. My best lap time was 1:07.66 which was slower than in practice. I finished 8th and of 16 riders I was first GS500 across the finish line. The GS is really out motored in this class to give you and examples 1st place was an Aprilla RS250, 2nd-3rd-5th-6th were all FZR400's, 9th place was an EX500, 10th was a Ducati 750, 12th was a RS125, 13th was a Ducati 750, and 16th was a 650 Hawk.

I almost forgot to mention that before the solo 20 race the Yamaha RD vintage race was held and there was a crash and red flag. The racer pitted beside of us had wrecked on his RD and his wife was just about to lose it, so my wife watched their 4 & 7 year old children and I walked her over to the race officials. We found out her husband was conscious but the paramedics suspected a mild concussion and a broken collar bone. They decided to transport him to the hospital and of course, she wanted to go with him.  I told her to go and we'd watch their kids. Now we had just met these folks, didn't even know their last name but somehow we felt the responsibility to help them because we know how easily it could be me going to the hospital next time. We (mainly my wife) ended up watching these kids with our own 7 & 12 year for about 5 hours. The 7 year old was fine but the 4 year old was a pistol and my wife had to jerk a knot in her tail more than once. We were both very tired by the end of the day Saturday.

My next race was vintage 5 and I rode my 91 bike. I got another good start but got passed by a few bikes over the course of the race. I ended up 5th out of 9 riders but first GS500. I've never raced against a KZ1000 but did so in the race and finished one place in front of him. The riders out front were on EX500's and FZ600/FZR400 hybrids.

Sunday May 16, 1999 We had at least a 10 hour ride to get home so I signed up for my normal Clubman race and the only race before Clubman that I could run my GS was D Superbike. The racers in D Super usually ride built FZR400's, new Aprilia RS250's, Honda 650 hawks, and the new SV650. Yes my little GS was severely outgunned on horsepower racing in D Super but it would allow us to pack up and leave right after the Clubman race.

First up after lunch was D Super. I started on the back row which was a blessing considering all the bikes in the class had at least 15 hp more than my GS. The first couple of laps went OK but then there was a red flag. We restarted the race and I was last or second to last going into turn one. I passed a couple of bikes during the first 4 laps and ran lap times in the 1:07's. On lap 5 I caught up to a group of 3 riders but had trouble getting past. They were road blocks in the corners and I was braking harder but they would pull me 5-6 bike lengths coming off the turns and onto the straights. My lap times increased about a second to the 1:08 range while I was trying to find a way around these bikes. Finally on the last lap I was able to get around two of the bikes and finished 12th of 17 riders. The bikes I finished in front of were two Ducati 750's, a RZ350, a Suzuki 400 bandit, one FZR400, and one SRX600. Again I was first (and only) GS500 to get the checkered flag. Even though it was frustrating to be faster in the corners,  I'm learning how to pass bikes with more horsepower which is important on a GS500.

My last race of the weekend was the Clubman race. I got a good start and passed a bunch of folks going into turn one by taking the outside line through the corner. Over the next 5 laps I passed a few more riders and just as I set my sights on two bikes in front of me until another RED FLAG! The officials called the race since we completed 5 of the 8 scheduled laps. Damn I'd like to have run those 3 laps but on the bright side at least I made it off the track without any incidents. I finished 6th of 11 riders and first in class (first GS500 across the line).

In summary this was just a peculiar weekend and I wasn't sure if I had a good one or not. After 10 hours of driving and time to think about it I was ecstatic to drop 2 seconds off my laps times even though none of my finishing positions were on the podium. I also took some conciliation in the fact that I was first in class (first GS500) in the four races I entered. It was a pain to work on the bikes so much Friday but I didn't have to touch them the rest of the weekend. Finally it felt good to help someone else out at the track when they needed someone to watch their kids.

June 12-13 1999 Roebling Road

Wednesday June 9, 1999 This was suppose to be the first weekend of racing during the 99 season without doing a lot of work on the bikes in between events. As luck (or Murphy's Law) would have it Wednesday evening about 8:30pm I found that my previously repaired gas tank had a slight leak.  I only went into my storage building to get some camping supplies out of the attic so I guess in was nice to find the leak before I started loading for the track the next day. I purchased a spare gas tank 4 weeks ago but thought I didn't need it, you can never have enough spares. So it's 8:30pm and I need to replace the gas tank. The hard work was to enlarge the stock petcock hole on the new tank so I could use the Pingle petcock from the old tank. Since my idea of a machine shop is a hacksaw and file I ended up using a hand file to enlarge the hole. An hour later after enlarging the hole then rinsing the tank with a little gas I installed the petcock. Now it's 9:30 still plenty of time for sleep so I decided I can't live with a black primer tank on my white bike. I grab a drop light, a can of white primer paint , and go outside to paint the tank. My wife must have thought I was nuts when she looked out the kitchen window and saw some mad man spray painting a gas tank in the dark. 10:30pm mission accomplished, petcock installed, filler cap, tank installed on bike, and no gas leaks!

Friday June 11, 1999 I decided to skip the Friday practice and just use the day to drive the 325 miles to the track and set up our pit area.

Saturday June 12, 1999 The race schedule for the weekend was changed to a WERA National event (the Atlanta Speedway race was cancelled) so the endurance teams had the track after 10:30am. What this meant for us poor little club racers is we only got one morning practice Saturday and that was it for entire day. My practice times were good running a few laps in the 1:27:5 range about 1.5 seconds faster than the last time I raced Roebling in March.

With the remainder of Saturday free we decided to make it quality family time. We drove to Tybee Island and signed up for a 2pm boat tour on the Bull River to look for bottle-nose dolphins. Since we had a few hours before the tour it was time to hit the beach with the kids. It ended up being a great day, a little roadracing, swimming at the beach, a dolphin tour, and dinner at a great seafood restaurant.

Sunday 13, 1999 During the two morning practice sessions I was able to lower my lap times to the high 1:26's and low 1:27's. I was happy with the times since they were now 2 seconds faster than my March Roebling race. After this the day just went downhill, during the next practice group a 600 novice squid decided to interrupt everyone else's practice with a red flag and ambulance ride. This ended the last 2 groups of practice because it's quiet time from 11-12. Racing was suppose to start at 12:00 but they decided to let the last two groups practice first then start the 15 sprint races. Races 1 through 3 took almost 1.5 hours to complete because of the red flags and ambulance rides, so much for getting home at a decent hour. At this point the race officials shortened all of the remaining races from 8 laps to 6 laps. I just wish they would prorate the entry fee when this happens. You pay for a full 8 laps and I've got something less than that the last two weekends with WERA. OK I'll stop my bitching.

My first event was race #4 Clubman. I got a decent start and was dicing for 3rd most of the race with another GS500 and a RZ350. The first and second place rider took off and ran 1:24's-1:26's while the 3 of us ran high 1:26's- 1:28's fighting for the last podium spot. On lap 5 I got a slide in turn 5 and decided that maybe just finishing would be a good idea. I finished a few bike length's behind the 4th place RZ350 and Will (GS500) finished 3rd about 15 bike lengths in front of me. So I ended up 5th of 14 riders and for the first time this year with WERA second in class (GS500) . Congrats to Will for running a great race on his GS500.

Race #13 - Lightweight Twins. I got a good launch only to back out of the throttle and throw my hand up quickly (to let the riders behind me know I was slowing) because there was a bike and rider in the middle of the track! The race was quickly red flagged. I guess even racers who don’t ride 600’s & 750’s sometimes have an accident. The poor rider who fell could be heard screaming throughout the pit area as they loaded him into the ambulance. A very sobering sound for riders and their pit crew. Restart, got a decent start and ended up dicing with the same RZ350 rider as in the clubman race. The RZ350 rider beat me to the line by about 5 bike lengths. I ended up 8th out of 16 riders.

My weekend ends with a hot oil bath from a Hawk POS(Mid-Ohio)

Friday July 9, 1999 "Wow Mid-Ohio is big" is my first impression of the facility. Second is the security guards are some serious folks and don’t even bring your bicycle to the track. That’s right, your nasty 2-smoke pit bike is fine but don’t you dare ride a bicycle upon Mid-Ohio property. Also don’t plan to camp in the paddock it’s not allowed unless you have a self contained RV and pay your $75 fee. We ended up pitching our tent in the designated camping area with hundreds of rowdy motorcycle campers and left the bikes in the paddock area. I would recommend you stay in a hotel unless you have your own RV for a Mid-Ohio race.

Friday practice got off to a good start with no bike problems. The track is great if you like lots of blinds curves with elevation changes thrown in. It’s a great roller coaster ride and about the only bad part is the cement patches. The patches are raised higher than the surrounding asphalt by about ½ an inch. I started off in the 2:05 range, basically dog slow but by the end of the day had lowered my times into the 1:53’s.

Friday evening there was one heck of a thunder storm that rolled into the track about 6pm. The tent stayed up and the canopy but the bikes were blown over by the tornado like wind. The 89 bike suffered a broken windscreen and some broken bodywork, with the 91 bike getting a bent footpeg and some new scratches.

Saturday July 10, 1999 The one and only practice session for the day didn’t go so well. The 89 bike stopped running like it was out of gas so I got a ride back to the pits in the crash truck. First race BOT F3 and I switched to my 91 backup bike and got a big wheelie at the start because the clutch engagement was right off the handlebar compared to the 89 bike. After getting passed by about 10 bikes I settled down and went to work passing as many bikes as possible. Just like most of the other races this season I ended up going slower in the race (best time 1:54’s) due to getting stuck behind riders with more horsepower on the straights but who ride slower than me in the corners. I ended up finishing 11th of 31 riders and first and only GS500. Most of the other bikes in the field were either SV650's, Honda Hawks, or EX500’s. I’m sure my bike was the only 40hp bike on the track so I was pretty happy to finish were I did.

Second race was BOT F-2 and it went off much like the first race except the field was smaller. I finished 12th of 16 riders and my lap times were in the 1:53’s due to the smaller field.

Sunday July 11, 1999 During the one practice session I dropped my times into the 1:52 range on the 91 bike (the 89 still wasn’t running). First race BOT F-3. I got a good start and dropped my lap times into the 1:51’s and by the half way flags I was dicing against two Honda Hawks. On the 5th of 8 laps I got a mist on my visor going through "thunder valley" and before I could wonder if it was water or oil I was down on the pavement. The POS Hawk that was so fast on the straights but a slug in the corner had just given me an oil bath. The bike and I never left the pavement so the bike ground through a good bit of metal and I used up the leather on my suit. I was fine, not even a bruise, but the bike may be in bad shape. The rear subframe is bent, possibly the mainframe, and the forks look tweaked. Hawkman continued and caused at least one other rider to fall in the puke from his bike.

Weekend Summary The vintage motorcycle days at Mid-Ohio is a big event with an estimated 30,000 people in attendance over the weekend, so be prepared this is not your normal club event with a 1,000 people or less. I met at least 3 people at the track who I’ve only previously corresponded with over the internet so that was cool and enjoyable part of the weekend. The bad parts of the weekend were the bikes getting blown over in the thunderstorm, the 89 bike stopped running on the track, and the hot oil bath which caused my wreck on the 91 bike.

While getting times on my pit buddies in the Skorpion Cup race I decided to get a few times on the class leader. The Skorpion leader was running 1:52's & 1:53's and earned $300 for his wins on Saturday & Sunday from MZ. Damn I was running faster on my GS and it makes 5-6 horsepower less than a Skorpion and has a smaller rear tire. Now I'm desperately trying to arrange my finances to purchase a Skorpion for the last two AHRMA races of the year. My evaluation of running a GS500 with AHRMA or WERA is the bike isn't competitive in any class offered by either club. The best case is you race your GS against an EX500 in WERA's clubman class. Also all the classes are superbike rules so anything goes with the motor and I'm not willing to invest in a finicky grenade for a motor. With my limited free time between races and my limited wrenching skills I really would like to race a stock based class. I figured my choices are 600 novice production with WERA (or middleweight meat grinder class, thank you very little), the Aprilia Cup series, or the Skorpion Cup series. Since I don't like two-smokes my best choice seems to be the Skorpion class. I even went shopping for a Skorpion while on vacation and the best part is they come in ORANGE!

Sunday July 18, 1999 I found the problem with the 89 bike. My brain finally kicked in a week after the race. Remember to always go back to the last thing you did to the bike. In May I had replaced the clutch plates and to do this you have to remove the timing rotor. The bike worked fine for two weekends before Mid-Ohio but without any lock-tite (or without using a torque wrench) on the rotor bolt it had backed out. Lesson learned, I hope.

Roebling Road race report September 18 – 19, 1999

Relaxed equals faster lap times?

I started for the track Friday at noon with a friend from work and my 13 year old son. We arrived at the track by 7:30pm and unhooked the trailer and set up the canopy. (Usually I pull into Roebling after 1am and try to sleep in the truck until the gates open at 7am so this weekend was off to a good start.) We were checked into the hotel and hit the hay by 10am which was a good thing since the alarm was set for 5:45am.

Since the last race I found and purchased an enclosed trailer. Now I could fill a book on the stories about getting this trailer road ready but I’ll keep it to a paragraph. I found a 6W x 8L x 6.5H enclosed trailer in the classified ads for $500 and couldn’t resist the price. It was definitely a POS but after two new tires, some welding to fix the frame, a new turn signal assembly, new rear doors, and lots of TLC it was ready for the races. Did I mention that the trailer was custom built about 10 years ago, oh I mean homemade. I ended up with about 50 hours of my own labor and about $350 of additional materials, so I think I still had a good deal at $850 for an enclosed trailer. It holds both of my bikes, some additional gear, and it’s tall enough I can stand up without hitting my head. If you can tow and afford an enclosed trailer I would recommend you budget one into your next race season. An enclosed trailer allows you to pack a week or more in advance of the race. Also you can drop the trailer when you get home Sunday night, since everything is protected from the weather, and go straight to sleep.

OK enough about the trailer now on with the racing. Saturday morning the weather forecast was for a nice day but rain was very likely for Sunday. I did a quick check of the schedule and added Vintage 5 to the Lightweight Solo 20 race I normally race on Saturday. I decided to wait and see what the weather was like on Sunday morning before entering any races on Sunday.

By 8:30am the bike was through tech inspection and I was ready for my first of two practice sessions. This was my first chance to try the Bridgestone BT-96’s at a track I had raced at previously in the year. I rolled out onto the track and got familiar with the bike again after a two month layoff from racing. There were a lot of bikes on the track during practice but I felt I was running a decent pace. My friend had a little difficulty with my stopwatch and only got one timed lap at a 1:28.7.

 

During the second practice session I tried increasing my pace. I was starting to get my knee down in most of the corners and I had the footpeg touch down fairly hard in turn 5. This is the first time I’ve had any problems with running out of ground clearance on the GS. My best time was a 1:24.9 which was 2 seconds faster than I went at the June race with a best lap of 1:26.9. The strange thing was I felt more comfortable on the bike than during any previous races or practice sessions.

After lunch I got ready for the Lightweight Solo 20 race. This is definitely a step up class for the GS500 with the fast bikes being SV650's, FZR400's, and Aprilia RS250's. I was gridded about mid-pack and got a good launch off the grid, but as usual once bikes with more than my GS's 40hp hit second gear I got re-passed. We got bunched as we entered turn one so I went to the outside of the turn and passed 2 or 3 bikes and passed another bike before turn two. I tired to keep pace with a couple of FZR400's in front of me but after about 10 laps they were pulling away from me. The final laps were pretty lonely, I was too far away from the battle in front of me and no one came around me until the expert leader lapped me. I ended up crossing the line 8th of 22 riders and first GS500 to cross the stripe. My lap times dropped to 1:24.01 during the race and the rear end never broke loose. With the BT-45's I was sliding the rear at a 1:27 pace so now I'm a believer in the new BT-96 radials.

The last race of the day was Vintage 5. Again, I got another good start and rode around two riders on the outside of turn one and passed another before turn two. For the first four laps I tired to keep pace with the two bikes front of me. One was a KZ750 and the other was the leader of the Vintage 3 race run simultaneously with our race. I closed in on the two bikes on lap 2 when one of them got out of shape going into turn 4 and they both had to slow. However, by lap 4 they were slowing pulling away from me and I ended up running the last four lap alone. I finished 4th of 9 riders and first GS500. My best lap was a 1:24.68 and my slowest lap was a 1:25.51, so I was very happy with how consistent I ran the race.

As predicted it was raining Sunday morning so I decided not to play in the rain and started home early. Saturday was a great day for me and I was satisfied with my riding for the weekend even though I didn't ride on Sunday. On the drive home I thought about Saturday. I was really surprised to drop almost 3 seconds off my previous best time but even more surprised to be so relaxed on the bike while riding at the faster pace. Hopefully in two weeks I'll have just as much fun at the AHRMA event at Talladega.

Talladega Gran Prix Raceway (Oct 1-3)

Hello everyone,

AHRMA was at Talladega Gran Prix Raceway this weekend. This year they ran the track clockwise which would be my first time running the course in this direction. I've raced at TGPR 4 times in the past but only in the counter clockwise direction so this felt almost like a new track during Friday practice.

I raced my GS500 in BOT F3 & BOT F2 on Saturday and finished 4th in both classes. BOT F2 was especially fun because I held 3rd for 3 laps until a Ducati 748 finally passed me on the back straight (gee what a surprise). I tried to re-pass the duck but never got close enough and finished about 15-20 bikes lengths behind at the flag. My best lap times were low 1:07's for the weekend.

I felt like crap when I woke up Sunday morning so I loaded up and started home skipping the BOT F3 race I pre-entered. I must be getting old because my low back was hurting on Saturday.

A note about the BT-96's I raved so much about at Roebling. I started Friday practice with the same Bridgestone BT-96's radials (110/70 & 140/70) I used at Roebling & Mid-0hio and they were completely bald on the right side by the final practice session on Friday. I couldn't believe the tire wear even though I know TGPR is hard on tires. I did a quick calculation of the lap/miles the tires had on them:

  1. Mid-Ohio 25 practice laps Friday only about 60 miles
  2. Roebling Road 50 laps on Saturday about 100 miles
  3. TGPR 40 laps on Friday about 50 miles
  4. Total about 115 laps or about 210 miles.

I can say the tires give great grip right up to the point they are bald. Where the bias ply BT-35 was sliding at 1:07 laps times the BT-96 didn't slide a bit. It's to bad I wore out the tires before I learned the track because the rest of the weekend I road on my backup tires (used BT-56 front & used BT-35 rear)

One more race for the year at Carolina Motorsports Park on October 22-24 with AHRMA.

 

Carolina Motorsports Park: A New Bike and a New Track

Friday October 15, 1999 Today I drove to Union Cycle ((704) 821-3719) in Monroe, NC and purchased a new 1997 MuZ Skorpion Sport Cup. MuZ sponsors a class with AHRMA for the Skorpions and I'd have exactly one week to get the bike ready for the last event of 99.

Saturday-Thursday October 16-21, 1999 Wow what a busy week trying to get ready for the practice day on 10/22. Let see each day was pretty similar, spend 15 minutes of quality time with the family (I have a very understanding family), hours drilling holes for safety wire, installing a steering damper, installing catch bottles, etc., and also trying to ride the bike on the street to break in the new motor.

By Thursday at 11pm I was ready to leave for the track. I had 200 miles on the motor and the bike was ready for tech inspection. Can anyone spare me a few extra hours of sleep?

Friday October 22, 1999 I started for the track at 5am with a full 5 hours of sleep to see me through the day. I guess Carolina Motorsports Park (CMS) would be considered my home track since it's only about a 3 hour drive from the house. CMS opened in July of 99 but I missed the previous 2 WERA events due to family commitments so this would be my first chance to see the track.

I arrived at the track after only getting lost once and was impressed with the facilities. Lots of room in the paddock and the bathrooms have heat & A/C. The track itself is nice and wide with plenty of run off room.

I passed tech inspection and got ready for my first practice session. On the third lap of my first practice session the rear seat cowling came off as I raced down the front straight. Luckily the cowling had a safety cord to keep it attached in the general vicinity of the bike. A little adjustment to the latch and some duck tape just for added measure seemed to fix the problem.

During the second session I made it to the second lap and the bike just cut off. I determined that I didn't have any power while waiting for the crash track to give me a ride back to the pits. Upon arriving to the pits I had 3 Skorpion riders respond in unison that "it's a blown fuse". Sure enough it was a blown fuse caused by the brake light connectors grounding against the frame. 2 cents worth of electrical tape fixed the offending wires and I was back in business.

I was beginning to doubt my choice of picking the Skorpion as my new racebike since I was now 0 for 2 finishing practice sessions. However the rest of the day the bike ran great it worked extremely well considering it was bone-stock and I didn't know the track.

A track walk with the rest of the Skorpion Cup racers (and one SV650 rider) Friday evening showed me a couple of turns that I really needed to pick up my speed to run competitively on Saturday.

Saturday October 23, 1999 The two morning practice sessions were a little cool but I finally started to feel like I was riding the bike a little smoother. The first race of the day was Supermono which is a unlimited displacement class for 4-stroke singles. I got a decent start as I headed for the inside of turn one. Most of the other racers braked early for the turn and I just slotted the bike up the inside and passed a few bikes (I continued this move during each of my remaining races). After a lap Bill Birchfield passed me on the bike straight before the "Kink" on his Skorpion. A couple of laps later Craig Hunley came by on his Skorpion. I could keep them in sight but couldn't match their pace to challenge for a pass. I finished in 5th place and was in the MuZ contingency money for $12.50

Next race was the Skorpion Cup class. I blew the first start because I didn't understand the officials hand signals waving the number 1 with their index fingers. They were trying to tell us this would be a one-wave start instead of the two-wave that we were told earlier in the day. It didn't matter because there was a bad wreck going into turn one and the race was red flagged. On the restart it was a two-wave start and like the Supermono race I went up the inside of turn one. Much to my surprise I was leading the Skorpion class for a couple of laps. First Blake Wilson passed me, then Bill Birchfield, and then Craig Hunley. I was able to out brake Craig going into the turn off the back straight, he passed me back a few turns later, and I out braked him going into turn one. I had the bike wiggling under braking and I think Craig decided to back off because he figured I'd lose it on the brakes. Across the checkered flag I held 3rd and made my first real MuZ money of $50. My laps times were in the low 1:57's and I had one 1:56 on the day.

Sunday October 24, 1999 During the two chilly morning practice sessions I was able to run of 1:57's which matched the pace from Saturday's races. The races were almost a carbon copy of Saturday. In Supermono I finished 5th and in Skorpion Cup I finished 3rd.

Summary The Skorpion cup class is great for me because the strict rules keep the bike's very close to stock. This helps me because I really don't have the time or expertise to run a superbike effort. On the down side when you get beat it's because the other rider out rode you not because of horsepower. It was nice to fine out that a stock bike can be competitive in this class because I was only losing about 2 bike lengths on the bike straight to the bikes with a pipe & jet kit.

The best thing about this class are the other competitors. Everyone is real friendly and willing to help each other out in the pits even though you'll be out of the track racing against each other. Here's to the good times of 2000 on the Skorpion.

Just a few final notes on the differences between the GS500 & the Skorpion on the track. I probably shift twice as much on the GS than the MZ. Top end is about the same. The MZ wants to stand up when you trail brake it into the corners. The MZ also feels top heavy compared to the GS. I was amazed that I could race on the stock MZ suspension and really didn't have a lot of ground clearance problems. The brakes feel stronger on the MZ than the GS. Also I could hang off the bike easier on the MZ, however my GS turns quicker/sharper than the MZ (of course this could be the result of stock settings). Overall the bikes a very similar with the biggest difference in the power delivery of the engines.

Frame Straightening...Bruce Brown Style

I thought you'd be amuzed with my fix to the bent subframe of my 91 GS.  If you remember this is the bike that went down at Mid-Ohio after a Honda Hawk puked it's oil onto me & the bike.  After inspecting the bike the major damage was a bent subframe, pushing the seat to the left about 3 inches and bending the exhaust pipe.

It's seems I have a GMD computrack machine in my backyard.  This organic machine consists of two large trees growing about 2 feet apart.  After removing the gas tank, foot pegs, and gear shift lever, the main frame of the bike was wedged between the two trees.  Force was then applied to the rear subframe with a come-along until the subframe was straight with the mainframe.  I to think I was going to drive 7 hours to Atlanta and pay $350 to GMD to have this straightened, what the hell was I thinking.  Yeah, I know a little crude but it works.
