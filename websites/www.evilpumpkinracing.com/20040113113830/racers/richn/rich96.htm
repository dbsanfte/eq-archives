Race Preperation

(4/01/96) The bike ( '89 - GS500E ) is currently 100% stock. I think it'll look a little different without all that street stuff and number plates.

(4/08/96) Bought a set of new Teknic leathers today ($800). They seem to have the proper armor protection in all the right places and I hope not to road test that theory. Still have to buy a new helmet, back protector and gloves.

(5/3/96) I ordered my tires this week. I ended up getting the ME33 Laser Comp K 110/70V17 ( front ) and the ME1 Comp K 140/70VB17 ( rear ). The tires cost me a total of $251 with tax and shipping.

April 29th 1996: I will be pulling the front forks off this weekend and taking 'em into the Motorcycle Performance Engineering Service center here in Minneapolis. The damage estimate is somewhere around $200-$250 to get the forks done up. More on the technical details later. I'll probably be taking off all the lights and other "unsprung" weight stuff also. June 14th is fast approaching.

May 5th: Went for a nice long ride on Saturday in the new leathers. Ouch! Then on Sunday the pumpkin patch went to the CRA's in-class racing school. Mostly they talked about all the rules (read the book to us more like it). The on track portion on June 14th should be much more informative. Tried to pull the forks off...not much luck. I was missing the 8mm allen wrench to loosen the fork clamps. Did manage to pull the headlight off and drill some safety wire holes. Burned thru a drill bit every 2-3 bolts.

Got my forks back...the damage to my wallet was much higher than expected. $400 I had Gold Valve Emulators put in ( $125) and straight weight springs ( $84 ) . We had a safety wire party with about half the crew that weekend. The owner of the shop I had the fork work done ( Doug Lofgren ) showed up to lend us a hand and offer advice. I had to cut about 1.5 inches off of the spacers to give the front end some sag with me sitting on it. The spacers when shortened properly came to just barely sticking out of the top of the fork tubes.

Took my rear wheel off and had the rear race rubber put on by a local shop here. While the wheel was off I put a new rear sprocket on ( 41 Tooth ).


June 14th - 16th: First Race Weekend

(June 13th 4:30 PM) Loaded the bikes on the truck and headed for the track. Got there about 7 pm. We set up the tent and then ran the bikes thru tech inspection. I had a problem with the throttle sticking open. Loosened the bar ends and passed with no problems.

(June 14th) Went to the new racers school. They just re-hashed some track etiquette and then gave us our orange "pumpkin" jerseys. We all met for a ride around the track on a pickup with an expert rider there to show us what the track looked like and where the lines where. I must say that the track looks MUCH different from the track then as a spectator. Can't wait to get out there!

Talked to the Tech Inspector about zip tying a small plastic pumpkin to the back of my bike. He just wanted to see how I mounted it to make sure it wouldn't fly off at 100 MPH. The pumpkin became a minor tourist attraction, as every racer that walked past my stall, pointed, laughed and sometimes ran to get their buddies. ;-)

Hit the track with the Ultralight instructors. About 2/3rds ( between turns 9 & 10 ) thru the first lap behind the instructor, I hear a popping sound from my right. The bike doesn't feel any different so I didn't pop a tire. I look to the corner workers to see if they are looking at me funny, nope nothing there. Must have been unimportant! :-) I continue around the track. Coming out of turn 3 I spot the corner workers finally taking an interest in me...It seems that that unimportant poping sound was my valve cover gasket blowing. :-( Smoke is pouring off the bike and my right leg and boot is covered in oil. Damn! 1.3 laps and I'm already squash! The corner workers were very helpful and I felt bad about the possibility of me leaving puddles of oil on the track for someone else to find, the hard way! After missing one opportunity to retrieve my bike, at 5pm I managed pay attention and get the bike picked up from the corner.

Lucky for me one of the racers/mechanic ( Jeff Japs ) was around and in a generous mood. We searched high and low for a new gasket, but couldn't find one anywhere. Luckily the blown gasket only had a small rip thru part of it. I pulled the valve cover off and Jeff professionally put the gasket back in with some gasket sealer stuff. Re-assembled it and presto! It worked! Now to see if it would hold and I could get on the track tomorrow with an instructor. Not getting the track time in made me miss getting evaluated and receiving my race license.

( June 15th ) Lucky for me and one other unfortunate, an instructor was available and willing to go out in the pouring rain to get us our licenses. I followed Drew Jaeger #14 Expert around. The first lap it started to rain, by the 2nd lap the track was soaked and so were we. A normal pumpkin by the name of Glen on a shiny new ZX-6R went flying past. The front straight is also a drag strip and once it got wet...it got very slippery. Start of the 3rd lap we had to get off the best line thru the drag strip to avoid a guy who gave it too much gas on the "drag strip ice". I was barely on the gas and my rear tire spun. I decided to take it VERY easy thru there. Once past the hazard, Drew motioned me past so he could follow me to see if I was a bonehead our not. I started to catch up to Glen ( ZX-6R ) and I ended up passing him in turn two of the next lap. Drew decided I wasn't a bonehead and made for dryer ground. They red flagged the practice session one lap later and I went in to find out whether or not I passed. I shouldn't have worried.

Got my license and quickly signed up for the one race I could race in for this day. I came in 9th out of 12 with a completely stock bike! My best time that race was 2:24. One of our hallowed ranks had some bad luck and got bumped off the track on turn 2. His Ducati 900 was unthrilled! All the new riders then had to go out and sit with the corner workers for the 17 lap trophy dashes. It was very informative, seeing the lines that the "fast" guys were taking and hearing from the corner workers what working the corners is really like. Highly recommend working a corner or two for your club if you ever have some down time during a race weekend.

(June 16th) First full day of racing for the pumpkins and Evil Pumpkin Racing. We are now officially down one ( Gary ) I sign up for 3 out of the 5 races I could have competed in. I raced only the Ultralight races, but after watching the last Lightweight race. I wish that I had signed up for one more race! By my 3rd and last race, I lower my best lap time down to 2:21. Still have a way to go.

The last race ( Ultra-Light GP ) I raced in was the most memorable. Coming out off turn 8, while setting up for turn 9 an expert on a 125 laps me like I'm standing still. Scared the bejesus outta me and messed up my concentration for turn 9. I thought for a millisecond that I wouldn't be able to make the turn, but damn it! if the 125 ( #37 ) could make the damn thing so can I! I commit to the turn and lay the bike into the turn. I watch the curb coming up as my line swings me wider and wider. The curb provides a slight bank and I'm able to get the bike back into the upright position in time to hop off the curb and back on the track. No time lost and that lap was my best one!

After the race, we loaded the bikes back on the truck and got everything packed for the ride back to MPLS. I went out to turn 9 to see the curb close up. Luckily a couple of our riding buddies were working the corner and let me come out onto the track/corner during the last race. Watching the final race from in the corner perspective was very informative. The Lightweight race was very close and had about 36+ bikes racing. I should have been in that race! Talking to the corner workers ( Scott "Red VFR Dude" & Eric "Black VFR Guy" ), I learned that my pumpkin was VERY popular with all the corner workers. All and all I'd have to say I enjoyed the short burst of fame. I think I'm going to leave the Evil Pumpkin attached for the rest of the year.


Aug 23rd - 26th: Second Race Weekend

Thursday afternoon we loaded up the truck and made our way to the track. We arrived around 7pm and wasted no time getting the bikes unloaded. I did some minor checks on the bike to make sure everything was still wired and that the catch bottle was vented. I didn't want to repeat last weekend's award winning performance!

Everything looked okay to me...so off to the Tech barn. Got the bike and leathers safely thru tech with the only minor complaint being with my throttle bar end squeaking when the throttle was turned. We then quickly set up camp, ate and went to bed with dreams of racing tumbling thru our heads.

Friday's practice session was split between me and Gary. I decided to help him break his crash habit by letting him ride my bike. By the end of Friday's practice session, I was down to a 2:19 and Gary was down to a 2:22. We thought about changing gearing to a 43 tooth rear sprocket, but after buying a new chain and thinking harder...we changed our minds. We moved our camp site to the family camp ground and everyone decided to have a BBQ. The food and campfire was a great way to get ready for the next day's races.

Saturday's practice saw me almost wipe out on turn three. I came into the turn pretty fast, dragged my footpeg & panic'ed. I straightened my line up, which caused me to swing wide and jump the curb. Managed to save her...and decided I need to do something about that footpeg fear of mine. I raced in the 2:19 range that day.

Sunday was a little better. I managed to get my time down into the 2:15's and considered my next goal of 2:10 as possible.


Sept 13th - 15th: Last Race Weekend

Friday the 13th saw me try to fix a idling problem and make things worse! A simple little leak saw me tear my carbs apart three times! I found and fixed the leak the first time, but messed up the float level and put a needle washer in the wrong way. Got the float level's fixed, but it took a another tear down and the help of Mark Foster to find the needle problem. I had put the washer that the spring uses to push the needle and slide down with on the wrong side. Once we got that fixed...the bike ran, but the idle still doesn't want to work. It keeps on idling way too rich. Anyone have any suggestions?

Saturday was much better! I managed to get out on the track for practice and even race all the races I paid for. I couldn't get my times down below 2:15, but still had a very good time by passing a few more people than I did last weekend. There was many Red Flags today as the 600 pilots decided to follow each other off of turn 3. Our races got delayed by about 2 hours and we ended up having to race 'em on Sunday.

Sunday saw almost no red flags and slightly warmer temps. I did manage to get a 2:14 during this race and get a few good passes for the Ned's video camera. We ended up running my bike in the 4 hour endurance race. Mike, Chris & I ran pretty consistantly in the 2:15 range for the entire race. Chris managed to get down to the 2:11 time during the last few laps of the race. We took 2nd place in the Ultra Light GP class!
