Loudon & Hot Dog Buns  ( 7/19/1999 )

It all started because of the hot dog buns. That's my story and I'm sticking with it! The week before the race saw the typical mad racers preparation frenzy. A big thank you out to Marty and John who tooled down to my place and helped me greatly getting the bike back into racing shape and looking good. Marty assisted with replacing the entire exhaust system and rearset and John pains-takingly sanded and repainted the war wounds. I spent my time lockwiring up the bike and replacing waffled bolts, number plates, and other random stuff. Finally, Thursday rolled around and we were done.

We left about 8am Friday morning and made it up to Loudon by 2pm. I signed up for open practice and got teched. Everything went smoothly and I headed out for practice which starts at 3pm. My first practice went okay and I was just getting the feel of the bike and the track. We also discovered that I was leaking brake fluid. We cleaned it up and put in some more but couldn't find the leak so off onto the track I went hoping none would make its way onto the rotors.

During the 2nd practice I tried going a bit faster and found that I was having difficulty getting the bike turned in for turns and running wider on turns then normal. I wasn't sure if it was the brutally hot weather sapping my strength or something else. After talking to another racer he suggested checking the tire pressure. Sure enough, I was at least 4 lbs under. We also discovered that I was still leaking brake fluid. This time it seemed apparent that it was coming from the reservoir container itself and dripping down. We checked it all out and it looked okay. I decided to reverse the gasket thinking that might be the problem.

After increasing the tire pressure I headed out for the third practice. I now was able to turn the bike better but I started sliding the front and rear around on many turns. After thinking a moment I remembered that these were tires left over from last year and new tires were going to be needed if I wanted to run fast.

Suddenly, I realized that I pre-registered for the wrong race so off to registration I went. An hour or so later I toddled over to Street & Competition to buy a helmet and new tires. I ended up getting the top of the line Arai(Quantum something I think) and a set of D207's. The helmet had a whole arsenal of gee whiz features for air-flow that I'll actually have to sit down, study, and play with so I understand it all. Worst case I'll read the bloody manual but tinkering with stuff is so much more fun. We also discovered that I was still leaking brake fluid. I tried finding a new brake container but came up short. Great.

Marty somehow managed to get our 2 room tent up by himself. That was a minor miracle in itself. I sent Marty out for drink and food supplies. After he got back we played crazy 8's for while then hit the sack since we were both wiped. Unfortunately, some noisy people pulled in late and kept me up. That, in addition to the mosquitoes that somehow found there way into the tent and were biting me continuously. I felt like a live meal and I wasn't happy about it. So basically the night sucked and I didn't get much sleep. I had about 20 bites all over me and found a hole in the tent where the little bastards found there way inside. I was pleased that at least I posted several kills on the tent floor.

Saturdays first practice I took real slow since I had the new tires on and I wanted to scrub them in. Somebody who couldn't spell computer was apparently operating one because we either didn't get any lap times from our transponders for Saturdays practices or if you did they were all mixed up between riders. Then I ate the hotdog of death that gave me diarherra and stomach cramps. And let me tell you, it's no fun getting in and out of a riding suit in 103 degree weather to take a dump with toilet paper that sticks to your butt.

After practice, I was able to find a new brake container and ram air rubber. So we slapped those on and were happy about it. I checked my grid positions and they were good. Front row on both races. Yahoo! Now if my inards stayed intact I'd be in good shape.

After what seemed centuries my race finally arrived. To my dismay, both of my races had between 50 and 60 bikes which is insane for Loudon since it is a small tight track. Once again I got an okay start but apparently okay doesn't work for Junior class and I was near last going into turn one. Mental note to practice starts. Once at turn 1, I turned on the heat and became aggressive. I caught up to a line of what I believe was 8 bikes all bunched together cranking through the turns and I had a quick flashback of watching bikes do that on TV. I thought wow, this is kinda cool. Then reality snapped back and I started hunting and passing, hunting and passing. The pace was extremely fast. It seems everytime I go up there the faster it gets. But hey, I've got new tires so lets use em, so I set up for a pass coming out of turn 3 where I carried slightly more speed then the guy in front of me. Then I set up an inside line for the next turn. Well, he laid on the gas like a weedwhacker from hell and we where literally as close to each other as one could get without touching. I thought about pressing his off button on his bike but somehow I didn't think he'd appreciate that.

So into the bowl we went both full out on the throttle. Past the first cone, second cone, then I started thinking, um, that was my usual brake marker that I just wailed by and when is this guy going to brake. Fortunately it wasn't a moment later when he grabbed for brake and I did too and laid it into the turn not a moment too soon. Somehow I held on and the bike did too. Onto the next racer. I kept up this blistering pace for at least 5 laps knowing I've only got 8 laps to make the most of it. Then making a pass down the straight with a better drive I ripped into turn1 and 1a. Then I decided to protect the my inside with a tighter line through turn 2. Upon exciting turn 2, I gave it the gas before I cleared the back tarmac and caused my bike to fishtail massively. I stood the bike up and tried to save it but it cleared the tarmac and hit the asphalt causing it to pick up major traction and launch me(highside) off despite my best efforts. Fortunately the rider behind didn't run me over(thank you) and I hobbled my way off the track. Me and the bike escaped with minor injuries but I missed my 2nd race. So, once again relatively happy with my performance but really pissed off about wiping out again.

I honestly think I am reaching a point where I am finally pushing the bike to its limits and I need now to carefully refine how I ride within those limits. I must be able to recognize them(hear, feel, see) and take appropriate action. Also, I must think about what the consequences of changed lines are and come off of autopilot when I do that. In the above case I was too early with the gas which would have been right on had I taken my normal line through the turn. Like I said, it was all because of the hot dog buns.

Loudon...6/12/99

Pregame As usual, everything came down to the last minute. My great friend John couldn't figure out the wiring for his Nissan Pathfinder and my trailer and so after I finished drilling out the holes to attach the new body- work for the bike and attaching it to the bike, I helped him figure out the wiring for his SUV and my trailer. By the time we got it all working it was after 1AM Friday morning and we were both very tired being up late all week. Him, painting my bikes new bodywork and me repairing the bike with new parts. So we reconvened at 8am Friday morning. It took about 2 hours to load up the trailer and make our way up to Loudon NH.

I forgot my friend drives like a Grandma so we got there 1/2 after the 3pm open practice started. By the time I was able to pass tech and get on the track for practice I had already missed 2 practices. Fortunately I made the 3rd and final practice session. I rode horribly since it was my first time out this year and I was literally looking at the track and finding out where the new turns were and pavement changes. Bikes were wailing past me on both sides since this was the 3rd practice session and most of the riders there had already worked out their racing lines in the previous practice sessions.

Saturday morning saw improvement as I was able to start riding somewhat normal again and pick up my old lines and work on some new ones. My times weren't great but I at least broke into the 1:28's. The 2nd Sat. practice went even better as I refined my lines and became more relaxed and smooth through the turns. I was surprised to find that I improved my best personal previous times by 1sec/lap consistently.(1:23's)

Racetime I signed up for 2 races on Friday evening for Saturday afternoon, Unlimited Superbike and Heavyweight SuperSport. Because I signed up for the races at the last minute my starting grid positions sucked. The weather was great. Nice and sunny and dry. One couldn't ask for better racin' weather. Oh yeah, somehow, half of the tent poles were missing so I had to use the canopy poles as tent poles so I didn't have any shade so I got baked in the sun for most of the day. The babes were out in there bikinis and shorts and skirts and I nearly crashed twice on my bicycle riding through the pit area getting parts and stuff. :)

Race 1 had me grid in the back of 10 bikes. I got an okay start but was still last going into turn1. I quickly settled into my racing lines but the main pack of bikes in front of me quickly got lost. There were 2 bikes in front of me that I could see now. I began pushing to catch the first one. Damn these guys were smoking around the track at warp speed. I knew I was flying at a much faster rate then in practice but I was having considerable trouble trying to catch up to anyone. Finally, at about 1/2 through the race the bike in front of me made some mistake, probably a missed shift, and I closed the distance and quickly set up for a pass. That left the next bike still ahead. Charging ahead I worked on number 2. Wow, this guy was hauling butt. Everytime I seemed to make up some distance, he would gain on me in another turn. On the last lap I was able to close to passing distance and finally passed him on the straight across the start/finish line by getting a better drive onto the straight. But because of the remote unit scoring I was only credited with passing the one rider. So, I ended up 10th outta 11. Boy was I depressed. :(

Race 2. Was less than 1/2 hour later so I didn't have much time to sulk about it. I just said to myself, well, just go out there and make it a practice session. So, that's what my mindset was when we gridded up for race 2. This time I got a slightly better start. I was just behind mid-pack going into turn1. I passed what must have been 1/2 dozen riders between turn1, 1a, and 2. They were all going so slow because they had bunched up but I was able to pick a clear line through most of them. Now I was riding in the top 10. Someone wiped out in turn3 as I cranked through that turn. I zipped around being very relaxed. As I flew into the bowl and 2 more riders had ridden off the track. I continued at warp speed. Then I saw 2 riders ahead of me on the straight going relatively slow. So, I went flying into turn 1 at ludicrous speed inbetween 2 riders. I past the inside guy but then the outside rider swept across directly in front of me. I immediately grabbed brake and attempted to dive to the outside of him but I was moving entirely too fast and nailed his rear tire which launched me 10 feet into the airing doing somersaults. The landing hurt as I bounced off the pavement, rolled, then slid about 100 feet or so. My right leg was in intense pain as I finally stopped sliding. After about a minute or so the pain subsided and the amulance carted my sorry butt off. Everything checked out okay, no broken bones, and I'm just really sore and bruised in my hips area. (Mr Happy is okay too). Unfortunately though, I didn't get the chance to finish the race and had an excellent opportunity for a top 5 finish with the possibility of a win. That in itself boosted my moral considerably. :)

The unlimited superbike class was all bikes that were FASTER than mine and I had to accept that fact as well as the fact that I just turned Junior and this was my first race weekend as a Junior. Most the other riders had been Juniors for a while and had more racing experience then me. Considering my improved start and performance in the Heavy SuperSport race I feel confident that a top 10 is not a problem and that a top 5 finish is definitely within the realm of possibility. The Supersport class riders had all bikes with simialar performance to mine so that advantage wasn't there for the other riders. I didn't realize how much advantage that made in the Junior class until I raced in both races. Funny though, if I stayed as an amateur I would probably be winning most of the races with the lap times I was running.

After I checked out of the med-center we packed up and left about 6pm on sat and got home about 1am Sunday morning. My buddy and I were both wiped from a whole week of late nights and an intense previous 2 days. Tired but happy anyway and looking forward to the next race weekend in July.

Recovering and slightly damaged.

8/10/98

Well, okay I guess. I ran GT1(only 8 laps though) and Heavyweight Sportbike. On the GT1 race I started A4 position, smack in the middle. I had an okay start and pretty much maintained my position through turn 1 & 2. Turn 3 and the straight are my 2 favorite passing spots and I took full advantage of them each lap. The pace was incredibly fast and the front 3 were gone after 3 laps. I worked on passing riders as best I could but found it difficult to pass in areas of the track that I was faster. I would come right up behind someone but couldn't get around them. I need to work on that in several places on the track so that I develop more passing zones. At any rate I was hummin' around the track at a decent pace, my best guess in the 1:25-1:27 range and I wound up placing 6th with number 5 just about 20 feet in front of me. Given some more laps I would have probably moved up even further. At 6th place I am fairly happy and I picked up 13 points which qualifies me for junior class. At this point in time though I think I'm going to stay in amateur class.

For Heavyweight Sportbike I started in the A5 position. Off the line I had an okay start but going into turn 1 I backed off because the guys in front of me backed off and that's when the front 1/2 of the pack swept in front of me from the outside. What a mistake that was. I went from like 15th to 30th in a span of about 5 seconds. Ugh. Well, I said to myself. I might as well race since the race just started even if I wasn't going to place well. So, to work I went. After the first lap, the bikes sort of streched out and I settled into some sort of pace and began passing 1 or more bikes each lap. Of course, the race only being 8 laps was quickly over before I could make any real headway but it was good practice for me nonetheless.

All in all in was a good learning weekend with no crashes. I feel confident that I can still improve my lap times by 1-5 seconds a lap without any modification to my bike. I just need more track practice time.

7/19/98

My racing up at Loudon NH this past weekend had mixed results. My Heavyweight Sportbike Race resulted in a nasty crash in turn 3. I got boxed in and the rider in front of me claimed that he got slowed up from a wall of 3 riders in front of him. He was going REALLY slow and I came in REALLY hot and I managed to tag his rear tire with my front in effort to avoid hitting him. This catapulted me over my bike and over him and his bike. After hitting the pavement and bouncing quite nicely my bike slammed into me. In particular the handlebars nailed me all but good in the groin at about 20-30mph. <Insert lots of pain and unhappiness here> NOw MY bike pushed me down the track an easy 50 or more feet. $1500, 15hours, and LOTS of pain killers had me and the bike back in runnable condition.

Sundays Superbike registration got screwed up so they placed me at the very rear of the grid, 29th position. Oh joy. Well, after the tremendous help from a bunch of guys at the track, one even went home to pull off the front rim off his GSXR to bring back for me to borrow for the day, as the one I had was bent beyond our best repair efforts, I wasn't about to let them down.

So down goes the green flag. I took 3 riders on the start and a couple more through turn 1. The track is crowded and I continue to pick off riders as quick as I can. The pace is fairly quick and I am very aggressive in setting up and executing passes. Soon I start running into 2nd wave traffic. Apparently, there was 43 bikes on the track and non-stop traffic which made any sort of decent lap times almost immpossible. Loudon is a very tight track about 1.7miles and 12 turns with a short straight. I battle my way through constant traffic passing bikes as fast as I can. I can't ever remember seeing so many bikes on the track even in practice sessions. There's bikes everywhere. The second wave bikes were apparently lightweight bikes which is why I was able to catch up to them. Unfortunately they were not in my class so passing them didn't get me anything but they did make good road hazards and also served to screw up any sort of decent line a heavyweight bike would make. Soon I see one of my buddies also on a GSXR750. He is smooth and laying down a nice pace but I am faster on several turns but not fast enough to make the pass. It took nearly 2 laps to finally make the pass. Hmmm...I think to myself. This guy `claims' that he often finishes near last on races and I had to really work to pass him. That's not good since there is only one lap left to the race. So, with a momentary clearing of no bikes ahead of me I lay down a blistering lap catching and passing 1 more 2nd wave bike before crossing the finish line. Results, I finshed 10th out of 29 riders which means I probably passed 18 riders not including all of the 2nd wave bikes of which I must have passed at least 1/2 dozen. Not bad for a days work. Granted, no trophies or plaques and only 3 points but I was happy with my accomplishment.

Pocono Burgers

As always, it was a mad dash for parts and re-assembly during the last week before the race weekend. Fortunately, not much work was needed to be done, just a few touch ups here and there. I was determined to do well since I had done well last year at Pocono pulling down two 4th place finishes. The game plan was to head out Thursday afternoon, set up camp, and be ready to roll early for practice on Friday. So much for plans.

Again, as usual, we left late, around 5pm right in the middle of rushour traffic. Oh joy. After I was 3/4 of the way to the track I realized I forgot the bloody sleeping bags since I had them in the trunk of my car and forgot to take them out weeks ago. Crap. Finally we get to the track and they aren't allowing anybody in. Just great. Finally one of the guys says that they will open up at 7:30pm for registration and camping. Of course every guy you ask at Pocono says something different and most of them are wrong. After remember the fiasco last year I made a split decision. Mom & Dad who were with me decided to go to my Dad's house about 45 minutes away to stay there for the night while I decided to drive all the way back home to get the stinkin' sleeping bags. Man was I pissed. So I drove my sorry butt home got about 3 hours of sleep and then drove back to pick up Mom & Dad. Fine.

True to form. Every year we have the combined LRRS & FUSA race weekend and Friday is a total mess. Last year we didn't get to hit the track until the afternoon and practice costs more than double the price than usual and is suppose to start at 8am. This year was only slightly better and we got started at about 10 or 10:30am...not great but it was at least an improvement over last year. So, Friday practice went relatively uneventfull, and I began familiarizing myself with the track again. Friday night came along and the yahoos were out in force. Now I don't mind folks doing burnouts, wheelies, and whatnot but not at all hours of the night. Up at Loudon we have a 10pm quiet curfiew and everybody pretty much settles down by 12pm. Not here, the clowns were at it ALL bloody night and not just a few people either. So, I mangaged not to kill anybody but didn't get any sleep either.

Then Saturday morning hits and I'm just a ball of joy having had no sleep. The claimed new facilities with showers were MIA and the only improvement to the old bathrooms was that we now had lights so we could see where we pee. Yeah.

I do practice on Saturday and discover I can't hear any bloody announcements which makes life more difficult. While on the track my bike dies for no apparrent reason. I pull over baffled and finally get the bike restarted and pull into the pits. I go over the bike with Dad and check and tighten all electrcical and fuel connections. I head back out the the next practice but still get the same problem. The bike works great for awhile and then wham, as if somebody pressed your kill switch it dies out. That plus the fact my lap times blew chunks just set me into a somber mood. I opted not to buy new tires given my bike's problem.

Mom decided to cook up some burgers so I figured maybe some food in the stomach would be good for the soul. Well, that was until Mom dropped the first burger on the ground and then gave it to me then flipped the next burger into the spare tire and fed that one to my Dad. Great, my own Mom is trying to kill me nevermind my bike! Somehow, lunch didn't help much. I tried to get some rest before my races but that was impossible since the day time yahoos were now at the same antics as the night time ones. I never knew somebody could do wheelies back and forth on the same road for over 3 hours straight..almost amazing if I wasn't ready to spray them all with WD40 and then send a match after them.

Finally my races come up and I get really crappy starts and don't do all that well. Knowing my bikes problems and the fact that I'm running on 3 hours of sleep for the past 2 days and old tires makes me do the intelligent thing and ride at a safe pace. Of course, that means you not only don't you win but you get your butt whooped but good. So, I ended up finishing in the bottom half of the pack for my races. Not great, but I had fun racing the slower guys.

Saturday night rolls in and some of my buddies show up and we kick back some brewskies and hang. By 10pm I'm completely exhausted and can't even manage the stength to go check out the rock bands and titty shows. Once again the night hoodlums are out in force making as much noise and ruckus as possible. Once again I thank God I don't own a gun because their would have been dead bodies laying all over the Pocono camp ground.

Sunday morning breaks and I can't believe my lucky rain tires held off the rain for the entire weekend. Amazing. For almost 2 complete years everytime I brought my rain tires to the track it didn't rain. One of the few times I didn't mind not using a brand new set of tires. I talk to a bunch of racers about my bikes problem and try all sorts of crap and magic but nothing seems to fix the problem So, I run Sundays races simliar to Saturday and maintain my bottom half placement but manage to keep the bike shiny side up and amazingly the bike only stalled on the warmup lap once and not during any of the races. My last race on Sunday was at about 7pm and I could barely see the flags through my tinted visor. Heck, the sun was already starting to set and there was barely any light left. I almost kept going after getting the checkered flag before my peripheral vision notified my brain that yes, that indeed was the start/finish guy waving the checkered flag.

Last but not least, was the gale force winds that snapped two of our tent pegs. I nearly peed myself when my Dad attempted to break down the tent and forgot to let the air out. This huge 2 room tent acted like a wind sail and was dragging my Dad around the camp ground. Picture this giant tent, 24' by 12', full of air being blown by 40mph wind and my Dad being dragged along holding on for dear life. After crying tears of laughter for several minutes I ran over to rescue him and my tent. After a brief struggle I managed to pummel the tent into submission and we packed up and went home. I think I slept for the next 2 years...

The Death Plague and the Trailer Save 7/28/00-7/30/00

It all began like a typical race weekend with the mad dash to put the bike back together Wednesday night. Dad stopped over and helped which made things a heck of a lot easier. Thanks Dad! Anyway, Friday rolled around and I headed out with my new old GMC 1986 Jimmy 4x4. At least this vehicle has a V6, working AC, and cruise control unlike my previous Ford Ranger. I had a slight sore throat but we made it up there without any major hitches and set up camp. I signed up for the open practice and ride-around session. In a stoke of genius, I apparently dumped some old low octane lawnmower gas into the bike and it was having starting problems. Gee, there's a shock. This problem was alleviated by adding some new fresh 111 octane race gas. That made the bike much happier and me too for that matter. Practice was just that and it took me a while to get back into the groove of how to ride the bike and the track layout after not riding a bike for 1/2 year. After practice, I signed up for Sat races and got teched. Hey, they got a new good looking young woman at the registration booth so that was a nice touch. There was some random betting as to how old she was...a racer thing I guess.

Since Mom & Dad decided to join me I got the luxury of staying at a hotel. Yahoo! Unfortunately, my Mom snores loud enough to wake the dead so once again, I didn't get as much sleep as I would have liked. It never fails. Sat mornings practice went better and I was almost up to race pace. I felt much smoother. I opted for a new set of rubber(D207GPs) since the tires I had were from last year. My first race was Heavyweight Endurance. I really suck at starts and unlike some I really do hit turn 1 at the bottom of the heap, but, in an endurance race you've got time so starts are less important then in sprint races. So, I just kept trucking along nicely. I got into a good rhythm and began cranking out nice, steady, lap times. I passed some people and some people passed me. I never seem to be able to keep track of whose who and where I am but I was reeling in a racer in front of me when I took the checkered flag. Funny I thought, I didn't remember getting the last lap flag. I later figured out that the lead experts past me on the last lap and I ended up losing a lap. :( Later, when I went to check my results I found out that I placed 3rd, my best finish to date! So, I was pleasantly surprised.

I was completely wiped after the endurance so I just R&R'd it until the Heavyweight Superbike race rolled around. My throat was getting pretty raspy and the snot attacks were starting. As usually I got my massively unimpressive start and got to work pronto. I pushed hard but the pace was very fast and I only remember passing back 1 guy that passed me. I forgot to stretch and ended up getting a leg cramp during the race. Despite which I still pulled faster lap times then my endurance race. I came in around 12th place but at least it wasn't last.

The Unlimited Heavyweight race got red flagged and moved to the end of the day by which point it started raining fairly heavily. I didn't have rain tires, didn't have money for rain tires, and I felt sick and tired so I bagged the last race. Once of the guys pitted next to me ran it and crashed, fortunately not badly. Sunday rolled around and I was sick and tired and the weather was overcast and calling for rain. I elected to go home. While driving home in the pouring rain and suffering from the death plague dad gave me I took an offramp a bit too quickly. I applied brakes but every time I did so the SUV & trailer just kept skidding down the off ramp. Hmmm...this is bad I thought. At the bottom of the off ramp which was coming up at about 40mph was a split. On the right split was a large Mack truck so I took the left split which also happened to be up hill and a blind turn. With some skillfull(see also minor miracle) maneuvering I managed to avoid hitting any traffic, thank God, and pulled over at first chance to check everything. Whew, that was a close one. Everything checked out ok. Fortunately Mom was chilling in the back seat and didn't realize what was happening until it was over and that was definitely a good thing. The remaining drive home was fairly uneventfull and I called it a decent weekend.
