2 Wins, Daytona & DeLand Skorpion Cup

I won the Daytona Skorpion Cup race, drafting by at the finish line to win by half a wheel!  Before I bore you too tears with all the details of my first win in 10 years (Talladega Formula 3 win 1990 on a 250 Ninja) let be back up and tell you what I did in the off season.

November 99 – February 2000 Off-season prep work & training   After the season ending AHRMA race at Carolina I started a workout routine.  I have a treadmill at home so at first I started to run 2 miles 5 times a week plus stretching, stomach crunches, & pushups.  Each workout lasted about 30 minutes, which is about all I can fit into my day with a wife, 2 children, and my full-time job.  During the early part of December I had to switch from running to walking on the treadmill because my low back and knees were giving me problems.  I must be getting old or I just like to think a man’s got to know his limitations.  Anyway I tried to walk 1.5 miles everyday but usually ended up walking 6 days a week.   Currently, I plan to add some more pushups to my routine since my shoulders & neck were getting sore from these first two races of the year. 

Over the winter break I spent quite a bit of time fiddling and prepping the bikes.  The GS500 got the rear subframe re-aligned using my organic computrack oaks trees in the backyard & a come-along.  Also JB helped me dial in my front forks with a change of fork oil to 15-weight, 4 inches from the top without the springs & emulators.  Although the GS forks have Racetech springs & emulators I was getting a little wobble/wallow coming out of fast corners hard on the gas.  JB diagnosed my problems as not enough rebound damping so we went with the thicker 15-weight oil.  The rest of the work on the GS was routine.  I changed oil & filter, cleaned the front brake caliper, greased the brake pistons, & installed EBC HH pads.  The rear tire was toast so I had break the piggy bank and buy a 140/70 Bridgestone BT-96 for the Suzie too.   

I spent why too much time fiddling with the steering damper on the MZ over the winter.  Finally JB took a look and made a few suggestions and now I’m happy with the installation.  I purchased an M4 can (stock head pipes have to be retained to be Skorpion Cup legal) and sent the carbs to Galen Miller of Bikeworx in Maynard, MA for rejetting.  Note, there are not many aftermarket parts readily available for the MZ, like a jet kit, so you have to work a little harder to find what you need.  I also purchased 3/8” shorter than stock dog bones for the rear suspension from Galen.  These dog bones raise the rear ride height thus putting more weight on the front wheel.  A 16-tooth front sprocket & a 40-tooth rear sprocket where also purchased to give me gearing choices (with the stock 15/39) to match tracks from Daytona to little Talladega.  The MZ got Bridgestone BT-96’s 110/70 & 140/70 just like the GS500 over the winter.  We’re waiting on Bridgestone to import a 150/60, which should be in the states by mid-April.  Like the GS, I changed oil & filter, cleaned the front brake caliper, greased the brake pistons (4 on the MZ), & re-installed the stock pads.  Funny the stock MZ pads are Ferodo.  I also did some sculpting with epoxy and fiberglass to my custom made front number plate.  I finished up by drilling a few more nuts & bolts for safety wiring and blessed it ready for racing.

A significant amount of time was spent on the Internet with the other Skorpion Cup racers.  We were trying to draft a set of rules that everyone agreed with and fit the spirit of the class better than the existing rules.  The old rules had very restrictive items like you had to run a DOT 110/70 front tire & a 150/60 rear tire, instead of just stating you had to use DOT tires.  You must use stock handlebars, stock air filter, etc.  It took us about 2 months to come with a new set of rules, which are very similar too most Supersport or Superstock class rules.  Next, we forwarded the new rules to Motorrad of North America the importer for MZ.  They were very happy to accept our rule changes because we represented the majority of the Cup racers for 2000.  It’s a good thing we talked with the importer because they were ready to open up the class to superbike rules, with the guy who spends the most money taking all the wins.  Every one of us wanted a basically stock motored class and the superbike type rules would have put a damper on the 2000 season. 

Let’s see were did I spend the rest of my off-season time?  Oh yeah, my custom (home made) enclosed trailer.  I found a welding shop to fix a couple of cracks in the frame.  I spent some time under the trailer reinforcing the floor, checking the wiring, & painting the rusted frame.  When I purchased the trailer the previous owner had mounted two strip fluorescent lights & two halogen lights, ran the wiring & conduit, but hadn’t installed the switches.  I made a list and went shopping at the local hardware store and spent $30 for the necessary parts.  After 4 or 5 hours of work I now have a switch for each light and 4 outlets for running other items like my drill. 

DeLand March 2 & 3, 2000  Thursday was practice and I was riding both the MZ and the GS500.  I’d get 20 minutes on the MZ park it and then jump on the GS and ride another 20 minutes.  This is the first time I’ve ridden the GS & MZ back to back on the track.  My evaluation, the MZ rocks!  My GS has the Race Tech springs & emulators, and a Penske rear shock but the MZ handles much better with stock suspension components.  Right after lunch I decided to test the traction limits of the front tire on the Skorpion while trail braking into a corner.  I found the limit and low sided the bike.  I was OK and the bike just had a bent handlebar & brake lever.  Also don’t try I stand up and walk until after you’re finished sliding.  I did giant moon walking steps when I tried to walk at about 30mph.  The giant steps caused a little pain & swelling in my left ankle for the rest of the weekend.  I was pissed from dumping the bike so when I got back to the paddock I jumped right on the GS500 and went out for practice.  The Skorpion was easy to fix before the next schedule practice, at least of thought so.  I went out for practice on the MZ and things were fine for about 3 laps then I grabbed the brake and the lever comes against the bar.  I did get slowed down for the corner but I had to clean my leathers once I made it to the paddock.  The rest of the day was spent bleeding the brakes and testing the bike only to find out after a couple of lap of working fine the lever pressure would mysteriously go away.  Trent Thomas another cup rider had the same problem at Carolina last year and his brakes were working fine this year.  His fix was to rebuild the master cylinder.  Great, I need a master cylinder rebuild kit and it’s 4:30pm and racing starts and 10:30am the next day.  Luckily the importer (Motorrad) is only about 30 miles from the track so I called them.  Yes they have a rebuild kit, happy happy, joy joy!  I meet Ray, the president of Motorrad, at Daytona around 5:30pm, got the set of o-rings for the master cylinder, and headed back to the track to install the kit.  Now those lights I wired in the trailer over the winter are going to come in handy.  I fired up my generator, grabbed a beer, and got some help from my friends.  After about an hour we had the kit installed and the brakes bleed.  Time to call it a day and take a well needed shower.

Friday, race day.  During the one practice session before the race the brakes worked great for the entire session.  First race Skorpion Cup.  I got a good start and going into the last turn to finished lap one we four wide.  I finished lap one in 3rd place but then red flag.  Helmut  #80 one of the other Skorpion riders had fallen in turn 1.  What seemed like a minor fall ended up being very serious for Helmut.  Helmut had cracked the first vertebra in his neck and had a bleeding blood vessel in his brain.  A few days after the wreck he had, had brain surgery twice and was in an induced coma.  Restart, and again I got a good start ending the lap in second place.  We started to spread out and the leader had about 10 bikes lengths on me two laps from the finish.  However the leader got into a big slide leading to the back straight and I gained about 5 bikes lengths.  Then in the last turn before the white flag he got pushed wide by a back marker and I passed both of them on the inside.  I tired hard to run a good pace the last lap, but not to anything stupid, and took the checked flag in first place!   During the race we ran 1:54’s

Next race, Supermono.  Well the two $30,000 Ducati Supermono’s & another trick single didn’t stick around to play and I ended up 4th at the flag. 

Last race BOT (battle-of-the-twins) F3.  My stock motored GS500 is really out gunned this year if BOT F3.  Most of the riders have traded in their EX500’s and are racing SV650’s with the remainder of the grid on Honda Hawks.  Can you say 40hp vs. 65hp, I thought you could.  I got a decent start until the rest of guys twisted their throttles to pass me into turn one.  I ran a fairly quiet race passing only one bike but saw a lot of guys over shoot corners and use the run off area to get slowed down.  Due to these rider errors I ended up 5th at the flag.  I ran 1:56’s during the race.

Daytona March 4, 2000  We entered the CCS Super Singles class on Saturday to get some track time before the AHRMA races on Monday.  The first practice session was the highlight of the weekend for me.  A little red BSA came past me in the first horseshoe and the name on the back of the leathers was Duhamel.  I saw the gray hair blowing around from under the helmet and realized in was Yvon Duhamel, cool!  I only ran a few laps in my second practice session on the 3.5 miles track because the rear was starting to slide under power.  I rode around the pits until I found JB so he could take a look at the tires.  I got bad news, JB said the tires were toast after racing at DeLand.  I wanted to race so I used my best sponsor (VISA) to get another set of BT-96’s.  Without any more practice I’d have to start the race on sticker tires.  On the warm up lap I had the front & rear sliding on the new tires almost dropping the bike.  Then on the grid I realized in my haste to get the tires changed I hadn’t checked the air pressure.  This was only a 5-lap race and I took things easy the first 3 laps with the new tires & the unknown air pressure.  By the 4th lap the bike & tires felt good so I started to pick up the pace running 2:30 lap times.  I finished 8th of 19 bikes.  BTW, JB had set the tire pressure for me in advance at 29 PSI. 

Daytona March 6, 2000  AHRMA  tried to curtail the Monday morning traffic jam by having tech & the riders meeting on Sunday at AMI.  Monday morning was a zoo anyway.  We arrived at the track at 6:45am and didn’t get parked in the paddock until 7:45.  Luckily our first practice wasn’t until 8:30am, other riders weren’t so fortunate with the first practice group starting at 8am. 

First race Skorpion Cup.  I was gridded on the 3rd row so I knew I needed a good start to stay with the leaders.  I got a good start but the leader (Craig Hunley) fell in the first horseshoe bringing out the red flag.  The restart went well and I was into 3rd place going up onto the banking for the first time.  Blake Wilson was leading with Bill Birchfield second and I was in third.  The race was an 8 lap event and we traded 1st through 3rd constantly while putting some distance between the 4th place rider.  I took the white flag first but Blake out braked me into turn one and Bill was close behind in third.  Blake was riding hard through the infield and lost the rear end as he headed up onto the banking.  I went out on the grass to avoid Blake and his bike.  Bill braked hard and went to the inside to avoid the wreck.  Now we were on the banking will Bill leading and me right behind going into the chicane.  Bill broke early for the chicane hoping I would go by but I just set up and waited for him to lead us.  Bill existed the chicane with about a 10 bike length lead but the wonderful draft at Daytona just pulled me up behind him.  I used his draft to sling shot around to take the checked by half a wheel.  Our lap times dropped to 2:25’s but slowed to 2:27’s on the last lap playing the waiting game going into the chicane. 

My final race of the week was Supermono.  Not much to report, I just went out and enjoyed myself know the bike wasn’t competitive against the 70hp bikes.  I finished 8th.

My final score for the weekend was $600 from MZ for the two Skorpion wins and $100 from Bridgestone.  As my wife pointed out to me, I didn’t make money but it helps to offset some of the expenses. 

MZ Air Filter Mods

As you know the aftermarket folks seem to ignore our MZ's for the most part, but you just have to get a K&N filter for the Skorpion.  So here's the scoop on getting a K&N Air Filter to fit the Skorpion stock airbox.  First I didn't think this one up I borrowed the idea from this website http://www.sirius.com/~guldam/muz/fixit.html

Order a K&N filter made for the 1986 Yamaha SRX600, K&N part# YA-6050.  I took a before photo (sorry for the poor quality) and as you can see compared to the stock filter it needs some plastic strips added to the top & bottom.

The filter size is almost exactly the same area as stock, with the K&N being just slightly wider by about 3-4mm.

Modifications to the K&N

  1. Remove the foam gasket from the underside of the filter
  2. Make plastic strips to attach to the top and bottom of filter so the K&N will be the same dimensions as stock.  I used an old number plate for the plastic.
  3. Attach the plastic strips to the filter.  I used JB weld.
  4. Install new foam gasket material.  I used 1/4" foam weather stripping from a local hardware store. (note shown in my pictures)
  5. Drill a hole in top of plastic strip for the airbox mounting bolt/screw.
  6. Last, you'll need to trim the opening of the airbox about 2mm on each side due to the K&N being wider.

The last two photos show the K&N from the top & bottom with the plastic strips attached.  Sorry the camera was out of focus.

If you have questions or the instructions don't make sense let me know.

New Track and Racing in the Rain

We (as in all of us motorcycle enthusiasts/nuts) have a new road course facility that just opened on the Virginia -North Carolina border about 75 miles north of Raleigh, NC. The new track is the Virginia International Raceway (VIR) which is a 3.3 mile road course which can be split into two smaller courses. I should say the track "re-opened" not new. This track has been closed since the early 70's but an investment group has brought in back to life. We learned in the riders meeting that VIR has spent about $30 million to date, and plan to spend another $30 million to finish the facility. The track is being promoted as a country club with the theme of racing instead of golf. The current plan is to host car & bike racing club events with limited spectators. They don’t have plans for any large spectator events which is just a well since the local roads would be quickly jammed.

This weekend CCS hosted the racing. We ran on the North course, which is about 2.2 miles with something like 20 turns and quite a bit of elevation change. The race track, paddock, registration building and tech shed were all complete and first class. The buildings even had wood shake shingles with copper flashing, ka-ching $. About the only item to complain about was the rainy weather and the red clay mud if you decided to turn your roadracer into a motorcross bike. The feel of the track at speed was kind of a combination of Road Atlanta, Summit Point, & Mid-Ohio, so it’s a great track, IMO.

Now on with the race report. . .

Saturday April 15, 2000 dawned rainy & cool, with a forecast of more rain and temperatures to stay in the mid –50’s. When I heard the forecast I decided to post-enter GT-lights (a 30 minute timed mini-endurance) figuring the rain would offset the power of the SV's & other over 60hp bikes and allow my 43hp MZ thumper to be competitive. Yeah, I know what a nut I am. Who wants to ride in the rain on a new track in the cold. Well me that’s who! I got my tech bike through tech about 7:45am and I needed to get rain tires installed for the first practice session at 8am. Thank goodness practice was running late but I was still going to miss the first session because JB (the "Bridgestone tire guy" jbtireguy@aol.com) was changing everyone’s dry tires to rains. So while JB was installing rain tires on my wheels Craig (my fellow Skorpion Cup competitor) let me take out his Bike in the first practice session to see the track. Craig practiced at the track on Friday in the dry and wasn’t thrilled about racing in the rain. So I owe a big thanks to Craig for letting me ride his bike, even though it did have Michelin Pilots, and Jim Lipsey for helping with getting the wheels back on my bike once the rains were mounted. Yes, I have to agree with what I’ve heard about Pilots in the rain, they do suck, but it didn't matter since I was just strolling around the course. For the second (and last) practice session of the day I had Bridgestone rain tires on my Skorpion. This was my first time on "real" rain tires and I was still learning the course so I didn’t exactly set any fast laps.

The rain stopped for about 30 minutes during the lunch break, but a light drizzle started just before the first race of the day, GT Lights. I was gridded on the back row since I didn’t pre-enter this race which was OK with me since I was more concerned about surviving than going fast. The philosophy seemed to work fine because after about 3 laps there must have been 6 or 7 racers off sampling the Virginia mud. I was even passing a few guys in this survival mode. About 10 minutes into the race survival mode turned into race mode as I started to dice with what seemed like a zillion SV 650’s. Each lap I was pushing a little more and trying not to breath which fogged my face shield & glasses. (Yeah, I wear glasses so what, just don’t kick sand in my face) Breaking for turn 1 became an interesting technique of breaking with one hand while trying to crack the fogged visor with the left hand and find my turn in point. I thought about pulling in but I like a challenge (or I’m just plain stupid). Probably about 15 minutes into the race and a few SV’s later I touched my knee down. I was a little surprised, but then realized the $295 I spent on rain tires was worth every penny. Rain tires are the ticket, period! I had only read the quotes of AMA pro’s & GP riders about the stick of rain tires and that they could lean the bike as much in the wet as the dry. Yeah right is what I’m thinking, but I’m here to tell you that even a mere mortal like me can get his knee down in the rain, very cool! Craig only got one lap time on me during the race at 1:57 which was only one second slower than he was going in the dry the day before on a similar Skorpion cup legal bike. Anyway I guess I passed more SV’s than I thought because I finished 3rd out of about twenty starters. This earned me $40 bucks in purse money and $30 from Bridgestone so all credit goes to the rain tires & my wonderful MZ Skorpion thumper. If your wondering, my buddy Craig decided not to come out and play on a wet track :-( Next time I’ll question his manhood or something so he’ll take the green flag.

Sunday April 16, 2000 was just as the forecasters predicted, dry & mostly sunny, highs in the mid-70’s. The morning started with the alarm going off at 4:45am so we could arrive at the track by 7am. VIR is about an hour and 45 minute drive from my house so I didn’t have to camp or get a hotel room. It almost feels like I'm cheating being able to race during the day and drive back home for dinner each evening. Once we arrived at the track it was similar to Saturday except I spent the first hour or so changing from rain tires to DOT's for the dry track. Even with the help of my son & wife, who carried my wheels to JB while I was changing gearing, I still missed the first practice session. I was entered in one race on Sunday, SuperSingles. This was a combined race with SuperTwins and they were flagged off first. Similar to the GT-Lights race on Saturday I spent the race dicing with SV650's. They were fast on the straight and turtles in the corners so I had to kind of force my way through in the tight sections. Once I was past my lap times started to fall with my quickest times coming on laps 4 & 5, with a best time of a 1:50.4 Somehow I managed to put some distance between me & the second place SuperSingle bike earlier in the race, thanks to the SV's for blocking. I finished 1st and my fellow Skorpion Cup competitor Craig finished 2nd. Not too bad for basically stock bikes in a superbike rules class. Note, this will change once our friend Keith Wakefield gets his 70-some hpTigcraft framed BMW single back on the track. With the win I earned another $50 in Bridgestone coupons.

You ask, "how do you like your switch to the Skorpion"? It's great! Relatively inexpensive to buy new & race, even cheaper than a SV650 & has full bodywork stock. The only modifications to date are adding a steering damper, shorter dogbones on the rear suspension, M4 can, jet kit, tires, brake pads, and K&N air filter, about $1000 in total. VIR was my fourth race weekend on the Skorpion (CMS, DeLand, Daytona, & VIR) and it has earned me over $900 in contingency money. See you at Carolina in May!

Wrecking twice in one weekend, well it SUCKS!

Just a quick report since I really don't want to talk much about it. I'm still pissed off at myself for crashing twice in one weekend.

Saturday practice and I'm starting to pickup the pace going through a 3rd gear, right hand turn. The next thing I know I'm off the bike sliding on my butt. No warnings from the bike such as sliding tires or scrapping footpegs, etc., just boom, off the bike. The lap before my fall I clocked a 1:55 which was a second faster than the last time I was at the track (this is suppose to make me feel better). I spent Saturday afternoon checking the bike over but damage was minimal, just a bent handle bar & broken water pump cover. Also I added more scratches to the stock bodywork & M4 can.

Sunday practice went OK and I got my times back down into the 1:56's. I take my grid for the first race, Super Singles, and get a terrible start coming off the line dead last in my class. I finally get going and tell myself to relax, "if you're faster you'll catch the guys in front of you". I passed the leader of the singles class on lap 2 and was lapping 1-3 seconds faster than the rest of the field. Lap 4, I see the second place rider off in the last turn, he's up and walking but holding his shoulder (I find out later he has a badly dislocated shoulder). I spent most of my race dicing with the back of the pack riders in the Twins race flagged off at the same time as our singles class. A rider on a TL1000 and another on a Ducati 748 were running similar lap times to mine but doing it very differently. Can you say fast on the straights but turtles in the corner, sure I thought you could. I know these two riders got tired of passing me on the straights only to have me come by them on the brakes or later in the corner but I was just trying to get a clean lap. On lap 7 the leader of the twins class passes me, but that's no big deal since I'm scored in the singles class. However, the next corner I crashed the bike, AGAIN! This time the corner was a 4th gear corner and I was traveling about 85mph. Again, the lap before I crash I clock a 1:55. Did I mention the Carolina track has curbs on the inside & outside of the corners for the guys who race 4-wheels cages. Going over a speed bump at 85mph hurts you & your bike. I survived with some bruising & swelling to the palms of each hand (now on may 22nd the swelling is almost gone), and my gluteus muscles are still sore (which gives me a gentle reminder of the crash ever time I sit down). The bike has a broken water pump cover, more fairing damage, and a bent handlebar. My leathers have a whole in the rear so they have been sent of to Barnacle Bill for repairs. On a positive note, if there is one, I'm still scored in 3rd place.

OK, so I'm 2 for 2 on crashing, just #$**@ great. I was ready to sale the bikes & trailer after the second crash Sunday afternoon and get a new "safer" hobby. The second crash was similar to the first, right hand corner, and off the bike without any warning. After getting a good night rest I wake up Monday morning with a clearer head and realize a pattern is developing. I've wrecked the bike 3 times this year all of the right side so I start to blame the exhaust can for dragging. Rule #1, racers need a reason for crashing, even if it is their fault. From my three previous seasons of racing I've averaged one wreck per season, so I'm seriously starting to doubt myself and the bike at this point. For the bike, I've decided to call in the experts. I dropped the bike off at GMD Computrack so they can check the alignment, do the geometry optimization, and upgrade the suspension. I'm hoping the Penske shock I have on my GS500 can be reworked to fit the MZ, which will help trim the bill. Also, I've sold my 1991 GS500 which will help pay for most of the bill. Stay tuned I have to wait and see if there's a fix for me. I do know I committed, or need to be committed!

Taking a break from racing - not by choice!

It's been a while since my last update so let me catch you up on things. You'll remember from my May report that I suddenly caught the Carlos Checa crashing disease (from his 1999 season), and fell twice during the weekend. Discouraged, sore, and bewildered on exactly why I crashed my small brain formulated some theories & a plan a few days after the crashes. I still think the muffler was dragging in right hand turns, removing traction from the rear, thus causing me to go boom. Anyway I decided to have the bike's suspension & geometry checked out by Kent at GMD Computrack of Atlanta just to rule out any problems with the bike. My wife looked into a few psychiatrists to check me out but none were willing to dive into the mind of a crazed racer.

My wife graciously let me take her to Atlanta on Mother's Day weekend with our children so we could drop off the bike at GMD. Over the next three weeks GMD optimized the geometry, installed Race Tech springs & emulators, and reworked the Penske shock from my GS500 to work on the MZ. I could tell you exactly what they did to modify my bike but then I'd have to shoot you. I sold my first born to pay for the charges and left early Saturday morning June 10th to pick up the bike. I arrived at the shop around noon and talked with Kent about the modified suspension, wheel alignment, etc. I left the shop around 1pm and set off for Carolina Motorsports Park about 6 hours away hoping to race on Sunday. Once I arrived at the track the adrenaline must have kicked in because I decided without a doubt to race on Sunday. Of course this meant I stayed up until 11pm prepping the bike for 8am practice. Just to recap Saturday for you, I drove about 12 hours or 625 miles (Raleigh, NC-Atlanta, GA-Kershaw, SC) and then worked on the bike for about 3 1/2 hours. Yeah, normally I cram a little more into a day but Saturday was a day off from work, so I took it easy!

Sunday Morning I awoke at 6:30am and remembered a few more items I needed to fix on the bike before taking it through tech inspection. Around 7am I walked over to registration to enter the Super Singles race, got my paper work & CCS got my money, then pushed the bike through tech. I couldn't believe it was only 7:45am which left me plenty of time for the 8am practice session. For the first practice session I planned take it easy and check out the handling of the bike because the suspension & geometry had been drastically changed from the last time I raced the bike. First lap everything feels OK, second lap things are fine, I start to pick up the pace a bit and SHIT I'm on the ground! Some hot shot rider is out in 8am practice trying to break the lap record and he takes my front wheel out! He'll always say it was "just racing" and I'll always say if you can't make a safe pass wait until the next corner. Geez, it was just practice I would have given you the damn corner to avoid going down. I didn't know winning practice was so important maybe I should check the rulebook to see how many points you get for winning practice - sorry just me being very sarcastic.

OK, this sucks the big one. I've busted my butt to pick up the bike, get to the track, and try it out before driving 16 hours to Grattan for the next AHRMA Skorpion cup race on June 17-18. I get back to the pits and realize there is blood running down my arm so I took off my leathers. I've got a nicely bruised elbow and road rash on my forearm, wow this is great. My racing buddies talk me into seeing the EMT's, they took a look and suggested I go to the emergency room. So I end of spending about 5 hours in the emergency room for 5 stitches and two x-rays which didn't show any breaks. I want the thank Suzie, married to Trent Thomas (a fellow Skorpion racer), for driving me to and from the hospital, and generally just taking care of me. Somewhere in the five hour wait to see the doctor I realize my arm isn't going to be in any shape to race the next weekend at Grattan, plus I wasn't sure I wanted go anyway. I cancelled the trip to Grattan and the July race at Mid-Ohio so I could heal mentally, physically, and financially.

Three weeks after the crash I finally took the bike out of the trailer to have a closer look. The bodywork was broken & cracked, especially the upper, the right rearset bracket was snapped during the fall, and bent levers & handlebars. The bike's not too bad as long as the frame & forks aren't bent (they checked out OK). However my leathers, gloves, & helmet need to be replaced. Anyone have an extra grand or two I can borrow? I think it over, add up the cost to return to the track (money I don't have), and make plans to return to the track at the end of September.

Fast-forward to the week of July 17th. I installed the headlight & tail light and road the bike to work this week and even took a Saturday morning ride through some twisties. The bike feels fine but looks ugly without the bodywork. I've ordered a new custom suit of leathers from Barnacle Bill (www.racingleather.com) in Kentucky, plus a new helmet & gloves from JB Motorcycle (jbtireguy@aol.com). Bill Birchfield, one of the Skorpion racers needs a buddy to make the race at Putnam Park on August 19-20. At first I tell him I'm going but just to watch, take lap times, and share the driving to the track. Well you guessed it by Sunday July 23rd I have committed myself to racing at Putnam.

I'll keep you updated about the new gear and the Putnam race. Keep the rubber side down!

Breaking the Habit

Putnam Park August 18-20, 2000 - AHRMA

I won’t keep you in suspense. I made it through an entire weekend without crashing. Can you believe it!? My wife, motorcycle, and medical insurance company are so happy.

My shipmates on the voyage to Putnam were fellow Skorpion cup racers Trent Thomas and Bill Birchfield, plus Bill's father. We used my F150 supercab as the support vehicle putting my motorcycle in truck bed and then put two bikes in the trailer for the trip. Creative packing around the bikes allowed us to pack enough tools, tires, spares wheels, helmets, leathers, etc. for at least 3 race weekends. I picked up passengers and bikes as I drove west through North Carolina and arrived at our hotel around 10:30pm Thursday night. The final mileage to Putnam was just over 700 miles and about 14 hours from my house. Whew, I'm tired just thinking about it.

Friday morning we showed up at the track for a GLRRA track/practice day. There was a riders meeting and we were made aware of the problem areas for Putnam, turn one and the final turn. Turn one was a problem due to mud and slit that covered the corner from a nasty thunderstorm Thursday night. Did I mention we made Trent drive through all the hard rain and wind the last 2 hours of the trip. The local fire brigade washed the corner clean Friday morning. The final turn caused concern due to the guardrail just off the edge of the track (yep, the same one John Ulrich of RoadRacing World hit) and the straw bales lining it. Other than this the track was very safe with lots of run off.

Due to my recent crashing disease, which caused the low confidence syndrome, I went out in the "slow" group and tried not to fall down. Amazing, I actually made it through the first practice session without crashing or being punted off the track. Maybe I can still ride a motorcycle. I moved up into the faster practice groups as my confidence increased and the lap times decreased. However all was not well in the Skorpion camp. Trent didn't have any front brakes, which usually isn't a big deal on a 43hp Skorpion but you do need them for at least one turn at Putnam. He spent the rest of the weekend playing in brake fluid, exchanging master cylinders, cleaning the brake caliper, bleeding, etc. but never found the gremlin with the brakes. Since Trent had already paid for practice he tried riding without front brakes! Doing his best Kurtis Roberts impression with just a rear brake he ran lap times in the 1:29's. In comparison I only ran 1:27's with front brakes! Bill was learning the track quickly and going faster than both of us.

Since I was only entered in the Skorpion Cup race on Saturday & Sunday I offered my bike for Trent for the Supermono race. Obviously Trent does look a gift horse in the mouth and declined my offer to use my bike. I think my black bike didn't match his leathers or something. Anyway the Supermono race was great even though I was a spectator. I watched Mike Kindelin Jr. on his Skorpion Cup bike (about 43hp & 380lbs) chase down and pass Al Charles on his Ducati Supermono (70ish hp, 260lbs and costs about a zillion dollars) on the white flag lap, turning 1:21's on his MZ. Junior led to what should have been the checkered flag but the starter had a brain cramp and threw the white flag again! Well there's only so long you can keep a superior bike behind you and Al Charles took the checkered flag but I'd say Junior won the race.

After Lunch we gridded for the Skorpion Cup race with the Supermono II class gridded in front of us. The Supermono II guys are given a head start since we're held for a 2 wave start. I think they do this so we won't lap them so quickly. In theory the Supermono II bikes (the only rule is that the single 4-stroke engine capacity is limited to 500cc) should be faster than us but most of the grid is filled with older Yamaha SR500's. Anyway, I finished 5th in class with the 3rd & 4th place finishers less than a second ahead of me at the line. Lap times where in the 1:26-1:27 range. No moments on the track so I was feeling pretty good about taking the checkered flag.  

During Sunday's mornings practice sessions I felt like I was going quicker and was feeling more comfortable on the bike. Part of this was being able to move around on the bike easier. My new leathers from Barnacle Bill (www.racingleather.com) had taken a couple days to break in due the thick leather in the suit. As I upped the pace the GMD suspension mods seem to give me more feel from the tires. I didn't have any lap times but I was definitely getting my confidence back.

I got a decent start for the Skorpion Cup race and entered turn one mid-pack. By the end of lap two I was up into 3rd and could see 1st and 2nd place bikes. On lap 3 I passed Bill for 2nd and could still see Mike Jr in 1st but he was starting to pull a large gap from us. The next 5 laps were some of the best racing I've had in a long time. Bill stayed right on my tail section as we passed the Supermono II bikes that started in front of us. We spooked a couple of the riders as we made our way through them but everyone held their lines fairly well. At the flag I took 2nd by about a bike length over Bill. We ran lap times in the 1:24's with one lap in the 1:23's. Yep, I feel better now. I think my mental road rash in now healed and I'm looking forward to Talladega at the end of September.

The new helmet and leathers worked great over the weekend. The new Shoei RFR helmet is the quietest helmet I've ever owned and the leathers made me feel better protected just in case of a crash.

On the drive home we saw the blimp over the PGA tournament and had a great meal at a Steak & Shake. I arrived home at 5:30 am Monday morning. Needless to say I wasn't worth a shit at work on Monday.
