My name is Terry Embury. I am a financial analyst at Palo Verde Nuclear Power Plant. I'm 36, married with 2 girls. I just finished my MBA last year before Daytona ROC.

I race with CCS in the Southwest Region, mostly at Firebird in Phoenix but also at Buttonwillow in California. I'm #161 Am. I will also make it to the CCS ROC in October at Daytona.

My bike is a 96 GSXR 750. Presently it has:

  • full Hindle race exhaust,upgraded from a Yosh slip-on on the stock header after my Daytona get-off
  • Dyno-jet jet kit
  • Sharkskinz bodywork, upgraded after the Air-techs after the same crash at Daytona were rudely dismembered
  • Race-tech gold valves and springs in the front and rear
  • 520 chain and sprockets, just upgraded to Pro-Tek after I realized that those wins would have given me more money in the pocket
  • Storz damper replaced by Rick Mancinelli after my last get-off at 110 bent the rod for the 4th time and caused the old one to leak
  • Attack Performance rearsets, with both footpegs replaced with new ones after my last crash
  • Graves front fairing bracket, replaced the stock after the infamous Daytona crash
  • frame savers
  • Yosh stator cover
  • Galfer brake pads
  • steel-braided front brake lines
  • Dunlop 207s.

As for me, I wear a Shoei RF-800 TC-2, replaced 3 times with Arai (nice helmet but very expensive to replace), another Shoei RF-800 and an HJC. I've got a new set of custom Vanson leathers, replacing my AGV rage suit, that has taken way more punishment than it was originally designed for, a new set of AGV Prisma gloves on the way, after going through 2 sets of AGV GTK2s, and a set of AGV 582 boots that will also soon be replaced by Alpinestar GP Pros, because I've ripped off both toe sliders and a seam is coming out.

And yes, the tally is at 7 crashes, 5 last season, including a concussion at Buttonwillow, a wrist broken in two places and hand broken in two places at Firebird (same crash), and a cracked knee cap and torn AC ligament from getting hit from behind, then run over at Daytona. The two crashes this season, in the first race of the season, I was in second place and lost the front at 110. Not a scratch on me, helmet never touched the ground, caused an oil leak, that I thought I had fixed, went out the next day in practice and fell in my own oil causing a small gash in my pinky finger.

Finally back at it.....

Well this past weekend was my first race weekend back at it since we finished last season in December. We had two races in January, but I told my wife I would take the first couple off to try to allow some injuries a little healing time and allow my wallet to cool off. Chasing a championship last year was expensive. I showed up for the first 2 races of the season and was like a crack addict going through withdrawals. It actually hurt, not to be out there. Every time I'd watch the guys I normally race with come by, I'd mentally place myself out there. To make matters worse, my brand new SV wasn't running. Finally got it figured out - the pulser died, but the parts, although they were air-freighted over-night didn't make it in. Rats...

Anyway, the weather report said cloudy on Saturday and 80% chance of rain on Sunday. Got to the track in the morning and unloaded. I didn't care that I was going to be gridded at the back of the pack at this point. I assumed that I was going to have everyone passing me anyway. It was about 45 degrees outside and the GSXR is very cold-blooded. It died trying to start, so I had to jump start it from the truck - exactly how race weekends are supposed to start out. Finally got it started and went out for practice. Its been almost 4 months since I've been on the bike, and I could really tell. I was as smooth as chunky peanut butter.

Slowly picked up the speed, but couldn't get within 2 seconds of my normal times - Oh well I'll pick it up in the races - always do. The GTO (Heavyweight Endurace) race started and we all ran into turn one. What a mess, I was ahead of one other guy, no problem, just calm down and be smooth, treat it as practice, nothing to prove in the first race back at it. Stayed with the pack until lap 2, missed a downshift going into turn one trying to pass another bike - AHHHHHH, swung way wide, butt cheeks slammed shut, he took back the position, and I got back into it, still one guy behind me, and I'm very close to 3 others. On lap 5 we start catching the slower amateurs. The guys in front of me get by a group of 3 on the front straight, I have to go around them in turn one - I'm not liking it way out here in the dirty part of the track. I get around them, but by then, the guys I was with had about 100 ft lead. I slowly start realing them in, when we catch another group of amatuers, same thing, they get by on the straight, then I have to sneak by in the tight corners. Meanwhile they get about 100 yd lead. I look down at my timer and I'm still 2 seconds off my normal times. I put my head down and start going deep into corners, rear is wiggling going in, then dancing for traction on the way out. I get back about half the distance, when my strategy suddenly bites me. Going in deep leaves much less room for error, and I missed a downshift into turn one again - ahhhhhhh, all the way to the outside of the track, but I kept it on, same thing going into the next turn,missed another shift and coasted through the turn. At this point I decided to just pull off the track. We still had 16 minutes left, and I wasn't going to make it pulling stupid shit like this. I stuck out my leg coming around the tower turn and pulled off. Better to take it home in one piece - wow what a mature decision - so unlike me.

A couple of guys came over to see if there was a problem. I was initially thinking I'd blame the bike, "Oh, it was running badly" or "Needed new tires", but I gave in and told them my skirt blew up in my face, and I had to pull in to get my nails done. (No offense intended ladies) The weather report was showing 80% chance of showers and neither of the tire distributors had brought rain tires. Six riders had rain tires and there were 3  sets of used ones for sale. Bids were going to break $1 million soon. To ensure it didn't rain on Sunday, I knew someone would have to make a sacrifice to the rain gods. There were no virgins handy, so I broke out my check book and wrote a big check for some rain tires.

I left the house Sunday morning and it was raining at my house. By the time I got to the track it had stopped and the clouds were breaking up. Ah ....the sacrifice was working. I picked 3 races at random to run on Sunday - Heavyweight Superbike, Unlimited Supersport and Unlimited GP.

The first race was Heavyweight SB. I got off the line dead last, and an old lady in a VW bus, running on three cylinders passed me on the brakes going into turn one. By the time we hit the front straight I had passed the old lady back, but wasn't gaining on the others. I just decided to run my own race and use it as practice. I just tried to relax and be smooth. I was watching my lap timer and was still 2 seconds off my normal times. Since the guys in front had all checked out, I had no one to follow and was wallowing in my own self-pity by myself. Going into turn 10, the lead amateur showed me a wheel on the inside. I slammed the door shut, and decided to show him what racing was all about. He said that 3 turns later I had stretched out 100 ft on him and by turn one 100 yds. I instantly cut off a full second. I was actually glad he was back there to kick me in the ass. I held those same times for the next few laps and slowly started gaining on the guys in front. The race ended. I was in 5th. I went over and thanked the amatuer for showing me his wheel and making my tuck my skirt back in. He laughed and said no problem and that I had really surprised him when I took off like I did.

2nd race was Unlimted SS. I was determined to just try to be smooth and not worry about lap times yet. The race started and apparently my skirt was tucked in pretty well, because I went into turn one mid-pack, Eric Haugo, had a nasty slide, caught it, then slid again, and almost had me for an enema. 2 guys got around us while Eric gathered it back up and got back in the gas. I decided to stick to Eric since he was close and let him show me the way around. Not running quite my old times, but we cut another half second off. I followed him for 3 laps, then waited for him to break into turn one, kept tucked, ..1, 2 brakes....AHHHHH slide it past on the inside, as soon as he saw my wheel he was back on the gas and railed back around the outside of me. I stuck on him like stink on... well you know. I almost caught him again in the turn 14 on the last lap, but had a nasty slide on the paint and  finished a bike length behind him. I finished 7th.

3rd race was Unlimted GP - the money race. These guys get serious when you start waving cash at them. Went into turn one with the pack, roughly half way back. The leaders checked out, leaving the rest of us to sort out 6th through 19th place. I stuck to Eric again and followed him around until I missed a downshift into turn one again, swung way wide, and barely kept it on the track. This little mistake gave him about 100 yds of breathing room that I couldn't make up for the rest of the race. I kept my head down and clicked off consistent times and slowly pulled some distance on the guys in back of me so that I wasn't feeling any pressure, and stayed there for the rest of the race. Finished 11th.

I got all packed up, and just as i was wandering around the pits, the skies opened up. Apparently my sacrifice only covered me and not the other racers. They had just started the last middleweight race of the day and the next 3 guys through the tower turn wrecked on the slick paint and rubber. They called the race and I headed home.

All in all, it was a good weekend. It was my first race as an expert, I didn't fall off and I got my first expert trophy for the 5th place. My times will be back by the next race and I'll have the SV Superbike ready by then. Should be fun.

First race on the new bike..... First Crash

Well this past weekend was my first race on my new 2000 SV650. My first race was GT Lights (30 minute race). I was gridded on the second row. The flag dropped and I got my usual start, and got off the line like a Volkswagon bus with a bad clutch, but luckily the bike pulled past a couple of people before we got to turn 1.By the time we pulled back onto the front straight, I had passed a few more and as I looked up I realized that there was only one guy in front of me - Tom Krause, #6 overall in our region and number one in this class. So, I chased Tom around for a few laps, slowly gaining on him, when in turn 10 in the backsection, I go to lean it in and Eric Anderson has his wheel right where I'm about to put my knee, I try to block a little, but he's already there, so I fall back to 3rd and chase him into the tower turn (T-14), as we pull onto the front straight, I pull past him and get my attention back on Tom. 2 laps later I catch Tom going into T-10, the same place that Eric came though on me. It screws up my line but there's really nowhere to get back around for 2 more turns, so I get through and Tom's right back on me, with Eric right on him. We run for about 6-7 laps like this nose to tail most of the time and we start catching lapped traffic. As we're going into T-1, there's an orange shirt amatuer (first time on the track), and he's on the inside of T-1, so I decide to go outside and as I move to pass him on the outside, Eric had passed Tom on the brakes into T-1 and forced himself inside the Amatuer. The amatuer bobbled and I had to go way wide to avoid him and couldn't get back into the gas because I was out in the marbles (dirty part of the track),so Eric then was leading with Tom in second and me back to third. I catch back up a couple of turns later and we run like this for a couple of more laps... damn I'm getting tired.

We pass the white flag and I know its now or never. Eric had strectched out about 20 bikes on Tom and I through the lapped traffic, so I knew there was no hope of catching him unless he screwed up, but Tom was only about 8 bike lengths away... so I try to go in deep int T-1 on the brakes, pass Tom, but now I'm going too fast into the turn *&^^&@* (loud cussing in my helmet), I swing way wide out to the rumble strips, but stay on the track, Tom comes back by me and I tuck back in, OK Tom, T-10 is where I'm going to get you, so I get as close to him as possible without actually being in his shoes as we come out of turns 8-9, I get on the gas hard, slip up the inside, damn too fast, try to bend it back in and lost the front right in front of him *&%%&% (much more loud cussing in my helmet). I pick up the bike and push it out of the way. I'm fine the handle bar is bent, and the brake foot lever is mangled and a another little problem I wouldn't know about for awhile. I found out later that I actually finished 4th because we had lapped from 5th back.

Back to the pits, I replace the tires, buy a handle bar, then I go to start it and there's no power. ????? I check the fuses, blown main fuse. As I'm about to replace it with my only other one, I'm thinking what could have caused that ? and I notice the starter button is mashed in. I pull off the control pod and sure enough it had grounded to the handle bar (motorcycles these days just fall apart on you) so I shove some duct tape (the racer's friend) in there and put it back together. First call  - AUUGHHHH, I quickly enlist the help of 2 guys in the pits who push me around unitl we get it started, and run the bike back to tech. Quick look over, and she says, "OK, did you stop by the ambulance ?" Second Call - Stop at the ambulance and they take some info and let me go. Third Call -I stop in front of the pits and just yell, "Someone hold the bike" and jump into my leathers, helmet on, and get to the track entrance just in time to get on the grid - no warm-up lap, completely cold brand new tires - "Can you say Disney on Ice ?"

The race started and I got a decent start, but had to back off going into turn 1, tires are sliding everywhere and I start talking to myself again, "OK keep calm, gotta go slow until the tires get scrubbed in and heated up a little, or we'll be on our head again, we can worry about catching up once we're sure, and what's with all the "we" stuff, who else is in here?" So the tires warm up and I pass a couple of people, and end up back in 4th again. Not too bad all things considered.

Sunday comes and I'm signed up for the first 2 races back-to-back. First race is Lightweight Superbike. I get a good start, and end up in third for the whole race, but its really close bewteen 2nd, 3rd and 4th.

Next race is Supertwins. Not sure why I entered this one, because we're racing Duc 748s, 916s, TL1000Rs and 2 of us SVs. I get a decent start, but you can tell right from the start, that we're lacking in power, but the 2 of us on SVs catch the lead 2 - a Duc 748 and a TL1000R coming out of T-2. We all come out of T-14 within 10 feet of each other, then, apparently there was a sale or something going on at the other end of the track, so they took off to see what was going on. We caught them again, by turn 4, then they'd gain a little on the short chute in the back and we'd be right back on them in 8-9. The guy on the Duc passed the TL (Roger Coyle) in turn 10 on the inside, and then Roger low-sided right in front of us in T-14 and we were dodging bike pieces and trying to miss Roger. That gave the guy on the Duc another 100 feet and then we never could reel him back in. I followed Eric around for the last lap and he went tight into T-14 knowing that I was going to try something and I took the normal line and got a great drive out of the turn. If the finish line would have been another 30 feet, I would have had him, but.....another 3rd.

Last race was the Lightweight Superbike. I got the best start of the weekend, went into T-1 in 2nd and decided to try to follow Scott (#4 in our region and #1 in this race) and get him to show me the fast way around. Ever hear - "if you don't want to know the answer, don't ask the question ?" well I got in too hot into the 8-9 combo and didn't think I could make it, so I ran off into the dirt, waited until the expert freighttrain came through and got back on (*&*&^%&&$$#@ (more loud cussing). I work my way back up to third and decide to wait it out until near the end of the race to try a move, because we always seem to slow each other down, passing back and forth. Well it came down to the last lap, Tom took the inside line everywhere he could and left me with the long way around, so in T-14 I decided to try to see if it would work. He took it tight on the inside and I got a good drive, but was just a little short again..... 3rd place for the 3rd time

Overall, it was a good weekend, I ended up with 2-4ths and 3-3rds, the bike still ran and I was still in one piece. It was the first time racing this bike and I'll be looking for a first here soon.

And the guy who bought my GSXR 750 crashed it challenging for second. Both he and the bike are fine...but it was very sad to see my baby mangled like that and me not being the one who had done it....

It's 107 outside, lets put on leathers and go racing...

We were back at it again this past weekend in Phoenix. For all of you that aren't familiar with Phoenix, its located about 20 miles from the SUN. It could have been a little hotter, but only if I was actually on fire.

On Saturday, I was entered in the GT lights (lightweight endurance) and Heavyweight Sportsman races.  We were on the Indy test track, which we've never raced before. The way you can tell the difference between a motorcycle track and an Indy test track, apparently, is by the gator bumps conveniently located at the apex of each corner. They start out smooth, then turn into large 6-7" ridges like a big ruffles potato chip on the outside edges - not very condusive to staying on the bike... but I digress.

Its 1:00 and over 100. The flag drops on the GT Lights (30 minute endurance), I get a decent start from the second row, and going into turn 1, I'm in second. Just as I'm about to turn in, I hear what sounds like a Volkswagon with open exhausts, and its Tom Lenz on his Buell coming up fast with his elbows out like a motocrosser. He bumps me out into the first set of gator bumps, I recover and am dropped back to 3rd. Tom then runs right into the side of Eric Anderson, who was leading, and I see what I thought was Eric's brake lever go flying by. As he tried to downshift and brake, he realized the lever was gone (turns out it was his clutch lever), he raised his hand, went wide, and I went inside to chase after Tom. 3 laps later, I passed Tom on the brakes coming onto the front straight.

After about 10 laps, I kept looking back to make sure no one was sneaking up, and I had a good 60 yard lead, so I decided to back off a little to save my energy for the last 5-6 laps. I did this for 3 laps or so, then looked back again to see Jim Kovacs looking right back at me. He's an amatuer, but had run up through the whole pack of experts and wanted to lead. We still had another 17 minutes or so to race, so I decided to let him go, and try to pick it up later in the race, besides, I still couldn't see the next expert. I ran around the track just trying to rest where I could and watched as they waved the half-way flags. 5 more laps went by and I noticed that Tom Krause had apparently had the same strategy, because he was starting to gain on me.

I figured we had about 8 laps now, so I put my head down and started taking it seriously again. I was gaining on Kovacs pretty quickly, and once I could see him again, I knew I could catch him. He was still riding pretty smoothly, but I could tell he was slowing. I passed him on the brakes in the last turn before the front straight, and just kept going. As we came down the front straight, the white flag was waved. It appeared that my timing was perfect. I just ran a fast smooth lap, not giving him any room to pass on the inside, and once I came back onto the front straight for the checkered flag, he had dropped back to about 20 bike lengths behind. First place. I was absolutely exhausted and sweat was rolling down my face. I pulled into the pits and some of my buddies took the bike. I could hardly stand up. Nothing like racing in a blast furnace.

Next race out was Heavyweight Sportsman. I had nothing left when Eric and Scott took off. Actually my skirt blew up in my face, and my panties were all wadded up and it distracted me. I limped around in 3rd for a while, wondering why the hell I wasn't out by the pool with a nice frosty mug of my favorite malt beverage, instead of subjecting myself to this. I looked behind to see if there was anyone close, and Chuck Coyle was looking back at me from about 6 feet behind. Yikes, get on the gas or get out of the way, so I tucked in my skirt, pulled the panteis out of my crack and picked up the pace. I was getting a little tired and coming out of turn 2, ran wide, hit the gator bump, and found myself testing those ridges I mentioned before. I managed to keep it up, but got a tank-slapper from hell, that may have broken the end of my thumb, I'm not sure yet. Its all bruised and I can't move it much, but I managed to stay in front of Chuck and finish in 3rd.

I am soooo glad this day is over...

Sunday comes and its 10:30 and already over 100, this doesn't look good at all. I was still pretty dehydrated from the day before. Ever felt like you're dehydrated and water-logged at the same time ?? So, I'm in the first 2 races back-to-back Lightweight Superbike & Supertwins.

The race starts and I get my usual start, and shot off the line like a volkswagon on 3 cylinders and a bad clutch. I run around for awhile, its all kind of a blur right now. All I remember was that it was painful, hot, and sweaty. Half-way flags come out and I'm all by myself in 4th, no one close behind, and the guys in front are too far to worry about. Coming out of turn 2, back in the gas, I get a quick "Vurp". (Vurp - Vomit burp, rises just to the back of your throat, then you have to swallow it again) Ickkkkk !!!!!. What the hell was that ??? I back off a little and realize that I' m overheating quickly. The race ends and I putt by in 4th, and pull into the pits. I'm in the next race. How am I going to do this ??? As I pull up to our pit area, my wife hands me a bottle of water and Chad asks if I want some water down the leathers, I kind of nod, and he grabs a bottle of water.... from the cooler.... and pulls back the collar of my leathers.... and pours....I almost came out of the leathers through the neck hole...it was ice cold, and while I was putting my eyes back in my head, he then poured it down the front. OK, ready to go now, by golly...

Next race is Supertwins. RC-51s, TLRs, Ducs & 2 of us on SV 650s. Run into turn 1 mid-pack, in 4th. Coming out of Turn 2 a TLR runs by in the straight to turn 3. We, the other SV and I, stay with the pack for the first lap until we hit the front straight. The bigger bikes pull about 6 bikes lead on us, then we catch up a little on the brakes, then they take a few more bike lengths on the next straight. I stay in 5th and get beat by an RC-51, TL1000R, 996 Superhawk. The Ducs were behind us. Not too bad, all things considered.

Last race of the day was Lightweight GP. By this time, I've had enough time to rest, have been drenched in wet t-shirt and poured bottles of water over my head. Its now 107, the perfect weather for racing.... I'm actually not feeling too bad though. Race starts and I go into turn 1 in 3rd. Tom Krause is in 2nd and Eric Anderson is in 1st. Scott is in 4th. He's normally way out front. We run around the track once very close within 3 bike lengths of each other. I decide its time to make a move on Tom, and wait until he brakes for turn 1, wait 1, 2, 3, see God, brake now HARD....AGHHHHH, tail is wiggling in the air, I let off the brakes and dive in, and run a little wide coming out of 2, Tom comes right back, and has Scott with him,  I get back into the gas, and it doesn't seem to be pulling, and they're getting away, then WACKA, WACKA, WACKA, shit !!!! motor is now making some hellacious racket, so I pull in the clutch, raise my hand, get off the race line, and look back to make sure I'm not going to get a motorcycle enema, and pull off.

Blown motor, not quite the way I had planned it...luckily it didn't seize on me and throw me on the pavement or come out of the cases and dump oil. So, I ended up with a 1st, 3rd, 4th, 5th, a check for $80 and a blown motor. The next race is in 2 weeks. I won't be making it to that one. Luckily I have a previous engagement with the California Superbike School at Road America for 3 days, then another 3 days at Texas Motorsports Ranch. Hopefully the bike will be back together for Buttonwillow in late June. See you there....

 

In honor of the recent Olympics

I will attempt a high speed dismount with a difficulty rating of 10.0

 So, I get my bike back from blowing the motor back in May. I take it to the track, and decide not to run the endurance race on the new motor so that there is no chance of overheating it just yet. I go through tech and tech finds that my oil drain plug wasn’t wired. I guess I just assumed that the guys rebuilding the engine had re-wired it, since they re-wired my oil filter. OK, no big deal, I take it back to our pit area and re-wire it. Then second practice session, I look down and I discover oil on my boot. Where is this coming from ? So we strip off the bodywork and found that the guys who did the engine had also replaced the stock airbox with individual pods, but just left the crankcase breather hoses sitting on the top of the engine. So I run down to Home Depot and get parts to route them to the catch can. What else can go wrong ?

 So they run the endurance races, and the only race I’m in today is the Heavyweight Sportsman. I go out for the warm-up lap, and pull into my grid position. Another rider pulls up next to me and tells me that something fell off my bike in Turn 1. I look down, and the radiator cap is gone. Great, one more thing that I had wired before, but they didn’t re-wire. I throw up my hands, and push my bike over to the inside wall. The race starts, and I’m not in it. Its been since May that I last raced, and I’m highly disappointed. The corner workers did a good job, and found my radiator cap, but it was mangled. I picked up a new one on the way home. What a sh*tty day.

 So Sunday morning comes, and I’m in races 1,2 & 9, lightweight superbike, Supertwins & lightweight GT. I go out for practice and nothing falls off and the oil stays where its supposed to – inside the engine.

 OK first race starts, and I get off the line pretty good in 3rd, then going into turn 1, I downshift twice, yikes – the bike kicks sideways a little and I realize that I’m a gear off and into the rev limiter. Can’t shift now, because I’m leaned all the way over for the corner. I come off the corner, and I’m supposed to downshift one for the next corner, so I just leave it where it is, and get passed going into T2 by 2 guys. Ok, back on track for gearing, and I chase the guys around the track. My little missed shift lost the lead group and I just ran around by myself in 5th, until one of the front guys’ tires started to go off. He started coming back towards me and I passed him right at the line to take 4th. My lap times were miserable – 1:15s, and my riding and shifting sucked. I was also sliding the rear quite a bit coming off the turns on the gas. I pull back into the pits, and one guy gives me some water, and I have another grab a tire gauge and go down 1 pound in the rear.

 The second race – supertwins – starts, and I get a pretty good launch, and go into T1 in 2nd. An amateur on a Duc 919 decides to pass me going into T2 on the inside, so I back off just a hair, and another expert comes by on my outside. The Duc loses the front trying to make the turn, and collects the other expert who had just come around me. Luckily their sliding took them out of my path so I maintainted my line and missed the carnage by about 2 feet, but not without gathering a couple inches of seat in my clenched butt cheeks. I’m back in second and chasing a TL 1000R around. They red-flag the race, and we start all over.

 This time, I get an ok start, but going into T1, a guy on a Honda Superhawk 996, passes me to put me in third behind the TL1000R. I chase them around the track and reel them in in the back section, only to lose them on the front straight. On lap 4, the Superchicken cuts through the cones, one of them catches his shift lever, and rips it off. It goes flying past me, and he throws up his hand, and pulls off the front straight. I look back and I’ve got almost the full front straight on the guy in 3rd, and about 10 bike lengths to catch the guy on the TL. I didn’t catch him, and ended up in second. Not too bad considering I was racing Buells, TL1000Rs, Duc 916s and a Superhawk 996, on a little SV 650. I cut off 1.5 seconds a lap to get back down to where I normally run in the mid 1:13s.

 Last race of the day. I’m feeling pretty good. I’m determined to give Scott Welch, who is the local lightweight fast guy, a run. The flag drops, and I get the launch of my race career. I can see in the corner of my eye, that there’s one guy with me on the inside. I shift, and he drifts back a little, then shift again and he’s gone. Holy Sh*t. I’m leading going into T1. I wait until the last brake marker, stay tucked, and hit the brakes. The bike squirms a little, then off the brakes and back in the gas hugging the inside of T1. Then into T2, I’m expecting Scott at any moment to show me a wheel, but nothing. The bike squirms going into T2, and I get it turned hard, back in the gas, and I come out of 3 both tires sliding, leaned over, I get it up and it catches and pulls a slight wheely and I still can’t believe I’m out front.  Into T8/9 combination and I’m hard on the gas coming out. I’m expecting Scott to put his wheel in on T10, because there’s a fast way around the track and there’s a good block pass way into T10. I know, I’ve used it several times myself. Apparently he wasn’t close enough to try it, and I go through still in first. Into T11 & 12 still not seeing his wheel yet. Coming out of T13, I’m expecting him to make a move in the tower turn onto the front straight, so I need a good drive to make sure he doesn’t get his chance.

 At this point I break from my description of the events, and go to the witnesses;

 The announcer actually said, “Holy Sh*t” over the announcing system. The announcer’s tower looks right down onto T13. Then there was about 15 seconds of silence, followed by, ”I just witnessed the most wicked hi-side I’ve ever seen.”

 John Jewett told me that I was all the way cranked over, almost to the elbow, when I got back into the gas coming out of T13. The rear stepped out almost 3 feet, then caught when it was completely sideways. I was thrown straight up in the air, hands still on the bars, a perfect pike position, before coming down on my head and back in front of the bike. Luckily the bike missed me. Luckily Scott was going to make his move on the outside of T14, because had he tried for the inside, I would have been thrown right in front of him. Somehow, the bike righted itself, and continued on, to almost block Scott’s path into T14, then it crashed head-on into a guard-rail. Meanwhile, I scrambled out of the way, to get out of the way of the freight-train that was following me. 

 The bike sustained some pretty nasty damage – completely demolished bodywork, front fairing stay, left clip-on and gauge pack. Also, the forks are bent, it took off one rear-set, and the MB Motorsports frame saver twisted on the frame.

 The doctor’s recommendation was a change in my diet to include heavy doses of Motrin.

 The witnesses all gave me perfect 10s for the dismount and landing, but the racers who were close, all gave me 9.85s because they had to dodge the bike. Actually I thought the bike was going to go for the win without me.

 At least I was out front when it happened. I’ll be back. Count on it.
