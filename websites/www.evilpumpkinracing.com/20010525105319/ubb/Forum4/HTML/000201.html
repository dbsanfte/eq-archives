Author Topic:   Mudflation
Knightlymuse posted 04-19-2001 12:07 AM CT (US)     
I was reading an interesting thread on the sony web site the other day and thought I would bring it here.

EQ is a closed environment. The server supplies us with an endless supply of bad guys to kill and loot and quests to complete. Kill a thousand Orc’s and the server will just make more. Since objects do not decay and very little is consumed (reagents, food, arrows, some throwing weapons, the cost of spells and some other miscellaneous stuff.) you end up where everyone has more and more which leads to ‘mudflation’. Common items cost less and less, rare items go for big bucks.

The life of a server: In the beginning everything has a great deal of value. Even the basics like food and water cost tons of money on the open market. Nobody has anything and life is hard. As the server ages, money becomes common, twinks become common, prices start to drop, everyone becomes a little bit more laid back and even give stuff away to newbies. The population of high-level characters starts to rise (+50 lvl) and a lot more of the hard stuff suddenly becomes easy.

This has all happened in the space of just two short years. Now my question is how does this progress over say the next two years? Five? Ten?

Sleethr posted 04-24-2001 09:48 AM CT (US)         
NO DROP

both good and bad.

From a economy standpoint, I think it's a bad thing. I mean, it would totally make you camp for an item you wanted for your character. Would give you zero alternatives to getting the item short of a potentially long camp.

Good from a "nice" factor...in that if you was a warrior and the SMR dropped in your group...the caster(s) would automatically get the item...no greed...all the drops would be a Need Before Greed situation.

So you would be competing with less people for the item you could've bought in EC for 10KPP that you saved up killing HG or what not.

NO DROP would also make tradeskills even more valuable and wanted. No more level 1 twinks running around in SMR's or Full Crafted. They would need to build or buy their low level gear from a craftsman. That would make skilled craftsmen so much more usefull and profitable.

However; then VI would need to come up with some money sinks...or you will end up with almost everyone having millions of PP sitting in their accounts with nothing to spend it on once they got thru the newbie tailor/smithed gear phase.

Would need to add more spell components that cost good sums of PP at the high levels and weapons/armor that needs repair...etc...

Just don't see VI making those kind of changes this late in the game's life.

I'm betting some future game will use the lessons learned about virtual economies going forward to build a better game though.
