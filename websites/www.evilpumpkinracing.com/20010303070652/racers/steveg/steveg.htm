First and only crash this year!

October 15th, 2000

This was my first time at Texas World on the new bike and I had alot of work to do to dial in the suspension.   My first race was Heavyweight SuperSport Expert.   I got a decent start and ran a clean race but ended up in 8th place.   Afterwards I added a little more compression damping to the front forks and got ready for the next race.   Unlimited GP...This is the first time I entered this race and because of the lack of points I was gridded in the last row of the second wave for the start.   The first wave was released and then they let us go.   I got an excellent start and made it to the front of our wave as we entered the second turn.   This was a 10 lap race for the cash instead of the normal 6 lap races.   I put my head down and really started pushing hard.   I made some pretty close passes but this was for the money so there was no time to be nice.   Lap after lap and one rider at a time I worked my way up, but time ran out.   I finished 12th from a 30th (dead last) starting position.   I started getting into a rhythm in the last race and I was getting more comfortable with the ZX6R.   My 3rd race was Heavyweight Superbike Expert, I was gridded on the outside of the front row.   I got the best start of my life.   Took the holeshot and led the race for 4 of the 6 laps.   On the fifth lap Larry Locklear got by me on the back straight, he had an incredibly strong motor in his bike, but what can I expect, I was running against 750 and 600 superbikes on a bone stock ZX6R with a pipe.   I was able to stay behind him in the draft and he went to the inside as we entered the horseshoe.   I went to the outside and started to go around him.   I had the bike at max lean angle with the foot peg buried into the ground and my head resting on the side of his upper fairing (we were kinda close to each other).   I made the pass taking the inside of the next turn but he was able to motor by me again on short chute that heads into the chicane before we get back onto the banking.   I tried to stay behind him on the straight but his bike was too fast and he was able to break the draft.   I closed on him in turn one and two but by the time we hit the back straight he pulled away again.   I made one last final push to get by him going into the chicane and I got on the gas a little too soon and nearly hi-sided myself into the next county.   I managed to save it but I lost my drive onto the front straight and had to settle for 2nd place.   Needless to say I was very excited to podium again since this is only my first year as an expert and especially happy with the new bike.   This Kawasaki ZX6R is incredible!   Thank you OutDoorWise.com!   Only one more race left...Middleweight Superbike Expert.   I was gridded mid pack on the outside and got another good start.   I made a move to the outside going into turn one and fell into 6th place.   The track was still a little wet in the back section so everyone was pretty cautious but things were going pretty smooth.   I had a good rhythm going for the entire day when all of a sudden things changed.   Larry Locklear blew his motor going into turn 4 and got of to the inside of the track...this is normally a good thing except he was carrying too much speed and could not get the bike stopped.   He got back onto the track at the apex of turn four and went straight across the track.   This normally isn't much of a problem, but this time a nice coating of oil was deposited all over the track.   I saw the smoke coming out of turn 3 but it looked like it was on the outside of the track.   No oil flags were out but it was too late.   A pack of us went through turn 4 and none of us made it.   It is amazing how fast you end up on the ground when you hit oil.   There you are at full lean cruising through the corner then all of a sudden all you hear is the grinding you bike makes as it slide out in front of you.   I did get to see it sliding away from me as I went feet first into the dirt.   Oh well, The only damage was to the right clip on and right rear set.   Not a scratch on the tank or tail section.   Thank you Terry at Hi-Sideracing.com for the frame sliders!   Due to all the carnage they red flagged the race and since we had finished 50% of the race there was no restart.   Official finishing position 5th.   At least it was the last race I had this weekend, so its time to go home spend some more money on parts and whip out the fiberglass repair kit.   Until next year...!!!   Visit my sponsors!

Endurance Round 6 - A New Team is Born

August 12th, 2000

Keith did it to us again!   After missing the last race, he told me he would not let us down again.   Well to make a long story short, he did not show up AGAIN! Todd sold his F3 after the last race so we did not have a bike that we could run in Mediumweight SuperStock.   I managed to talk Derrick into letting us run his GSXR 750, so we created a new team to run in Unlimited GP.   This is going to be a blast and alot of work!   We decided to run Michelin Pilot Endurance Compound Slicks.   These tires are incredible, thanks Dave at Island Racing Service.  

Let the work begin.   The week before the race, Derrick had Ottis Lance of Lonestar Motorsports do some head work to his bike.   The head was milled, ported and a full radius valve job was performed.   Needless to say the bike is unbelievably fast.   I chose to scrub in the new Pilots for the race and see how Derricks bike handled.   I can only say that it had to be the worst handling bike I have ever ridden.   I just sold my GSXR and I know how it should perform, but this thing was almost unrideable.   When and if you could get the brakes to work to make a turn the thing would NOT turn in to the corner.   Trying to pick the throttle up mid corner was useless, because the bike would stand itself up and run wide.   If the turn was not as smooth as glass the thing would porpoise so bad mid corner the front and rear would take turns pushing and sliding.   It was frustrating to say the least.   Adjust the suspension, right?   I know, but Derrick has been riding the thing like this so long he is used to it and was hesitant to let me make changes.   Eventually I prevailed.

  1. Forks were raised 2mm (lowering the front end).
  2. Fork rebound damping 1 turn from max.
  3. Fork compression damping 3/4 turn from max.
  4. Fork Preload/Sag unchanged.
  5. Shock rebound damping 1/4 turn from max.
  6. Shock compression damping 1/2 turn from max.
The thing is now on rails!   Lets go racing!

Derrick takes the start from the second to last row (new team no points).   He rides for 21 minutes and averages 1:32's.   Todd gets the next stint and rides for 30 minutes averaging 1:37's.   Not bad considering he has never seen this track or ridden a bike with this much power.   I bring up the tail and run for 25 minutes averaging 1:27's.   Funny thing is, we are all still faster on the 600.   Oh well, we continue without a problem and our pit stops are getting much faster.   We need a quick fueling gas can but this is our first year endurance racing.   The race continues without red flags and on our second sessions Derrick rode for 19 minutes (old man) and averaged 1:33's, Iron Man Todd rode for 1 hour 2 minutes averaging 1:34's (quite an improvement) and I rode for 42 minutes averaging 1:27's again (getting old) hehe.   Derrick took the checkered flag 22 minutes later averaging 1:35's.   luckily no one on our team crashed and there were no red flags.   We finished 5th in Unlimited GP and had a blast!! Next round is at Brainerd September 15th, 16th and 17th, for the 2000 Evil Pumpkin Reunion.   See you there!   Visit my sponsors!

Endurance Round 5 - Boy is it HOT!!

July 15th, 2000

I should have guessed that this was going to be "one of those weekends".   The endurance team owner, Keith Kerber, somehow forgot to mention that he could not make it to the race.   Did I mention that he also had the bike?   Luckily Derrick and I were able to convince a fellow racer to let us use his F3 for the race and we now have four team mates again. Welcome Todd Thompson to the team.  

We get to the track, slap on some new rim skins, register and tech the bike with no problems.   Practice goes on without any incidents and we are ready to race.   Todd takes the start and the 5th round of the CMRA endurance series begins.   Todd is a first year novice and we have taken him under our wing.   Todd gets a black flag on the 18th lap because our transponder is not working.   The transponder is replaced 10 minutes later it is my turn.   There isn't much of a chance that we could win our class so I decide to work on technique.   You get plenty of passing and being passed practice in an endurance due to the large assortment of bikes.   I'm still working on riding smooth and maintaining corner speed.   In order to ride smooth you must stay relaxed, especially in an endurance.   If you are fighting the bike and hanging on too tight then you being to get arm pump and that will only tire you out faster.   The heat was another factor.   It was 115º with the heat index and a few riders tried to ignore it but failed.   They ended up in the hospital with heat stroke.   After 30 minutes I was baked.   It was Derricks turn.   Todd and I rode 3 sessions and Derrick rode 2.   The 4 hour was over and we managed 9th in Mediumweight SuperStock.   We did not crash, the bike did not break and it was time to go home and rest.   Another fun weekend in the books.   Visit my sponsors!

Where's the Team? Are we getting smaller?

May 17th, 2000

Time flies when you are having fun!   We set out on another journey to the land of College Station, Texas.   Home of the Aggies!   Another round of endurance racing at Texas World Speedway.   We again stayed with my aunt Margaret and uncle Robert for this trip and again special thanks are in order.   Team Gulf Star Racing (ie Evil Pumpkin Racing South) is at it again.

Keith took the bike home to Houston after the last endurance race at Hallett so numerous repairs still needed to be performed to get the bike ready for competition.   The right handle bar is replaced and our spare bodywork is fitted to the bike.   As we are getting ready for practice Jeff Lee decides to let us know that he is going to ride for another team :-( We understand and agree that it is in his best interest.   Jeff has become a good friend and I will miss having him on our team.   Good Luck Jeff!   Well we still have 3 riders left for this 6 hour endurance.   That is until Keith informs us that he does not want to ride because he is having some monetary problems.   Derrick and I offer to pay his share for this race and he can pay us back at the next round.   He declines and now it is only Derrick and myself running a 6 hour endurance....JOY!!!   I know, just think about all that track time.   That is not the real concern.   Derrick and I cannot afford new tires, the entry fee and gas for the race.   We were not expecting this to happen.   We opted to run the tires that were on the bike.   They ONLY have a 4 hour endurance on them :-O   Well at least it is not raining!!!!

Plan of attack for a 6 hour endurance with two riders and bad tires:
  1. Easy on corner exists to save the tires.
  2. Stay relaxed and do not over exert on the first session.
  3. Try and run at least 45 minutes...even if it is 105º
  4. Do NOT Crash!
  5. HAVE FUN!
We flipped a coin for the start. "Tails", I win.

And away we go!   I get another great start from the end of the second wave and catch the tail of the 1st wave going into turn 1.   The field begins to stretch out and on the 7th lap I was pushed off the track by Team Crash Clinic #4.   I was trying multiple passes on the previous 4 corners and he kept running wide or slamming the door underneath me in the turns.   I went for an outside pass on turn 7, one of the fastest corners at Texas World, and just as I was passing him he changed his line to the outside.   I went straight off and into the grass at around 120!   As the bike was squirming around beneath me, my left foot flew off the peg and went to the ground as a rudder.   Front brake, rear brake, front, rear, etc... the wheels kept locking up as I tried to slow things down.   Well low and behold, 3 rolls of expand a steel were right in my path.   As a last ditch effort to stop before hitting the steel I locked down both brakes and started to flat track the bike sideways...   (Thank goodness for all those years moto-cross racing!) The bike came to a stop with the front wheel touching the middle roll of steel!   Needless to say I needed to make a pit stop to clean myself.   I slowly started heading back towards the nearest corner on the track which was now about 200 yards away.   I rolled onto the track with my hand in the air to let everyone else know I had a problem.   On the way back to the pit I noticed I could not move my foot around on left side, I had ripped the sole halfway off my boot.   Pulled in to the pit, duct taped my boot together, checked the tires, removed some debris from the bike then told Derrick to get ready for his turn.   I went back out for three laps to check the bike.   Everything seemed fine.   Lucky us!!   We changed riders and went to go clean myself...j/k!

Derrick rode for about 25 minutes and luckily Jeff came by to ride one session for us.   Jeff went out for 26 minutes.   It was my turn again and we were not even close to riding for 45 minutes like we needed to.   We had to ride for 45 minutes so the person not riding could get at least a 45 minute break.   I stayed out for 43 minutes and had fun!   We fueled up again and Derrick went out for his second session.   34 minutes later he came in...   I could not do it.   We parked the bike for 17 minutes :-(   Did I say "at least it is not raining!"?   You guessed it!   My first lap out, the bottom fell out of the sky.   Our Michelin Pilots were, how should I put it? TOAST!!!   It rained and it rained and it rained.   I rode and rode and rode.   1 hour and 7 minutes later I came in.   The rain had stopped and a dry line was forming on the track.   The DOT tires are slick on the left side and it was Derrick's turn.   I had to ride until the rain stopped because Derrick is not comfortable in the rain yet.   He rode for 38 minutes and it began to rain again so he pulled in.   I did not have time to rest, so I could not ride again just yet.   While we rested I checked the tires and they were officially shot.   However, we must take the checkered flag so we don't DNF.   55 minutes remained in the race.   We decided to park the bike until there were only 8 minutes left in the race.   48 minutes later I hopped on to finish the race.   We finished 12th in class and 41st overall.   Even though we parked the bike for over an hour in the pit.   Derrick and I had a blast.   That was the most track time I have ever had in a race, 2 hrs 24 minutes!   Since we did not buy tires for the race I spent a grand total of 130.00 dollars for entry fee, gas, and food.   That cannot be beat!   The next endurance race is 2 months away and since I am selling my sprint bike to get a 2000 ZX6R, I won't make it to Oakhill next month...   See you in July!   Visit my sponsors!

We crashed again! :-(

April 29th, 2000

There really is something to be said about a shiny new paint job and a race bike.   Neither last very long!   Well this is the second time Derrick and I repaired and painted the bodywork on the F3 and it has hit the ground every time it gets new paint.

The week started as usual with last minute repairs and paint followed by numerous questions about where we were going to stay for the night.   Endurance racing has it perks and set backs.   Organizing all the team members is a chore and getting everyone's schedules to coincide is even more difficult.   But, the bang for the buck factor has no competition.

We arrived at the track around 7:00 am and went straight to pit road to look for a pit area for the endurance race.   We decided to pit next to a familiar team who just happened to save a spot for us.   Thanks Gabe, John and Chris!   We set up shop, registered, passed tech and got ready for practice.   So far, so good!   Practice went pretty smooth with only one little problem with the bike, a leak from one of the coolant plugs.   Problem solved, new tires mounted and we are ready to race :-)

It is Jeff Lee's turn to start a race so out he goes.   Jeff gets a pretty good start and the race is on!   About 25 laps into the race the first red flag is waved.   As usual, the corner workers did a great job of cleaning up the mess on the track!   Jeff still felt good so he was going to take the re-start.   Off we go again!   Jeff stayed out for another 20 minutes and came in for our first pit stop.   Fuel the bike and Keith goes out for his first session...we have our fingers crossed.   8 laps later another red flag and Keith does not come in with the rest of the bikes.   $H!T...Not again!   Keith did not cause the red flag but he was involved in a collision that took him out hard.   He was OK other than a concussion and some bruises.   Time to do some repairs and quick!   The right side fairing was destroyed, the right clip on was bent down and the GP Tech fairing bracket was bent, but not destroyed.   Thank Goodness for duck tape, safety wire and zip-ties.   It was my turn to go out and the race had started about 10 minutes before we finished the repairs.   Upon rejoining the group I noticed that the front end was a bit out of alignment.   When the front wheel was straight the bars were almost at full lock to the right!   Back to the pits for an adjustment.   I pulled in and headed straight for the armco barrier in front of our pit.   A few good whacks against the armco with the front wheel and she was straight again.   Good old moto-cross trick!   Back out for some more fun.   About 33 minutes into my session the bike started to cut out.   In the frenzy to repair the bike we forgot to top it off with fuel!   Double turds!!!!!   I got pushed in by Robert Brothers on his faithful XR 100.   Thanks Robert!   Derrick went out for his 1st session and lucky for him nothing happened.   Jeff went out for his 2nd session and got black flagged for the remnants of the left fairing about to fall off.   More safety wire to attach it to the header and away he went.   16 laps later another red flag, but this time it was not us!   I got the opportunity to do the re-start and absolutely nailed the start.   Starting from the 3rd to last row of the second wave, I caught the tail of the first wave by turn 2!   Things were going great for 11 laps when another red flag was thrown. Only 20 minutes remained in the race so the officials called it.   We finished 12th in our class and 41st overall.   Terrible finish but we had a blast!! Hope your headache goes away soon Keith!! The next endurance race is 20 days away.   The credit cards are beginning to smoke!   Visit my sponsors!

Endurance Racing is the BEST!

April 8th and 9th, 2000

Well this was the first time the CMRA/CCS had the opportunity race the new MotorSports Ranch track in Cresson, Texas.   I must say that I love this track.   It has a mix of everything: blind turns, elevation changes, off camber turns, and some very fast decreasing radius turns.   On top of that it can be run clockwise or counter clockwise.   Did I mention it is only 45 minutes from my house?!! :-)

Keith, the team owner and the one who crashed the endurance bike at the previous Texas World race, drove up from Houston Friday.   We finish reconstructing the bike around 2:00 am.   Thank God for zip-ties and duct tape!   I get a shout out to Andre' and Nick for their help, Thanks Guys!!   Four hours later we were headed to the track.   Footnote: The CMRA had to limit the number of endurance entries to 50 due to the length of the track (1.7 miles), so we had to wait until registration was closed to see if we made the grid.   I guess someone really loves us (Thanks Connie!), because we made the grid.   53 Teams were going to battle for 4 hours and we were the 53rd team!!! (I guess offering my first born to Connie helped.....J/k)

Practice came off with out a hitch...so let the race begin.   Derrick started the race because he was the only rider that did not get a chance to ride at the previous round...wonder why!!   Race Strategy: We all ride 30 minutes, because we are all out of shape.   At the 30 minute mark we will show you the pit board and you can keep going if you have it in you.   Sounded easy enough...Right?   Well at the 25 minute mark Derrick comes into the pit and did not signal us on the lap prior.   Can you say caught with your pants down...   Oh well..Jeff Lee goes out and about 30 minutes later, the first red flag is thrown.   The only problem is that one of our Good friends Matt has just done a superman impression right in front of pit-row (entering the rattlesnake)!   He got bit!!!   Matt is done and not moving at all!   Next thing you know people are flying over pit wall to see if they can help.   Matt knocked himself silly and broke his collar bone in eight places.   He is recovering well and is back to his old spirits.   Get better soon Matt!   We re-fueled the bike and it was my turn.   "Relax, have fun and DON'T WRECK" Thanks guys!!!   We gridded in our original position (Dead last)   I could barely see the flag when it waved but since everyone else was going I figured what the heck.   After a few laps the pack thinned out and I was having a blast.   This little F3 is so easy to ride.   All of a sudden...the rattlesnake tried to bite me too.   Somebody forgot how to use the brakes and decided to use our bike as a means of bumping off some speed.   I wanted nothing to do with it,   so after he proceeded to ride his front wheel up my back and crash...his bike got hooked up to ours for a second.   Hmmmmmmmm, seems like the little F3 is down on power or I just gained 400 lbs.   I looked back and I had some extra weigh.   Luckily as I entered the next corner his bike detached.   After that things were pretty boring...We cycled through our rotation and I was to finish the race.   Nothing spectacular happened but we did finish 9th in MediumWeight SuperSport, 33rd Overall.   We packed up and headed home for the night.

The Differences between a 600 and a 750!

I thought this was going to be a great day!   First practice, I head out and on the third lap someone decided to take the same line I was taking through the back section.   The only problem is he tried to do it while I was still in my line!   BAM!!!!   I get hit hard on the left rear.   It was time to check the tire pressure in the front tire anyway, so I guess he decided to hit me and send me over the bars for a tire inspection!   I cannot remember exactly what happened as I left the track but for some reason I saved it.   I just got lucky!   Oh well, Things can only get better, right?   Second Practice is pretty smooth but I keep running wide coming out of turn one.   "No problem I can just trail brake the rear to tighten up my line during the races," I thought.

First race: Heavyweight SuperSport Expert
I am gridded on the 3rd row outside and I wish we had a generator for my tire warmers.   Low budget racing at its finest!!   I get a half ass start, get passed by the entire field going into turn 2, and I am sliding all over the place.   This is going to be a long race!!   I must admit that these Pirelli Evo's heat up faster than the Pilots.   After half a lap I am getting back into a rhythm and mistake number one pops his head up.   A 750 can achieve a higher entry speed between turns when compared to a 600.   Needless to say I blew that turn and I swore vengeance on that snail that slid by me.   I passed the snail late on the brakes for the next turn, Hah!   But he got back by me as I missed that turn also :-(   What is the deal!!   I had 600 on the brain.   I backed off and tried to compose myself for the remainder of the race.   I regained a few positions and brought it home in one piece.   14th place...gee wiz!! I have got to get it together for the next race and find a generator to borrow...hehehe!

Second race: Heavyweight SuperBike Expert
Well I could not find a generator so here we go again on cold tires for the start.   I was gridded on the second row outside and I got a much better start, but the same thing happened going into turn 2.   This time I think the entire 2nd wave got by me.   Must relax, must relax, must relax, how can I relax at a time like this!   This was mistake number 1,000.   I started pushing as hard as I could and I made it back up to 8th place when.....OOOOOOOOOPS!   I was trying to make up time going into turn 1 and I went in waaaaaayyyyyy to hot!   I had it cranked and felt the front push hard, so I barely touched the rear to take the load off the front tire and then the rear started to slide.   OK now I am in a two wheel slide with my knee buried in the tarmac holding the bike up and I am going completely sideways towards the edge of the turn.   MAJOR PUCKER FACTOR!!!! I knew I was running out of track and I took a quick glance to see if I could finish the slide.....NOPE!!!   Time for some off-roading!!   This is not the first time I have gone the track at a high rate of speed.   Derrick told me once "He wished he could ride on the track as well as I do off the track."   Very Funny.   I saved it again....but my luck is wearing very thin.   I got back on the track about an 1/8th of a mile from where I went off and decided to just putt around and finish the race.   Man do tires get slick when they are covered in dust.   I almost low sided going into the next turn.   There were only two laps remaining and 5 people got by me during my off track excursion.   14th place again!!   I found a guy who wants to buy my bike, so I am gonna sell it and focus on the endurance series this year.   I might get a Y2K GSXR 750 or hold out for the 2001 GSXR 600.   I hear they are FI and about 7 lbs lighter than the already feather weight 2000 750.   Time to save my pennies :-)   Visit my sponsors!

You gotta take the good with the bad

Mar 4th and 5th, 2000

The Beauty of Having Relatives Near the Track!

My aunt Margaret and uncle Bob live in Bryan, Texas.   They invited our group to stay with them for the weekend and we gladly accepted.   Thank you again Aunt Margaret and Uncle Bob.   This was the first time we did not have to sleep at the track :-)   We arrived Friday night to a nice spread of food and drinks, we ate, drank and went to sleep!

Saturday morning came all too soon.   We drove the 15 miles to the track and set up shop.   Where is Keith (the endurance bike owner/transporter)?   We had the bodywork and he had the bike.   Keith and company finally made it to the track, but practice was starting and we still needed to put the bike together.   Well, we got it together but the 1st practice was over :-(   The 2nd round started and since I had ridden the bike at Oakhill a few weeks prior we let Derrick go out to get familiar with the bike. So This Is What It Is Like To Be An Expert!?

Feb 21st, 2000

The Benefits of Pre-Registration

Well, here we go again....   After 3 long months it is time to pull out the bikes, change the oil and head to the track.   This week we are heading back to what I now call my home track.   Oakhill raceway in Henderson, Texas.   Roadracing World  recently did a tire test at Oakhill with Mark Junge as the Tester.   If you want to find out what Junge thought of the track, check out the tire test article in the January 2000 issue of Roadracing World.   During the off season we (the team) were able to rent the track on occasion to make sure we still knew what we were doing.   Come to find out those practice days were priceless...   This last weekend came off without a hitch.

Lets talk about the benefits of pre-registering for the first race of the year.   My advice to all newbie or wanna-be racers is this...Call the organization you want to race with and pre-register for your first race.   If you have not completed riders school, ask one of the officials if you can register before taking riders school.   That way you can go to the track go through riders school and be gridded at or near the front for the start.   It makes a world of a difference!!!   I registered 2 months before this race and I was gridded on the 2nd row for both of my races last weekend, I will tell you how it help shortly.

On to the first practice...   I still had a little rubber left on my tires from one of our last test days at Oakhill.   The Michelin Pilots are a great tire but as every race knows money can oftentimes fight brand loyalty.   I decided to give the Pirelli's another chance.   The new EVO's are 100% better than the Corsa's and a little birdie told me that after Daytona the new semi-slick EVO's are supposed to be available.   There is a picture of the new EVO in the March Issue of Roadracing World.   Can wait to give them a try!!
In an attempt to minimize the wear and tear on the new EVO's I ran the worn out Pilots for the first practice.   This was my first practice with the veteran experts.   One phrase, "Damn they are smooth and fast!!"   My hat is off to you!!   Well you can't let it get to you and I didn't.   I was running at a good pace and maintaining my line when veteran expert (whose name I will with hold for now) decided to be a total assho*@   We were coming out of turn three and heading back up the hill into a little left hand kink...   He pulled along side and forced me to the extreme inside...well in order to make the kink I backed off a little   (this is only practice). He then tries to take the front of my bike with him into the next turn!! For the record...next time he tries it I am NOT letting off the gas and I am gonna swing wide push his ass off the track (we will see how he likes it).   Don't get me wrong folks if this would have been a race I would let it slide after all "that's racing!", but I spoke with a long time veteran Expert and local fast guy about the offending rider and he said "yeah he is just really aggressive." During practice? Oh well, I am finished venting.   After a near highside going into turn 4 I decided to pull off and get the new tires mounted.

Second Practice, New Pirelli EVO's are mounted...Lets see how they feel!?   After scrubbing them in I started to pick it up a bit, The first thing I noticed is that the sidewalls are alot stiffer than the Pilots.   Junge mentioned this and you can really feel the difference.   I could not keep the bike hooked up going through turn 1.   Luckily both tires diameters are almost exactly the same as the Pilots so I did not have to change the ride height on the bike.   Instead, I softened the compression dampening 1/2 turn on the front and softened the rear compression 4 clicks (On the high speed dampening circuit - Penske Shocks Are the Best!)   Well the practice sessions are over so it is time to sit back, wait and enjoy some racing!!

There was alot of great racing and the track record was broken again by Ty Howard...He ran a 1:24.91.   Ben Spies, riding a Team Valvoline Emgo Suzuki GSXR 600 walked away in the Middleweight SuperSport Expert race.   There were many more exciting races and congratulations to all the racers!!

Race Number 8 - Heavyweight SuperStock Expert
I was gridded on the 2nd row (because I pre-registered).   I did not have a chance to test my suspension changes but it was too late at this point.   I got a horrible start, guys were going buy me like I accidentally put it in reverse.   So this is what it is like to race with the experts?!   They certainly know how to start!!   I guess I was in 8th or 9th going into the 2nd turn and the leaders were already checking out!   "What am I doing here?"   "Wait a minute I am racing, get your head together and GO!!!!"   Things were already looking better (they stopped passing me :-)   I held my position and held it and held it and held it.   That is about it no near wrecks and I actually got a little faster after the half way point (probably because my arms were so tired I could no longer fight the bike..I just let go and let the bike work beneath me.)   I turned the best lap time I have every run at Oakhill (1:31.84).   Goal number 5 accomplished, so far... Top 10 finish, even if only 14 guys were in my class :-(

Time to sit around and watch some racing.....Tick tock tick tock.

Finally Race Number 18 of 20 - Heavyweight SuperBike Expert
Again I was starting from the 2 row, but this time I was gonna start out in drive, NOT reverse!!   Green flag drops...time to GO!!   This time I got a good start.   I was in 5th going into turn 2 and I was holding my own.   I was coming out of turn 6 when Ottis Lance, my local fast guy friend came by on the inside. Ottis is very fast, I wanted to follow him around for a couple of turns to see how I was doing.   I was following him through turn 7 and then heading into turn 8 he got pushed off.   I figured he went down considering where he went off, one more position for me alright.   Don't feel bad for Ottis, little did I know he did not crash he was off the track for nearly 15 seconds, but it was not the last I would see of him during this race!!   I was in fourth and Harry Tomlinson was about 3 seconds out in front.   It was the 6th lap when all of a sudden Ottis passes me again on the inside of turn 6...   I get another chance to learn something.   This time I really push to stay behind him.   This time he makes it through turn 8 and we are closing on Harry.   Ottis is pulling me along and leaving me at the same time.   He gets by Harry and I get stuck.   But know I am right behind him with 2 laps to go.   I follow him around the track, we get the white flag...LAST LAP!   I get right behind him through turn 1 and as we approached turn 2 I dove to the inside and got on the brakes as hard as I could.   The bike was dancing around but I got it slowed enough to make the pass and hold my line.   Now the only problem will be to keep him from passing me back, after all he is a veteran fast guy.   I focus on holding the inside line...If he is gonna pass it will have to be on the outside.   We make it around the track with only two turns left.   We go into the (school house) turn 7 really hot and I had to flick the thing into the turn harder than ever....   I guess it was a little too much...we were coming out of the turn and heading to turn 8 (last turn) when I noticed "I can't downshift!!!!" I needed to be in 1st for the last turn, but the bike was in 2nd. we came around the turn and there is about 100 yards to the finish, I have never abused a clutch that bad in my entire life.   I feathered that thing like it was my old KX 125.   "It worked, held on to 4th place!!" Come to find out I drug my shifter through that turn and it broke off!   It was the last lap, almost last turn, of my last race.....Damn I got lucky!!   Visit my sponsors!
