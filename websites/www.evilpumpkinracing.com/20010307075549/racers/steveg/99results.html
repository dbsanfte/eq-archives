A Little History

Hello race fans.   My name is Stephen Guynes and I race with the Central Motorcycle Roadracing Association and the Central Roadracing Association.   I am 29 years old and started riding motorcycles 18 years ago.   My first bike was a 1981 Kawasaki KDX 80 dirt bike.   I have been around bikes my entire life and one could say it is my one true passion, next to my lovely wife Frankie.   In 1990, I bought my first street bike from a friend for $900 bucks.   It was a 1985 Ninja 600R that needed a considerable amount of work to run much less look good.   It had the usual Vance and Hines exhaust, K&N individual filters, Stage III Dyno-Jet Kit, and Ignition advancer already installed, so from a performance standpoint it was ready to go.   A few years and 50,000 miles later I sold it to some guy for $2,000 dollars and put the deposit down on a 1997 GSX-R 750.   At the time I was working with Doug Polen's brother James at a local shop and Doug was starting his own racing team riding the GSX-R's.   James and I would go to the race shop to help whenever we could.   One day I helped diagnose a problem with one of the Super Sport bikes and Doug asked if I wanted to help out on a regular basis.   What do you think I said?   "Hell, Yes!!"   I thought I was pretty handy when it came to turning wrenches, but nothing can compare to what I learned over the next two years. There are too many tricks to list and I can't just tell everyone everything yet.   Keep following the adventures of Team Evil Pumpkin Racing for more information! :-)

My First Race Weekend

Mar 22nd, 1999

Tales From A Newbie Racer

The week before my first ever race I was filled with anxiety and questions.   I had taken riders school a month earlier, with my friend Derrick, and our bikes were not ready to compete that weekend.   So we had to wait and wait and wait for the next race at Texas World Speedway, the fastest track that the Central Motorcycle Roadracing Association (CMRA) utilizes.   We arrived on Saturday hoping to do one of the required two days of corner working, but we were a little behind schedule.   Off to a great start!!   Well, we helped out some friends that were endurance racing and they ended up 1st in class and 3rd overall on a 1998 GSX-R 750.   During the endurance race we had the opportunity to talk to Ottis Lance about the track conditions and asked for any and all the advice he could offer.

I tired to get a good nights sleep, but that was impossible.   I had too many thoughts riding through my mind.   Woke up about 6:30 to a 2 stroke alarm clock, cooked some breakfast and inspected the bikes for the 10,000th time.  Everything seemed to be in order. Tech opened at 7:00, so I rode over and for the second time I was told that this and that needs to be done for the next race.   Don't get me wrong, but these guys need to inspect the bikes using the same book.   At riders school they told us what needed to be done and we did it.   Now they tell us something else must be done.   I know, I know the joys of club racing but come on.   When I built Doug's bikes not once did I have to do anything extra.   Then again the AMA is much more consistent when it comes to tech. For all the guys that are thinking about racing if you think it might need safety wire or silicone do it.

Time for practice (Big Bike Novice practice).   We file out in pairs and I get my first look at the track.   It is a 2.9 mile track with 14 turns.   Practice is a success, I did not crash and I felt pretty comfortable.   There are two practice sessions and my friend Derrick is not happy with his riding.   We talk about it and I agree to tow him around for a couple of laps during the next practice.   We go out and I could not control myself, so I took off.   After practice we join the riders meeting and the officials let us know about the track conditions and tell everyone to be safe.   We look at our grid positions and I am in the last row of the first wave.   JOY!!!

Race Time.   The race was made up of three classes.   Heavy weight's in the first wave, Medium weight's in the second wave and light weight's in the last wave.   We go out for the warm up lap and grid for the 8 lap race.   It takes a while for us to grid because half of us have never done it before and my bike started getting pretty hot (220 F).   The flag drops and I get a good start.   I make it into second place by turn one.   I follow the leader into turn 6 and he goes right through the turn into the grass.   I am in first!!!   I had no idea what to do!   I guess I started to slow by lap 3 because a pack of 600's went by like I was on a YSR.   Damn it! I have to pick it up, my corner speed sucks.   I put my head down and followed the four 600's for another couple of laps and started regaining ground.   On lap 6 I got a great drive coming out of turn 14 onto the banking and passed two of the 600's.   "O.K. I'm in 3rd place just hang on!" I thought.   Going into turn two I see a wheel on my inside, so I get pushed wide coming out of two.   Not the place I wanted to be.   We run side by side for the short chute leading to turn three a tight right hand turn and he has the line.   I go deep and out brake him into 3.   We are catching the 1st and 2nd place guys pretty quick.   Still hanging on to 3rd.   We get the white flag, only one lap to go.   My 600 friend and I caught the leaders and I figured that if I can stay right behind them I can motor past on the final straight.   I miss a shift going into the horseshoe, but I was already on the brakes so I shifted up into 3rd gear and got a bad drive out of the corner.   I caught up going into the esses and got another good drive onto the banking.   I put my head down and nailed it a little earlier than normal and my baby responded.   I made the pass that put me into 2nd and it was over.   I was so pumped I knew I got 1st in class.   Little did I know that the guy who finished first on the 600 had registered as a heavyweight.   He got my trophy, but I was more than happy with second place for my very first race.

Heavy weight Provisional Novice 2nd place 17 points- so far!

Second Race

Apr 27th, 1999

Moving On Up and A Visitor From A Far Away Place

My friend Derrick and I missed the 3 round of the series due to lack of funds.   We were not about to miss another exciting adventure at Texas World.   One last hurdle had to be cleared however to become full fledged Novices.   The dreaded corner working.   There is one catch that may exist for all soon to be racers.   Ask the riders school instructor(s) if you can bring a friend to do corner working with you.   Derrick and I took a friend a piece with us to do corner working and they counted as our second day!!   One 8 hour endurance race with 4 crashes in our corner and we were done with our corner working.   Corner working has its advantages to a new racer.   We happened to be in the last turn before going onto the Nascar banking.   During the race we took turns climbing the high banked turns that were not being utilized by the course to gain a new perspective on the lines that the fastest riders were running.   You could see the entire track from the top of the banking, but I focused on the horseshoe and the esses that lead onto the banking. It paid off big time.

After another not so good nights rest, the first morning practice went as expected.   Except that we were running the 1.8 mile or the short course.   The unfortunate accident that RYAN SMITH #19 had at the previous round at Texas World prompted officials to run the short course for the remainder of the year or until turn 8 was fixed.   After the first practice we were sitting in our garage space when a gentleman approached.   He was wearing a baseball cap and medium dark glasses.   The camera around his neck reminded us of a tourist who stumbled into the pits.   As he got closer he spoke, "Are you Steve?"   "Are you an under cover police officer?" I responded.   "No!" the man said.   "I'm Steve."   He called himself Rich so I asked if he was here to sponsor me!   With a light chuckle he said "Hell, No!!"   Hey everyone meet Rich Nelson he is an Evil Pumpkin.   My friends gave me a pretty weird look at that one!   I was glad to see that Rich could make it to the race. We talked for a while and he took some great pictures that day.   The second practice was called and I again agreed to follow Derrick around the track for two laps to see how he was riding.   I followed him for two laps and then I was supposed to let him follow me for for two laps so he could learn my lines.   I got tired of going slow and getting passed so I again took off.   Sorry! Derrick.   We got back to the pits only to find Rich going into withdrawals due to a lack of racing.   I should have given him my leathers and bike for one of the practice sessions.   Next time, Rich.

My first race of the day (Heavy weight provisional novice) was right after the riders meeting.   The points I acquired from the previous round put me in Pole Position for the 6 lap race. The flag dropped and so did my clutch.   I got a great start and first going into turn one.   This time I was going to win or wad (Haga Style).   The race was going pretty well until I decided to see who was behind me.   Note to self and new racers, never pull your head up and look over your shoulder on the main straight at 160 mph.   It will almost take your head/helmet off.   I did get a quick glance before my helmet covered my eyes.   There was only one guy behind me Mr. Robert Dietz, so I told myself I had to push it a little harder.   On the fifth lap I started lapping the back markers and my friend Derrick.   One of the other provisional novices slammed the door going into the esses and I had already committed to my line. I jumped the damn curb and passed the guy in the air only to have another guy right in front of me.   I hit the brakes hard and it started to slide to the wrong edge of the track.   I slowed down enough to recover and still got a good drive onto the banking.   I was able to hold on to first place and turn the fastest lap of the race, even with my airborne adventure in turn 10.   Great race!!

For my second and final race of the day, I decided to see how fast the veteran novices were.   It was the 8 lap Heavy weight novice race.   Between practice and the races I kept muttering to myself to relax.   Doug told me if you are not relaxed your are not going to go fast.   Before the next race Rich took a piece of red duct tape and wrote "RELAX Dummy" on it.   It now resides on my speedo as a reminder and I believe it works.   Since I had no points for the class I started 26th of 28 riders in the 2nd wave (last row).   The first wave was started and they were already into turn one before we started.   VERY DEPRESSING!!   I got a good start and almost made it to the front of our 6 row deep wave.   I took the outside line around turn one and passed a few more guys.   I knew there was no way I could catch the leaders.   By lap 2 the back markers from the first wave were in sight.   I focused on one pass at a time.   I had no idea were I stood during the race.   Nothing spectacular took place during the race.   I just ran the best I could.   Ended up finishing 9th so I can't complain.   Another weekend down without a mechanical problem or a crash.   In my book that makes for a fun and successful weekend.  

Heavy weight Provisional Novice 1st place 20 points- Overall Points leader with 37 points.
Heavy weight Novice 9th place 7 points- Currently 25th Overall for the season.

Third Race

Jun 21st, 1999

Burning Bikes and Hitting Parked Trucks

A few weeks have past since my last race weekend at Texas World.   I missed the last round at Hallett, Oklahoma because my dad was getting married.   This race was run at Oakhill Raceway in Henderson, Texas.   Oakhill is a very technical track.   It is narrow, tight and hard on tires.   I'd been there three times, but never raced.   My first Oakhill experience was terrible.   It was raining like hell and I was running a slick on the rear of my bike for riders school.   Don't ask!!   The other two times were practice days and they were fun.   My fastest lap during the two practice days was a 1:38.66 .   This was 8 seconds off the fastest novice times.   My Pirelli tires were not making the grade.   I bought a used set of Pilots for this race.   I have heard many things about Michelin's from Doug and Ottis and I am happy to report that they are truly great tires.   During my first practice session 1.5 seconds fell off my previous best time.   By the fourth lap of my second practice I dropped almost four seconds running a 1:34.79 .   The Pilots were much more slide predictable than I thought they would be.   A boost in tire confidence always helps.   On to the race!

I only had enough money for one race.   I decided to try the Heavyweight GP novice race.   As usual my grid position sucked because I had no points for the class.   I was all alone in the sixth row.   Starting in 14th place at Oakhill is not much fun.   The track is so narrow and tight it is difficult to pass more than one person at a time on the start or the rest of the track.   The green flag dropped and I started to get a great launch between the fellows in front of me, when they decided to exchange phone numbers and some paint down the front straight.   I had to hit the brakes and swing to the outside.   Oh the joy of running in the rubber marbles going through turn one A and one B.   I made it past a few guys on the outside and got a good drive up the hill into turn 2.   I was in 10th place coming out of turn two and collected two more positions late braking on the inside of turn three.   The leaders were running strong and the odds of me dropping another 4 seconds per lap were very low.   All I wanted was a top ten finish.   Things settled for the rest of the first lap and I had to wait and be consistent.   I settled into a groove and made it into 7th place by the third lap.   Paul Padron was in 2nd place and crashed coming out of turn 4.   The easiest position change all day.   I could see a pack of four riders in front of me and I tried to catch them but I came into turn 7 too hot and had to stand it up hard on the brakes.   I went two bike lengths off the track, turned around and took off.   STUPID MISTAKE!!!   I was catching those guys until my off track excursion.   Luckily I had put enough distance between myself and 7th place that no one passed.   I put my head back down and held on for the remaining two laps.   The leader was gone and the other five were out of reach.   I finished 6th with no broken bones or bike, Good race!

My race was the 5th race of 20 to be run, so I was able to watch a lot of action.   The worst crash of the day happened towards the end of the day.   The rider whose name I forget (sorry) was going into turn one A when he lost the rear end and high sided at about 100 mph.   He was thrown over the safety fence and hit the side of a truck in the pits.   Luckily he stood up under his own power, but I bet he will be pretty damn sore.   I hope he feels better soon.   It was a bad crash!   After that one of the racers we were pitted with (another Steve) had the distinct pleasure of riding the fire ball of death.   While coming out of turn 6 his motor decided two part itself out.   A rod here some nuts and bolts there and a bunch of oil.   Well, the exhaust pipe did not like the hot oil treatment it was getting so it decided to catch fire.   Apparently, Steve did not like the hot Texas weather so he decided to call it quits.   He rode right up to the tire wall and dismounted rather quickly.   After the corner workers and some other people in the pits put out the bike and the grass fires, it was business as usual.   There is nothing more exciting than roadracing!!

Heavy weight GP Novice 6th place 10 points.

Brainerd International Raceway

June 29th, 1999

An Eye Opening Experience

To start, I would like to thank Rich, Todd and the rest of the Team for a great weekend!   It all started Thursday night with the flight up to Minnesota where I met Todd at the airport.   We drove straight to his house to put the head back on his R1.   We finished at 3:30 a.m. went to sleep and woke up at 8:00 a.m. for the two and a half hour drive to Brainerd.   We pulled into the pits and I met the Team.   There was still a lot of work to be done to get ready for practice.   Rich already had the forks pulled off his GSXR 600 ready for a fork seal change and Race Tech Gold Valve installation.   I started working on the forks and Todd went out for practice on the R1.   A few minutes past and Todd came back in with a problem.   The bike would not pull past 7000 RPM.   I finished Rich's forks a few hours later and we tried to find out what was wrong with the R1.   The Yamaha manual is terrible when it comes to cam timing.   It totally contradicts itself about where to line up the cams.   At the end of the day we discovered that both cams were one tooth off.   Luckily no damage was done to the valves.   R1 is fixed and the GSXR 600 is ready to race.

Friday Practice.   If you have never raced at Brainerd, it is something that must be experienced.   Turn 1 is a wide open 6th gear turn on the 600's.   We are talking about 165 MPH!   I went out with the slow practice session and had my first taste of the track.   It was great!   I was running in the 1:59 to 2:00 minute range.   The guys said that it was not bad for a first timer.   I talked with Rich, Todd and the others about the track and it helped a lot.   Practice was finished at 6:00 p.m. and I felt pretty good.   After dinner we all hung out and I had the opportunity to get to know everyone a little better.   This is a great group of guys, I mean racers.  

First Race.   I entered the 15 lap trophy dash for medium weight superbike novice.   As usual my grid position had much to be desired.   It was my first race with the CRA and I had no points in the class for a better grid position.   I started on the outside of the 6th row.   The track is so wide that they line up five bikes on each row!   I was in the 26th position.   The flag waved and off we went.   I got a pretty good start and made it to the 4th row.   Being in a pack of 20 bikes going 6 wide into a 150 MPH turn is the craziest thing I have ever done.   You end up telling yourself  "WHAT THE HELL AM I DOING HERE!!"   The pack made it through turn one and headed into turn two at a relatively slow pace about 140 MPH!!   I was able to pass a few more going on the outside of turn two and a few more going deep on the inside of turn 3.   "The beauty of tire warmers!"   Things started to spread out on the first lap and I was able to make a few more passes here and there, but this was a 15 lap race and I was not riding my bike.   I did not want to throw Rich's bike away with some stupid move.   I fell into a rhythm and focused a one pass at a time.   At the half way mark I noticed that the fuel light started to blink.   "WHAT THE HELL!", We had almost filled the tank for the race.   With only three laps to go the light went solid, NOT GOOD!   I decided to go another two laps and I had made into 7th place.   Leading up to the last turn before the start finish line the bike started to miss and sputter.   Rich was in the next race on the same bike so I had to pull in on the white flag lap.   "Damn it!!!!!" Only one lap to go!   I might have been able to nurse it through the last lap, but I did not want to risk it.   No sense in ruining Rich's race out of selfishness.   I pitted and we refueled for the next race.   To my surprise I was awarded 10th place because I had lapped some of the riders.   Not a total loss.   My times dropped into the 1:57's for the 3 mile course so I felt pretty good.   My day was over so we watched Rich, Todd and the others race.  

Later that night Rich and I were up talking in the garage and a group of guys and a couple of girls came into our garage.   They sat down in our chairs and started asking questions about what it takes to get started in racing.   We talked for a while and they left.   I had an uneasy feeling about the group so before I went to bed I covered the helmets and some of the equipment with towels to try and hide it.   We woke up the next morning and two sets of leathers walked away in the night!   Luckily Rich knew one of the medics that came in with the guys that joined us the night before.   In passing he asked her to look out for the leathers.   10 minutes later she came back with both sets of leathers and the guys who stole them.   They apologized and said it was "A PRANK!".   We thought otherwise.   Security was called and the guys were taken to the offices.   The police told the guys that if they could not come to an agreement the officer would take them in front of all the racers and let us handle it!   The thieves quickly admitted that they hand stolen the leathers and they will be charged with a misdemeanor.   Justice Served and leathers safe!   Back to the racing.

Sunday Race.   The medium weight superbike novice race had 41 racers.   I got a decent start and totally screwed up turn one.   I turned in too late and had to get off the gas so I would not go off the track at 140 mph.   Luckily only 1,000 people flew by me.   Piece of cake right?  Maybe the race gods were smiling at me because someone high sided going into turn 3 and took out a few other riders.   I hate to move up due to someone else's misfortune, I would rather beat them on the track.   But then again, that's racing.   The night before we walked the track with Rich and Todd and they really helped show me the lines and some places to look when racing.  It paid off.   I put my head down and started regaining my ground.   On the third lap an FZR 600 dove in underneath me in turn 3.   I sat back and followed him around until turn 10.   He went to the inside and took the outside line.   I got on the gas before he could and passed him on the outside.   The GSXR had the motor on the mile long straight, but he was really quick in the back section.   He stuffed me in turn 9 on the 5th lap and I passed him going down the straight.   This was turning out to be a good little battle.   Each lap he stuffed me in turn 9 and I passed him on the straight.   On the last lap I ran into turn nine on the inside to block the line.   He did not have the motor to get by between turn 9 and 10 so, I was able to hold my position through turn 10 and onto the straight.   I was able to run 1:57's with out working as hard as the day before.   I am still not as fast as some of the veteran Brainerd novice racers but I held on to 16th with out running out of gas.   The weekend was over and I did not crash Rich's bike.   Decent Lap times, Did not crash, and new friends.   I'd say it was a very successful racing weekend!

Texas World Speedway

July 18th, 1999

Blazing New Trails

For the first time all year we made it to the races a day early and the bikes were ready to race, but was I?   I hadn't ridden a bike since Brainerd and my 750 does not handle like Rich's 600. "No problem!" I thought. "I have plenty of practice time tomorrow to re-familiarize myself with the beast."

We need to discuss a racers state of mind and the differences between tracks.   Do not stay up TOO late talking with friends the night before a race!!   4 hours of sleep will not help the concentration factor, especially if you had a few drinks while talking to your friends.   When it comes to the difference between tracks, Brainerd's turn 1 is not the same as Texas World's turn 1.   They are both very fast, but very different.

First Practice + worn out tires + bad concentration + different bike = what?.   Answer: A 140 mph off track excursion!   "This is not how I wanted to start a race weekend!"   After a warm up lap I was heading into turn 1 thinking about Brainerd's turn 1 and for some ungodly reason decided to keep the throttle pinned coming off the banked straight onto the flat turn 1.   Mr. Physics decided to step in and give me a lecture on G-forces, but I was asleep in class.   Did I mention that turn 1 is a flat decreasing radius turn that flows right into turn 2!   Well, I did not make the turn.   I stood it up a decided to try and ride it out.   A wise man once said, "Street Bikes do not make good Dirt Bikes!"   Believe it or not I rode it out.   I had friends corner working in turn one and they did not see it, because it was between turn 1 and turn 2, turn 2's responsibility.   They did hear it on the radio.   They heard turn two call for a crash truck and ambulance.   The next thing they heard was "Control this is turn 2. Cancel the ambulance and crash truck, somehow he saved it."   I made it to the Nascar banking and pulled over to look for damage and pull the freshly mowed grass out of my @$$.   I was shaken but not stirred.   I got back on and headed for the pits for further inspection.   Lucky Save!

Second Practice, same tires, different state of mind.   One could say that I went a little slower for the first half of this practice.   I only went off the track one time coming out of turn 6.   Ran wide and did some flat tracking getting back on track.   Piece of cake!   I did have the chance to follow one of the fast novices for the last 3 laps of practice.   #85 Mitch Ballard, I studied his lines and found his slow and fast areas.   I started to get my rhythm back and then practice was over.

First Race: Heavy Weight GP Novice.   We were running the long track this weekend (2.9 miles).   I had only run the long track once before on  My First Race Weekend .   My previous best time was 2:12.8, so my goal was to try and knock at least 10 seconds off my previous best time.   I was gridded on the 5th row outside.   The green flag went down and my front end went up.   I dumped the friggin' clutch.   When the front end landed I must have hit the shifter because it went into neutral.   I had to put it back in gear and try again.   Needless to say I got a bad start and had my work cut out for me!   I was the last bike from the first wave going into turn one.   I pulled my head out and started going for it.   By the second lap I had made it back to 13th from 19th position and then I fell into a pretty pretty large gap between groups.   At the half way mark I got passed by Mr. Stevens #129.   He is an AMA pro flat tracker turned roadracer on an R6.   I had to match his pace.   I got behind him only to witness his insane cornering techniques.   The guy was sliding that R6 everywhere.   He was very fast in the back section, but I would keep him in reach, catch him on the straight and pass him going through TURN 1 on the outside!!   The first time I did this, lap 4, I though I was going to repeat my first practice excursion.   I peeled it over one last time to try and save it and it stuck!   I could not believe how far my GSXR would lean and stick, especially with the trashed Pilots I was running.   These are the best tires I have ever had the privilege to use.   I made the pass only to be passed right back going into turn 3.   Mr. Stevens and I went around again and it was the same story.   I passed him going into turn 1 and he got me back going into turn 3.   This was turning out to be a really fun race.   We started to catch the group in front of us and we made it past a few more guys for the remaining 2 laps.   On the last lap I had to keep him behind me.   I made the pass in turn 1 and went to the inside of turn 3, where he had been passing me.   I made turn three stick but he got me going into turn 4.   This guy could slide a 57 Chevy.   I pushed harder to try and keep him close enough to pass on the final straight, because I had the motor.   We made it to turn 12 and I screwed up the Esses, we both got a good drive onto the straight, but it was not enough.   I finished with my front wheel beside his rear wheel.   This was the best race I had all year.   My lap times dropped to 1:56.8!   Much better than my 10 second goal.   I ended up in 9th, so my top ten goal was also achieved.   Only one more race for the day.

Second Race: Heavy Weight Super Stock Novice, in the rain.   We had been lucky for most of the weekend with only scattered thunderstorms, but the bottom finally fell out of the sky.   The track was soaked with standing water in turn 1 and 2 and my tires had seen better days.   The wear bars were no longer visible nor were the grooves on the sides of the tires.   I could not afford a new set of DOT's let alone a set of rains so I decided to run with what little I had.   This was my first race in the rain and you only hear horror stories about the sealer on the track when its wet.   I was gridded on the 6th row inside and got a decent start.   The leaders were on rains and they took off never to be seen again.   I just went around at what felt like a snails pace and it showed.   I got passed left and right, and only passed a couple of guys during the whole 5 lap race.   I could have run slicks and had the same traction.   Every little puddle and patch of tar made the bike squirm and wiggle.   I could not turn or brake, but the tires would hook up on the straights.   I saw two low sides in the first two laps and decided to just finish the race.   I cannot afford to repair the bike and I am not in the points battle for this class.   I did finish and got 11th place, probably due to attrition.   Next race I will be on a new set of Pilots, so if it rains I will see what they can do.   At least I got some practice in the wet.   I will do better next time!

Heavy Weight GP Novice 9th place 7 points- 17 points for the season in two races.
Heavy Weight Super Stock Novice 11th place 5 points- 12 points for the season in two races.

Hallett Motor Racing Circuit

August 22nd, 1999

Passing Practice 101

It was a week before the race and Rich decided it was payback time for the thrashing I gave his GSXR 600 at Brainerd .   Rich, Ned and Mark were meeting us in Hallett for the upcoming weekend.   It will be a small Evil Pumpkin Reunion.   The bikes were ready and we arrived shortly after the Saturday endurance races.   After unloading the bikes and the gear I decided to walk the track.   I missed the previous round at Hallett, so I needed to see what the track looked like before the sunset.   Derrick decided to go along and I have to say that the pictures of the track on the Hallett Website do not do this track justice.   The elevation changes and blind turns are something that must be experienced.   The surface has seen better days and the layout is very demanding but overall it looks to be a fun track.   Rich walked out and met us while we were looking at "The Bitch".   The name of this turn suits it very well.   We finish walking the track and making our mental notes, so back to the pits we went.   Most of our group had been here before and for two of the riders it was there home track.   We did some bench racing and talked about the track while we ate and had a few drinks.   We needed an early start in the morning so we called it a night about 11:30.   Luckily Rich brought the new rear Michelin Pilot I had purchased from Tom Mason at MRT services and the front wheel from his GSXR 600 with the Performance Machine Cast Rotors mounted to it.   Hallett is a braking track so we needed any advantage we could find.

First Practice: I needed to scuff in the rear tire and hook up behind a fast guy to check the lines I had made note of when walking the track.   Derrick Thomas is one of the fastest novices out there and he was in our pit for the day.   We both had new rear rubber so he had to scuff his tire also.   I followed him around for a while and got a feel for the track.   My lines held up pretty good with only a few minor changes, GOOD!!   The bike felt great and I was running 1:31's to 1:32's.   Now it was Rich's turn.   He went out and ran 1:34s to 1:35's on my bike.   I was surprised, but glad he adapted so quick.   Looks like it is going to be a good day!

Second Practice: Time to up the ante.   I started moving my brake markers in and working on the parts of the track where I was have trouble.   I fell into a good rhythm and started running 1:30's.   Not even a close call so far, something must give.   Rich goes out for his last practice and started pushing harder, I guess he is getting comfortable on the bike.   He turns some 1:33's and I started getting worried.   Good Job Rich!

I had to wait for Rich to run his two races before I had my chance.   It was pretty cool to watch my bike circulating the track and I just hoped it would continue to do so.   Rich ran two great races and placed 12th in both.   Running 1:29's is pretty darn good considering he was riding and different bike on a new track.

First Race: Its my turn!!   Heavy Weight GP.   The race schedule changed and they grouped the experts with the novices.   It did not bother me too much because I knew they would not get in the way.   Boy! was I wrong.   I got a great start from the second wave and made it to 8th place (novice) going into turn one.   Everyone got in line for "The Bus Stop" and luckily none went down to cause a pile up!   I made one pass on the outside going into the next turn and it stuck.   The top 4 guys were getting away and I got stuck behind a slow expert and a novice.   The race was only 7 laps so I did not have time to sit back and wait.   I watched the two battle it out for two laps and found a weak spot for both of them.   BRAKING!   The only way to pass them was out brake them on the inside of the last turn before the straight.  I set up on the inside of "The Bitch" (a tight right hander) and swung wide on the exit heading toward the left handed horseshoe ahead.   I kept it pinned past my brake marker and as soon as I past both of them I nailed the brakes.   The rear end rose up higher than normal and the leader tried showing me his front wheel entering the horseshoe.   I was still too hot and pushed him wide but the pass was a success.   One lap later they were no longer in sight.   5th place and closing on fourth!   Number 85 Mitch Ballard is a top four novice and I knew he would be hard if not impossible to pass.   Luckily he was soft on the brakes going into "The Bus Stop", I dove in underneath him on the brakes and made a clean pass.   4th place and I can see another expert back marker and 3rd place novice.   I had two laps left to close the distance.   I started pushing a little too hard and nearly lost the front a couple of times.   I caught the expert back marker but it was too late.   The race was over, if only I had one more lap I might have battled with 3rd.   I got the points I need for a better grid position next time, so we shall see!

Second Race: Here we go again!   Heavy Weight Super Stock.   The experts and novices were grouped together again!   JOY!!   We lined up and away we went, I mean away I went!   I got pushed off in turn 1.   Dammit!!! I was not about to let this happen.   For some stupid reason I kept on the gas in the grass and showed everyone my flat tracking style.   Rooster tail and all!   I made it back onto the track, threw my gloves off and started fighting my way back through traffic.   Dead Last and 23 novices to pass in 7 laps.   This was going to be interesting.   I figured the best way to do large amounts of passing is to group packs of riders and consider it one pass.   It was a thought!   One by one I started regaining my ground.   I pulled some pretty hair brained moves but I was on a mission!   The best place to pass was coming out of "The Bus Stop".   Everyone went wide on the exit and had to make a turn in the kink before the next real turn.   I stayed tight and when they went wide I nailed it straight through the kink and passed them on the outside going into turn 5.   I do not remember how many people I passed there and I did not care.   Where ever I could pass I would, clean or not so clean.   With only seven laps for the entire race there is only so much you can do.   I ended up making it back to 9th.   The good part is that my times really dropped during that race and I felt comfortable running those times (low 1:26's).   A really fast expert time this weekend was in the 1:21's.   Someone said they got one 1:23 on me but I am not sure about that, YET!!

Heavy Weight GP Novice 4th place 13 points- 30 points for the season in three races, 11th overall.
Heavy Weight Super Stock Novice 9th place 7 points- 19 points for the season in three races, 20th overall.

Texas World Speedway

September 19th, 1999

Are There No Limits?

The weekend started early Saturday morning without any of the usual delays.   Derrick and I loaded the bikes and the gear, I kissed the wife and away we went.   The drive to Texas World is about 3 1/2 hours, but it took about 4 hours this time due to road construction.   Luckily no one took the pit that Josh Center and Scott Forsythe saved for us, Thanks Guys!!   We unloaded and started making our way around the pits to check out the competition and visit with some friends.  

Sunday Morning- lawn chairs do not make good beds.   Its 6:30 a.m., my back hurts, and those damn 2 stroke pit bikes need silencers.   I decided the night before that I cannot afford the last round of the season in Topeka.   I decided to run 3 races instead of the usual 2 races.   Heavy Weight SuperStock, Heavy Weight GP and a new class Unlimited Superbike!  

The first big bike novice practice was called and I went out knowing that there were two places on the track that I needed to improve on, turn 3 and going into the horseshoe (turn 8).   Texas World is not a heavy braking track but you still need to be aggressive on the brakes in these spots.   I also made a promise to myself that I would not repeat the off track excursion in turn one that I made the previous round at Texas World!   Everything felt great during the practice, except that it was only two laps long.   I moved my brake markers for turn 3 and 8 as far in as I could, in the given time, and I matched my previous best time in only two laps.   I felt a little more comfortable turning that time vs. the previous round.   Practice is over and it is time to go back to the pits, check the bike and think about the track since we were not out there very long (Damn Red Flags)!!

During the second practice I had to start pushing a little harder to make up for the abbreviated 1st practice.   For the first time I got a huge two wheel side going into turn 2!   I finally found the stinkin' traction limits, but it felt good!   I dropped a second off my times and it soon felt comfortable.   At least things are moving in the right direction.   Time to sit back, reflect and wait for my first race.   My beautiful wife Frankie decided to meet us at the track and bring food for everyone!   Fajita's anyone?!   It does not get much better than this!!!!!

It was time for a new front tire.   Thirteen heat cycles were on the Pilot and that is just bad Karma!   $175.00 dollars and 20 minutes later a new Michelin Pilot (Soft) front tire was making its way onto my bike.   No more practices to scrub it in on so I have to do it on the warm up lap! JOY!!!   Luckily, my first race was the Unlimited Superbike race.   I did not have any points in the class and I was gridded on the last row of the second wave.   We took off for the hot lap and I had heard all the horror stories about new cold Pilots being greasy, so I took it easy!! For the record, whomever made the comments about the tires being greasy and sorry when new, must be sponsored by Dunlop!   The Michelin Pilots are the Best Racing Tires to Date!!

We lined up....and away the first wave experts went! We were soon to follow.   I got a great start and hooked up going down the middle behind Jeff Lee #327 Novice.   We all fumbled around jockeying for position when Jeff and I got pushed to the outside going through turn one and two.   While they were slowing each other down on the inside we figured we might as well rail around the outside.   We got a good drive going into turn 3 and slammed the door on the pack.  I was glued to Lee going through turn 5, I set up way outside going into 6 and dove down through the sealer.   I drove past Lee on the back straight to move into 8th place (novice).   I closed up on Wayne Cunnings going into the esses and got a real good drive onto the front straight.   I passed Cunnings and set my sights on Zach Didier.   I closed on Zach going into turn one and got right behind him in turn 2.   I followed him through the next series of turns and again set up wide going into 6.   I went low trough the apex and passed Zach going down the back straight.   This is a great place to pass, on the the 3rd lap.   The next plate I saw was a white plate with the number 284 on it!   Hey, that is Steve Finley, a friend on his new GSXR 750! Friend or not it was business time!   Finley has only had the bike for a couple of weeks, but he has been racing for many a year!!   Well I fell in behind him and watched for a lap.   I don't get to do this often.   He made a mistake coming out of turn 6 and ran wide so I had to take advantage of it.   I made the pass and knew he was right behind me, JUST RELAX!!!   Only two laps left!! I could see a bike in front of me but he was pretty far up there.   John Mundy #416 is a regular top five finisher, so either he is running slow or I am going pretty good!   I was closing and closing fast but on the last lap he still had me by 100 yards or so!   I kept closing, but I did not have the time.   I crossed the finish line about 4 bike lengths behind him!!   Maybe next race!!

Heavy Weight GP - We were running with the Experts again.   I got a great start from the second wave and this time I hooked up on the inside behind Mitch Ballard #85 Novice.   It looked like everyone was going to make it around us through turn one and two, but since we were already on the inside soon to be the outside going into turn 3, we were in the right place.   It worked, everyone packed up trying to get in line for turn 3 and no one went in on the inside to slow us down.   Towards the end of the first lap, I followed Ballard and Padron going into the esses and got a real good drive onto the front straight.   I passed both of them and never looked back.   #224 Tracy Smith and Mitch were in tow and we circulated for a few laps.   We were closing on Ben Fondry.   When Jim Bishop and Scott Stevens got tangled up exiting turn 7 and took each other out.   I made it past Ben and had two laps to go, I was in 4th place.   On the last lap John Mundy missed turn 3 and went off the track.   He did not crash, but I graciously accepted 3rd place with Ben on my tail.   The two of us could see 2nd place runner Derek Thomas #445, but we would not catch him.   I finished 3rd and turned my fastest times (1:54's)!   One race to go and I was getting very tired!!

Heavy Weight SuperStock - This was my last race of the day and maybe for the season!   I had enough points to be gridded on the 5th row inside.   The hot lap went as usual and we lined up.   Off we went, I had my best start all day and made it into 8th place.   We went around the first lap and settled into our running order.   By the 2nd lap I was in 6th place and gunning for 5th.   On the 3rd lap, I was going into turn 1 behind John Mundy, he was low and I was taking the high line coming off the banking.   At the entrance of the turn he ran wide as I was passing him and we almost touched, I had to straighten it up for a second to avoid him, but then I peeled it back over and I ran around him on the outside with both wheels sliding again going into turn 2.   It was the greatest pass I had all year!!   I could have touched my elbow to the ground going through turn 2!   I felt really strong and Padron was right in front of us.   I braked hard and late for turn 3 and caught Padron.   I passed him on the outside going into turn 4 and stayed on the gas.   I was in 4th and 3rd was in reach!   Tracey Smith #224 and his R6 were pretty quick in the back section.   I managed to stick to him and going onto the front straight I got a great drive and made the pass.   3rd place was mine!!   There were only 2 laps left on Tracey was not happy.   I could here him right behind me for the next series of turns and I had to remind myself to relax and breath.   I pulled away and on the last lap I found Jeffery Brice #489.   I was closing on him going into the horseshoe and I got closer entering the esses, but it was not meant to happen this race!   Next year I will start acquiring points at the beginning of the year and get better grid positions!   I will take a podium finish anytime!!

The wife approved Topeka!!!! See you there. We are running the Saturday Endurance Race!!

Heavy Weight GP Novice 3rd place 15 points- 45 points for the season in four races, 9th overall.
Heavy Weight SuperStock Novice 3rd place 15 points- 34 points for the season in four races, 12th overall.
Unlimited Superbike Novice 5th place 11 points- 11 points for the season in one race, 17th overall.

Another weekend in the books, best finishes all year and Still no crashes! Knock On Wood!!!!!!

Heartland Park

October 23rd, 1999

Boy Was That Stupid.....

To make a long story short, I crashed BEFORE the race even started.

At least I got that elusive first crash out of the way.   Other than a hairline fractured ankle and broken hand the weekend went pretty well.   We made it to the track Friday night had dinner with The Northern Pumpkin Patch and Bill Hirstius, my best friend in Denver, then we all enjoyed a few after dinner beverages to finish off the night.   It was in the low 30's Saturday morning and the first practice came off with out any problems.   My bike was running rough around 8,000 to 9,000 rpm and we finally decided it was due to bad gas at the end of the day.   I tried lowering the needles in the carbs to lean out the fuel curve transition onto the main jets, but it did not work.   The bike started running bad at 5,000 and the 8 to 9,000 range.   JOY!!!   I jumped on the bike and brought it around the pit wall to warm it up on cold pit road.   I went up around the corner towards turn one then turned around to go back to our pit.   On the way back I nailed it in first gear to see if the motor was going to clear up and the next thing I remember I was on the ground sliding.   The tires were too cold and I was told that I did a 30 mph donut and then lowsided.   For the record I will never ride a bike again without my gloves.   Apparently I threw my left foot down to catch the sliding bike and my ankle just folded, then as the bike was lowsiding I was thrown to the other side and when I landed my hand whacked the pavement.   I broke the little bone between my middle finger knuckle and my wrist.   I could not even flip myself off for being so stupid!   Priority one was to hit the kill switch on my bike, because it was still running.   I limped over, hit the switch and then just sat down.   This has got to be the dumbest thing I have ever done (in hindsight).   Oh well, thanks to Todd and his slowly being parted out GSXR-750 we got a replacement shifter side footpeg assembly and bar end.   The left handle bar was bent down a little but there was not enough time to replace it.   I called in our back up rider Josh Center and told him to get suited up, he had to take my place.

Poor Derek had to start the race without a hot lap, cold tires and haven never raced my bike.   I was relegated to the pit crew for the remainder of the day.   It is very hard to watch your bike go round and round the track.   We ended up 9th in Unlimited SuperStock.   Good job Derek, Derrick and Josh.   They brought it back in with no extra damage.   Bill, thanks for being part of the crew.   The season is over, and at least I have time to heal and rebuild the bike for next year.   On a positive note it was the last race of the year, but it certainly was not the way I wanted the year to end.   But as Arnold would say "I'll Be Back!"

Until next year........
