New Bike on the Block

The new SV-650S was all broke in and I have to say, "Wow! What a fun streetbike!". I'm kinda sad to be converting it to race trim. That will leave me without a streetbike, but the old GSXR is probably looking forward to some attention. I know Brad Frey has been giving it some loving, but thinking I might need to keep the GSXR away from him now though. Funny thing is, that after riding the SV, I'm really not looking forward to riding the GSX-R600 on the street now. The SV is just so much lighter handling and easier to ride on the street.

Charge!

That was the theme and I'm sure that Visa and Mr. Greenspan is happy with my selfless patriotic attempts to single handedly help the economy! Lots of parts to get for the SV. Only sticky part is that the '03 SV changed just enough to make race parts for it hard to get. Frame sliders that protect the radiator for one and SharkSkinz body work with the mandatory belly pan for two. There is an entire swarm of other miscellaneous go fast goodies that are in short supply for the 03 SV's too. Makes for some frustratingly long wait times for parts.

With 2 weeks to go until the Mid-America Motoplex round, there is the "Wear in the hell can I work on the bike?!?" problem with living in an apartment complex. Sure, I have a garage stall, but no work bench, air compressor or other handy garage type accessories. I sure am glad Commander Jenspeed has his training weekends and a well stocked barn full of spiffy tools though! I took the pristine SV down to his place during a training weekend and spent most of the time installing the go-faster goodies that had arrived. I installed the Rear Swingarm Spool/Slider things, the Woodcraft Rearsets ( nice rearsets btw ), the M4 Exhaust, the Power Commander III box and the new 520 chain/gearing kit. The exhaust...wow...now that sounds like a racebike!

The Racetech Springs, Emulators and the Fox Rear Shock was still on order. Racetech decided to give the 03 SV new part numbers for their stuff, but from what I understand, the parts are the same as the '02 SV stuff. However; the "new" part numbers seemed to make Racetech take longer to send out for some reason. I'm mystified. The suspension goodies finally arrive during the week of the MAM round. Hmm, looks like I might be busy installing that stuff at the track. Not an idea I'm too fond of either.

Thursday, 08/21/03

I load up the GSXR and the SV Wednesday night so I get an early start on Thursday. MAM has a first come first serve rule for the stalls. I was slightly worried about the Tularius and Dark Dog teams having a track day and opening it to all who wanted to come too. However; the worry about a packed pit was misplaced since there was still some open stalls on Sunday. I did get my pick of some good spots though, right in front and in the middle.

I unloaded the bikes and then decided I could burn some time by taking the wheels off the SV and getting some race rubber mounting while the lines for Mason’s Racing Tires were non-existent. Fresh Michelin Pilots mounted and for about $50 cheaper then the tires for the GSXR cost! Win! However; Geezus, what a pain in the butt those SV wheels are to change out. Getting them off wasn’t too hard, but re-installing the rear was a PITA. For starters, the rear brake caliper isn’t attached to the swingarm. It’s held in place by the rear axle and a slot in the swingarm that prevents it from twisting. Then there is a spacer on the caliper side and on the chain side that like to fall out with the slightest bump. So, took 20-30 minutes of swearing to get the wheel, 2 spacers, caliper, axle bolt and rear swingarm aligned enough just to get the rear wheel back on. Unless I find some trick, I’m thinking that the rear is a two person job from now on. I sure do hope I never need to change wheels during an endurance race! The front was much better, but the speedo sensor did provide some fun alignment issues too.

I solve the belly pan problem with a simple $2 aluminum turkey pan from the grocery store. Hard part is mounting it though. Not a lot of space between the pipe and the engine block to slip the pan in between. Some pliers, leverage and elbow grease goes a long way though and I eventually get the pan tucked in and safety wired in around the lower half of the motor enough to constitute a "belly pan". Not the best looking in the world though, but if it passes tech, then good enough!

Friday, 08/22/03

Paul has some more goodies for me! A new Pitbull rear stand, the Fox Rear Shock, Racetech Springs and the Racetech Emulator! Hmmm….the rear stand is easy, but do I really wanna try messing with the suspension stuff?!? The SV is waiting and I just wanna ride! I decide to mess with the suspension stuff later in the day. My buddy, Keith is riding up from Kansas City so I can just work on that stuff while waiting for him at the end of the day. At least, that is what my plan was.

I duct tape the Helmetcamera.com thing to the side of the fairing, route the cables to the rear seat, plug them in and then cushioned in foam, I place Ned’s super expensive digital handycam in the trunk for some on-bike action!

It’s been 2 years since my last visit to this place and I’m on a new bike with stock suspension! Let’s see how fast I can go! Weeee! MAM is a fun track! Next session, take the GSXR out in the medium bike practice. It’s fast and takes some effort to get around the track, but doesn’t seem too bad. Well, until I jump off it and then hop on the SV for the slow practice. OMG! The SV just feels so much easier to ride! I end up parking the GSXR for the rest of the weekend.

After a couple of sessions, I follow Paul around for a few laps and get some good video of him. We look at it back in the pits and decide that it’s a pretty good training tool to help each other figure out the track and spot opportunities with our lines. So, we end up spending the next couple of sessions following each other around to try different lines and do the replay action back in the pits. The on bike action proves pretty popular. After a full day of riding, I notice some unusual wear patterns on the tires, but I never really had any issues. Wonder what is causing the wear? The stock suspension doesn’t seem all that bad, hmmm..maybe I can just be lazy and wait until I can work in Paul’s garage again?

The action wasn’t completely over for this evening though. Red Lobster proved to be pretty exciting too. Paul’s wife, Jennifer is a huge fan of all-u-can-eat crab legs. It’s not something one would expect from someone who looks so sweet, innocent and petite, but she really was incredible to watch as she destroyed 3-4 platters of crab legs in the time it took me to eat my salmon! Adult crabs need to add her picture to the list of natural predators that their kids need to look out for! I half expected the manager to come out and complain about her eating Red Lobster into to bankruptcy!

Saturday, 08/23/03

The temps are supposed to be in the middle 90’s today! Ouch. So, I sign up for 2 races today. The 8 Lap Lightweight SuperSport and the 14 lap Trophy Dash. After 4 years of racing the GSXR, It was kinda weird hearing the calls to grid for the Middle Weight races and not going out for them. I did get to play spectator a bit more though and watch the 600’s for a change. Geezus, #52 Rob Jenson is fast!

My first race of the day and on the SV comes after lunch, so I nervously head out when they call my grids. One good thing about it being so hot out is that the tires come up to temp fast! After only ½ a lap during the warm up lap, the tires are feeling pretty good! Due to the heat, the CRA decided to try a fast grid procedure this weekend too. Instead of the usual 3-4 minute wait at the starting grid, they do a 1-2 minute wait. Worked out pretty good and seemed like we got started before the tires could even drop in temp.

However; I got my usual suck ass start! L So, heading into turn one in the back of the pack, again! I ended up finishing up a lowly 10th out of 14 riders. I spent most of the race chasing Billy #313 around! Damn upstarts on old SV’s should not be messing with my ’03 SV! We played tag for a few laps then he passed me under braking into Turn 1 and I just could not find the speed to get cleanly past him. I blame the brakes for that! I did run a string of 1:46’s and 1:45’s for most of that race, but my last lap I clocked in at a 1:44.7! Kinda weird since around lap 6 my stock brakes decided to fade away. It’s a tad distracting to go into turn one and pumping the brakes! Note to self: Braided brake lines are a must!

By the time the Trophy dash comes around, everyone is just melting. Myself included, so my plan is just to survive! Drink plenty of water prior to getting on the bike and take it easy during the race. Let the other riders tire themselves out and then pass them! Okay, so the take it easy part got thrown out of the window pretty fast. I get a slightly better start this time, but still nothing to write home about. I’m still almost dead last into the 1st turn, but at least I wasn’t 20-40 feet behind like I was last time! There is Billy again! Wee! I put a pass on him and expected to not see him again this time! Bzzz! Wrong! After 2-3 laps, he passes me under the brakes into turn 1, again! The bastage! Taking advantage of my poor stock suspension and brake lines is just not fair! I decide to just keep the pressure on him, relax and maybe he will mess up! That strategy seemed to be a good one, since when the halfway flag was displayed, I was kinda surprised and didn’t feel tired at all. Go Billy Go! Oh wait, it looks like he has more motor then me too and my brakes suck. Okay..don’t go Billy! Don’t go! Basically, I end up finishing behind him again, but I vow revenge once I have the suspension and brakes I can trust! Muhahaha! I got a 5th out of that race, so all in all, not really that bad. I ran pretty consistent 1:44’s too, with a low time of 1:44.3.

This night’s dinner was at Applebee’s and it continued on the all-u-can-eat theme for the weekend when we all ordered the Applebee’s all-u-can-eat riblets special. No excitement to report there though. We all got equally stuffed, but didn’t even come close to driving Applebee’s out of business.

Sunday, 08/24/03

"It was hot, damn hot! Hot enough for crotch pot cooking!"

The morning was pretty pleasant at around 85 or so, but the high temp was forecast to be in the low 100’s with a heat index of 110. So, being a wuss, I decided to only do one race and do the first one I could legally run so I could get packed, on the road and home before too late. Not much drama to report here since everyone, including myself was just working on not melting. They shortened the race down to 6 laps from 8 due to the heat. I ended up finishing 13th out of 14 and behind Billy, again! I ran pretty crummy 1:45’s and 1:46’s. So, not too thrilled, but just glad to have survived the race.

It's been expensive the last 6 months...

Well…more then just the last 6 months really. It all started last summer when the poor old 1998 GSXR started to run badly. So, I took the baby in to see Brad Frey between races. He found a valve that wasn’t working right and managed to fix that, make a few other tweaks and dyno tune her for the complete package. I think the damage to my wallet for that doc visit was $1500 or $2000. She ran pretty good, almost better then new actually! I was pretty stoked for the Sept Endurance race.

Brad and I teamed up with one of his friends ( sorry, it’s been awhile and can’t remember his name, but good guy ) for the 5 hour endurance race. We did pretty good and finished 12th overall, but we gambled with the wrong class and ended up a lowly 5th in our class. However; by the end of the 5 hours of racing, the old GSXR was running badly again. Sounded like it was only hitting on 3 cylinders, so it was back to Brad for the winter.

I decided that it might be time to quit racing. It just wasn’t doing anything for me. I was always fighting for last place, the new 600’s where so much faster right out of the box, the bike needed more $$work$$ and I just felt slow. So, I sold my DR-Z400 to get it out of the garage and get some cash and signed myself up for the Freddie Spenser school ( http://www.fastfreddie.com ) in Vegas. $2100 + $200 for airfare + Hotel. Cha-Ching!

Holy cow! That class kicked butt! I learned a lot and for the first time in years…I really felt excited about racing again.

The valves were fried and the cylinders probably didn’t really need to be bored out, but Brad talked me into it. Some stainless valves, a fresh bore job, new pistons, new clutch plates and lots of other things that added up to $3500…*cough* and she dynoed out at 110HP. Not to bad for a 98 GSXR that only had 92-94HP stock.

I ended up missing the first race of the season at MAM, so I was really looking forward to the May BIR round. Get the "new" bike out there and see what she can do with them pesky R6’s, CBR600RR’s and ZX6RR’s! Well, she did pretty good actually. I could actually stay in the R6’s draft down the front straight now instead of watching them pull away. I was turning some pretty consistent 1:52’s and a few 1:51’s. Not what I was hoping for though. I really wanted to see some 1:49’s.

The bad news is, she was smoking some. I could really see the smoke when just getting on the gas from an idle. Smoke not a good sign to me, but I’m not a mechanic. Maybe the oil in the bike was too good, so I took out the thin fully synthetic race type oil and put some good old 10W40 non-synthetic Golden Spectro and a fresh filter. That seemed to help, but not a lot. To get some seat time and practice what I learned at Freddie’s school, I ran some track days at the Dakota Tech College track that Steve Bauman ( http://dctc.brinkster.net ) is running. That track is darn fun! I love those tight and technical tracks the most. Really gives you a good workout and time to practice your technique.

I also decided that perhaps the GSXR was due to be retired. So, what to do? Buy a new 600 and spend the cash to get it race prepped just to do it all over again in 2 years or get an SV-650? I opted for a 2003 SV-650S and for a training bike, a Yamaha TTR-125LE! Cha-Ching and Cha-Ching!! That little TTR is hella fun out on the dirt and it makes a good pit bike. I can’t wait for the lakes to freeze this winter and do some ice racing with the TTR!

So, the June weekend rolls around and I tow the GSXR up to BIR for some fun. I sign up for 3 races on Saturday with the 17 lap trophy dash being the last one I had planned to run. Practice on Saturday is pretty good. I manage to run some easy 1:52’s, so I’m looking forward to the races.

I get my normal crappy start for the 1st race, but I don’t mind so much because watching all those bikes go into Turns 1 and 2 at speed gives me the hebee geebee’s. I just wait for things to settle then start picking people off. I ran one lap in the 1:50 time and worked my butt off, so I wasn’t feeling to happy with myself at the end of the race. I did manage to work myself up to 18th place though, so I got some good passes done and had some fun. Definitely doing better this year!

Between races and during lunch I did some spectating and forgot to refill my gas tank. So, with the 3rd call to grid for my 2nd race being announced I decided that the bike probably had enough gas for a tiny 8 lap sprint race. I wasn’t even racing in my class. I was racing in the 750 class for some extra track time. So I wasn’t that worried about it.

Wouldn’t you know it, I was having a great time when the 4th lap came around and the low fuel light started blinking. I saw a 1:50.04 pop up on my lap timer and almost crapped my pants. Soooo close to a 1:49…so close! I was running nice and relaxed. Everything was in sync! I finished that lap, then pitted.

I was so pumped for the trophy dash now! A 1:49 was in my reach! So, I topped off the tank this time and relaxed some. After the bike had cooled, I decided to take a look at the coolant levels. I had noticed the temp gauge hit 230 and was worried that the coolant might be a little low. I open the radiator cap and nope, coolant level looks fine, right up to the top there, but what is that stuff floating on top?

Looks like Oil, in the radiator! Damn it!


So, I pack the bike up and call it a weekend.

Plan now is to get the GSXR fixed..again, then convert it back to street and race prep the SV. Damn Visa company is going to be damn happy with me this summer. Maybe sell the GSXR and buy another SV to ride on the street. It’s a damn fun bike.

With the 2003 SV having a totally new frame and other minor changes, I’m sure the race parts hunt will be "exciting". Stay tuned!
