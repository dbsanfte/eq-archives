Product Review: Yoyodyne Titanium's PFM Brake Rotors

Summery:  Much better than OEM Stainless stopping power.  Better feel and control which increases braking confidence ( watch out! ). Cheaper than OEM rotors.  Still managed to warp them on my GS-500E after two weekends of racing.  Currently running them my 98 GSX-R600...not expecting to warp them. ( GSX-R600 w/ dual front vs. single front on GS-500E )

During the first race on Sunday, June 28th, it seemed like my brakes were kind of mushy.   I had to pump them up every other lap going into turn 3,  then they would be fine for another lap or two.

After checking my rotor...I discovered that it was a little warped and I had one more race to run!  Hmmm...take the chance?  Sure!  I'm an idiot.  :-)    The good news is...I didn't crash,  but I got a little slowed down by having to pump up the brake on the entrance to turn 3 all the time.  After I got the brake feel back at turn three...the brakes would work just fine,  but come the next lap...mush!   I dueled with #180 again...and he managed to stay with me a little longer due to the turn 3 brake situation,  but I did manage to lose him.  I was only able to keep Mike in sight ( at a distance ) for the race and finished 3rd behind Mike in 2nd and Travis #74 on his rotax powered monster Ultralight GP bike. 

Now to check the damage on my wallet and hope that I can afford to make it to the next races on July 18th-19th.  A new rotor from Suzuki will cost me around $210 ( with local shop discount )...so, if I want to race.   It looks like I'll be spending money even before I get to the track...again. :-(   Chris Morken ended up warping his rotor this weekend too...I wonder if I can find someplace that will give a "group" discount.

After shopping around with my local Suzuki dealer, Frog Specialists, EBC & Yoyodyne Titanium,  I decided to give Fred Renz and Yoyodyne a try.  Fred was very friendly, knowledgeable and sympathetic to the plights of a struggling amateur racer.   The rotors for my Suzuki GS-500E retails for $199,  but Fred went for a "racer" discount.  The discount turned out to be VERY friendly!   Normally Fred will include Titanium Bolts & Fasteners with the rotors he sells.   I asked about brake pads, told him about the EBC HH pads I was using and asked him for a recommendation.  He said the HH pads are good,  but they will decrease rotor life.  He recommended an organic pad, like the EBC Kevlar Green, Galfer Green or similar.  Seeing how pads are a little more important than some way cool titanium bolts,  he went ahead and swapped the bolts for a set of Galfer Green pads.   This was Monday, July 6th...less than two weeks to get the rotors ordered & shipped from the manufacturer in England.  I'm thinking that I might not be racing,   but Fred assured my that he could have our rotors to Chris and I before the our next race on July 18th. 

Tuesday, July 14th,  Bad news...Fred calls me to let me know that the shipper in England lost the box!  He thinks that the earliest he could get me the rotor will be on Saturday.  Delivery at the track is spotty at best,  so I told him not to worry.  I guess I can save some money this weekend.

Thursday, July 16th, Good news...Fred calls again to inform me that the manufacturer had shipped a new box on Wednesday and he had them in his shop today. I didn't think that the Concorde had an air freight service. :-)  After he checked the price of next day air from Fed Ex, Fred asked if tomorrow was a good time to have my rotors.   Hmmm...Yes?!  That evening saw me cutting & grinding the snap rivets holding my old rotor onto my bike and checking the condition of my oil overflow catch bottle.

The rotors arrived at my office bright and early on Friday.   After inspecting the contents and looking at the bill I was very pleasantly surprised at the damage.  Fred had included the shipping & the two sets of Galfer Green pads for free.  That, plus the discount on the rotors saved me around $250 dollars. 

I called Fred to let him know that his "care package" had arrived and asked him if there was a break in period/routine for the rotors.  He said that they would have a little less stopping power for my first few sessions...as the zinc coating wore off.  Then they would turn blue under race use and stop like a son of a gun. That's supposed to be normal for cast iron rotors.  He said that under normal street use the cast iron rotors would return to a polished silver color.

I got up to the track Saturday morning, took the bike out during practice to get the new rotor & pads broke in. After about 5 laps the rotor was pretty much ready to go and was providing excellent braking power & feel. I started moving some of my brake markers in as I got used to the stopping power of the new rotor and Galfer Green pad combo. I put the Yoyodyne Titanium   stickers on the tank. With the freshly painted & shiny orange tank, the stickers almost makes me look like I'm a pro or something! :-)

The first race ( Lightweight SuperSport ) was with a wet track and I was racing a few GS-500's, but mostly FZR-400's in the Lightweight class. I got a 4th in that race & beat all the other GS-500's in the race. I was able to out brake the other GS's. mostly by getting most of my braking done in the dry patches.

The 2nd race ( Ultralight SuperSport ) was dry and I was only racing against other GS's. Again, the brakes are where I was able to get some easy passing done. The racing was a lot tighter than normal, due to the starting officials deciding to save a few seconds by starting the Experts & Novices in one wave -vs- the normal two waves. It was a little scary being held up by some of the slower experts and going into some of the turns 4-5 bikes wide with all of us jockeying for position. Adding lap traffic into the equation, only made things worse. I managed to earn a 1st in this race. For the first time, I was lifting the rear of my bike and chirping the front tire under heavy braking in turn 9. With hindsight being 20/20, that's probably not a good thing to be doing with my bike in turn 9. Not because it's not safe, but my bike is down a few ponies than the rest of the fast GS-500 pilots that I'm racing against. Making a pass under braking in turn 9 only means that the built motor folks will walk past me in the straight between 9 & 10 or in the mile long straight from 10 to turn 1. I'm usually able to open a 1-2 second lead on just about anyone that I pass in turn 3, which means that, built motor or not, I never see them again after the next lap. I also managed to get my first slide in this race. I was coming real hard & fast out of turn 10 and practicing relaxing ( like Twist of the Wrist II advocates ) when the bike started sliding. It felt pretty darn good! Like the good book says, I stayed on the gas, didn't try and tell the bike what I thought it was supposed to be doing and just let it happen. After that lap, I never was challenged for the lead again during that race.

The third race, an 18 lap trophy dash ( the normal sprint races are usually only 6 laps ), having really strong brakes lead to an over confidence problem. On the first lap, I tried to out brake my team-mate, Mike, in the entrance of turn 9. I was doing pretty good, the rear was lifting, the bike was almost down to a safe corner speed, I had shot past my team-mate on the inside. The only problem was, that Mike didn't see me making the pass. He turned in and ran right into my left foot peg, which slammed us both to the ground and off the track at a painful velocity. We hit the rumble strip, got thrown up and into the air, landed ( ouch ), his bike hit me ( I think ) and then when the world stopped moving. I crawled as far as I could, while trying to catch my breath. The bikes had only minor, easy to repair damage ( yeah GS-500! ), but I separated my shoulder and was way too sore to race on Sunday. Mike had popped his back and got a ride to the hospital in the ambulance, but was okay & determined enough to race again on Sunday. I'm glad that Mike was able to race on Sunday. He was the #1 Novice going into the weekend, the oldest in CRA history to be in the top ten and I didn't want a crash to keep him from losing his 45 point lead over the weekend. Even though the old buzzard had to be helped on & off his bike, he still managed to capture one first that I know of and a few more podium/high points finishes.

I can't wait until the next race weekend in September! I think that I'll be taking the bike out to our local practice track in a month or so ( after my shoulder feels better ) to work on my technique a little more.
