5/4/98: Spending money summary

I decided to work on my bike's setup and suspension this year. With all the money that I've dropped into the setup issues, I'm hoping the engine won't blow. I decided to finally get rid of the stock bars and get clip-ons. I wanted Pro-Tek two piece clip-ons ($130 + $15 for a set of spare bars ), but after waiting for two weeks for Pro-Tek to actually spend the 30 minutes or less that it would have taken to mill the 37mm $110 fork clamps, I gave up. I did the math. It costs $15 for two replacement bars w/ the bar ends, so that must mean that either clip-ons have HUGE markup/profit margin or those things that clamp onto the fork tubes are really hard & expensive to make. I ended up going to a local shop (St. Paul Hitching Post on Concord St ) and getting the Grave's two piece clip-ons that they had "accidentally" ordered for me. I took them home and after tearing the front end all apart, I discovered that one of the clip-ons was the wrong size. Damn it! Back to the shop, returned the set and had another express ordered and hopefully here before I leave for the race.

I also, ordered a pair of Novella, Ltd. (847) 359-2666 Rear sets to raise my footpegs and give me a little more clearance. Waiting for those too. They're should be here at the last minute too.

Tires…I went with the ME-Z1's this year. I got a 150/60/17 for the rear and a 120/60/17 for the front from 1-800-CycleGear. Saved about $10-$15 per tire -vs- supporting the local shop.

Gloves…after seeing Freddie Spenser's hands during a show on SpeedVision, I decided that $30 just wasn't enough protection for my fingers. I order a pair of AGV Prisma gloves for $80 from Competition Accessories. ( I probably shouldn't provide all this FREE advertising, but I'm hoping that it helps the few readers that we have get setup & save a few bucks ) Anyways…the gloves look like they'll provide darn good protection. There's padding and armor everywhere and the extra wrist strap is a good sign.

Shock…yes, I finally broke down and racked up the credit card again. $299 for Works Performance Shock. ( they have a good web site with all the prices & right now, Works($299) & Penske($700+) are the only places that you can get a GS-500E shock made ) I was hoping to have the shock by now…but again. If it wasn't for the last minute…nothing would ever get done. I ordered about 1 month in advance, but that doesn't look like enough time for the faint of heart.

May 8th-10th: First Race weekend

I put the Novella rearsets on my bike on Friday while at the track ( BIR ). The gear shifter rear set went on beautifully and performed MUCH better than the stock gear shifter, better feel & smoothness when shifting. The brake side had some technical difficulties. The brake linkage has a square hole punched in two metal brackets that set the rear brake lever height. The hole's where punched with a slightly wrong angle, which resulted in the rear brake lever being too high to be of any use. I did manage to use it once for an emergency stop when my new brake pads didn't stop me in time to prevent an off course excursion under heavy braking.

Anyways, the solution is to cut the rear brake lever piston bolt to make it shorter and allow the rear brake lever to rest further down. ( did that make any sense ? ) My team-mate's rear sets didn't have that problem, but another guy at the track had the same problem and the cutting thing is what he recommended. I still haven't done that yet, due to the simple fact that I'm lazy and almost never use my rear brake to slow me down.

I'd have to say that my overall impression is good, with the above exception, and I think they helped take 2 seconds off my laptimes from last year. I took some corners at a much steeper lean angle than I would have dared last year, due in part to the foot pegs not dragging and causing me to adjust my lean angle. I did touch my toe sliders a few times during rapid s-turn changes, but I think that is due more to the stock rear shock sagging too much. Can't wait for my Work's Performance shock to get here!

Everyone at the track who has the rearsets loves them and the people who don't have them generally want them after sitting on the bike to see what they feel like. I noticed my knees aching from the aggressive ride position much more than normal, especially after the 17 lap ( 50 mile ) trophy dash race we had on Saturday.  

Friday's practice sessions were kinda scary in the latter part of the day. As more people started rolling in and the faster bikes ( 750's etc...) got onto the track, the little GS's with their blazingly fast 120MPH top end where extremely overmatched. It was more than a little disconcerting to be leaned over in turn 1 WFO and have three or more 750's blast past you at a good 30-50 MPH faster. Things also got hairy in the corners. I had a 916 or a 748 in 916's clothing brush me in turn 8. I got some satisfaction in seeing the guy stand it up on his front wheel under heavy breaking while trying to pass 5-8 bikes in turn nine and ride off the track. :-) My laptimes for Friday were a disappointment. I was running 2:14's & 15's. On the plus side, the on bike timer from Hot Lap works great and provides excellent feedback while riding. Friday night I walked turn 3 & 4 ( darkness fell before I could do more) and I highly recommend getting up close and personal with the corners. I noticed things I never saw before. Like the excellent brake markers in turn 4 exactly 50 meters before the entrance to the turn. No one else on the team has ever noticed 'em before and I'm keeping quiet! :-) Speaking of brakes, I took the EBC Gold HH pads off ( due to wear ) and replaced them with a set of EBC Black Kevlar (FA) pads. I would have replaced the EBC HH pads with the same, but I had left my replacement set in the car at home. The car was due to join me Saturday morning. I decided to go ahead and put the EBC Black pads on anyways.

The new rear tires changed my gear ration ( surprise! ) for the better. I ran a stock front and a 41 on the rear. I never hit 6th gear on the straight while last year, everyone else always complained about redlining it in turn one in 6th. I think my bike "might" be little short of some horsepower -vs- the other non-stock motor bikes. I dunno. We all ran the same tires last year...so I think my next major investment will be a complete engine rebuild and 1mm over pistons. That will have to wait until winter, so I'll just have to do more with less!

Saturday's practice was much better when the CRA finally decided that they had enough bikes to split the practice sessions up into Slow ( 600 novice and less ) and Fast ( 600 expert & bigger ). Not as many courage checks and a more productive practice session for me. I registered for three races today. I signed up for the Lightweight Super Sport ( FZR400's & EX500's ), the Ultralight Super Sport ( GS500's ) and the Ultralight GP 17 lap trophy dash. I took 8th in the novice class with the LWSS race. I managed to beat a Hawk GT 650, a few EX500's & FZR400's. I was pretty happy with the results. I still only managed to get into the 2:12's for one or two of the 6 lap race. I wasn't very happy. I took 2nd in the ULSS race behind my team-mate Mike Dittrich #192. He was in the lead from the first lap and checked out ala' Scott Russell in the Daytona 200 race. I think he did something to his "allegedly stock" motor over the winter. I managed to get one 2:11 out of the race with a bunch of 2:13's. I chalked it up to a fluke.

During the trophy dash I got as high as 2nd ( in the ULGP class ) while racing against a GS500 fitted with an FZR400 front end and fairing. I t was some good racing, but come the straightway I'd get passed. Damn depressing! I managed to finish 3rd and got one 2:10 with lots of 2:12's +/- .75 seconds. Having the timer on the bike was GREAT! it allowed me to pace myself for the 17 laps much better. It was really nice knowing exactly how many laps I finished and what my pace was.

I changed my rear sprocket to the 39 with the hopes that I'd get a little more top end, replaced the stock brake line with a braided brake line & replaced the EBC Black Kevlar pads with my favorite EBC Gold HH Sintered Pads.

Sunday's practice, I set the new pads by running two slow laps ( finally...an excuse to be going slow! ) and then relaxed until the second practice session. The HH pads are MUCH better! I spent the 2nd & 3rd practice session re-readjusting my brake markers to account for the better stopping power and just enjoyed myself. I was running 2:13's & 14's pretty easily. I registered for two races, the Ultralight Superbike & Ultralight GP 6 lap sprint races.

For the ULSB race I managed to get only a 5th. Fausto ( the GS with the FZR 400 front-end ), 177 & I had some pretty tight racing, but towards the end 177 managed to get past both me and Fausto to get the 3rd place. I think a 125 or RD400 took the 1st place and Mike ( 192 ) took 2nd.

For the last race, Fausto & I ended up fighting for the coveted 3rd place position. This time I got past 177 and kept my eye on the sneaky devil. I caught Fausto, let him know I was there then didn't fight him until we had opened up a sizable lead on 177. Fausto's front end slipped a little going into turn 3 on the last lap & I managed to pass him later on that lap on the exit of turn 6. I somehow managed to keep him behind me to finish the race in 3rd. Lap times for this race started with a 2:12,2:13 then dropped to the 2:10's for the last 3 laps. I was happy!

Team Absolute Riders Practice Session

I finally received my Works Shock a 3 weeks ago, installed it, painted the bike Evil Pumpkin Orange, put a new clutch in & changed my oil.  After putting all that work into the bike,  it only seemed right to take it out and try it out before heading up to BIR.  Lucky for me Glen Christianson ( CRA Expert #44 ) has worked with a local college ( Dakota County Tech ) about 3 miles from my house,  to use their road training facility.  The track is a just over a mile in length with 12-13 corners ( depending on how you count a corner ).  It's MUCH more tighter and technical than the wide open BIR.  The Ultralight & Lightweight bikes are definitely the bike to have here. 

I ran home after work and loaded the bike on my trailer,  stopped at the corner gas station for some gas, then finished the hard remaining 2 miles to the "track".  The day was absolutely PERFECT.  Sunny and 80 degrees versus the last three sessions that I corner worked for that it rained or tried to rain on.  

After unloading the bike,  I registered and paid the $50 rider fee.  It sounds like a lot,  but it's definitely worth it.  After enough people where there,  Glen gave the mandatory rider safety talk.  The talk was a little disorganized ( compared to the rider meetings that the CRA has ) with people coming and going seemingly at will.  For the racers,  it was all review of basic track rules.  Like what flag means what and a reminder that this is a practice versus race, so no stuffing people in the corners.  I just hope that some of the squids that didn't seem to be paying attention...noticed that little point.

I was a little disappointed with the 10 minute session length that Glen had setup.   I was expecting it to take 2-3 laps to get warmed up...then just as you started to get the hang of things...it would be over. Has I ever WRONG!  After 10 minutes of careening  through the corners,  I felt like I had spent 30 minutes at the gym.   What a blast! 

I was passing 600's, 750's...etc.  I was very rarely being passed...except in the tiny straight-a-way.  If I could pass a 600 in the first few corners,  I wouldn't see them ever again.  Their straight line speed was no match for the mighty GS-500E in the corners.  I managed to drag my knee through some of my faster left hand corners.  It seems like the left handers feel more comfortable.  I must be distributing my weight differently...or something.  Turns 5 & 6 were the two left's that I could just rail thru. Discovering early on that the two turns could be treated as one,  made that section of the track much faster for me.   I made more passes in that section than anywhere else.

I had much fun and after the three hours of 10 minute sessions,  I was fried.   All the work I did to the bike seems to be paying off,  but having never been on that track with my old setup...I wouldn't really be able to measure anything until I got to BIR on Friday ( 28th of June ).   

2nd CRA Weekend ( June 28th - 30th )

Friday June 28th:

The day was sunny and in the 80's.  A good day for practice.  I checked & set my tire pressure to what I thought would be good for the late morning air & track temps.  ( 29 front & 32 rear ).  After the Dakota County practice on Monday,   my first few laps felt SLOW.  However; my lap timer told me a different story.   I was running 2:10's without even trying.  By the end of the day I had managed to break into the 2:07's and I even dragged my right knee thru a few corners.  I guess the combination of the shock & Monday's practice paid off.  I tried paying more attention to how and where I was putting my weight in the corners.  I wanted to get most of my body weight off of the inside footpeg and put it on the outside peg where it belonged.  Once I started doing that on a more regular basis,  the right hand corners kept on getting smoother and faster.

Saturday June 29th:

Again...a perfect day for racing.  During practice I was able to run in the 2:08's pretty easy...so I felt pretty good about the two races that I had registered for today.   I registered for the Ultralight Sport Bike 6 lap sprint race and the Ultralight Super Bike 20 lap Trophy Dash.  I got the standard  unbuilt GS-500E start, which means that almost everyone else had managed to open a few bike lengths worth of  lead going into turn one.  I was able to make up for that in the corners & by the end of the 3rd lap I had managed to catch the 2nd place guy ( #180 ) and then on the forth lap...pass him.  I had to time my pass for turns 3-6 or by the end of the front straight,  he could motor up and re-pass me.  Very frustrating!  Mike ( #192 ) and fellow team-mate was the only person in my class left in front of me...and for an old fart...he's awful darn fast.  A comfortable 2nd wasn't too bad. 

I decided to put an extra pound of air the tires to help them run cooler for the 20 lap race.  Set 'em at 31 front and 34 rear.  The 20 lap Trophy Dash was pretty much a repeat of the first race...with #180 and I doing a little dueling for position,   but I was able to lose him by the 5th lap. On the 3rd lap, I managed to break into a 2:05 laptime.  I was flying!   Mike checked out again so I was left with 15 laps to make sure #180 didn't  re-catch me & to pace myself to keep from getting tired.  I was hoping to maybe catch Mike in the later stages of the race...when the old fart got tired and slowed down. :-)  However; He did more than just slow down.  He ran off the track ( actually he got run off by a squid piloted FZR-400 ) and went for a swim in the turn 8 swamp.  I went by and saw him standing in the water, we waved to each other,  I looked back and saw no one in my line of sight ...so I slowed down to an easy 2:10/09 pace.  I started practicing a few new lines and then upped the pace again on the last 2 laps ( I was starting to feel guilty ) and I turned another 2:05.   My first 1st!  Now all I have to do is actually beat Mike to make it worth something.

Sunday June 30th:

I put new brake pads on,  my old ones would have probably given up on me if Saturday's Trophy Dash had been 2 laps longer.  My first practice session was used to set the pads.  I ran the second to see how things felt and guess what I should run for tire pressure.  I set the pressure to 30 front and 32 rear.  I then decided to write that in my log book and discovered that I had done that in the May event too.   The temp for the day I had logged in May was the same as today...and so was my psi settings.  I guess I'll just leave things alone, log things better and read what I log next time.

I was only going to register for one race today,  but while at race registration I discovered an extra $20 so I registered for the Sportsman Lightweight & Ultralight Superbike races.   The Sportsman race turned into a repeat of the previous days races with the exception that I had a mushy front brake in turn 3 and I managed to close to within 30 feet of Mike by the end of the race.  If I had only had 1 more lap,   I might have been able to blast past him on the exit of turn 10 and beat him to the finish line.  That will just have to wait until next time. During this race, it seemed like my brakes were kinda mushy.  I had to pump them up every other lap going into turn 3,  then they would be fine for another lap or two.

After checking my rotor...I discovered that it was a little warped and I had one more race to run!  Hmmm...take the chance?  Sure!  I'm an idiot.  :-)    The good news is...I didn't crash,  but I got a little slowed down by having to pump up the brake on the entrance to turn 3 all the time.  After I got the brake feel back at turn three...the brakes would work just fine,  but come the next lap...mush!   I dueled with #180 again...and he managed to stay with me a little longer due to the turn 3 brake situation,  but I manage to lose him.  I was only able to keep Mike in sight ( at a distance ) for the race and finished 3rd behind Mike in 2nd and Travis #74 on his rotax powered monster Ultralight GP bike. 

Now to check the damage on my wallet and hope that I can afford to make it to the next races on July 18th-19th.  A new rotor from Suzuki will cost me around $210 ( with local shop discount )...so, if I want to race.   It looks like I'll be spending money even before I get to the track...again. :-(

 

 

3rd CRA Race Weekend Report

Saturday, July 18th:

I got up to the track Saturday morning, took the bike out during practice to get the new rotor & pads broke in. After about 5 laps the rotor was pretty much ready to go and was providing excellent braking power & feel. I started moving some of my brake markers in as I got used to the stopping power of the new rotor and Galfer Green pad combo. I put the Yoyodyne Titanium   stickers on the tank. With the freshly painted & shiny orange tank, the stickers almost makes me look like I'm a pro or something! :-)

The first race ( Lightweight SuperSport ) was with a wet track and I was racing a few GS-500's, but mostly FZR-400's in the Lightweight class. I got a 4th in that race & beat all the other GS-500's in the race. I was able to out brake the other GS's. mostly by getting most of my braking done in the dry patches.

The 2nd race ( Ultralight SuperSport ) was dry and I was only racing against other GS's. Again, the brakes are where I was able to get some easy passing done. The racing was a lot tighter than normal, due to the starting officials deciding to save a few seconds by starting the Experts & Novices in one wave -vs- the normal two waves. It was a little scary being held up by some of the slower experts and going into some of the turns 4-5 bikes wide with all of us jockeying for position. Adding lap traffic into the equation, only made things worse. I managed to earn a 1st in this race. For the first time, I was lifting the rear of my bike and chirping the front tire under heavy braking in turn 9. With hindsight being 20/20, that's probably not a good thing to be doing with my bike in turn 9. Not because it's not safe, but my bike is down a few ponies than the rest of the fast GS-500 pilots that I'm racing against. Making a pass under braking in turn 9 only means that the built motor folks will walk past me in the straight between 9 & 10 or in the mile long straight from 10 to turn 1. I'm usually able to open a 1-2 second lead on just about anyone that I pass in turn 3, which means that, built motor or not, I never see them again after the next lap. I also managed to get my first slide in this race. I was coming real hard & fast out of turn 10 and practicing relaxing ( like Twist of the Wrist II advocates ) when the bike started sliding. It felt pretty darn good! Like the good book says, I stayed on the gas, didn't try and tell the bike what I thought it was supposed to be doing and just let it happen. After that lap, I never was challenged for the lead again during that race.

The third race, an 18 lap trophy dash ( the normal sprint races are usually only 6 laps ), having really strong brakes lead to an over confidence problem. On the first lap, I tried to out brake my team-mate, Mike, in the entrance of turn 9. I was doing pretty good, the rear was lifting, the bike was almost down to a safe corner speed, I had shot past my team-mate on the inside. The only problem was, that Mike didn't see me making the pass. He turned in and ran right into my left foot peg, which slammed us both to the ground and off the track at a painful velocity. We hit the rumble strip, got thrown up and into the air, landed ( ouch ), his bike hit me ( I think ) and then when the world stopped moving. I crawled as far as I could, while trying to catch my breath. The bikes had only minor, easy to repair damage ( yeah GS-500! ), but I separated my shoulder and was way too sore to race on Sunday. Mike had popped his back and got a ride to the hospital in the ambulance, but was okay & determined enough to race again on Sunday. I'm glad that Mike was able to race on Sunday. He was the #1 Novice going into the weekend, the oldest in CRA history to be in the top ten and I didn't want a crash to keep him from losing his 45 point lead over the weekend. Even though the old buzzard had to be helped on & off his bike, he still managed to capture one first that I know of and a few more podium/high points finishes.

I can't wait until the next race weekend in September! I think that I'll be taking the bike out to our local practice track in a month or so ( after my shoulder feels better ) to work on my technique a little more.

 

4th & Last CRA Race Weekend Report for the 98 Season

Friday, Sept 18th:

I noticed that the front rotor was dragging again. It can't be the rotor already! I took her out for the morning practice sessions and did a few 2:09's, which I didn't think it was too bad considering the head wind coming down the front straight. Checked the tires and decided that they were a little to thin on the right side to trust my life too and bought a set of Pirelli Corsa's 150/60R17 & 110/60R17 for $285 from MRT. While the wheels were off I checked the brakes. The rotor was dragging on the caliper, but I preferred to believe it was the other way around. My Dad & I bled the brakes. That seemed to help a little. That evening, a few of the more experienced mechanics took a look at it and came to the conclusion that the rotor was warped. Damn! It wasn't nearly as bad as the first rotor I warped & raced on, but it was still not the safest way to consider racing. I decided to only race 4 out of the possible 7 on my bike, the 4 hour Endurance race on Mike's bike and call it a weekend.

Saturday, Sept 19th:

Unfortunately for my wallet, John Rydi ( a friendly GS-500 pilot ) came to my rescue with a mostly new stock rotor & carrier for only $100. I couldn't pass that deal up. I put the rotor on and presto...no more dragging on my caliper. I got the thing back together in time to make the last 20 minutes of the last practice session for the day, but after I got to the track entrance...red flag! New tires with no chance to scuff them in before the first race. I planned on taking it easy. I scuffed the tires in as good as possible during the warm up lap. I then took the first lap kinda slowly, but I didn't feel any problems with the tires so I picked the pace up and managed to finish a respectable 3rd.

The second race was the Sportsman Lightweight race.  I'm not sure what Sportsman LW is or isn't, but a GS-500E is fairly competitive and it's good track time.  I got my traditional good launch and last to the corner stock GS-500E start, but I did manage to get in the top 5 by the end of the first lap.  I'm not sure what position I finally finished in,  but I can say it was a good race and I beat all the GS's that I needed to beat.  :-)

The third race was the UL Superbike.  My traditional start, etc...  Fausto was on a loaner bike with a built motor.  I was kinda suprised to see him ahead of me after the 2nd or 3rd lap.  I managed to get a good pass on him and I then reeled Mike into striking/drafting range for the last lap and a half.  Seeing how he is my team-mate and after the points. I decided not to make a play for the lead.  He didn't seem to have much confidence in my decision and was weaving all over the place in an attempt to break the draft.  I watched him drag his pipe a few times and got a little worried that he'd throw it away for no reason.  Oh well,  he didn't throw it away and it felt kinda good having an Evil Pumpkin 1-2 finish.

My last race of the day was in the UL GP class.  I don't remember much about that race, except that I finished 2 bikes behind Mike and that if I had had another lap.   I might have been able to finish right behind him or even pass him.  I was whipping out some 2:06 and 2:05 lap times in that race -vs- Mike's 2:07's.  I need to get better starts!

Mike and I decided to use his bike as the "A" bike for our Endurance machine and my bike as the "B" ( backup ) bike.  We looked at his tires and decided to swap the new tires & wheels from my bike to his bike.  Unfortunately, we waited until Sunday to do that.

Sunday, Sept 20th:

We got the bikes thru tech and our pit setup.  We did the wheel change during the practice sessions and I was hoping to get the wheels done in time for me to take his bike out for a few laps.  We failed...oh well.  His bike is setup exactly like mine, with the exception of his not having a tach.  So we decided to not worry about it and go race.  Mike started the race and we planned on switching riders & re-fueling every 20 laps.  After about 12 laps,  I heard over the PA that bike 192 was down in turn 3.  Damn!  Then a red flag came out.  Not for Mike,  good!   He didn't crash, but had somehow lost spark in one cylinder.  He managed to limp the bike into the pits and during the red flag we feverishly tried to find and fix the problem.  It turned out to be a broke coil connector wire.  We stripped, wrapped and taped the wire back on and had the bike running in time to just miss the red flag re-start.  Mike got out of the pits just as the last wave was restarted.   He then crashed in turn 4 while the tires were still cold.  Mike was totally un-hurt and the medics where as astonished as he was that he crashed.  He got a ride back to the pits, we swapped the DBcom transponder and he took my bike out.  We lost all the laps that we had run on his bike,  but we should be able to at least finish the race.  After about two hours on Mike's mostly worn tires, the rear started to break loose a little in turn 3.  I still managed to turn a few 2:05's and I was told that I even ran a few 2:04's.  No crashes this time, for either of us, and we managed to get a respectable 4th in our class. 

We did luck out with our class pick. We had two classes to choose from, Ultralight SuperSport and Ultralight GP.  Only 5 bikes signed up for ULGP -vs- the 9 in ULSS.   The 4th place finish, plus the 9 races Mike ran on Saturday  should be enough to ensure his #1 Novice finish for the year.  I sure hope so!  I have no idea what my final rank will be,  but I hope I'm able get back into the top 10 with my finishes from this weekend.

Until next year!
