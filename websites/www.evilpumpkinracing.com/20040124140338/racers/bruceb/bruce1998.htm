Race Prep Costs

What follows is a description of the modifications and price (for reference purposes) to race prep my 91 GS500. My thought was to keep the motor stock and concentrate on the handling & suspension. I had to replace many of the stock parts because the previous owners left the bike outside in the weather and neglected doing maintenance except for changing the oil. The chain was rusted, the front brakes pad material was getting thin, the rear brake pads were worn to the metal backing plates, the front tire didn't have much tread, rust on the pipe & frame, the brake fluid looked like it had algae growing in it, the air filter was dirty, but the rear tire was new. Cosmetically the bike was in almost perfect condition except for a small dent in the gas tank from a parking lot tip over.

As you can see racing is not cheap. You can go cheaper but I tried to put together a bike I could race for many seasons because I don't plan on racing a larger motorcycle. If you can find a well maintained race bike this would probably be much cheaper than taking a street bike and prepping it for the track.

What changes give you the best bang per buck?

Definitely the rebuilt forks and the new rear shock. You can't go fast if the bike is using up half its travel just from sitting on the bike. However; I would consider going with a cheaper shock like Works Performance which would save you about $500.

Why the change to an RF900 master cylinder?

If you read my July 98 Road Atlanta race report you'll see that I was having a problem getting the bike stopped. The local Suzuki shop had a wrecked RF900 so they tried the master cylinder on my GS, presto brake problem fixed. The brake lever never felt this firm even before I started having the problem at Road Atlanta.

 

 

Roebling Road Raceway, Friday ( June 12th)

Friday ( June 12th) morning arrived at Roebling Road Raceway (close to Savannah, GA) a 1.9 mile 9 turn road course. Unloaded the truck and registered for a day of practice in the hot humid conditions (all weekend was in the 90's with high humidity). First 20 minute session went better than I expected, my best lap times were 3 seconds a lap faster (1:29.5) then on my 250 Ninja (1:32.5). Spent the next two practice sessions trying to get smooth and comfortable with the track which I haven't raced at in 7 years. During the first practice session after lunch I had an unscheduled event occur, you guessed it off the track and into the dirt. Did I mention my practice group had 600's and I believe a few 750's thrown in just for fun. Let's see we have bikes with 40hp mixed with 100hp or more, gee does anyone see a problem here. Anyway I'm just past the apex of turn 3 (at about 90-95mph) when the next thing I know I feel something solid against my left side. A guy on a 600 decided he wanted the same piece of track I wanted. All I can say about the incident is I believe his front tire must have left the black mark I have on my left boot and left leg of my white leathers. I never saw his bike or I'd given him some room, I wasn't racing anyone and just trying to put in some practice laps. After I got back up I went over the see if the other rider was OK and he told me "sorry dude". The more I thought about his comment the more I figured it was he who ran into me because racers never admit they've made a mistake unless they've really f----- up. At this point I figured I should sell the bike and take up chess as a hobby. I picked up the parts that were know missing from the bike (number plate, foot peg, and front brake pad) and starting pushing it back to the pits. I meet Gary Merrill (from the list) and he gave me a hand with the bike, thanks Gary. I decided to try and fix the bike for Saturday practice which required new clubman bars, clutch lever mount, bending the foot bracket into place, zip tying the number plate, and installing new brake pads. By now it was almost 6pm, the bike was ready for Saturday morning practice, and I was in a much better mood.

Saturday. I starting pushing the bike to tech when the steering stop welded to the steering stem broke/fell off. Oh well it passed tech anyway and I'll get it fixed before the next race weekend. I did about 6 practice laps and the bike was OK but this was it for the practice on Saturday. There was a 6 hour endurance race for the big boys with their big toys and they got the track for remainder of the day. The family and I spent the rest of the day touring the historic district of Savannah.

Sunday. Two short morning practice sessions followed by lunch. I only registered for the Clubman race which was the 3rd race. My grid position was on the last row which was fine with me because there was no one to run into me on the start. I got a good start and passed a few bikes before turn one. I ran the most consistent lap times of the weekend 1:29's - 1:31's and ended up 6th novice out of 12 riders and passed at least 3 of the white plates (experts).

The bike ran great, even though it gets passed by just about everything going up the front straight. I'm surprised to find out that with the 80 series Metzeler's and the suspension changes the only thing that touched the track was my knee (other than the wreck of course). I had a great time and it sure is a lot more fun than playing chess.

Road Atlanta race report July 3 – 5, 1998

The family and I arrived at Road Atlanta Friday evening (July 3rd) and set up camp for the weekend. The fireworks started about 11:00pm Friday night. There is a stock car oval speedway within walking distance of Road Atlanta and the speedway shot off fireworks after the last race of the night. It was great because my kids were able to see the fireworks from their sleeping bags in the tent.

Saturday morning I registered for a 20 lap race after lunch and a 8 lap sprint on Sunday. Next I wheeled my bike through technical inspection and got my sticker to enter the track. We would only get one practice session each day so I was ready for 3rd call at 8:30am announcing my practice group could enter the track. I took it easy the first few laps to try and learn the track and when I increased my pace suddenly I found myself with an extremely mushy feeling brake lever. I usually brake with two fingers and I had the lever coming in and touching my two fingers on the bar. I tried for a few laps braking with four fingers but now the brake lever was coming in against the bar. This was caused a very anxious feeling coming off of Road Atlanta's mile long back straight into the new 90's, and finding yourself without the braking force you are use too. About the time I decided to come into the pits the practice session was over.

I spent the remainder of the morning bleeding the brakes and generally trying about any suggestions that were offered my other racers. When third call came for my race I decided it was time to take a break from working on the bike and eat some lunch. My wife twisted my arm to find some A/C so we walked up to the Grill/Pub at Road Atlanta and had a few beers since my race day was over. After the beer and the A/C I found a friend to help me with the brakes. I bought a new bottle of DOT 4 brake fluid and we started bleeding the brakes again. After about an hour the feel came back but by this time I couldn't tell if it was like it was before the problem or not. At least the lever wasn't touch the bar with you grabbed it.

Sunday morning I went out for a few practice laps to try the brakes and they were good for a few laps. About the third lap I had the same problem with the brake lever becoming mushy and coming in against the bar. I pulled into the pits and called it a weekend.

My wife said she was proud of me because I didn't throw my tools at the bike or start a screaming swearing fit in front of the kids. To tell you the truth I was just to tired from working on the bike in the hot sun and I figure these things will happen from time to time no matter how much bike prep you do before the race.

On a positive note my gearing of 15/43 seemed to help close up the rpm spacing in 4th, 5th, & 6th gear. The bike now hits about 10,000 rpm in 6th gear which matches what I had calculated using my gearing spreadsheet.

 

Roebling Road race report September 18 – 20, 1998

 

RAIN! RAIN! AND MORE RAIN!

I rushed around for a week or more trying to get everything packed and ready for our late Thursday evening departure time. 11:30pm finally on the road to Faulkville, GA, I must be a (motorcycle) nut, what other activity but Roadracing would having me taking a vacation day and driving all night. We arrived 5:30am Friday morning and waited for the 7:00 registration for a practice. Wonderful, just before we start to register the rain starts and would continue on and off throughout the day.

Safety tip for Roebling Road in the rain. The patches are very slick and rest of the pavement has good traction in the wet. Turn 8 is the worst because it's mostly all patches. I spent the day trying to find lines that would avoid the patches while braking and cornering, of course these wet lines are completely different than riding in the dry. The rain caused a lot of riders to skip practice for the day and wait for a dry track. Usually the track is filled with riders during a practice day but with the rain sometimes there was open practice with no one out on the track. A few times it was just me and my racing buddy on his EX500 out on the track. The last open practice session was announced about 3:30pm and was going to last one hour. It was kind of like the scene from Caddy Shack where Bill Murray is the caddy for the priest in the pouring rain, "I don't think the hard stuff is going to come down for quite a while yet. I went out in this downpour and decided to just get comfortable riding in the rain, so I ending up out on the track for a least forty minutes. The GS is a great rain bike I could pass and ride with those hot shots on the 600's. The only changes I made to the bike for the rain was to soften the rebound 1 click and soften the compression 2 clicks.

Saturday morning's practice session started with a wet track but soon had a dry line around most of the track. Turn 5 would have puddles most of the weekend because water was seeping up through the track. The track is only about 50ft above sea level, being so close to the ocean, which makes the water table fairly high. After lunch I had my one race of the day Light-weight Solo 20. This is a 20 lap mini endurance race with FZR400's and Honda Hawks being the most popular bikes to ride. I got a great start and passed the 2 bikes gridded directly in front of me and then we hit second and third gear, and I found myself going into turn one in last place. During the race I was riding conservative because the track had been so slick in the rain. I was having a difficult time changing from riding on a wet track to now racing on a dry track. I started to up the pace and ended up passing a few riders before the finish. The only big event was the bike had a series of oscillating wallows in turn 9 the fastest turn on the track. After thinking about why the bike handled so poorly in this turn it hit me that maybe I adjusted the compression damping the wrong way before the race. That was it, I had softened the compression dampening 2 more clicks instead of putting it back were it was before Friday's wet practice. I ended up in 6th place of eight novices.

Sunday the track dried quickly after an overnight shower. I started the Clubman race the same as the day before, quick off the line but was passed by 3 or 4 bikes before turn one. After the first lap I started to have a dice with another GS rider and a rider on a 500 Honda Interceptor just ahead of us. As me and the other GS rider swapped positions the interceptor gained about 20 bike lengths on us. At the flag the other GS rider beat me for 4th place by about 1/2 a bike length and the 3rd place Interceptor was about the same 20 bike lengths ahead. So I ended up 5th of 12 novices. The bike now handled great through turn nine with the compression damping adjusted to 12 clicks out (instead of 8 clicks out like in Saturday's race). I was a little disappointed to run about a second slower a lap than when I raced at Roebling in June, but I had a hard time adjusting from a very slick wet track to dry track that had good grip.

Now I'll prepare the bike for the last race of the year at Talladega Gran Prix Raceway with AHRMA. Prep work will be my usual clean the bike thoroughly while inspecting everything, lube the chain, and change oil & filter. The mechanical work is so time consuming riding a "stock" motored GS500. This will be my first AHRMA weekend so I'm looking forward to the event.

Talladega Gran Prix Raceway race report October 2 – 4, 1998

Friday October 2, 1998 (Practice Day)

We pulled an "all nighter" driving 450 miles through the night to arrive at the track around 4am. About 7:30am I went through registration which was a breeze since you have to pre-register for AHRMA roadracing events. I wheeled the bike into the tech area. This would be my first race weekend with AHRMA so I was a little apprehensive about going through tech inspection. No problems, I got my bike and protective gear approved without any difficulties. Unlike the WERA tech inspectors, which at times act like traffic cops, the AHRMA guys were friendly and just wanted to make sure the bike would be safe for you and the other racers. Friendly advice, always read each organizations rule book about preparing the bike, actually prepare the bike, and tech inspection shouldn't be a problem. In addition to what I had safety wired for WERA I had to add a catch bottle and safety wire the throttle cable to the throttle cable housing to meet the AHRMA requirements.

It cost me $50 to practice Friday and it was worth every penny. I rode at least 55 laps over the course of the day so that's less than a dollar a lap. A $1 a lap is very inexpensive track time. After the second practice session I tried making the rear suspension firmer because the bike was moving around a lot on the track. First I'd ride a session with one click more of rebound damping and then one click more of compression damping. My lap times weren't showing any improvement as the bike got stiffer and I was still having a problem getting the bike to turn and hold a line through the fastest corner on the track. By the end of the day's practice I had the suspension settings exactly were I'd started the day. I spent the evening wondering how I could go slower on my GS with 40hp vs. my 250 Ninja I raced at Talladega in 1991. From memory I thought I'd run lap times in the 1:08 - 1:10 range on the 250 and I was only down to the low 1:09's on the GS by the end of practice. (After returning home and checking my notes I found out my times on the 250 were 1:10's - 1:12's)

Saturday October 3, 1998

Today there would be three practice sessions before lunch and then the races would start. AHRMA has the same race format for Saturday and Sunday most race weekends. So I would run Battle of the Twins Formula 3 on Saturday and Sunday, kind of like the format for World Superbike, only much slower. After the first practice session I decided to take one more click out of rebound and one more click out of compression to make the suspension softer. This seemed to make the bike work better even though it was moving around more at least I could feel what it was doing.

I got a good start from the last row of the grid and entered the first turn ahead of a bike or two. The field quickly spread out and after about 2 laps a Honda Hawk came past me. I tried to stay with the Hawk rider but he slowly pulled away and I thought I was running alone until I heard a bike behind me on the last lap. I concentrated on hitting my reference points and stayed in front of the bike behind me. It turns out it was the other GS500 in the field and he (Joe Templin) was within a bike length at the finish. I finished 8th overall (but first GS) with the top riders on Honda Hawks, a 650cc Ducati, and one 850cc Moto Guzzi. I was really happy with my riding because I felt like I was consistent and once I saw my lap times they were all between 1:09.2 and 1:10.2. The GS is definitely outgunned on horsepower in this class but handles well enough to keep it out of last place. I found out from the 3rd place finisher on a Hawk with 70hp that he was running 1:05's and 1:06's. He was surprised I could run 1:09's on a GS with a stock motor so that made me feel pretty good.

ARMHA had a barbecue Saturday evening and the food was excellent. This organization is a lot of fun to race with and I hope to do more events next year.

Sunday October 4, 1998

After the first morning practice session I decided to take advantage of an offer by another racer (Craig Hunley) to ride his bike. Bad idea, I rode an MuZ Skorpion (660cc 4-stroke single) during the second practice session and loved it. The bike handles at least as good as the GS, probably better, and it actually has a motor. In stock form the bike makes about 48hp and it will just launch you out of a corner with that big single tractor motor compared to the GS. Craig said the front end felt better on my bike, GS with Race Tech parts vs. stock Skorpion, and the motor would really spin, GS 11,000 redline vs. 6,800 red line, but didn't make any power. Yeah I felt the same way, my GS had a better front end compared to the stock Skorpion, but the GS felt gutless after riding Craig's bike. For the last practice session I rode my bike to prepare for the race.

I got another good start and was into the first turn with 3 or 4 bikes behind me this time. Again the same Hawk rider came by after about 3 laps and I tried to keep him close but it didn't matter because he ran off the track a few turns after passing me. About lap 6 I got a slide coming out of turn one which ran me wide at the exit and Joe (GS500) got by me in the short chute before turn 2. I keep Joe within a few bike lengths for the remainder of the race but finished about 2 bike lengths behind at the flag. So I finished 7th (2nd of out 3 GS500's) and had a big grin on my face from the race. After the race I looked at my rear tire, it was almost bald on the left side from all the left hand corners on the track. Gee I guess this is why I was getting into a few slides in the race.

 

1998 Year in Review

Great year back to roadracing after 7 years away from the track. I was able to attend two more races than I first thought the budget would allow. I tried an AHRMA event as planned so I could compare it to a WERA event. AHRMA is clearly a more friendly atmosphere and the racing is more sportsman like. All the racers follow the rule that it’s the guy from behind responsibility to pass safely. The GS is a good relatively inexpensive racebike that handles well and is also cheap to maintain. For next year I've found out that radial tires are the way to go on the GS instead of the bias-ply tires I've been using. Camping at the track isn't that bad and it sure saves a lot of time and money. I've been thinking about having a another bike to race and the MuZ would be nice to have. AHRMA has a Skorpion only class that actually pays money down to 6th place, a win will get you $250. I'll have to wait and see if Santa will bring me another bike for next year.
