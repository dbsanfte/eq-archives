Race Report

Well, delete now if you don't like rambling stories. I loaded up Wednesday night so that I could get a good start right after work on Thursday. I got on the road by 5PM Thursday for the 4 hour ride up to Brainerd and BIR for Friday practice, Saturday sprint racing and Sundays 5 hour endurance. I went without my wife and dog for the first time and I was kinda liking the extra space in the car so that I didn't really have to cram stuff all over the place.

Anyways, I got to the track at about 9 pm. One time saving tip is to use a rusted out 77 caprice classic as your tow vehicle. You never need to check the oil! When the valve knocking noise get so loud that it drowns out the 200 watt/channel stereo, just pull over, add 4 quarts of 10-40 and continue on. A big time saver. Anyways, I got teched early and headed to the hot tub at the hotel. Very nice.

Friday dawned cloudy and cool. Practice started at 10:00. I put 31 psi front and back into my Z-1s as per recommended by the BIR tire guy last time at the track. The bike was running perfectly and we went out for the first practice (slower bikes). I rode around with a really fast guy named Marty on a GS500. Marty pits right next to me, so it was pretty cool riding with him. It was mostly 30 minutes of getting back into the groove as I have not been to the track in over 2 months. I didn't get any times, but I felt like I was doing pretty well. I was dragging my stock pipe through most right handers. It felt fine. In between the two morning practices, Marty and I talked about hanging off and general technique stuff. I know that I have to hang off more and I set out in the next practice to work on that.

I got back out and started working on the corners. I passed several guys and even a couple on F2/3s. Very cool. I got into a pack with 1 expert FZR 400, two novice FZR 400s and an expert GS-500. I got sucked up by the 400s on the straight and then I could catch up in turns 3-9. I was working up to speed to maybe see if I could work a pass on some of them. Mostly I was wildy happy at staying with the 400s. I had never done that in the past. Maybe they were practicing on strange bikes getting ready for the endurance or something, but whatever, it felt really good. I had gone through ever one of BIRs 10 turns in my head for months and the one I felt I could make time up at was turn 5. Four is a sharp left hander which is quickly followed by the sharp right handed 5. I wanted to get on the gas out of 4 and stay on it through 5 without using any brakes. I knew I should be able to. I had the 400s in my sights and bent it into 5 on a neutral throttle. The pipe was scraping away and bang. Everything turned black.

I had leaned it over a bit much and levered the tires off the track. Lowside ensued. It happened in a blink and I really didn't have a chance to think about anything. Anyways, slide, tumble, slide, tumble etc. I got up and noticed every thing worked, ie feet hands etc. I also had a shooting fire in my right shoulder. I got away from the track, out to the woods. The corner workers were awesome, right there and helping me out. Ambulance a few minutes later. ER a little while later. Broken collarbone, sprained ankle and abraded right hand bird finger. Big pain untill I got "The Shot" thank you very, very much.

The bike is unbelievably good. The stock pipe is shot, scrape on master cylinder, right bar end is gone and grip is slightly torn, brake lever is broken and clutch lever is bent. The tach has some scratches and the speedo/tach housing has a crack. The front fender is slightly scratched on the leading edge. Not another scratch! Tank, frame, engine and everything else is perfect. I still can't believe how good the bike looks. I gotta thank my Evil Pumpkin racing teamates for coming to get me at the hospital and loading everything up, so I could get on the road Friday night. I got home to Rochester by 9:30 PM Friday. My wife saw me get out of the car with a crutch and clenched her fist so hard that she cut the palm of her hand with her own fingernails. I'm doing fine, although not as spectacularly as the Hawk. I already ditched my crutch even though the ER doc thought the sprain would not get better for 4-8 weeks. This sling thing my shoulders are in really help the pain, but sucks for typing and generally doing stuff. I've been totally checked out by all the Mayo docs on Monday and got the stamp of approval. If anyone has a pipe, stock or better, let me know. I'm also looking for any of the above broken bits.

Things I learned:

  1. Dragging your pipe is cool only to a certain point.
  2. Dragging your knee is cool only if it is not closely followed by your shoulder, head and butt.
  3. It's only practice on Friday, not racing, stupid.
  4. I've got to hang off way more.
  5. Riding in an Ambulance will dramatically increase your lap times.
  6. The driver [Ambulance] cornered like a rookie.
  7. It's really hard to eat french fries and drive one handed.
  8. I am a quick healer.
  9. Working at the Mayo clinic has some really nice benefits.



Team Lawrence Won't Return for the '98 Season!

Rochester, MN - Breaking story from Team Lawrence, a subsidiary of Evil Pumpkin Racing. Team Co-owner Jim Lawrence released this statement: "Do to several circumstances beyond our control, we will not be fielding a race team in the 98 season. We have gone through a most thrilling, yet troubled season and at this point, our fledgling racing effort has been dissolved". Team Lawrence's inaugural 97 season was marked by both dispare and promise.

The team posted several back marker finishes in the Central Roadracing Association's Lightweight class. The team ran a stock Honda NT 647 as their flagship platform. The combination of rider Jim Lawrence and bike was just not up to the task of running competitive times. However, in the last race weekend, the team seemed to be on the verge of finding some speed, but the weekend and season was brought to a grinding halt as Lawrence lowsided himself into the St. Joseph's Emergency room. When asked for a statement for this article, lead rider Jim Lawrence, had this to say: "Well, I'm really upset. It looked like we were starting to get some things sorted out and I was begining to figure out how to get through some corners. The team had talked about getting some performance bits for the bike over the winter and coming back really strong next year and now this happens. Well, it's just disappointing. I don't really know what's going on with the owners or anything. They all seemed so supportive and upbeat about the whole season. I guess I had heard some minor disagreements about the direction that the team should take and maybe some resourses needed to be allocated differently, but no one on the team suspected it would come to this."

Crew chief and mechanic for Team Lawrence, Jim Lawrence, had this to add: "Yeah, what Jim (lead rider) said is pretty accurate. No one saw this coming at all. I had the bike in the garage and was just starting to go over it and get going on finding some horsepower. We were looking at pipe and jetting combinations and maybe some body work for next year. Then, just like that, I got the word that the team was going to be broken up. I don't know what's ahead for me."

Rumor swirling about the paddock is that the two principle owners of the team could not work our some differences about funding for the 98 season. It seems that there were two different ideas about the level of finacial as well as personal involvement that should be put into the team. The Co-owners were apparently unable to overcome these differences and continue to field a team. All team members were release from their contracts so that they could find equitable postitions with other teams or in the fast food service industry.

Team Evil Pumpkin Racing Saddened by Lose of Team-Mate

Burnsville, MN - The shocking news earlier this week by Team Lawrence has left a huge gap in the emensly popular Team Evil Pumpkin Racing (EPR) team's '98 roster. Team EPR is unsure how it will affect the overall performance of the team and it's championship drive for the much anticipated '98 race season. "We thought Jim was well on his way to finding the fast line around the track. Jim was putting faster and faster qualifing times at the end of the '97 season and we where sure that his crash would have broken what little of a minor mental barrier was left and allowed Jim to really lead the team in '98.", said Rich Nelson ( Team EPR Spokesman and CRA racer #194 )

The crash in question left rider Jim Lawrence with a broken collar bone and very minor damage to his SuperSport prepared race bike. The damage done was said by Team Lawrence mechanics to be easily repaired to race form with the stock pipe being the most damaged part. Other racers and mechanics in the area commented that the damaged pipe was in such a condition that rider Jim Lawrence would have been able use it and gain the inch of clearance that caused his dramatic crash during a blistering practice session.

Editors Note: Team Lawrence will be missed ( especially the beer ) and if Team Lawrence wants to come back and race the super fast GS-500 of rider Rich Nelson for a few races of the '98 season...rider Jim Lawrence will be welcome to do so.
