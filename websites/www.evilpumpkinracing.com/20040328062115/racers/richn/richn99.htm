First Race Weekend: May 7th - 9th

Overall...EPR had a good weekend. My old bike ( I sold it to the new EPR team-mate Brian F ) had some mechanical problems, but Brian was able to complete the school and get his license. He's already got an offer from one of the local GS-500E tuners to help him get it working before the June race weekend. 

Friday sucked...too much rain and cold. I only got out for two practice sessions and that was on my 207 Street tires. I managed to get some 2:01's so I was happy.

Matt Hanson on his 98 GSX-R600 didn't have any problems and is looking forward to the June weekend. He's running in the 2:10's...and he's "excited" to realize how much room he has improvement for. :-)

Todd Poindexter ( Expert #84 ) on his R-1 had his front-end wash out on him in Turn 4 during Saturday practice, but suffered no damage. Frame sliders and leathers work! He did catch a lot of shit for it though. :-)

Saturday, I put on my Pirelli slicks, PFM cast iron rotors & EBC HH pads. I got about 2 laps of practice. Just enough to scrub the tires and bed the pads & rotors. Having slicks I could only race the GP Trophy dash and it was the last race of the day. I had lots of time to burn.  So...I ran around and watched some of the Super Sport sprint race action.

My 14 ( cut from 17 ) lap Middleweight GP Trophy Dash was the last race of the day.   I got a lousy first ever start on the GSXR and was the last bike going into turn one. During the race, I managed to pass about 4 other slow experts then with about 5 laps to go...the first 4 fast novices buzzed past me ( darn upstarts! Don't they know that they aren't supposed to pass EXPERTS!? :-) ).  I did manage to pass fellow Expert, Pete from Metro Hitching Post on his GSX-R750 on the last lap in turn ten! I knew that there would be no point in passing him anywhere before the last turn. He went into turn 10 slow and I went in fast.  I passed him on the outside of turn 10 and had a good enough drive to almost beat him the 200 meters to the flag stand.   Fun! My times from the start of the race ( with a 2:03 ) to the end of the race ( 1:55 ) dropped by 8 seconds.

Sunday, I practiced going thru turns 1 and 2 faster and faster.  It wasn't as easy as I had hoped.  The survival instincts really kick in when you're going into a turn at 150 MPH!  I had a minor moment while doing my braking and downshifting for turn 3...I lost the bolt that held my shift lever.  I hit my brake marker, grab the brakes, tap down a gear, then goto tap down for the other two down-shifts and there is nothing there!  Do'h! Up comes the arm as I limp back to the pits in 4th gear. Replaced and lock-tited it's replacement!

I was in the slow practice group ( by choice ) and due to tons of pumpkins in the corners, my best time in practice was a 1:57.  Next race weekend, I think that I need to go with the fast practice to avoid all the pumpkins. My first 6 lap Sprint race was Heavy Weight SB ( 750's ) , bad start, but I'm getting better and most of the bikes on the grid were 750's! One lap was a low 1:55 that Corey timed as a 1:54. I think that was the lap that I got a sweet rear wheel power slide  coming out of turn 10. It felt pretty darn cool!  I did notice that I'm holding onto the bars too tight going thru 1 & 2, my right hand/wrist was so tired that by the end of the race I was worried about being able to hold on. 2nd race was MWSB, I still ended up behind everyone at the start. Having all those bikes funneling into turn 1 at 150PMH is a little intimidating. I just let my tires warm up and then started picking off the riders in front of me. My laptimer ran out of juice...so I have no idea what my laptimes were.

Last race, MWGP...I finally got a better start than at least one person. Eddie Karow #39 on his R6 dropped from the front row to almost even with me on the 4th row! I managed to keep close to the pack and pass one or two bikes going into turn 1. I kept it conservative in the first lap...then started picking up the pace and passing the people in front of me. With my right arm tired from the first two races...I worked on relaxing and learning more about my bike & the track. Eddie decided to pass me back on the front straight going into the second lap...so I just followed him for a lap then blew past him in turn one. I never saw him again. I passed a few more bikes. It was like being on the mighty GS-500E all over again. Pass in the corners and get passed down the front straight with the motors. I battled one expert for the lonely 21st spot and lost to him in turn 10 by going in too hard, losing my drive out of the corner and getting passed by him with his much better drive out of the corner. Lesson learned...again. :-) DbCom recorded an official best lap at the 1:54.3 mark. I was happy..mostly.  Now I need to drop another 5 seconds to even come close to a respectable finish.  I think that I can eek out a few more seconds in turn 1 and 2.  According to my trainer ( Pit Seed Ned ),   I'm not leaning over far enough.  He says that I  have about 3-4 more inches of ground clearance to work with.  Back seat riders!  :-)   I can also add more horsepower ( cha-ching! ) and pickup a second or two between the turn 9 to 10 straight and on the front straight.  

Next race is JUNE 25, 26 & 27 .   See ya then!

Work, Fun, Rain & Bad Gearing!  June 25th - 26th

Pre-Race Weekend Problems:

  1. Blown fork seal 1.5 weeks before race weekend. Ordered a new fork seal and while on the phone with the parts guy at Metro-Hitching Post…I decided to buy a Race Tech Gold Valve kit and stiffer springs. I was going to let the shop install them for me, but Steve "volunteered" to install them for me when he got to the track Friday morning.
  2. Fixed W.E.R. dampener…it was pretty easy and I felt good about my mechanical accomplishment.
  3. While mounting the dampener…I tried to torque the bolt to spec and I ended up pulling the thread right out of the lower triple tree fork clamp points on the left side.
  4. While installing the 520 chain kit:
    a) Didn't have a 32mm socket to take the front sprocket off.
    b) Had to buy one at Sears
    c) After solving that problem…I thought that the new chain was too long and removed a link.
    d) Chain wasn't too long, but now it was definitely too short.
    e) Had to buy new chain.

Friday:

    I spent the morning pulling my forks off so that when Steve got to the track, he could get right to work. Steve & Tood arrived around 10:30 am and Steve got right to work on the forks. He had the forks done in about 3 hrs. Yeah, Steve! J I re-installed them and put the front wheel back on. I also installed the new Performance Friction brake pads that Fred Renz @ http://www.yoyodyneti.com was kind enough let me try out. Not for free, but at a very good price. More on that later. Steve helped get the front and rear ride height dialed in.

    The bike was ready to go in time for the last two hours of practice on Friday. I took it out first to test the "new" front suspension and bed the new pads to the rotors. It was pretty good, but with the stiffer springs the front-end now tended to jump back up too fast. We added another ½ turn of rebound to slow it down. After making that adjustment… Steve took it out. Using the zip ties on the fork tube trick…we noticed that it was bottoming out to fast…so we also added 1 turn of compression to slow that down too. Taking turns, Steve & I ran the remaining practice sessions until 5pm. I ran a few 1:55's during practice and felt pretty good about the next day's racing.

Front is currently set to: 7 lines preload w/ .95 weight Race Tech springs, 1.5 turns from max compression, 2 turns from max rebound.

Saturday:

    Steve & I ran our 2 each practice sessions. I ran fast bikes, while he ran slow bike practice. My Hotlap Onboard laptimer decided to stop working. I replaced the 3 year old battery with a new one…but no luck. So…I didn't get very many laptimes. The few that I got said that I was running in the 1:55-1:57 range. It was during my last practice session that I had a close call. Those Performance Friction pads and PFM cast iron rotors really have some stopping power! I was braking hard on the entrance of T4 and that was causing the rear to chatter. After talking with Steve, he suggested using my rear brake just prior to dropping anchor on the front to help settle the chassis. That worked the first time I tried it at practice speed…so I tried it again the next time around at race speed. I think I used the rear too much. The rear chattered and hopped all over the place. The turn was coming up rather quickly so instead of really panicking and running off the track…I pulled the clutch in and just coasted to the apex…then eased the clutch out and continued on my way. I think that I need to work on that a little more before I try it again.

    Steve & I didn't know how the 15 lap Trophy dash races were going to be broken up for the MW, HW & UNL SuperSport & GP classes. I figured that they would run the MW Novices in the 2nd race and the MW Expert + HW & UNL in the 3rd race. We tried to play it safe. I signed up for the HW GP race and Steve signed up for the MW GP race. We guessed wrong. The MW SS Novice & Expert got the 2nd race, while the MW GP Novice & Expert got combined with the HW & UNL for the 3rd Trophy dash. We couldn't both ride the bike during the same race! Upon hands and knees we pleaded with Leslie to let us switch classes. Leslie took pity on us and Steve ended up being gridded in the 2nd race in the MW SS class, while I stayed in the 3rd race in the HW GP class.

    My first race of the day was the St. Jude's Benefit race. My $30 donation netted me a 3rd row grid position. I was looking forward to it, especially when I found out that Gareth Jones ( Chief CRA Riding Instructor ) was going to be participating in it. Prior to the race he "claimed" to be running in the 1:57 range, but I figured he was just sandbagging me. I told him that I'd just be happy trying to follow him around for a few laps.

    I got my usual crappy start and followed the pack thru T1 & T2. That's when I spotted Gareth. He dove under me in T1…school is in session! While following Gareth, I made a few passes then we both got hung up by #714 (John DiSanto) & #39 (Eddie Karow, my arch-nemisis). The smooth passer that Gareth is…he managed to get past them in no time. It took me a couple of laps before I was able to setup for a smooth pass. I got past John in T1. I was hoping to nick 'em both in T1 & T2, but I didn't feel like pressing the issue with Eddie in T2. So…thinking that I had plenty of time…I waited for a good pass opportunity on Eddie. Going into turn 3 it looked like Eddie was going to try and take slow tight inside line to T3…so I couldn't pass him there. However; I could back off and get a good drive out of T3 to stay close between T3 & T4. Going into T4, Eddie hit his brake marker. His marker was about 20 ft before mine so I streaked past him on the outside, then dove to the inside and rocketed out of the corner. During all this…Gareth had manage to gain about a 2 second lead…so I then worked on trying to catch him again. Came out of T10 and darn it…checkered flag! Just as the fun was beginning!

    While watching the Ultra-light SuperSport race from T10, I saw one rider lowside it in T10 and push another rider off the track. The pushed rider was heading straight for the T10 wall! I noticed that it was rider 256…Tate Guckin! Tate rode thru the gravel trap trying to slow down, while I was telling him to drop it before he hit the wall head on. From my angle it looked like he wasn't going to stop in time. He exited the gravel trap, ran across 10 more feet of grass, then hit the 20 ft wide access road before the wall and…stopped about 10 ft later. Collected his wits, turned right to get back on the track and rejoined the race. A few minutes later…the heavens opened up and rain poured onto the track. Tate finished the race and decided that racing in the rain, sucked!

    Steve & I got a little concerned about the rain. We only have slicks. Luckily, we had about 1.5 hours before Steve's first 15 lap Trophy dash. It stopped raining just before the 1st Trophy dash and the Sun came out again. The track dried and we were happy. Steve got a good start and had his first experience with T1 with 46 bikes all trying to occupy the same space at the 150 MPH! He kicked butt! He was in 7th place with 1 lap to go when he ran out of gas. He nursed the bike home and pitted on the white flag lap and still manage to eek out a 10th place finish.

    For my race, the sky was starting to look a little dark. Being on the grid with R1's, GSX-R750's & R6's…I got a crappy start. I wasn't last into T1, but I did have a R6 blow past me on the entrance to T2. On the 2nd or 3rd lap…just as I was entering T9, I noticed a rider highside it on the exit of T9. The bike was laying on the edge of the track and about 5-7 feet from the bike…there was the rider crumpled on the track. I had seen an R6's wheel just before T9…so I wasn't too sure where he was. I didn't want to cut sharply to the right and take the chance of hitting the R6 that might have been there. So, I took the safest course offered and cut between the fallen bike & rider. The R6 & I split the fallen rider between the two of us. Next time around, I'm happy to report that the bike was off the track and the rider was walking. However; while getting setup for T10 I noticed drops forming on my visor. Looking further ahead, I noticed that the track was a little shinier than usual. Crap! Rain! I promptly dropped anchor, tip-toed thru T10 and exited to the pits. Got to my pit and the sky opened up again. :-(

Sunday:

    We awoke to two sets of missing leathers! Crap! Mark's & Matt's leathers had walked off during the night. I checked with one of the last people that we chatted with prior to going to bed and she said she'd check with her "friends". It turned out that one of her "friends" had decided to make a midnight raid on or gear. She was very pissed & embarrassed about the whole thing. Bad guys got caught, while Mark & Matt got their leathers back in time to practice. Happy Ending!

    The weather was partly cloudy with a good tail wind going down the straight and a chilly 65 - 70 degrees. Perfect conditions! I got some good practice time, but Steve & Tate had some trouble dealing with the speed difference in the "slow" practice group. There were a few complaints about the speed differences and near misses between fast 600's and slow 500's. I think that the CRA needs 3 groups comprised of Ultra-Light/Lightweight in the Slow bike group, Middle Weight in the Fast & Heavy Weight/Unlimited in the Super-Fast group.

    Steve & Todd had to get back to the cities to catch a flight back to Texas so…Steve signed up for the first MW Superbike Sprint while I signed up for the HW Superbike & MW GP Sprints. Steve had a good race, but he didn't trust the patch in T10 and was taking a very wide outside line thru that turn. Todd & I cringed everytime he came thru the turn. I didn't even want to know what he looked like in any of the other turns! J He ended up finishing somewhere around 15th in a field of 46. Pretty good!

    For my first race of the day, I got a good start for a GSX-R600 in a race full of GSX-R750's. However; I was still last going into T1. I was turning 1:55's, but it wasn't good enough to even stay close to the 1:53's that the slow 750's where running. I could hang with 'em in the back section, but as soon as we hit the front straight…off they would go. For my second and last race of the day, MW GP, I got a good start and I even managed to beat a few bikes into T1! #407 ( Mike Boch ) flew past me going into T2 and I decided to try and keep up with him. I was doing pretty good…until we hit the front straight.

    I was running out of steam about 300yrds from the entrance of T1. I had the wrong gearing! I needed to drop back to the 16/46, but I didn't have a 46 rear sprocket to use and it was way too late. I would gain a second thru the infield…then loose two between T10 & T3…not fun!

Things to do before the next race weekend in July…

  • I NEED to buy some more gear selections.
  • The slicks are pretty much shot…so I need to buy new tires. I will probably get a set of those new Pilots.
  • Sign up for the Jason Pridmore STAR School when he comes to BIR this summer. Done!
  • Ride at Glen's Team Absolute Rider Practice sessions to get some more practice in.

Team Absolute Riders Practice Session (July 19th, 1999)

Prior to this event, I needed new tires, new fork seals installed ( $106 @ Metro Hitching Post ) and I spent $260 to enroll for the Jason Pridmore STAR school at BIR on Aug 2nd. So...in an attempt to keep my expenses lower...I opted to skip the July 16th-18th CRA race weekend.

I bought a set of the new Michelin Pilots Soft Compound from Mason Racing Tires and had him mount them while I was at the track spectating. I paid $352 for the set. It's kind of depressing to be spending all that money on race tires...and not be racing. Oh well...

After I got my wheels back on the bike, I checked the Michelin's diameter and compared them to the stock 207's measurements. I discovered that the Michelins would have the affect of raising the front 2mm over the 207's. So to get the bike back to "stock" geometry, I would need to drop the front by 2mm to set it to the stock config. Being a little lazy after spending the weekend at the track...I decided to leave it alone and see how it handled.

The track is only 3 miles from my house...so I left the headlight and taillight on the bike, but put my stock "race" side panels back on and rode the bike to the gas station. Filled the tank with some 93 octane and grabbed bottle of water ( it was hot ). Rode the remaining 2.5 miles to the track and signed up for the "Fast" group.

I went out with the intention of taking it easy and just riding to get some seat time in to evaluate the new Pilots. After the second lap, that all went out the window. The bike was handling like it was on rails! I was leaned over so far, I had my knee pressed to the side of the front plastic, just behind the frame slider, and I was still dragging my knee thru the corners! There were a few corners that it felt like I could have dragged my elbow...if I had thought to try. I don't know if that's the "correct" method or body position, but it feels good to me. Maybe Pridmore will have something to say about it in two weeks.

As much fun as I was having, I might have been going too fast for some of the other riders in the fast group. Even though I would remind myself that I wasn't racing and passing the bike in front of me wouldn't win me anything...I still "had" to get past the "slower" bike.

I didn't have too much trouble in the first session. I was still getting up to speed, figuring out what gear I needed to be in and finding my brake markers. Things got a little more interesting during the second session. I got out towards the end of the pack and it wasn't long before I caught up to a group of 4 riders struggling to keep up with Jessica on her red F3 ( #310 for her fans ). This was going to be interesting. How in the world am I going to get past these slower bikes in the corners without causing them or me to crash in the process?! They all had new F4's & R6's, which have more motor...so passing them on the straight was out of the question. The best I could hope for was a better drive out of the corner leading to the straight and for them to brake early for the chicane before turn 1 ( Knox Landing ). Not a good plan with that many bikes and unknown riders.

So...coming into T1, I go wide and carve in deep to pass the 1st bike...carrying the speed into the next turn I manage to blow past the 2nd bike on the outside. That kills a little speed so I have to wait a few more corners for the 3rd bike. I take the 3rd bike going into the waffle section in the bike, then carry the speed and pass the 4th bike on the outside between the two right-handers leading onto the back straight. This puts me behind Jessica going thru the cone chicane and sweeping right leading on to the back straight. I get on the gas early after I pass the cone chicane to get a good drive. The rear slides a little under power as I go past Jessica on the outside of the back straight sweeper. Fun!

I blast thru the off-camber left at the end of the back straight and rail thru the kinky hairpin leading onto the front straight. I look back and I notice that I've already pulled 2-3 seconds on Jessica and crew. I make one more good lap, but as I'm entering the front straight chicane, I loose concentration for a tick. Not good! I turned in too late for the left-right chicane. I got the left part, but I was too fast and to far to the right to have any prayer of making the right turn for the straight section before T1. I drop anchor and use the pit return road to slow down and enter pits. Pull bike into the pits and clean out leathers. :-)

Okay...time to slow down! I vow to take it easy in the next session. I can't toss the bike away before I have a chance to take the STAR school in two weeks! I also over hear some of the slower fast group riders complaining about all the "dangerous" passing going on. Not by me, but someone on a blue bike. #6 on his R6? Couldn't be me! My bike is Blue & White!

Third session, I try to take it easy...and I almost succeed. I get out with the first few bikes this time. This should allow me to have a few laps without running into any of the slower bikes. There are only three bikes spread out in-front of me. One of them is Mike on his blue FZR400, I shouldn't have any trouble getting past him in the straight, but the other two are racers on F4's. Me and the first F4 split Mike going down the back straight, but I'm behind and un able to pass the F4. I line up for a better drive out of the off-camber and use the drive to pull up beside him on the entrance to the kinky-hairpin. I kind of out brake him by using less brakes and carry more speed going into the kink part of the kinky-hairpin and get a good run thru the hairpin to leave him to far behind to challenge me before the front straight chicane. I rapidly run down the first F4 in the back section, but he's not making any mistakes or leaving me any openings that look big enough and safe enough to pass. I set up to get a good drive leading onto the back straight and manage to blow past him when he braked for the off-camber. Using much less brakes and carrying more speed them him...I pass him on the inside before the off-camber. It was a pretty easy pass and I went in & out so much faster then him, that by the the time I got thru the kinky-hairpin he was 2 seconds behind already. Did I mention that the bike felt like it was on rails with those Pilots?

For the 4th and my last session, Glen came over to tell us to slow it down and not do so much race-like passing. Everyone pointed their fingers at everyone else and I had Jessica point her finger at me! Ouch! Okay...I'll take it easier this time...really! Well...that was the plan anyway. Boyd Whiteoak (CRA Expert #357) on his 900RR, gets out of the gate in front of me and behind a 2 or three "slower" riders. Boyd carves his way past the first bike in his path and the proceeds to run really fast. I managed to hang with him...until we hit the front straight. That 900RR is fast! I did manage to catch some slower riders again...but I tried to go easy on them and not loose too much time in the process. I passed one guy on a R6 by getting a better drive out of the left water tower turn that leads to the back "waffle" section ( labeled "Defensive Driving" on the map ).

After I had pulled in at the end of the session, I was told that the State Trooper that has been hanging out and watching the fun...wanted to get some radar speeds on the three fastest guys. I somehow managed to get picked. It was Fryer, Whiteoak & I that got to take a few extra laps to get a top speed run down the front straight. I didn't hear the "few laps" part and thought it was just one lap. After just finishing a 10-12 minute session, my arms felt like rubber for the first few corners. So, I'm thinking..."take it easy...this is just a fun lap. Don't worry about it until you get to the kinky-hairpin." I'm just starting to get back in the groove and I'm looking forward to railing thru the kinky-hairpin to get a good drive down the front straight for the nice police officer. However; I blow the entrance into the hairpin and get a horrible drive out of the turn and onto the straight. Nothing I can do about that...twist throttle and go thru the gears! I figure that since I don't "need" to make the right part of the chicane... I can grab an extra gear instead of backing off for the left part of the chicane. I manage to get radared at 106mph. Fryer got 118mph and Whiteoak got 124mph. I do the left and drop anchor to return to the pits. Whiteoak managed to run wide in the chicane that lap, but even though he was leaned over at 100+ MPH and in the grass/gravel...he still managed to "make" the turn and do the remaining top-speed laps. Everyone watching from the pits thought his line thru the chicane that time was pretty impressive. I'm guessing that I could have got up to 115mph on the second lap...if I had known that we had 3 laps to play with. Oh well...it was a fun night.

If you haven't gone to it and are local to the Twin Cities...I highly recommend that you try one of Glen's Team Absolute Rider Practice sessions. There's much less race style passing going on in the slower groups...really! :-)

Jason Pridmore's STAR Motorcycle School @ BIR - Aug 2nd 1999

Summary:  Good school with plenty of track time.  The instructors are friendly, knowledgeable and willing to share their knowledge.  I highly recommend the school for Advanced street riders to Novice 1st or 2nd year racers,  but find it lacking for an expert level club racer on their home track or higher skill level racers. Provides a good review of the basic skills that most experienced racers "should" already know and be working on trying to improve.  If you are a fast local racer...seek out Lance and get him to follow you around...if he can't help you...it's time to a) arm wrestle Jason to get him to come out and rip you apart,  b) enjoy the track time and listen to the classroom sessions to see if there is anything new for you to learn or try or c) angrily demand you money back.  I recommend "b".  Jason looks pretty fit and anger is so counter productive!  :-)

Long Version: I was looking through RRW&MT earlier this year and I glanced at the racing schools section to see if their were any coming to BIR this year.  I noticed that the STAR school was scheduled to run there in August...following the AMA pro race there.  Flash forward a few months...I'm now a CRA Expert,  in license,  on a much faster bike from my previous Novice years.  I'm feeling a little low in the confidence department.  Prior to the first race weekend, I'm worried about turn 1 and turn 2.  Am I going to be able to go through them fast enough?!  

I solved that problem...but then the problem of..."Yikes!  I'm going fast and here comes turn 3!"  Brakes!  The GSX-R600 is SO MUCH faster than the GS-500E that I used to rail thru the infield with.  I manage to get my laptimes down to 1:55's by the end of my first weekend.  I'm feeling pretty good about that,  but I need to drop them another 5 seconds to even come close to the front and 10 seconds to have a chance of winning.  

Second race weekend comes and I can still only manage 1:55's and a few 1:54's.  Talk about a real downer in the confidence department.  What am I doing wrong?!  ( Besides gearing...see Work, Fun, Rain & Bad Gearing!  June 25th - 26th )   Time to research the Jason Pridmore STAR School.  I notice that www.motorcycle.com has a STAR school article...so I read it and I am glad to find out that the school is broken into Advanced street and race orientated class sessions. Cool!  I can get some help in the going faster while racing department at my home track.  A win/win scenario. 

Anyway...in the month and a half prior to the school,  I have plenty of time to analyze my poor performance.  Part of my problem was with the bike,  my gearing...that's easy to fix.  The other problem is with the out right speed coming into some of the faster corners.  After somehow conning Fred Renz @ www.yoyodyneti.com out of some PFM cast iron rotors & Perfomance Friction pads...I know that I've got very good brakes. Yet, I'm still braking too early.  I'm also not looking far enough ahead, which relates to the "braking too early" problem.  I'm also not doing any trail braking and I'm not very relaxed on the bike.  Hmmm...I can work on that.   

I decide to scrub the new Pilots & sort out the suspension in at Glen's Team Absolute Rider Practice session on Aug 19th.  I also want to work on looking further ahead and trying to keep the death grip ( relaxing ) on the bars to a minimum.  I end up having a real good session ( see Team Absolute Riders Practice Session (July 19th, 1999) ) and I'm able to work on most of my issues.  However; now I have some new ones.  Like am I hanging off too much...not enough...with bad body position.  Is there something else that I need to work on?  Help!  :-)

So...with that confidence boost under my belt,  I'm feeling pretty psyched about going up to BIR, watching the racing and attending the school on the 2nd.  I had also managed to talk Dave and Nina into going to the school...so I knew that there would be a few friends up there with me.

7am Aug 2nd: Dave, Nina & I arrive at the track, unload and fill out the many waivers.  We get our bikes through tech inspection.  They check to make sure your tires have tread,  your brakes have pad left on 'em and that the bike appears mechanically sound.  After tech,  everyone gets called up to the classroom for an introduction to the instructors and the rules for having a good time.  The biggest rule is "No passing in the corners",  but the "race" group might have that restriction lifted in the afternoon.  

The entire class is then taken out for a few laps to familiarize everyone with the track.  Jason then stops everyone in Turn 1 to explain how the turn works and show everyone how much banking there is.  When you're on the bike...the banking doesn't seem all that great, but when you actually stop and look...there's a pretty good angle there.  Jason then took us out to turn 3.  He told everyone about his reference points for the turn and explained his preferred line thru the turn.  He recommends coming into turn 3 closer to the middle of the track instead of high and wide like you would think.  Due to the fact that the turn is more than 90 degrees,  a traditional wide entry is only going to get you passed on the inside.  Jason then had us all follow him out to turn 9 where he also went over the unique characteristics of the "Bus Stop" turn.  Then we all headed into the pits to split the street group and the race group.  The street group went to the classroom, while the race group played follow the instructor while they showed us the "line" around the track.  After about 2 laps,  my low fuel light came on.  Damn!  I knew that I should've filled up before I went out,  but in my excitement...I forgot.  Oh well,  I stayed out for a few more laps,  then signaled that I was going in and pointed to my tank to let the instructor know why.  Unfortunately...everyone behind me...followed me into the pits.  Do'h!  Sorry guys. 

First classroom session,  Jason goes over relaxing and breathing.  The theory being,  that if you're not relaxed or breathing right...you're working too hard on making the bike go where it wants to go anyway.  Just relax & breath in thru your nose and out thru your mouth is what Jason advocates.  I've been working on relaxing on the bike since last year...trust me...it ain't easy!  However; it DOES work.  I ended up trying to work on relaxing and breathing during the first session.   My problem is that I can relax...but then I'm just limping around the track...so before the 2nd lap was done 219 on his GSX-R750 rockets past me on in the front straight. Full speed ahead and rocket thru turn one like I'm used to doing.  I manage to keep him in sight and gain thru the back section,  but after a few more times down the straight...he pulled away from me.  Plus,  about every 3-4 turns...I would remind myself that it wasn't a race...don't push it to keep up with him.  I didn't feel like I was learning or trying anything after the first session...so I figured that I had better stop trying to go fast and just go out in the next session and "play". 

That decision was re-enforced during the next classroom session when Jason went over turn 3 again in more detail and we talked more about lines,  body position and steering techniques.  I took the 2nd track session a little slower.  I just relaxed...the whole time and just went out and had fun.  I was still going at a fairly good clip and doing so allowed me to notice that I could relax in turn one.  I'd relax and focus on my breathing down the front straight...then inch over on the seat to the right,  give the handle bars a tug to pitch the bike into turn one...then just relax and let the bike carve thru the turn.  No effort needed after the initial steering input is completed,  until the exit where you have to "tell" the bike to straighten itself up for the small straight going into turn 2.   I did that for every turn.  Breath, steering input, relax, breath.  After the session was over,  Garath told me that he had timed me at a 1:55.  Hmmmm...a 1:55 and I wasn't even trying.  I wonder what will happen when I start upping the pace?

I was hoping that an instructor or two would be showing up next to me on the track to "show" me what I'm doing wrong and provide some feedback on how to correct it.  No such luck.  I guess I'm too fast and/or  too squidly for most of instructors at BIR.  In their defense,  they were running street compound tires and none of them had ever been around BIR at speed ( except for Jason & Lance ). I didn't find that out until it was too late.  I finally noticed that the two guys "pitted" next to me were getting help from Lance.  Seeing how they had AMA number plates...I figured that they knew him and had worked out a private deal with him.  Hindsight being 20/20...I should have wormed my way into their "group".  Lance seemed to be providing them with some good info and feedback.   I decided to hunt him down after lunch.  However;  that plan went to hell when I was reminded that he would be taking over the classroom sessions while Jason took people out for 2-up rides around BIR.  Again...with hindsight...maybe I should've signed up for one of those rides.  

I ended up talking to Garath over lunch about what I was doing or not doing...he was able to help me work out my T7 gear problem and provided some good advice for turn 2.  The session after lunch...I hunted down Mark? Gullardo? on his TL-1000R and asked him to follow me around to see what I might need to work on.  I took it easy the first 2/3 of the lap to warm up the tires then picked up the pace.  I lost him by the time we got back to the front straight.  Slow down...let him catch up and I get passed by Abbot? on his yellow GSX-R600 and a student.  Then the Tl goes by...so on the gas I go.  I pass them both before turn 3 then almost lose it trying to look behind me in T9 to see if anyone was still behind me.  Way back there is the Mark's TL...darn.  Give up and run my own pace. for the remainder of that session.  

I did have a small moment towards the end of that session.  As I'm coming down the front straight...I notice a bike pulling out onto the track at pit exit.  "Please don't drop down...please don't drop down".  He starts to drop down as a close the distance with 165 showing on the speedo.  Do'h!  It's Jason w/ a passenger.  Double Do'h!  He does leave some room on the inside. However; just to play it safe and not piss Jason off...I decide to back off and leave both him and the passenger room by timing the pass for the small straight between one and two.  Clean pass with 10-15 feet to spare then drive to turn two.  Due to the pass...I blip the throttle on the down shift just before I pitch it in ( vs. a second prior like I normally do ) and I immediately start getting on the gas. ( Per Garath's lunch time bench talk )  Jason's behind me and I'm pulling away fast...almost too fast.  The rear slips a little and I'm sure that it left a nice long rubber mark for Jason to see.  1) I was relaxed...so I had no trouble dealing with the slide but, 2) did I have to do that right in front of Jason?!  That reminds me to slow the F down and by the time I get back to the front...session over.  Time to hunt for a faster instructor.

I ask Lance who he recommends and he tells me to talk to Jason's "Uncle" ( can't remember the name..sorry) on the Blue & White GSX-R750.  On the way out Gullardo? on the TL-R meets me at my pit and tells me that I looked good,  but there was no way he could even hope to keep up with me.  He did mention that it looked like I might be a little tense under braking cause my head was shaking up and down. I think that he said that it was in Turn 3 that he saw it.  Dunno...I'm thinking it might have been Turn 9 when I tried to look behind to see where he was,  then missed my shift.  Oh well,  nice guy and it was nice of him to follow up on it.  As I was getting on my bike, he stopped Jason's Uncle on his way over.  I'm sure that he warned about how nuts I was!  :-)  He probably just asked him how his day was going,  but the "nutter" version is much more entertaining.  

He had some trouble keeping up too.  Mostly in T1 and T2.  His front would push a little...so I ended up losing him too.  However;  Eric #358 on his R-6 decide to be there for me to play with.  I had some fun.  I would pass him...then he would pass me.  Mostly by drafting down the straight or out braking me into turn 3.  He did a draft pass going into T1, but I just took the high fast line and passed him right back.  I think that we was a little surprised about that. He would get me under braking going into T3,  but I could easily take him later on that lap.  I lost him for a couple of laps,  then just as I was all nice and complacent,  he zoomed past me under braking into T3 again.  Surprised me so much, that I missed my down shift!   Time to play again!  He pulled out a few bike lengths on me in traffic that lap and then more down the straight and just as I was starting to reel him back in...times up.  :-(  We talked about it after words and he said that he was behind me and tried to pass me on the exit of turn 2,  but I never slowed down like he was expecting me to...I was actually pulling slowly away from him!  He more than made up for by the pass in turn 3 though...oh well.  Good fun and lots of track time.  I just wish that I had some lap times and better instructor feedback.  Jason's Uncle did track me down later to tell me that from the way I pulled away from him that I looked pretty smooth to him.  

That's one thing that I think that the school should do...get the DbCom system or similar to track the student's lap times.  That way they could "see" and "feel" their improvement gains.

We get to the track area about 10 minutes later...now where in the world is the entrance?! After driving around for another 5 minutes trying to find the way into the pits...we arrive.

    We quickly register and then wait for Steve & crew to show so we can go out to dinner. It's a little windy and it's beginning to feel a little cold now. Hmm...I'm from Minnesota...no problem! Todd, Matt & I play macho and give the Texans a hard time about being cold. After we go out to dinner and come back to the track...it's really cold out now. Okay...time to put my sweater on as the Texans break out the parka's. Some people! :-)

    Next morning comes way too soon and we begin the mad scramble to get our bikes teched and the pits setup. We are still getting things setup when they call the first practice session. Matt goes out first on his bike, gets to turn 2 and crashes. I then go out on my bike and tip-toe around the track for the first 3-4 laps. The tires take forever to warm up! I'm just beginning to get the tires warm and having fun and the session ends. Then Todd goes out then we get Matt to try it again, but on my bike...with the tires still warm. He goes out and lowsides in Turn 9,  again...the first lap. :-( On my bike! crap! The brake reservoir is broke, the instrument cluster bracket broke again, & the speedo lens cover has hole in it. We pull the brake reservoir off of Todd's street GSX-R750, drill holes in the bracket and safety wire it together and a piece of clear tape fixes the speedo lens. I go out again and the bike is just fine.  While we are doing that,  Todd & Matt talk me into taking Matt's bike out for some practice.  Ummm, okay.  I take it out and don't even do one lap.  I didn't crash and the bike felt the same as mine, but I had no confidence in his brakes.  The lever felt a little mushy and when the brakes did grab...they grabbed hard.  They felt like they only had one setting...on & off.  I didn't enjoy it at all.  I pulled in with out even completing a lap. Todd and Matt looked at me kind of funny...guess I'm spoiled.   I sure do appreciate the Performance Friction Pads & PFM cast iron rotors now!  After riding my bike...Matt wants get a set too.

Race time!

    About 30 minutes before the race Steve decides to take his bike and turn it around.  He drives it up an access road leading into the pits and goes to turn around.  I guess he wanted to play a little and he gave it some gas as he was turning...the engine hit a power spot and the rear wheel broke loose.  Down Steve goes...crashes...without even starting the race.  He breaks a bone in his hand, sprains his ankle and breaks the left side foot peg.  Now he can't race cause his hand quickly swelled up and he didn't bring his extra foot pegs.  Again...Todd's 750 saves the day.  So he borrows the rear-sets from Todd's 750 and directs the able bodied and non-crashing members of the Evil Pumpkin South team with the repairs.

    They made the start and we almost didn't.  Todd went to the bathroom with about 5 minutes before the start.  Not a bad thing at BIR where the bathrooms are only a 50 yards away from the starting line.  Topeka has the bathrooms about 3-5 hundred yards away from the start line.  I take the bike out to the pre-grid for Todd, waiting...they let everyone go for the warm up lap...waiting.  Finally...here comes Todd...running all the way from our pits to our pre-grid position.  I would have taken the bike up to him sooner...but I had the bike stand with me and I didn't want to just leave it laying in the middle of the track.  Can't ride the bike and carry the stand at the same time.  I finally start to feel bad for Todd and move the bike 100 yards closer to him...wasn't his fault that Topeka has the bathrooms so far away from the pits.  :-)

    We where gridded in the 16th row and second wave for the start. Todd gets as good of a start as he can and as he gets a few more laps under his belt,  he starts cutting seconds off his laptimes until he hits the 1:38/1:37 mark.  He bounces off 1:37 a couple of times then settles for a 1:38 constant laptime.  After running the bike until the low-fuel light comes on, he brings the bike in for Matt.  We top off the tank and off Matt goes.  Seeing how Matt still hasn't completed a lap...we are all a little nervous about that.  He does one lap...then on his second lap...Red Flag!  Back in the pits, we are a little worried.  We're all hoping it wasn't Matt that brought out the red flag.  Come on Matt...all the bikes are coming in...where are you?  Ah..he makes it!  

    For the restart, we find out that we are now in the 4th row!  Hot damn!  We're doing pretty darn good for never having been to the track before.  Matt's a little worried about doing the restart so he talks Todd into doing the restart.  The green flag drops and as we watch the bikes go by...Todd is in 5th place!  Wow!  Maybe Todd should just stay out there!    Playing with the leaders, he does one extra lap and turns the bike over to Matt.  With the tires all warm and a full tank of gas,  Matt goes out again.  

    We have one little scare a few laps into Matt's session.  While timing him, the watch gets close his lap time and no Matt.  What happened!?  Maybe we missed him...please...let that be the case.  Luckily it was and even better...we missed him because he had dropped 5 seconds off his previous laptimes.  Matt ended up running a safe and consistent 1:45.  He brings it in a little early, we top her off and now it's my turn!

    My goal is to run the same times as Todd.  However; I'm only expecting to run 1:40. After about 8 laps I break the 1:40 barrier and quickly settle into the 1:38 times.  Yippie!  I can't give a lap by lap account and I don't think anyone would care to read such a punishing account anyway, but I do remember a few high-lights.  

    I remember passing a lot of people...that made me feel good.  I rarely got passed and when I did it was by a 750 or R6 down the front straight. Well...except for Sean Mowery on his FZR-400.  We played for a few laps.  I could almost keep up with him in the infield and pass him down the straight.  We did that for a few laps...then we hit some traffic and he checked out on me.  Darn FZR-400's aren't supposed to be that fast! 

    I passed Derrick, Derek & Josh a lot.   Steve's 750 is slow!  :-) I think that the best pass on them was while passing Derrick in Turn 9? ( not sure how the turns are numbered, but it was the one just before the bridge )  I went by him on the outside and if I wish I had a picture of it...cause from my angle it would have been a real nice shot.  Both of us leaned over, side by side.  Way cool!   

    I got passed by #10 on a 750 or something.  He went by me down the front straight like I was standing still!  However; I caught and passed him in turn two.  I was behind him and 2 other riders going into T2 when the other two riders in front of us decided to lock handle bars.  It looked like #10 was going to try and go around them on the outside which kind of got him stuck behind the mess. I was able to dive under all three of them.  I kept #10 behind me until the front straight...then just as I was wondering where he was...ZOOM!  A good 20MPH faster,  easy!  Wow,  what a motor...good rider too.

    We ended up finishing 4th in our class and about half a lap down from the 3rd place finishers in our class.  ( 18th over all. )  We did have one of our competitors come over and "complain" about us lapping his team.  He wasn't very happy when he was told that it was our first time here too.  Ah...brings a smile just thinking about it...then I remember #10 & Sean on his FZR-400....keeps the ego in check.  Can't wait until next year!
