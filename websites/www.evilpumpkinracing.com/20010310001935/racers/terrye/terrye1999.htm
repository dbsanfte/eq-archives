1st of the Season

Before Crash After Crash

This past weekend was the first race of the season for CCS in the Southwest Region in Phoenix. I had a new paint job and the bike was feeling great. (I know, new paint = wreck waiting to happen)

To make a long story short, I was running in second in the heavyweight amateurs, 25 minutes into a 30 minute race and crashed at a little over 100 on the back section sweeper. Front tire completely worn out. I've never worn out a set of tires that fast. Only had one race weekend on them. Found out later that I was running 2 seconds a lap faster than I ever had before on that track.

It was a very long slide - didn't even touch my new helmet. (You have a long time to think about what's going on sliding this fast, I picked up my feet and spread out my arms to let the suit take it) Not a scratch on me. Couldn't start the bike, and got a ride back to the pits in the truck. Found an oil leak where it ground through the starter cover. Borrowed one from a friend and replaced tires at the track and was ready for the next day (I thought).

One lap into practice on Sunday, bike was smoking, pulled off. Tightened everything up and drove around the pits revving bike. Couldn't find any more leaks. Back out for tire scrub, 2nd lap, taking it pretty easy rear came around to where my front should have been - quickly noted that this was not how I usually take this corner. Hit the ground pretty hard on the other side of the paint (naturally). Oil all over the bike and my leathers. One gash on my little finger and a severely bruised ego and my race weekend was over. Wasn't exactly how I had it planned, but ...,

 

First Non-wrecking weekend in 6 months

So, I took off all day Friday to wait for the UPS guy to deliver a stabilizer from Rick Mancinelli. Thanks Rick, Couldn't have made it without it. I was going to leave work early anyway to make sure everything was ready for the race on Saturday. I had an oil leak that developed from my last get-off in January - a long slide at 110 ground through my starter cover and into the engine block. I had to get it welded up and apparently it was just a hair too high on the weld because I tried 4 times to get it to seal with different types of gasket materials and RTV. Thought I had it fixed until I started the bike up Friday afternoon and noticed the ever-so-slight drip. Ripped it all apart at 5:30 on Friday and filed the welded part down ever so slightly, as not to then make it too low. Used RTV to seal it up and loaded it, untested to take to the track early Saturday.

Got to the track early Saturday, they direct us into the pits by the drag boat lake at Firebird instead of our normal spot. No big deal. We get parked and unloaded. I'm a little anxious about if the bike is going to leak, so I try to start the bike right away. RRR RRR Rrr, nothing. Now the battery's dead, or so I thought. Just my luck. I jump the bike from the truck and it won't start with the truck battery. Started the truck and tried again. Bike fires up, Oh thank you Racing Gods (RG), just one more favor - don't let it leak oil - please.

Out on the track and I'm taking it pretty easy, getting off onto the hot pit lane every few laps to check for oil. Nothing yet. Kept doing this through all of the practice sessions, then after practice, I dropped the lower to do a thorough inspection. No leaks - thank you RG.

Everytime I have to start the bike I have to enlist a couple of mules to push me around the pits in hopes of an easy start. Never is, takes at least 2 people, just past the verge of puking from over exhaustion to finally get it going. Found out later that it was the starter, when we took a known good battery and put it on the bike and got the same result. I must have somehow caused the starter gears to bind when putting the cover back on.

I have a new set of Vanson leathers, but I'm hesitant to put them on until I'm sure the oil leak is fixed - I realize this is really stupid, seeing how I've fallen off 7 times in my old leathers and they're torn up pretty badly, and the new ones would offer much superior protection, in case of that fall in oil, but still I wear the old ones anyway so my wife won't kill me when I get home and tell her that not only did I wreck the bike - AGAIN, but I also wrecked my new leathers. Luckily, it was all for nothing, I didn't fall off.

I took a fourth in Amateur GTO on Saturday, a 2nd in Amateur Unlimited Supersport, a fifth in Heavyweight Superbike and at the end of the day, about 4:30, I got a 6th in the Amateur Heavyweight Superbike. Note at this point that just for the hell of it, I pressed the starter button and the bike started. My mules were greatly relieved. In the last race, the leaders got away from me and I got stuck behind some other guys and once I made it around them I was about half of the front straight behind. The race was 6 laps into a 9 lap race and this was the first time I've ever made the conscious decision to just let them go. Before, I would have tried to chase them down and risked those nasty falls that have been plaguing me for awhile, but this time I decided that my position (6th) wasn't in jeopardy and I was too far behind and way too tired and hot to try to put in the kind of effort required, so I just took it easy and decided to take it home in one piece.

Just for me, taking home the 6th in that last race was fine, rather than falling off fighting for 5th or 4th. I guess there's something to be said for riding smarter. I'm going to have to try it more often.

Just one question, since these were my very first pieces of wood, do I value them at all of last year's costs and call them $3,000 trophies, or is the first one the $10,000 trophy and all the rest $5 pieces of wood ? Is this what its all about ? Racing side by side swapping paint at 110, just for a lousy $5 trophy ? Yeah I guess it is, and it was great.

Stopwatch Error

Well my pit crew (wife and friends) were having trouble getting lap times on me this weekend at Firebird. I have a pretty precise internal clock that was telling me I was going WAY FAST, but when I got back to the pits, I was informed that I was only doing 1:14's-1:15's. The really fast guy was going mid 1:08s and 1:09s. 1:10 is pretty fast, so I figured I was at least 1:12s-1:13s. Well after looking at the stopwatch, it was obvious that it was at least 3 seconds slow. Another guy timing me confirmed it, his was off too, by about the same 3 seconds.

So, I came up with this procedure for my wife to make sure that my times were more consistent. I said, now wait until I pass that cone, then start the stopwatch, now keep an eye on the stopwatch and as soon as it gets to mid 1:12s hit the lap button, don't worry that I haven't passed yet, just keep your eyes on the stopwatch so that my times can be consistent, then once the bikes come off the track, go ahead and hit stop.

This procedure has considerably made my times faster and faster and I no longer have to worry about that "stopwatch error". Hope this helps anyone else in the same situation.

If you ever want to find out who your true friends are, call them from 220 miles away at 5:30 at night and ask them to come and get you. All in all it could have been worse, but not much.

The Firebird War Zone

This past weekend was a CCS twin sprints weekend at Firebird International Speedway in Phoenix, Az. The carnage on Saturday was like a war zone. There were helicopters flying in and out like an airport. It was strangely reminiscent of the AMA fiasco at PIR.

Saturday, the races were on the small track (14 turn - 1.1mile) at Firebird. The first race of the day was the Unlimited Supersport/Formula Forty. I got a pretty good start, finally, and came out of turn one in third. I passed the guy in 2nd going through the left kink for turn 2. Chad Rollin was leading me by about 6 bike lengths into turn 3. I was on his rear tire by the time we hit the front straight. I passed him down the front straight and he passed me right back into turn one. We went around the track one more time me right on his rear tire, when a red flag came out. Two guys bumped into each other in turn 14 (last turn onto the front straight). As I passed them, both bikes were right in the middle of the track one guy was down and not moving the other was trying to give him a hand. Jim Davis was the guy down and he was helo'd out.

The race was restarted with original grid positions, same scenario as before same pass into turn 2 to put me in 2nd. Chad is still leading. I followed him around for a lap, then passed him down the front straight again. This time I took the inside knowing he was going to want to be there. Hey this was the first time I had ever led a race. I stayed ahead of him and as we started catching lapped traffic around lap 5, I was able to pull out about a 60-yard lead. About this time I saw Steve Peters, another Palo Verde guy that pits with us, on the ground coming out of turn 6. He was up and walking over to the hill so I assumed he was OK.

With 2 laps to go, I looked back going down the straight and Chad was still back there but about 60 yards away. Apparently I let off and caught some more slower traffic. White flag comes out and as I'm entering turn one, I look to the inside and there's Chad's wheel as he's passing me on the brakes. Damn it, I have no idea why I didn't take a defensive line that time, but I didn't. I hung right on his tail hoping for a good drive off the last corner to take him at the line, but another lapper got between us and he finished about a bike length in front of me. First time I had ever led a race and I blew it. 2nd's not so bad, but that ever-elusive first would have been nice. I was running consistent 1:06s. A very fast time is 1:03.

I came off the track and saw that everyone from our pit was gone. Steve's brother came running over and told me that they were going to fly Steve out. Apparently he didn't know his name or where he was. His helmet was pretty busted up. I made it over to the ambulance in teim to see them take him on the back board into the ambulance for transport. He was totally white and had gone into shock. Helo number 2.

A short while later, during a lightweight race, a guy was laying in the track near the turn 2 kink. I didn't see the wreck, but it was a strange place to go down. He wasn't moving. I didn't know him, but within 15 minutes there was another helicopter landing in the parking lot. Helo number 3.

I leaned it over and went wide, 15,000 people went by me and there was only 3 left behind me going out of turn 1 out of 24 bikes. Oh great. Now I have to pass everywhere just to try to catch up. I was goint butt-slow and, not to offend the females out there, but was "riding like a girl". A real old girl with a walker and oxygen mask. I managed to pull back up to 8th before they found pity on me and waved the checkered flag. I really need to pull my head out before the next race. It was getting really hot and my stomach wasn't doing too well at that point.

I was trying to hang out in the 180 degree shade, attempting to get rehydrated, when I noticed that the racing had stopped again. I was wondering what was up and wandered over to the fence.
