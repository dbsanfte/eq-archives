The Story of the Clan MacLear in Azeroth
- Recorded by Agravaine, Scribe of the Clan

So much has already been lost in the three Great Wars, and now the prospect of a fourth war fills me with sadness. In these dark times we welcome you who seek to join our ranks, you who seek to become MacLear. When you join Clan MacLear you become one of us, and as such it only seems fair to tell you where we come from. Sit down and order yourself a drink, this may take some time.

I suppose the best place to begin is at the beginning. Long ago, before the first war ravaged these lands, my people, the Clan MacLear, came to these lands from places far away. They came from lands covered by eternal winter, where the ground was always white with snow. It is said that their land was doomed, and so they tried to contact others of their kin for aid in the coming times. The two groups created a portal linking their homelands together so that they might better communicate, but something went wrong and their portal was redirected. My ancestors awoke on the shores of Crystal Lake in Elwynn Forest many years ago, thrown there by an unruly magic. Members of both communities had been thrown here, but only the humans came to Azeroth. Others of our Clan were thrown to other lands, but I get ahead of myself.

Finding themselves stranded in a new land, my parents and the rest of the Elders decided to make the best of their situation. They created a small village in the woods where they could live together in peace. They grew comfortable in their new home and began raising families. When those of my generation reached our teen years, we thought the stories of other worlds that our parents told were only fantasy, the kind of stories that all parents tell to their children. It wasn’t until I reached my twenties when I learned how true my parents’ story had been.

Now we come to the conflicts that have shaped MacLear into what it is today.

The Orc War

When I was still a young man, a sorcerer known as Mediv opened a Dark Portal and unleashed the orcs upon our world. The kingdom of Azeroth had the best army in the known world, but none were prepared for the fury of the orcs. When they came through the Portal, it was as though Hell itself had come to destroy us. No matter how many orcs we put to rest, there were always more right behind them. The struggle didn’t last long, a month, two at the most. The orcs washed over Azeroth like a wave washing over the shore.

Our Clan fought in a vicious battle at the pass between the Redridge Mountains and Elwynn Forrest. Aramus, one of our finest battle leaders, knew that this would be the last step before a siege of Stormwind itself, and led us with fierce determination that day. He spotted an opening in the orcish lines and pressed an attack. The orcs were surprised, and the band of MacLears reached and took Stormwatch Keep, where the orcish commanders had set their command post. The victory was short lived, however. Lord Lothar did not receive word of the attack until it was too late, so reinforcements were never sent. The orc numbers were simply to great and, not two days later, Aramus' band was driven out of the keep and broken on the shores of lake Everstill. If any survived from that expedition, it was only until that night when the orc cookfires were lit.

When he realized that the war had been lost, Lord Lothar gathered up as many people as he could and boarded them onto the few ships that remained in our fleet. I still remember how the flames from Stormwind Keep lit the sky as we sailed away, leaving our homeland behind. Not more than a few thousand of us escaped Azeroth. Many of my kin lay dead in the fields and forests of our homeland, cut down fighting a foe that some of our Elders remembered fighting back in their old world. We sailed northward on the Great Sea until we landed on the shores of the kingdom of Lordaeron. Lord Lothar met with their noble king and urged him to prepare for war. He told of the suffering and destruction endured by the people of Azeroth at the hands of the orcs. He also assured the king that the orcs would not stop with the destruction of Azeroth, they would be coming and unless these lands were prepared, they would be destroyed too.

The king took Lothar’s words to heart and made his people ready for war. He summoned to his side all the human kingdoms in the land, Dalaran, Alterac, Kul Tiras, and Stromguarde. The dwarves of Ironforge heeded the call to battle as orcish raiding parties began penetrating into Dun Morogh, and even the elves of Silvermoon came to join the mighty alliance as some of their elven scouts were rescued from the orcs by a small band of human fighters.

The armies of Lordaeron and the survivors of Azeroth led the Alliance to war and met the orcs on the shores of Lordaeron and the swamps of Loch Modan. The war raged for months, with great losses on both sides. Lord Lothar called for peace talks and the orc warchief agreed to talk. Those blasted orcs betrayed his trust! They ambushed him and his entourage, killing them all where they stood. After that, the Alliance fought with renewed vigor and pushed the orcs all the way back to the Dark Portal in the ruined lands that had one been Azeroth. Blackrock Spire was burned to the ground and the arch-mage Khadgar destroyed the Dark Portal in a terrible storm of magical energy. The orcs and their allies the ogres, trolls, and dragons were scattered to the winds.

It was during this war that MacLear met their kin among the dwarves of Ironforge and elves of Silvermoon. Our human Elders were not the only ones who had been sent to this world. Their stories of being allied with dwarves and elves, while seeming foolish to us when we were children, made perfect sense in the aftermath of the second war. Seamus, the Chief of the Clan, met with the dwarves and elves who claimed kinship with our Elders in the old lands. Sure enough, our Elders had spoken of many of them by name. Reunited with our ancient kin, the Clan MacLear grew and extended to include our kin among the dwarves and elves.

With our homeland restored to us, MacLear and the other survivors of Azeroth set out to rebuild. Some of our allies stayed to help us rebuild while others stayed to chase down the last remnants of the orcs. There was relative peace for several years.

Many orcs were captured and placed in prison camps, others escaped into the mountains and went into hiding. Guards kept constant vigil on the mountains should the orcs ever return to our lands. One day, the orcs did move again. They came down from the mountain in great numbers, attacked the camps and freed their fellows, Then stole ships from a nearby harbor and set sail for distant lands. Their sudden attack caught us off guard after so many years of peace and left us in little condition to give chase. A small fleet was sent after them, but most of the ships never returned home. Those that did claimed that there had been a horrible storm and most of the ships had been lost. We weren’t satisfied that the orcs had died, but we had larger concerns at the present time. There was still he matter of rebuilding Azeroth and raising Stormwind Keep from the ruins. To make matters worse, a new threat had appeared in the northern kingdoms, more specifically in the realm of Lordaeron. There was a plague spreading at an alarming rate, and those that fell by it rose up again as undead to ravage the land.

The War of the Scoruge

Lordaeron sent out a call to assemble the Great Alliance again. MacLear heeded the call of their old allies and joined the human nations, dwarves, and elves in battle once again. Arthas, the king’s son, let our armies to battle. The boy was a capable general at the beginning, but as it dawned on him that this was a battle he could not win, his sanity began to slip. He took to killing his own people in the streets before they could turn into ghouls. He sacrificed his own men so that he might hold back the tide a little longer. He even gave up his humanity to defeat what he believed to be the cause of the plague. The poor fool found out too late that the plague was only the beginning of a larger, more terrible attack on the world. His sanity shattered, Arthas turned against the Alliance and took up the cause of the undead Scourge. He killed his own father and brought the kingdom of Lordaeron to its ruin.

Jaina Proudmore took up the mantle of leadership for the Alliance forces, but Arthas’ power had grown too quickly. Every citizen and soldier that he killed bolstered his own ranks and under his driving attacks, the northern kingdoms of Dalaran and Silvermoon were utterly destroyed. The mages of Dalaran scattered as refugees to the remaining human nations, while the elves of Silvermoon suffered a far more crushing defeat. Arthas came upon them without their knowledge. They were completely unprepared for an attack on their homeland, and when Arthas ravaged their forest like a flash fire, none survived. The only high elves that survived the slaughter at Silvermoon were those few who were on the front lines of battle for the Alliance, trying to drive the undead back from the very gates of the last human kingdoms in the north.

Eventually, the undead overwhelmed the northern kingdoms, and the human survivors of the Alliance fled to the island nation of Kul Tiras or the southern kingdom of Azeroth. The dwarven and human forces of the Alliance made their stand in Loch Modan, guarding the few passes through the mountains with fierce tenacity and the undead advancement was held to the northern kingdoms.

It was about this time that Jaina had a strange vision. A man in a cloak told her to travel to a distant land across the Great Sea and speak with those who would become her allies in a still greater battle for the fate of the world. Jaina called for volunteers to go with her, and MacLear stepped up to the challenge. We brought with us out dwarven kin, and the few elves who had survived, and anyone else willing to come risk death.

When we were assembled, Jaina cast one of her spells and our band of warriors found ourselves in a dark yet strangely lush forest. Jaina instructed us to set up a base camp and wait for her return, then she vanished into the air again. When she did return, the things she spoke of were too much for belief. The orcs were here in these lands, and they were to be out allies in this coming fight! The orcs weren’t our only allies though, there was also a strange race of blue elves that were to fight beside us against the Scourge and their demonic leaders in this land. Jaina explained to us that the Scourge had been created by demons that were bent on the utter destruction of our entire world. All the races would need to come together to fight off this evil, or we would all perish in fire and be damned.

I don’t mind telling you that I wasn’t pleased at the idea of allying with the orcs! I had been fighting against them for the better part of my life and now we were expected to forget all the horror, the destruction they brought upon us and help them! But orders are orders, and Seamus agreed to follow Jaina’s plan. Our three forces met at the foot of a great mountain in the forest, the Elves guarded the one pass up the mountain, while the orcs and our forces guarded the forest. There was a horrible battle. So many good men died, yet I was surprised to see the orcs fighting just as fiercely against the advancing undead as we did. The elves provided support as well, ranks of archers appeared as from nowhere in the trees and showered the undead with arrows. Sadly, the undead were too much for us and we were eventually overwhelmed. Jaina gathered our last survivors and teleported us to safety far from the mountain. We watched from afar as a huge demon walked up the sloped of the mountain and touched a tree, one that the elves claimed held the spirit of the entire world. The elves had set a trap for the demon though, and as he touched its bark, the tree exploded in a torrent of flame and magical power. The demon was destroyed and the flames rolled down the mountain and burned the undead camp to the ground, taking nearly all the Scourge with it.

The expanding ring of fire, while destroying the Scourge, also destroyed the forest for miles around the sacred mountain, the homeland of these mysterious Night Elves lay in ruins. Having been allies with the elves in this last battle for the fate of the world, and knowing the pain of losing a home and having to rebuild, Seamus and the MacLears offered to stay and help the elves rebuild. As the months of rebuilding went on among the elves, the MacLears met still more who had come from the old lands of their Elders.

Brathia, a young elven huntress, had great knowledge of the Elders - far more than befitted her young age. The Druids of the Night Elves surmised that her parents had passed their knowledge on to her - a feat apparently not unheard of amongst the elves. Seamus liked her immediately, and doted on her as though she were his own daughter. She proved to be an invaluable source of knowldge - seeming to possess all the memories of the Elders - and comfort - helping the MacLears and the Elves to come together as would not have been possible without her.

While the fires had destroyed most of the Scourge, and their demonic leader Archimonde, not all of the Scourge had been destroyed, and several small bands of them still roamed through the ravaged woods. To make matters worse, the demonic essence of Archimonde had spread with the fires and tainted many of the forest creatures with its dark will. The MacLears took turns with their elven hosts patrolling the woods and destroying the last remnants of the Scourge in the elven lands. It was on one of these patrols that MacLear met with both terrible loss and great fortune.

The Chief and the Bloodstone

Seamus was leading a small band of MacLear, accompanied by an elven archer and priest, on a routine patrol of the nearby woods when they came upon something none of them expected. One of the last remaining Frost Wurms, reanimated dragons possibly the most powerful minions of the Scourge, attacked them from out of nowhere. As fate would have it, the beast attacked the priest first and wounded the man badly, taking him out of the fight. Seamus and the rest of the MacLear charged at the beast when it landed as the archer let loose with her arrows into any exposed places she could find. The battle was long and intense, and MacLear eventually brought the beast down, putting it at last to rest, but the cost was high. Many of the MacLear had been wounded in the battle, some very badly, and with their priest near death also, there was little they could do. The archer ran back to the camp for help as the few who could still stand tended as best they could to the wounded.

The archer returned with more priests faster than any thought possible, and they were able to heal most of the wounded, but something about the wounds Seamus had received was strange. He had been the first to charge the wurm and had received the brunt of the beast’s attacks. His warrior’s spirit was strong, but his wounds were tainted with some dark presence and the priests were unable to close them in the field. The elves and MacLear carried Seamus back to the camp where further efforts to heal him also proved futile. Late that evening, Seamus died.

The loss was staggering to MacLear, and the elves too mourned the loss of a man who had died fighting for the safety of their homeland. The MacLear were making preparations for the funeral when an elf noticed something very special had been placed on Seamus’ pyre. One of the MacLears had picked up a small red stone that had fallen from the wurm when it had been slain. They had placed it on Seamus’ pyre as a token of his last victory in battle against a great foe. When the elves realized what the stone was, they got very excited. They explained to us that the stone was a Bloodstone. Apparently when a dragon dies, the last drop of blood to fall from its body hardens into a blood-red stone that contains the essence, power, and courage of the beast within it. The Scourge had used Bloodstones to reanimate the wurms, and when this one died its Bloodstone was released. The elves said that, if we would allow it, they could purge the stone of the draconic essence and instead imbue it with the valor and courage of Seamus. The stone would not capture his soul, only become a lasting token of his strength as a warrior. We thought such a rite only befitted out leader and we agreed to the ceremony. Elven priests and druids gathered around the stone and began chanting in their strange tongue. The stone started to glow softly and pulse with a strange power. A drop of Seamus’ blood was dropped onto the surface where it was absorbed into the stone. The glow and pulse of the stone changed, grew somehow softer and more gentle. It resonated a kind of noble kindness and fierce courage, the very things we had come to love about our leader. When the elves finished their ritual, the stone indeed held the strength of Seamus. Just being near it filled you with courage and determination, it steeled your heart.

The Clan found themselves without a Chief, and with no clear heir. His sons had all been killed in the wars that I've told you about, and so they turned to the woman who had become like a daughter to him, who had so helped the Clan and the elves to come together, and who held the wisdom of the elders inside her. And so it was that Braitha that was given the Bloodstone and the mantle of Cheiftess. One of the Clan skilled in fine jewel work fashioned a setting for the Bloodstone and made it into the necklace that our Chieftess wears to this day.

The bloodstone held other powers that we weren’t at first aware of. The battle with the wurm was far from the only battle fought in those woods. There were still other Scourge to deal with, and the creatures tainted by demonic essence. It was after one of these battles that Braitha discovered some of the more interesting properties of the bloodstone. She was tending to a wounded man that she had gone on patrol with, holding a bandage over a deep cut he took in a fight with some crazed wildkin. She had been wounded as well, taking a shallow cut across chest. The bloodstone, hanging from the chain around her neck, swung over her cut and touched some of her blood. After a priest came to heal the man she had been tending, Braitha checked her own cut, bringing her hand up to her chest to see how deep the wound was. Her hand brushed against the bloodstone and some of the other man’s blood rubbed off onto its surface. After the priest had tended to the man, he rose and checked on Braitha. Her wound was minor, but even minor wounds deserved healing. The priest cast a healing spell over Braitha’s chest, with the bloodstone necklace getting caught in the wake. The stone began to glow once more and the blood from Braitha and the warrior melted into the surface of the stone! Instantly the two of them felt a strange connection, as though their spirits had bonded together in some way. They found on the walk back to the elven camp that if they thought a certain way, they could communicate with each other through thought.

From then on, all Kin of MacLear have shed a drop of blood onto the Bloodstone. It links us all together in the bonds of blood kinship and links our minds together that we may never be far from aid or each other. MacLear is a family and the Bloodstone serves as the bond that our family is based on.

So young one, that is our history. If you wish to join the Clan and be as Kin with us, then speak up! If you prove yourself worthy, you may add your lifeblood to the stone and join yourself to us!
