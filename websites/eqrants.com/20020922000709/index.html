Pay to Test. Doesn't Seem Right

Recently Sony/Verant/LucasArts announced the SWG Beta 1 going live. Also with the announcement was the troublesome news that the beta would cost $6.00, + shipping.

Uhh... hang on, what was that? You have to pay to test? Come again?

The CD's/materials cost too much they say. Hey, Verant is banked by Sony and LucasArts, neither of them are hurting for money. I think sending off some free CD's to people that are going to test the game is the least they can do. And don't start with "People in betas barely ever test. They are just there to play the game before everyone else." Yeah, true enough, but they are still in the beta TEST. Not the retail test which will be taking place shortly after the game is released.

They also are providing no download option, so if you want in you are paying no matter what.

I know that a lot of people will say that $6.00 is nothing, but that isn't really the point. They are charging the dedicated fans, who would probably gladly pay $30, to get in because they know that the fans are desperate to play and will pay. What better way to make a few extra bucks than to charge the dedicated fanbois for the priviledge of testing.

Also on the pay to test front, Microsoft recently sent out XBox Live beta letters. Now when Xbox live was first annouced as beta, Microsoft said they would send the beta kit and a free demo game to test. Great, count me in!

Not so fast.

Now the deal is that if you want to test, you have to pay for the full retail kit. $50.00 please, thanks.

Some will say "But it is the retail kit, you will have to buy it eventually anyway!" Ok, fair enough. But to them I offer this. Suppose Xbox Live stinks. Suppose you beta test it and you hate it. You think it is the worst thing since Han shooting first and you don't want the retail. Well tough shit, they already MADE you buy it to test. What then? You are out $50. Thank you, bend over!

A fair way to do it would be for Microsoft to get a CC# from you, NOT use it, and send you a kit (you know, much like RMA's work). At the end of the beta test if you decide that Xbox Live is for you, they charge your card and all is right with the world. If not you send the kit back, at their expense, and you get on with your life.

See, the problem with that solution is that it makes too much sense. And if the last few years of online gaming has taught us anything, it's that making sense seems to make no sense.
