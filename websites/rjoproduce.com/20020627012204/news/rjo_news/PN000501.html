RJO Now Procures Year-Round, Offers 
Full Category Management Support

by Rand Green
excerpted from The Produce News May 1, 2000


Fresno, CA-On Wednesday, April 26, RJO Produce Distributing, here, announced some major new directions for the company as well as a name change that better reflects both current and future operations.

The company, now called RJO Produce Marketing, which has been primarily a West Coast procurement office for several fruit categories, has been expanding its procurement to other areas in the last year and has also been providing category management support to its retail clients for the past two or three years.

Now, according to Rob O'Rourke, who founded the company along with his brother, John O'Rourke, in 1993, the company has added a full-time category management department and on the procurement side will be focusing on year-round global sourcing for the commodities in which it specializes.  Those commodities are tree fruit, strawberries, citrus, grapes and melons.

According to a company press release, the new company name reflects RJO's expertise on both the supply side and demand side of the business and "better describes the company's commitment to year-round sourcing, category management and emerging technology."

The company's logo has been updated as well, a stylized globe replacing what had been an outline of California in the logo design.

In 1999, RJO's inspectors started traveling outside of California, inspecting Florida strawberries as well as produce from Chile, Mexico and Central America at various American ports of entry, said Rob O'Rourke.

"We now have a lock on our five produce categories," he said. "Wherever they come from, any time of the year, we'll be there.

"There is simply no substitute for being in the field," O'Rourke continued.  "Our inspectors carry cell phones, laptops and digital cameras, but everything comes down to good judgment.  We simply want the best produce for our customers."

From the beginning, RJO has functioned as the "branch office" of its customers' buying departments, according to O'Rourke.  But in 1998, the company began to strengthen its partnerships with supermarkets "by adding services such as consumer research and promotional analysis."

Supermarkets "have welcomed our category management skills," he said.  "We track all the factors that influence produce quality, price and availability, then evaluate that against scan data and recent consumer behavior. We distill a large volume of information into something our customers can use to make better decisions.

RJO has recently hired Matt Ruegge as a full-time category specialist within the company's growing category management department, O'Rourke said.  Ruegge will concentrate exclusively on category management activities including produce branding, display design and promotional analysis.

Previously, Ruegge worked at Copper Hills Fruit Sales as a sales representative and web site manager.  Prior to that, he had worked for RJO as an inspector.  He holds a bachelor's degree in agricultural business from California State University.

Ruegge joins procurement specialists Todd Klassen, John O'Rourke and Ray Spinelli at RJO's Fresno headquarters.

Quality control Manager Jerry Aujero divides his time between the office and the field, where he supervises inspectors.

"Tying all RJO services together is the strategic use of technology," according to the company press release.  The company was among the first "to embrace the most advanced agricultural software and make use of electronic data exchange with its customers.  In the months ahead, RJO will enhance its web site [www.rjoproduce.com] to deliver more information and functionality to its customers."

"Technology has been a great tool for RJO," said O'Rourke.  "But what has built our business is getting results for our customers."

Some things have not changed.  " We have the ability to provide an independent, non-biased assessment" for a customer's procurement needs, "which we have always been able to do,"  O'Rourke said.  "So when a customer works with us, we are able to make decisions on what works best for them, not on what we have to sell."

The same is true on the category suppor5t side.  "We can make merchandising and marketing recommendations for these retailers" on an objective basis he said " We are not trying to move a certain label or [product from] a certain region.  We are trying to figure out what is best for the retailer in the category.

"On the back side, that will do a lot of g0ood things" for growers and shippers, he said, because it will help move products through retail channels " in the fastest and most efficient way possible."

Being able to provide procurement on a year-round basis has become very important, O'Rourke said. "One thing we found when we got involved in category management is that [if] we are supporting the category on a year-round basis from an analytical standpoint, we need to support it [year-round] as well from a procurement standpoint.  They go hand in hand."
