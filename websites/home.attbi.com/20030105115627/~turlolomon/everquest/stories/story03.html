The Assassin of Tunare

By Turlo Lomon, Heir Baron to Lomon Manor

There I was, before the guildmaster of the Scouts of Tunare, once again turning in a box of pixie dust. Supplies was short for this guild. I had to go to the Champions of Faydark, a rangers' guild, to obtain the specially crafted constructs used to house the fine powder. It has been a few days since I agreed to help them wipe out the pixie menace. It wasn't difficult work, but boy, was it messy. I was about to leave, exhausted from my extended stay in the field, but decided to stop in and have a cup of tea with a friend of mine in the area. Laren was an official member of the scouts, and him and I had numerous conversations about my joining them, which I politely declined. Today, our conversation took matters into a whole new direction.

"Turlo, you have done well with eliminating those pests. I have also been informed of your assistance freeing some of the slaves at Crushbone."

"Well, to be honest, I didn't do it for you or for the scouts. I did it for me."

"For you? How so?" Laren looked extremely puzzled at my comment.

"I can not stand the thought of anyone being chained up. People are individuals. Besides, it helps for good relations in the future."

"Interesting. I also understand you were awarded a Scout Cloak for your efforts. Some of our most seasoned veterans are still aspiring to earn theirs, yet you choose not to where it."

Choose? More like I can't remember where I left it. Probably at some tavern where I drank to the point of oblivion. Still, I couldn't quite admit that... "You see, Laren. I choose to assist the Scouts of Tunare because you need help. If I joined you, what would my own guild think? Situations have become quite precarious as it is. Problems with Carson at HighPass..."

"Funny you should mention HighPass."

I stopped my babbling for the moment. "Since when do the Scouts of Tunare have interest in HighPass Hold?"

"Since the craftsman of our Scout Blades was found dead three weeks ago."

This puzzled me even more. "Please explain."

"Fenn Kaedrick, one of our Senior Scouts, has been on the case since the first day. Our last communication with him placed his location at HighPass Hold. However, since then, all communication has stopped. This is where you come in."

"Me?" I wasn't too wild about where this was heading.

"We need an outsider. One that can make his way to HighPass and asks the necessary questions without alerting the wrong people. Turlo, you are that outsider. We need you." Laren reached into a pouch and pulled out a large coin. My first thought was about how cheap the Scouts of Tunare really are.

"Are you willing to avenge the craftsman?"

I sighed. "Yes, I am willing." The things I do to keep good relations. Laren handed me the coin.

"Very well then. Seek out Fenn Kaedrick in HighPass Hold. Talk to the merchants. He usually uses them to deliver communications."

I nodded, then excused myself. He nodded back. Looks like I need to buy some supplies for the trip to HighPass. Luckily, a vendor had some fresh fish a traveler had sold him, which I promptly bought his entire supply of. An hour later, I was freshly stocked with fish fillets and fish rolls. Nothing like real food for an adventure.

I was about to start heading toward the Butcherblock Mountains when I was once again stopped. This time, I was approached by a Junior Guildmaster of the Scouts.

"Hail, Turlo. And goodday."

"Thank you, sir. Is there something you wished to talk to me about"

"Indeed. Are you aware of the three tokens that every Scout of Tunare hopes to one day be awarded?"

"Yes, the Scout Cloak, the Scout Blade, and..." I drew a blank.

"The Scout Silvermesh Leggings. Yes, all of these are nothing compared to the Gem of Tunare. Do you know of that gem?"

"No, I can't say that I do."

"It was found when a lightning bolt from the heavens blasted a tree in half. At the base of the splinters, we found this gem. A gift from Tunare himself. It is not very valuable compared to other gems, but it is the source of our inspiration."

"And this has bearing on me...how?"

The guildmaster straightened up. "It was stolen."

Why me? Why? "What information do you have?"

"A thief by the name Faldor Hendrys infiltrated past our guards and stole the gem from our very guild house."

"Good for him," I muttered.

"What was that?"

"Nothing, sir. Please continue."

"His brother, Elmion Hendrys, is currently adventuring near his estates. He should have more information."

"I shall leave immediately."

The guildmaster thanked me, and I quickly departed, not wanting to run into any other officials of the Scouts of Tunare. It was going to be a busy week.

The trip to Butcherblock Mountains was uneventful, as well as the journey through its valleys. I arrived the docks in norhern Faydwer and as luck would have it, a ship had just arrived. I negotiated passage with the captain, whom was excited to have a real cook on board. After preparing meals for the crew, I retired below decks and decided to sleep. It might be awhile before I get a chance to do that again.

The days passed and soon we arrived at Freeport. I pushed my way through the crowds, and headed out the west gate, not in the mood to deal with the local street vendors. Making by way through the Common Lands, then to Kithicor Woods, I decided to detour to Rivervale, home of the Halflings. It is a pleasant little town, and gave me a chance to rest after my long journey.

I awoke early the next morning. Traveling back to Kithicor Woods, I headed to the southwest, and found the mountain pass leading to my goal. As I entered, a party was fighting a wandering band of orc beserkers. I believe they also had a shaman amongst their group. It did not matter to me, as I simply ran past the brawl, but not before relieving one of the orcs of his purse.

I skirted my way past the buildings hidden away in the canyons, until I reached the summit, where HighPass Hold itself awaited. Nodding at the guards, I walked on in. They liked me here. I hope they will continue to do so after I have left.

My first stop was the merchants. They were supposed to know who this Fenn Kaedrick fellow was. Unfortunately, all that I talked to had no clue who he was. I perused the guest book of the inn on the first floor while waiting for the innkeeper to get to the desk. She had no clue either. I was about to be overcome with despair, when I realized there was a clerk in the prison who sold the confiscated possessions of the incarcerated. I walked down the steps, and approached her.

"Hail, miss. Might I ask you a quick question?"

"Please, m'lord. Feel free." She batted her eyelashes at me. Cute.

"Do you, by chance, know who Fenn Kaedrick is?"

"What? Why would anyone want to talk to that rogue? Someone actually bailed that vile criminal out of our own jail. Who would do such a thing? Him and that damn high elf..."

"Excuse me, I am sorry. I believe you misheard me. Do you, by chance, have a water flask in?"

I walked away quite soberly afterwards, my face dripping with water. "Women," I muttered to myself, hoping Aysha wasn't around to flog me for saying such a sexist remark.

I wandered the jail, smiling at guards as I walked by. They seemed a little apprehensive with my being there, but I was granted full access by Carson himself. None of them were going to argue that. Amazing what doing several small favors for a mob boss will get you.

I stepped into the torture chamber, smiled at the professionals sharpening their tools, and glanced over at the cell dedicated to hold doomed prisoners. A high elf was huddled in the corner like a ball. I walked over and opened the door. The locks in this place were badly maintained, and very few actually held. Fear is what kept the prisoners inside.

"Hello?"

"Guard! Please keep that dark elf away from me. I can't take it anymore." He started sobbing. Pathetic.

"I am not a guard."

"You are not?" The tears quickly disappeared as fast as they issued forth. "Who are you? Are you going to rescue me?"

The two people outside seemed not hear our conversation. I hope this was true.

"No, I can't rescue you. Not just yet. I need something. Information. I was told you would know the answer to a simple question."

"Would you please make her stop?"

"I will see what I can do. First, I need to know where Fenn Kaedrick is."

"He is gone. He left. Bailed out. He did tell me he would wait on the rooftop until he got a chance to rescue me."

That was what I needed. I thanked him, putting my hand on his cheek, and told him I will try. As I left, I passed some coins to the woman in charge, and asked her to go easy on him. She smirked. Well, at least I tried.

I made my way up to the top floor. The layout was familiar to me. I have even spent a few nights here in the past when I worked for Carson McCabe himself. But the roof, how would I get up there? On the fourth floor, I went to an empty bedroom. At least I thought it was empty. A maid was making the bed, but ignored me, thinking I was the next guest to use this room. I walked out onto the balcony to see if there was a way up.

As I looked down at the courtyard, people traveling to and fro, street vendors hawking their goods, I remained puzzled. How...my eyes wandered...the railing...the window sill...the rooftop. I believe I have found a way. Hopping onto the railing around the balcony, I walked over to the window sill. All that goofing off walking on the top of fences paid off. A quick jump, and I was on top of the window sill, which gave me enough reach to pull myself up to the rooftop. So far, so good. A man was looking at me while I brushed the dust off of my gloves.

"Hello there. I am the City Ratcatcher. Major infestation up here."

My first impression was that this man was as bad a liar as I am. I pulled out the coin and showed it to him. "I believe this is for you, Fenn."

"So, you are the one Laren sent. Very well then. I had a bit of a problem with the local magistrates, but I discovered who killed the Scout Craftsman. It was Xentil Herkanon. But be warned, he travels with two bodyguards, not to mention he is favored in the higher rankings of the Circle of Unseen Hands. And he leaves tonight."

I sighed. It figures I would piss off the Qeynos guild. However, the name did ring a bell. Before I had a chance to question him further, Fenn disappeared. I thought only I knew that trick. I didn't have much time if he left this very eve. I jumped back down to the balcony, than off that, to a rock ledge on the side wall. From there, I worked my way back down to the courtyard. No one seemed to notice my unorthodox entrance.

I quickly ran back through the front gates, to the inn I had visited earlier. The clerk seemed bored as usual when I pulled the guest book over and verified the name I saw earlier was still there. Xentil Herkanon, Room 2. I thanked her, as she shrugged, and walked quietly down hallway to the rooms. Room 2 open. Two large men walked out carrying a trunk, leaving the door wide open. A third man remained inside.

Walking into the room, I closed the door behind me, then asked, "Excuse me, are you Xentil Herkanon? I have an important message for him."

"I am him." He eyed me nervously as I paced the room. "What is the message?"

I walked up behind him, and whispered, "Don't fuck with the Scouts of Tunare." My poisoned dagger immediately slipped in between his shoulder blades. Slamming me back against the wall, he only dug the dagger in deeper. We wrestled around a bit. He actually got in a few good whacks. But then it happened. He collapsed to the ground, weakened by the spider venom now coursing through his body and the blood loss from my original wound. He looked up at me, well aware what was about to happen, but not having the strength to scream. I quickly cut his throat, not wanting to prolong the agony he must have been feeling. Pulling out a hatchet, I finished the job. I neatly placed his severed head, wrapped in a blanket, into my back and sneaked out the side entrance. None of the guards had been alerted. I left the keep, and decided to make my way to Qeynos. My only stop was at the League of Antonican Bards to write Aysha a letter and let her know of my progress and whereabouts.

The trip to my home city was fortunately uneventful. I passed some brigands on the roadside, but they just smiled and waved at me. It is good to be amiable. As I passed through the Karanas, and headed through the Qeynos Hills, the town I spent my earlier years at grew in the horizon. It had been a long time since I ventured into these walls. I headed to Crow's, my old guild's store front, and decided to pay Hanns a visit. He deserved an explanation of the events in HighPass. When I ventured through the secret door to the inner sanctum of the Circle of Unseen Hands, Hanns greated me with a warm embrace.

"Turlo! It has been too long. How goes your journeys?"

"Hanns, I need to talk to you. There has been a situation." I was feeling nervous, waiting for a dagger to stick into my back at any time.

"Pah, that HighPass assassination? No worries about it. Actually, it has done a rather good thing. True, I was upset at first..."

"A good thing?" I still felt nervous.

"Yes! The Scouts of Tunare have opened negotiations with us. We are no longer at odds. We have also been informed of what else you came here for."

"And this does not trouble you?" Why do I still feel nervous?

"Faldor is no longer a member of this guild. He broke a rule. Never hold out on your guildmaster, especially in such a large take. Do what you must, Turlo. We hold no ill will."

I was shocked. I was stunned. I needed a drink.

"Thank you, Hanns. It was good to see you again. Does Faldor know of my arrival?"

"No, all was kept quiet. He doesn't even know he has been kicked out of the guild. I am letting you tell him that part."

"Understood. I will not let you down, Hanns."

"One thing, Turlo. As a favor to me, in addition to the Scouts."

"Yes, Hanns. Anything." Nervousness is now gone. Paranoia has set in.

"Make his death painful."

I nodded in agreement, as distasteful as this business can be, and made my way up to the bar after checking over my gear. After downing a quick ale, a friendly pat on my back caught my attention.

"Turlo? By Bristlebane, it has been years!"

I turned and looked surprised. "Faldor? It has been a long time. Let me buy you a drink. We have a lot to catch up on."

I bought a round of ales and let him guide me to the back room. This was going to be too easy.

"I understand you had a bit of fortune as of late. The Gem of Tunare?"

"What?" Faldor looked surprised that I would know about the theft, and a look of fear crossed his face. I quickly rolled across the table and stuck my stiletto into his back. Green mist filled the air, as the bloodclaw enchantment imbued his body with pain. The black ichor at the end of my blade seeped into his body.

"How did you know?" he gasped. "No one knew I took the gem. No one."

Snake venom, a very lethal concoction, started coursing through his body. He tried to fight me. Actually, he put up a good fight. This particular poison did not weapon the victim, but rather infused pain through out, as it slowly ate away his insides. The patrons right outside the door ignored everything. Apparently, Hanns had already told them this was going down. Faldor did not last much longer, and as he fell, I quickly searched his pouches for the gem. I found it. So far, so good. I wiped my stiletto on Faldor's tunic, then sheathed the blade. After straightening my tunic, I exited the room, and walked calmly out of the bar. It was at this time, I decided to puke.

Cleaning myself up in the small pond outside Crow's, I decided to head to the League of Antonican Bards to check if Aysha received my letter. It was really quiet amazing how fast mail transferred the globe. I guess having a group of extremely skilled druids all specialized in the techniques of gating really help them out. I was in luck. Aysha had written me, and in fact, the letter had just arrived.

,Dear Turlo,

I received your letter and could only wish I was there to assist you in your journey. As you know, matters at Mistmoore Castle delayed me. With the information you sent, I was able to track down Elmion Hendrys at a campsite outside the Estates of Unrest. When questioned about his brother, actually his half-brother as he rudely corrected me, he started ranting about how he was a rogue, and could be found in Qeynos. I am sure you know where to start your search. Good luck, and I hope to see you again.

Friends always,

Aysha Atreides, Bard of Quellious

I laughed. How else was I supposed to react? I quickly wrote back and informed her I was on my way to Kelethin. After having a nice meal of beer braised bear, I headed to the north gate, and started making the long journey to Aysha's homeland.

Three days later, I arrived unscathed, althought extremely tired, at the lifts of Kelethin. I waited for the lift to arrive, and then made my way along the treetops of the wood elven city to the guildhall of the Scouts of Tunare. Apparently the news of what I accomplished had arrived before me. The hall was packed. As I entered, the guildmaster approached, Loren at his side. I kneeled down, and unwrapped the bloody and slightly decayed head of Xentil Herkanon. There was a gasp of awe in the room. Second, I took out the Gem of Tunare from my pouch, and placed it in the guildmaster's outreached hand. A second gasp from the audience was made.

"Turlo, you have done better than any of us could have expected. I, once again, would like to offer you membership in the Scouts of Tunare."

"I am sorry, sir, but I can not." Haven't we gone over that more than enough times?

"I understand. But allow us to present to you a Scout Blade and a set of Scout Silvermesh Leggings. You may not wish to join us formally, but have it be known, you will always be accepted among us as one of our own. We would like to grant you a title as well."

"A title?" Now that is something I did not expect.

"Arise, Assassin of Tunare."

I stood up and smirked. I turned to every elf where I was congratulated and given a hand to shake.

"Thank you, sir, this is indeed an honor."

"The honor is ours, Turlo. You look fatigued. Please, take our guest cottage outside and use it as long as you wish."

"Thank you, sir. That is most generous of you all." Fatigued? Boy, do they have a way of understating things.

After excusing myself from the crowd, I went outside, puked, than found the guest cottage. Sleep would be good.
