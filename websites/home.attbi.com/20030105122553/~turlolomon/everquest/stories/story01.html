An Evening at Crow's

By Aysha Atreides, Bard of Quellious

The first time I walked into Crow's, I was fresh into town, road-weary and nearly starving. I had asked around the grand city of Qeynos about where a traveler could get a hot meal and a drink. Someone had recommended I try Crow's. I soon realized that they either had not been very familiar with the city themselves, or they were toying with a foreigner.

I found the place easily. It looked like any other large brick building from the outside, rather nondescript, except for the pool of water out front. I opened the door and although it was late afternoon, could barely see through the darkness and tabacco smoke. After the door closed with a bang behind me, my eyes adjusted to the dim light. There appeared to be few patrons at that time of day, but there were several pretty barmaids. Most did not seem to be working very hard. Every eye in the place was turned to me, and all conversations came to a halt.

The big man at the bar scowled at me and said, "We don't serve strangers here. You had better leave now!"

I turned a hot red right up to the points of my ears and tried to stutter an apology. I then turned and ran back out the door.

This all occurred months ago. It seems I was to visit Crow's place again, but at least not alone.

When Turlo Lomon told me our first stop after our lunch was a local bar he frequented, I didn't expect for it to be this place. He swung open that big wooden door, not paying attention to how it banged against the wall.

"Hey-ya, Crow," he said to the big man I had remembered standing behind the bar. "This is a very good friend of mine, Aysha."

Crow still looked like I wasn't wanted there.

"Is the back room free, Crow?" Turlo asked.

"You want the back room, eh?" Crow smiled slyly and glanced my way. He said something to Turlo in a low voice that I couldn't hear. Turlo just laughed. He has that exasperating manner of not answering questions. He is a master at avoiding the answer either by responding with a question, changing the subject or just laughing. I had no idea what the barkeep had said, but I felt the tips of my ears grow hot just the same.

"Oh, and, Crow," he added as he led me away from the bar, "M'lady bard here is a bit fastidious, so clean off the table by the time we come back up."

He led me down a short hall that dead-ended. There was nothing of interest there but a cheep, poorly done oil painting of a poker game.

"Now, Dear, listen to me," my friend gravely began. "That big husband of yours will not be happy with me if I let any harm come to you. I am taking you to see the Guildmaster of The Circle of Unseen Hands. We must see if we can get you accepted by the underground society. Otherwise, without me, you could be attacked in this part of town."

"Stay close to me. If you lose me, just stop and I'll come get you. Understand?"

"Yes, Turlo." My heart was pounding.

"And another thing-do you have your invisibility song memorized?"

"No, but give me a sec." I sat down and opened my songbook in my lap. I few minutes later, I had the Song of Sonorous Clouding committed to memory. If played on a flute, the magical song would veil those with me in a cloud of mist. I pulled my flute out of my backpack and held it in a tense fist.

"Follow me," Turlo commanded.

He proceeded to walk towards the cheep painting and disappeared! I stupidly looked around me until he reappeared in front of the painting.

"It's a secret wall. See these cracks? Come on. Follow close." He stressed this.

Through the wall we went, and entered a dim, dusty passage. Nondescript shapes were suggested in the shadowed corners and turn offs. Harsh laughter echoed up from somewhere further down the corridor. I followed nervously close to Turlo. So close, that I could smell deadly-sweet aroma of the poisons with which he coated his blades. We passed through one or two more secret walls. I was too intent on not losing him to take notice. I knew I would never have been able to find my way back out on my own.

The final hall ended with a room. We stopped before entering.

I could see a few people in the room, again looking at me as if I did not belong there. A big man dressed in fine, but simple clothing sat at a table looking over some books.

"That is the Guildmaster, Hanns," Turlo whispered. "He's the one that you need to talk to, but just wait until he speaks to you first. If he asks what you know of my business, tell him 'There is a mole in HighPass.' It's a code."

We entered the room. I tried to hide behind Turlo's back.

"Well, Turlo!" the big man stood and greeted my friend warmly. "Come back for some more training have you?"

"Not this time, Hanns. I am here on business. I've brought someone I think you should meet."

"I see! And, yes, we certainly should meet!" I could feel Hann's eyes scan up and down my body. My ear tips grew hot. "Been to Kelethin, I see? Done a little business there too!"

"Not the kind you are referring too, Hanns. This is Aysha Atreides, a Bard who has proven to be a very loyal and trustworthy assistant to me. I think you should accept her into the Circle."

Hanns thought this was rather funny.

"You think she should! Of course you would think that! A companion like her, any man would want to trust. But I do not trust her. Leave her with me for a few days and we can see if she can earn her trust."

"No, Hanns, I need her help with a few things. But I don't want her molested as she goes around town on my errands. I need at least to be assured she will be safe."

Hanns held out his hand for mine, the first direct gesture he had made toward me. I put my gloved hand in his. He looked into my eyes, probing, and squeezed my fingers painfully. I knew it was a test of my strength, but if he broke my fingers, I would never be able to play my instruments again. I stared back into his hard eyes and grit my teeth, trying not to flinch. I wondered if he could see the beads of nervous sweat trickle down between my breasts.

"What do you know of Turlo Lomon's business, my girl?"

I remembered the phrase Turlo had told me to say.

"There is a mole in Highpass," I stated clearly.

He laughed again. "Of course there is! There are moles everywhere!"

"I like her, Turlo. Don't worry, we'll watch her. We'll watch her REAL close! But no will touch her. As far as her being accepted into the Circle, that may never be possible. She will have to prove her worth and her loyalty to me. I know Bards like to sing, and we wouldn't want to have to "quiet" her. Don't bring her here again until she has done something to further our cause on her own."

"I see my word is still not good enough for you, Hanns. You will see." Was that anger that flashed in his emerald eyes?

We turned and left, following the passages back up to the main room or the bar.

"Looks like you could use a drink, My Dear. I could too. Don't worry, we'll get your faction up with Hanns. At least the city streets will be safe for you now."

He went up to Crow and ordered two Blackborrow stouts.

"I have a meeting with Faldos Hendrys," Turlo instructed the barkeep. "Have him come to the back room when he arrives."

I was led to a small room in the back of the bar that held a small table and a few chairs. I took notice that the table was clean, but very badly stained and gouged with knives. Small pictures and messages in various languages had been carved into the wood.

"Who is Faldos Hendrys?" I asked. I had never heard him mention the name before.

"Faldos is a thief." He answered. "He stole a valuable gem from a guy in Kelethin who is paying a great reward for it. I intend to steal it back and return it to its rightful owner. I am hoping you will help me."

"Of course! Sounds like fun. What do you want me to do?"

"Well first off, when he gets here, I just want you to be your usual sweet, charming self. When he is distracted by you, I will pick his pockets for the gem. But we must be prepared to defend ourselves if he catches on and wants a fight."

"I am always ready to sing a fighting song!"

"Problem is, I know he comes here every night, but I don't know when. We will just have to wait for him."

So, we waited. I had another stout until Turlo told me I was getting tipsy and should drink only water until my alcohol tolerance improved.

He told me a few horribly funny Halfling jokes, and I tried to teach him what I little I knew of the Dwarven language. He pulled out his stiletto and began twirling and spinning it through his fingers as I had seen him do before. I pulled out my Dragoon dirk and proceeded to carve the lyrics of a song into the wooden tabletop. It grew late and we grew bored. Still no Faldos.

Finally, with my head cradled in my folded arms on the table, I began to doze off.

"OK, Aysha," I awoke with a start at hearing the sound of his voice in my dream. "I am tired of waiting too. Let's get you to the inn, and we can try again tomorrow night."

Half asleep, I followed him the short distance to the Lion's Mane Inn. He asked for two rooms, much to the surprise of the clerk. With a kiss on the cheek, he said that he was going over to the adjoining tavern to see if there are any interesting rumors to be heard.

"Don't worry, you'll be safe here. No hard ground to sleep on tonight! I'll be back at my room shortly. It's just next door to mine.... Should you need me for anything"

Ignoring the unspoken suggestion, I said, "I think I will awake early tomorrow and go see if there is any work for me at the Bard's Guild. I need to go somewhere where I feel wanted."

His brilliant green eyes held mine.

"You are always wanted," he said.

I beamed a smile at Turlo Lomon, turned my back and headed up to my room.
