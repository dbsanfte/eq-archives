Cookie Conspiracy

 

You know the time of year. Young girls in earth tone uniforms have tried in the past few months to sign you up for the purchase of delicious Girl Scout Cookies. I admit, I am a sucker for those things. Thin Mints, Samoas, Tagalongs...but I digress. You try your best to restrain yourself, knowing full well, when delivery time comes, twelve cases will end up at your doorstep.

This year was no different. A friend of mine at work, Devin (see the Y2K Compliance Statement elsewhere) has a daughter who is six. He approached me at work, knowing full well he had found a sure-fire way to make his daughter the best seller in her troop. He was right. The paper was signed, and the wait began.

A month passes, or perhaps two. I was never that good with the whole concept of time. Finally, I was told the cookies would be arriving in two weeks! I was ecstatic. It IS something I look forward to each year, which is once again reinforcing the idea that I need a life. But something was amiss in the Cookie Empire. A few things actually.

I saw someone walk out of the building with three boxes of cookies under his arm. The release date was not for two weeks. How could this be? I questioned Devin, who then passed me along to his wife, Kim. Kim...well...adores me. But probably not the way you are thinking. She feels I am one of the most pick-on-able people she knows, and the taunts began! I backed off. I can't win an argument with Kim. Hell, even Devin can't. How could I stand a chance?

The next day it happened again. Someone walked out of the building with cookies under their arm. Now, Devin knows my fascination with sweets, and could have very well passed this information to his daughter. His daughter would then mention it at a Troop Meeting, and suddenly, the entire nation of girl scouts was out to prevent me from obtaining my cookies as early as people who don't deserve it as much as I do. Is this fair? No.

But, that's how things go in my world. If it doesn't work the way I want it to, it is wrong. I did get the cookies. One day later then Devin promised because he ran out of time and had to leave work right after a training class. Go fig. How dare he put his personal life before my cookies. I should smack him. The problem is, he would smack me back, and most likely, quite a bit harder.

Where is this story going? Well, that was the fun part. Now let's get to the SERIOUS stuff.

Not all girl scouts sell cookies door to door. Some sell them at grocery stores or the entrance to a park or zoo or something equally as wholesome. What is the price of a box of these delicious cookies? $2.50. A reasonable rate, and a price that has stayed constant over the years. Yet, here I see children, and mothers of children, selling the boxes for $3, $4, or even $5 a box! And people would buy them! Can you say markup?

How can the United Federation of Girl Scout Flunkies allow such business practices to take place? Wasn't the whole point of selling cookies to raise money for your troop? Apparently, some people, and this is backed by the parents, so you know where their streak of evil came from, feel the need to up the prices of girlscout cookies to make a personal profit. I don't mind supporting an organization that teaches girls useful skills and leadership training for the future, and at the same time getting cookies, but I do have a problem supporting some mom's crack habit.

What is going on with America today? And why are these boxes disappearing so fast? I can't stop eating the damn things.

Anyway, someone should look into why certain troops, or even individuals of those troops, have upped the set price of their cookie products. I doubt the money goes to where it is intended to. Shoot, a professional girlscout can earn her way through college if she finds the right corporate contract.

THINK OF THE CORRUPTION!!!!!!

With a well organized troop, a troop master could become the next Bill Gates! This could be your neighbor! Do you want to live next to Bill Gates? I think not.

Well, that is enough of my babbling. Most of this is in jest as you might have figured out, but it does actually bother me seeing people up the prices on a product for personal gain. After all, I didn't think of it first.
