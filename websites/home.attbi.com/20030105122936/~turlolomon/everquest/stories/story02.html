Song & Verse in the Bowl of Eyes

By Turlo Lomon, Heir Baron to Lomon Manor

Finally the rain stopped. Shaking some of the mud off my boots, I turned to my companion, Aysha Atreides. "We are almost there, my dear bardette. I can see the fires of the torches in the distance." She muttered something in agreement, obviously not pleased how the rain ruined her lovely reddish hair. A few more minutes and we arrived. The note I delivered ahead apparently reached it's course. Paul, a fellow guildmate was awaiting us at the entrance. I pretended surprise.

"Aysha! Look who is here! It's Paul! Paul, please join us out of the rain and listen to Aysha's lovely music. It southes the soul."

We exchanged greetings and all was well. Although we were on the doorstep of Runneye Citidel, a series of caves overrun by goblins, I felt somewhat safe. The goblins knew better to leave me alone, as long as I agreed to not venture too deep into their home. I occasionally used it as a shortcut, but this was not one of those times. Aysha took a break from her singing, so I decided to entertain our guest. Aysha started playing a bit more lively of a beat, and I tried my best attempt at singing.

Paul is a Blaster,
Master Caster,
Wheeler Dealer,
Kill Stealer,

He'll hide above you,
Not shove you,
Blast around you,
Not through you,

Paul is a Blaster,
Master Caster,
Wheeler Dealer,
Kill Stealer,

You can't catch him,
He's long gone now,
Can't say he's sorry,
He's gone to town now,

Paul is a Blaster,
Master Caster,
Wheeler Dealer,
Kill Stealer,

You've been camping,
Now your kill is missing,
Who's that selling?
What you've been searching?

It's Paul...the Master Blaster.

Paul was estatic with joy. He liked it! He really liked it! I heard a chuckling over my shoulder. While I was occupied, Scytalix, the heralded leader of our guild, emerged from the caves I feared to enter alone. His bronze armor was splattered with the blood of goblins. We greeted each other, as he mumbled something about the goblins looking for a new king. Our group was complete. Aysha felt rejuivated from our short rest and the site of her husband, and no longer complained that the ground was still muddy. We decided to venture forth into the Gorge of King Xorbb.

Who is this King Xorbb? I have never seen him. Shoot, no one I knew had ever seen him. As we started our excusrion, a lone minotaur decided to interrupt us. It was quickly dispatched. Further we marched. I only stopped to show off my ability to fall safely by jumping off a pillar jutting out of the ground. Aysha quickly skolded me for showing off. She doesn't always understand why I feel the need to practice every single skill I have learned over the years. One day...

We approached a section of the canyon where it split into two directions. I know the left path eventually led to the Eastern side of the Karana plains. We took the right path. At this point, I scouted ahead. Yes, there was guards. One...no, make that two. I signaled back at the party I was going to lure them back, to prevent alerting anything further in the canyon. I snuck up...and whipped out a shuriken at the closest. Soon, three (where did that last one come from?) charged up the path towards me. I quickly started running backwards, whipping out a few more shurikens before I tripped, my head slamming against a rock stunning me.

I thought I was done for. Weird lights floated in the air about me, most likely the result of ruptering some blood vessels in my head. Then I realized Aysha was there. Her songs, empowered by her unyielding will, slammed into the beast, causing it to forget about me. Scytalix engaged the other two himself. Finally regaining my senses, I quickly glanced for Paul, and didn't see him. No worries, we can take these beasts on with out him. Backstab. One dropped. Shield bash. As one stunned and dazed attempted to run away, I quickly dispatched it. The green haze of my magical stiletto filled the air and skulls ripped into the minotaur and tore it appart from the inside. I love my bloodclaw stiletto. A blast from the skies finished off the final one. Oh, there is Paul. The guards cleared, we ventured further into the bowl. I hummed.

Sing a song of six bards,
A pocket full of plat.

Four and twenty trained mobs,
Will make you go splat.

The tank was at the zone,
Waiting for a SoW.

Along came a named mob and
Crushed him on the go!

I am sure they did not find it as amusing as I did. Soon, a strange monument rose up in front of us. It was immense. We ventured along the southern edge of it and tried to remain alert for any surprises. Unfortunately, it wasn't enough. Above us, a ray shot out at slammed into me. I was knocked across the bowl formed by the walls of the canyon and slammed into solid rock. Ouch. I hurt. But I was not going to let what ever did that get away with it. Turning around, I saw Scytalix fighting an extremely large, slimy, icky looking eye that floating a foot off the ground. No time to think. Must react.

I charged forward, whipping out shurikens, knives, and what ever else I could find as I closed. They deflected off harmlessly. The creature seemed immune to the blows of Scytalixes sword, and this concerned me. Luckily, he was able to stand up to the shear force of this foul creature. I heard Paul uttering an incantation, and as the last syllable was spoken, a shimmering globe appeared around the eye which then shattered. I immediately struck out with my stilleto, and struck I did, deep. Skulls ripped through the eye and encircled it with that ever familiar green mist. It fell to the ground as a pile of ooze. During this time, I had not noticed what Aysha was doing, so I listened for her singing. The crack of a whip answered my questions. Aysha was engaging an animated sword and shield, obviously the pet of an enchanter. The eye? If the eye was slain, than...?

"You dare trespass in the domain of King Xorbb?" An even larger eye slowly floated down a ramp leading up the monument's side. "Fear the wrathe of Lord Scyrl!" I had the strange feeling we were in trouble.

Paul shot out a ball of fire from his hands and shouted "Go!" I didn't question his command. I ran forward past a pile of molten steel whipping out shurikens, only this time they connected. It got the creatures attention. Aysha slid up to it, her Alphonzo's Accelerado song playing in full force. She did not stop in time. A ray struck out and hit her full force. Scytalix and I both shouted "No!" and attempted to carve up this alleged Lord into fish bait.

I felt the crack of a whip across my back. Ok, Aysha accidently hit me in combat. That's ok, it happens. Scytalix and I seem to be doing some significant damage here. Crack. What is going on? Scytalix glanced back at his wife with a strange look. "Enough of this trifle." With a sudden lunch, Scytalix punched into the side of the creature and ripped out...well...something gross. Lord Scyrl fell to the ground, a glazed look over his...eye? Crack.

Scytalix stepped between me and his wife, asking her what was wrong. Crack. "Damn it, woman, quit your lashing now or you'll feel the flat side of my sword." Crack. Paul ordered everyone to stand back and quickly cast a spell at Aysha. The ground itself rose up and latched on to her feet. In a few minutes, she seemed to have regained her senses. She cried.

"What was I doing? It was horrible. I don't want to hurt my friends, my husband!" Scytalix walked over to comfort her. I sighed. Paul snapped his fingers and the ground eased back from Aysha's legs. "I hate to interrupt this precious moment, but we have a problem."

An eye, more immense than I had ever seen, slowly floated from a dias at the top of the monument. Around it, two smaller eyes seemed to provide escort. Fresh meat dripped from their jaws, as the largest of them turned to us and spoke.

"I am King Xorbb. You are...dinner."

How am I supposed to respond to that? A ray shot out from him, striking what I think was the wall. I muttered "frack" and started booking it to the goblin tunnels where the eyes would be too large to enter. Scytalix...Paul...they were right behind me. I glanced behind me and saw all three were still chasing us. Where was Aysha? In the distance, playing her mandolin, I saw her dancing in circles around King Xorbb himself. Double frack! Was she charmed again?

King Xorbb seemed a bit slower than the two eyes escorting us. They were quickly gained ground as we approached the tunnel. Rays shot from their eyes encircling goblins standing as lookouts in flame. The goblins quickly joined chase as well. Paul quickly climbed up the side of the hill outside Runnyeye to orient himself for battle. I tossed my stiletto end over end in my hand, and decided it was time to hold my ground. Scytalix seemed to agree, readying his double-bladed axe.

The concentrated efforts of the three of us, first on one eye, and then the other, proved devistating. They did not stand a chance. We were finishing off their summoned pets and the charmed goblins as King Xorbb approached, Aysha in tow. Although we one the initial battle, we were too weary to attempt to fight the King himself. Scytalix and Paul retreated into the caves, while I stood at the entrance hoping Aysha would break free in time. The King fired ray after ray at me. I dodged most, and than something distracted me. A pouch having on something sticking out of his mouth. Diving by him, I snatched the pouch and ran into the caves.

Aysha was gone to us. The news from observers high up in the cliffs said that Aysha followed King Xorbb into the depths of the canyon where it came from, both slaughtering all they came across. Huddled groups of adventurers hid in the caves, side by side with goblins, trying to determine how to eliminate this threat to both groups. A friendly cleric passed out bandages, while another cast curing spells. Paul sat in the corner meditating. I was trying to formulate a plan when Paul spoke first.

"King Xorbb is mine." I was shocked, appalled, intrigued. What was Paul capable of? Scytalix and I agreed to cover him. We were given some last minute supplies by the fellow travelers trapped in the cave. They wanted the king gone just as much as we did, although I am sure for different reasons. The three of us decided to exit the cave.

Carnage was everywhere. Many bodies were strewn about, with various forms of death apparent. Some seemed to have even killed each other. A few had the tell tale marks of sonic damage to them. Bard songs. Aysha's work. King Xorbb will pay for making her commit such vile acts. Slowly, others popped out of the cave, offering assistance to the three brave warriors willing to stand against the world for one of their own. After discussing the options with my group, we decided I would be the one to scout out ahead. A shaman imbued me with the spirit of the wolf, to enhance my running speed. I decided to pull out my bow, took one of my finest arrows, and find this horrid king.

With the spirit of the wolf guiding me, I quickly followed the carnage left behind in King Xorbb's wake back to the center of the gorge. In the distance, I could see King with a mass of minotaurs encircling him. I took aim. Carefully...every ounce of training going into this one shot. I released. The arrow did nothing but bounce off his hide, but it at least got his attention. I started running as fast as the spirit of the wolf would carry me, King Xorbb, an army of minotaurs and Aysha in tow. Some people call this luring. I call this baiting. Think about it. In an attempt to calm my nerves as I dodged boulders and muddites back to the entrance of Runnyeye, I started to hum once again.

Lock your doors and hold your purse tight.
Who knows what evil lurks in the night?

From a pauper's coin to a king's crown,
Nothing is safe when he enters the town.

Luck is foolish, as it flows,
But Fortune follows where Turlo goes.

Calm me it did. I always loved that little diddy. I approached Runnyeye in blinding speed. Many people were there, ready to meet the onslaught of minotaurs and other fell beasts that chased me. Finally, they are learning to fight together. Scytalix hefted his axe, Paul put down his book and started mumbling some incantation. The battle was about to commence.

A score of minotaurs came first, axes in hand, and a glazed look in their eyes, not that I would want to be that close to the horns on their heads. A group of warriors engaged them hand to hand. Spells flew at them from previously invisible mages. Scytalix and I helped out where we could. If a back was open, the minotaur soon fell as my stiletto plunged into its vitals. Scytalix decapitated one as it tried to flee, it's charm obviously broken. Still...Paul waited...his incantations getting louder.

King Xorbb arrived. Muddites around him, along with a few goblins, engaged the already weakened warriors consisting of our front line. Still, skill overcame exhaustion, and they were quickly felled. On my signal, the shaman who helped me earlier caused roots to slither out of the ground and encircle Aysha, holding her in place. It did some good, keeping a fellow companion from seriously harming her friends by accident, but small throwing daggers were flying through the air and hitting...well...anything but a combatant. I guess charm doesn't give someone skill. Good thing.

King Xorbb engaged the front line, rays of fire engulfing warriors in flame. A few fell. A few went flying through the air and then fell. Paul finished his incantation. A blast of energy filled the air. The hair all over my body stood on edge from the electricity in the air. A shimmering globe around the king shattered into a million pieces and still the blast did not stop. Smoke filled the air and I lost sight of what was happening. When it cleared, the broken body (?) of King Xorbb lied croaking on the ground. Several people who were too close to the blast site were badly burned if not out right slain. That was one powerful spell!

Scytalix was first to approach the fallen king, with me close on his heels. King Xorbb looked up with his single eye, black bile oozing out of its mouth. "For all your atrocities, perish!" In a large over head swing, Scytalix took his axe and cleaved King Xorbb for all he was worth. He looked definitely deceased now. However, since he was not a normal creature, I poked him with my stiletto just to make sure. I forgot that sometimes my blade has a mind of its own, and the familiar green skulls filled the beast, ripping the corpse into small pieces and splattering Scytalix and I with black slime. Close by, I heard crying. It was Aysha.

"So much...so much death." Aysha was in that state known as hysteria. I backed away, while Scytalix rushed over to comfort her. At this time, I realized I never checked what was in the pouch I "obtained" earlier. Glancing inside, I saw an extremely large blood red ruby. I tried my best to cheer up my favorite bard.

"For services rendered unto the king, your payment." I then poured the pouch into her hand, the ruby rolling out like an orange. I try my best, but I just don't deal very well with women who are hysterical.

To put the matter behind us, we traveled to High Pass for a night of drinking. Aysha was buying.
