The Adventures of Drolgin Steingrinder, Paladin of Underfoot

The return to Kaladim

I had roamed for too long. The sandblasted lands of Ro, the Commonlands and the bustling human city of Freeport were no longer strange to me, yet none felt like home. I yearned for the familiarity of the Butcherblock mountains and my childhood city of Kaladim. I had neglected my family and my church for too long. The closer I came to my decision, the more right it felt, and an exhilaration crept into me. I felt sad, leaving my newfound friends behind, but the tug of Faydwer was too strong. On a cold november morning, I boarded the ship for the Butcherblock port.

I had left a note for Farnight and Lilcharm in an Inn near Freeport that I knew they frequented, detailing my plans for travel, and inviting them to join me, had they the inclination.

How different was this second ride in the Ocean of Tears! I must have seemed but a babe the first time I crossed that body, gawing here and yon. Now the world of Norrath held few surprises for me, I thought. I was wordly, knowledgable of all Faydwers creatures. There was no challenge Lord Brell could present me with that was not within my abilities. How naive I was.

Upon reaching Bucherblock, I was amazed. The familiar landscapes that had once seemed so dangerous, so fraught with danger round every corner were now as a garden. I laughed at the innocence of youth, as I ran as fast as my legs could carry me to Kaladim. Gazing upon the giant statue, I almost burst into tears. I had been away for too long.

I went searching for my kinfolk first. My Father would be in his workshop, I was sure, with my uncles and cousins. As I opened the door to the small house that I knew so well, I was filled with a foreboding sense of dread. The workplace was empty. Cobwebs covered the tools of his craft, the fireplace had been left unused for a long time. In puzzlement, I ventured into Kaladim proper to search for my family.

I found my answer, and a cruel one it was. My third cousin on my mothers side, young Regnor Cobblepot, was standing near the entrance to the mine. He was clearly shocked to see me, and haltingly told me of the sad fate that had befallen our family. Apparently, an apprentice of my uncle Morlin, as skilled a blacksmith as you would find, had stoked a fire wrongly. The furnace had exploded, killing all in the building. Including my father, my youngest brothers and my three uncles, who had been visiting his brother at the time.

The shock left me numb. My mother had waned away after the death of my older brother. Excepting few distant cousins, none of them proper Steingrinders, there were no Steingrinders left but me. In but 15 years, a proud family, once counted amongst the noblest of Kaladim, had been all but wiped out.

In a daze, I wandered through Kaladim. I saw the look of pity in peoples eyes as they recognized me, and I filled with anger. Not even thinking to visit the hollowed halls of Underfoot, I left Kaladim. There could be but one cure for the sickness raging in my heart. Battle.
