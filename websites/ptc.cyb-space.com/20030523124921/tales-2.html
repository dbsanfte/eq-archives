The Adventures of Drolgin Steingrinder, Paladin of Underfoot

Chapter 2, The Estate of Unrest.

Through the haze in my mind, I recalled stories told over campfires in the desert. Tales of a haunted estate near the Cauldron of Dagnor. An estate filled with creatures of the dead. Indeed, if anything were to douse the fires burning in my soul, this would be it. With grim determination I traveled through the Mountains, hardly noticing the other travellers. At one point, a goblin crossed the road in front of me. Not even caring, I chopped its head from its shoulders with a single swing of my weapon, and continued my journey.

Upon reaching the entrance to the estate of Unrest, I gave pause. Remembering the teachings of my father, I took a moment to observe my surroundings. Many a weary, battleworn traveller was here. Some clad in the furs of cold, far away Halas, others garbed much like the nomads I had seen in the desert. Amongst the strangers was a face I recognized. Sitting a scant distance away was a gnome I had met once before, a gnarled little man by the name of Quae. His childlike appearance hid the fact that he was a powerful wielder of magic, and I hailed him. He remembered me well, and we decided to join forces against the undead. Quae was a braggart, a liar and, I have no doubt, a thief, but his bantering tone nonetheless lightened my spirit somewhat as we entered the courtyard surrounding the once proud estate.

Our lightness of heart disappeared, though, as we looked upon the once proud manor. I have never before nor since felt such a tangible evil. It was as if the very air around us was filled with all that is unholy, penetrating to the very core of our beings. One could not walk nor breathe without sensing the hate in that place. Quae called me over. Ever the clever one, he had spotted a group of travellers that had set up a camp near the wall of the courtyard. "Safety in numbers", he stated. I could only agree, and soon we were unrolling our bedpacks on the lichen ground.

We were not allowed to finish our domestic task, though. Suddenly I heard a horrifying scream from behind me, followed by a wild, cackling laughter. I spun, and found myself face to face with an apparition from the nightmares of children. Twice again as tall as me, it’s head as a pumpkin on All Hallow’s Eve but with ghostly lights glowing from the sockets. The creature was bent over Quae who lay lifeless on the moldy ground. I called upon the power of Lord Brell to smite the abomination. Nothing happened. I still do not know why my powers failed me, but can only speculate. Perhaps the rage I felt had removed me from my god, perhaps Lord Brell saw fit to teach me a lesson. I know not.

If it were indeed a lesson, it was a wise one. The rage that had been building inside me since I learned of my fathers cruel and needless death was unleashed upon the creature as I swung my halberd wildly. All the skills I had gained, the lessons I had learned fighting every manner of evil creature, were forgotten in that instant. I was the wild beast fighting for survival, the mother protecting her young, the unjustly treated overthrowing the oppressor. I fought without thought or care for my life.

Had I cared to live, I would have died. In the end, it was my reckless abandon that saved me. The fury of my swings forced the mindless creature before me to focus upon me, leaving its back uncovered. Out of the corner of my eye, I saw Quae try to stand, his arm a bloody pulp, his face burned with magical fire. As he slowly rose, his brow furrowed in concentration, he called upon his arts. For a moment, I thought the strain had been too much for him. He stumbled, face white. Then he rose again, and placed a hand upon the creatures shoulder. It shuddered, as though a cold wind had stolen the will to exist from it, and fell to the ground. I stared at Quae in disbelief as the fury of battle left me. His wounds were whole!
