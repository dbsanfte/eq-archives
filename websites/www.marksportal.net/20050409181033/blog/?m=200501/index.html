Sun 23 Jan 2005

Without Shame

Posted by Mark under The Narrow Path
No Comments 

I just finished watching the The Lord of the Rings: The Return of the King, which is quite a great end to the trilogy. While there were many great lines in the movie, this particular one from Theoden rang out in my ears.

My body is broken. I go to my fathers. And even in their mighty company I shall not now be ashamed

As Christians, I think we sometimes forget about those who went before. Their bravery, sacrifice, and unwavering devotion to Christ. Will I be able to hold my head high at the end of my life as I stand before God, His Angels, and the multitudes of believers who went before me? Listen to Paul’s words in the Book of Romans as he describes the place we enter when we are in prayer:

“But you have come to Mount Zion, to the heavenly Jerusalem, the city of the living God. You have come to thousands upon thousands of angels in joyful assembly, to the church of the firstborn, whose names are written in heaven. You have come to God, the judge of all men, to the spirits of righteous men made perfect, to Jesus the mediator of a new covenant, and to the sprinked blood that speaks a better word than the blood of Abel.” (Hebrews 12:22-24, NIV)

That is a mighty gathering. I hope that when I am finally called away from earth that I will be able to enter that great assembly without shame.

 

Sun 16 Jan 2005

Nobody Special

Posted by Mark under The Narrow Path
1 Comment 

I’ve been reading the “Chronicles of Narnia”, which is a collection of children’s books written by C.S. Lewis. They tend to have a heavy Christian theme in them which I really enjoy. In one of the books, “The Horse and his Boy”, the talking horse named Bree was downcast because he had been out-shown by “a child, a mere foal”. Bree was proud because of all the great acts of bravery he had done while in the land of the Calormen and had expected that, due to that bravery, he would be a great horse in the land of Narnia. While Bree was sulking, an old hermit came up to him and said,

You’re not quite the great Horse you had come to think, from living among poor dumb horses. Of course you were braver and cleverer than them. You could hardly help being that. It doesn’t follow that you’ll be anyone very special in Narnia. But as long as you know you’re nobody special, you’ll be a very decent sort of Horse, on the whole, and taking one thing with another.

Equally I think there are many on earth whose good works shine in the midst of all the bad that are out there, but once we are all in heaven we will be amoungst MANY people who will have done equally or even greater works. We must never be proud about the good works that we do on earth, for they are just the normal works of those who are of God.

 

Tue 4 Jan 2005

The Call of Abraham

Posted by Mark under The Narrow Path
No Comments 

I’ve always thought that God called Abraham out of Ur to go to the land of Canaan (Gen 12:1-3). Imagine my surprise when I found that wasn’t the case. Going back to Gen 11:31-32 it states that Abraham’s father Terah had it in his mind to go to the land of Canaan, but they ended up stopping in Haran and stayed there. It wasn’t until after Terah died that God came to Abraham and told him to complete the journey his father had started.

So now I wonder, why did Terah wish to go to Canaan? I suppose one reason Terah wished to leave Ur was because of his son’s death, but I can’t be certain. Did God ask Terah to begin this journey to Canaan?
