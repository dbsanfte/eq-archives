Yosemite Weekend

Posted by Mark under General
 

I just got back from a great weekend of hiking at Yosemite. I wasn’t quite sure what to expect as I really haven’t been exposed much to the area nor the people that I went with. Much to my delight, it ended up being a great time. We spent Saturday driving around and taking leisurely hikes, which mostly seemed to be a time just to get to know each other and hang out. It was enjoyable and by the end of the day I felt comfortable with everyone there.

On Sunday we woke up early and made the long trek to Half Dome (or “Half Doom” as a friend calls it). I had no idea what this hike was going to be like, but was amply warned that it was a hard hike. For some reason “hard hike” never equaled “up hill the whole way” in my mind, which is exactly what it turned out to be. Being someone who doesn’t play sports or do any other strenuous activity, besides typing on this keyboard, part of me thinks I did rather well. I was huffing and puffing most the way, which is to be expected, but ended up making it to the bottom of half dome, alive. About 2 miles before I hit that point though, I hit a wall I’ve never really hit that hard before…exhaustion. I just crashed. Up to this point I knew I was pushing myself beyond my limits, but I had never expected to hit the point where I literally could not go on. The people I was with must have been “super-human”, because they were going up this hill like it was nothing. Some didn’t even seem to be breaking a sweat. So I waved them along and told them to continue their ascent without me, I had resigned myself to the fact that my body was physically incapable of going any further. It took a bit of persuading but they finally continued their journey up the hill.

Let me take a second to mention something that I had never noticed before. During my hike up this trail I found that having people around me at certain times actually hindered me. When people were around me I just could not focus. It was the weirdest thing. When I was hanging back or was alone up ahead(yes, this really did happen ) I found that I had the strength to continue. I would think about God or His angel that He has assigned to watch over me, and for some reason this strengthed me and I could go on. It also got my mind off the hike, which was nice. Yet when people were around me I started comparing myself to them and getting down that I wasn’t in better shape or wasn’t as smart as they are, or good looking as they are, or one of the million other things you can use to compare yourself with. At any rate, it was interesting to discover that I could push myself harder by myself than I could with other people around.

So back to the hill…I had my friends continue up without me and I just sat there as my mind and my body waged war with each other. My mind trying with all it’s might to force my sore body to continue past a pain it had never felt before(not to mention the blisters all over my feet). As I sat there, up walks another member of our group that we had thought turned back with some others. He was another one of those “Super-humans” who didn’t break a sweat climbing this monsterous mountain. Again, I tried to persuade him to “move along”, but he would have none of that. If I had to stop every 3 feet, so be it. “There’s no rush getting to the top”, he said. So with every ounce I could muster we continued up the mountain. Though I didn’t stop every 3 feet, it sure seemed like it…and I felt horrible for it. “It just gives me more time to rest”, he’d tell me with a smile. I’m sure this was all true, but I still felt bad for having to stop so often, not to mention the snails pace I was going.

That final 2 miles seemed to be the longest in my life. I’m not sure how long it took, but it felt like hours. Upon reaching the bottom of Half Dome I collapsed, feeling proud that I had made it that far. As I sat there twitching , I looked up and saw a set of stairs leading up to the final leg of the trip. I’m not quite sure how far up it went, because, I regret to inform you, I did not continue. After resting for a bit I started up the stairs but ended up heading back down due to my fear of heights. You see, the stairs were nothing but stones stacked on each other on the side of this rock mountain. One false step and there was an easy tumble down to the bottom. So I sat down on one of the rocks considering if I should continue up or not. Around that time this huge gust of wind thunders past me. That was too much. So I headed back down to the base and awaited the rest of the group. I had hiked up hill for over 8 miles only to stop short of reaching the goal by less than a quarter of a mile. To be honest I’m starting to regret that decision, but I’m sure it’s easier to regret when I’m sitting back at home safe and sound…and breathing normally again.

Like I said, all in all, it was a great trip. Though, as usual, I ended the trip feeling down on myself for various reasons. The first thing, as I mentioned previously, was my physical condition. Though I’m sure I did great for someone who isn’t active, that’s not quite good enough for me. I was weak and my weakness affected other people. Nothing serious of course, I just slowed them down, but I hated that weakness. I would have had no problems waiting for someone who was physically weaker, but me being that weak person..I have a big problem with that.

There is more to add, but it’s late and I need rest. Maybe tomorrow I’ll get back to some of the other reasons…we’ll see.
