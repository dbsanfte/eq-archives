Main Point: God does not want us to focus on judgement, but on forgiveness and mercy

As you can see in your bulletin, this talk was titled "Beyond Justice". I actually borrowed this title from a song I was listening to by Susan Ashton. It is called, "Beyond Justice to Mercy". The song is about a couple who have been fighting with each other. Both angry at the other. Both hurt by the other. Both thinking they are in the right. So the songs chorus goes on to say, "Let us reach out beyond justice to mercy, going more than halfway to forgive". This is what I'd like to touch on today. Justice, forgiveness and Mercy.

Let's begin with justice. Webster defintes justice as "the maintenance or administration of what is just especially by the impartial adjustment of conflicting claims or the assignment of merited rewards or punishments". I am not sure if that makes sense to any of you, but I had to re-read that several times before I finally understood it. Basically it's saying that justice is the practice of upholding whatever is just, either by punishing the offender or compensating the victim. In the US, if I ran a stop sign it would be justice that I, the offender, received a ticket. The ticket being my punishment for my offense against the victim, which in this case is the law.

Now with that in mind, let's head over to Lev 24:17-23, which was read earlier, let me re-read it to refresh our minds.

"If a man takes the life of any human being, he shall surely be put to death. The one who takes the life of an animal shall make it good, life for life. If a man injures his neighbor, just as he has done, so it shall be done to him: fracture for fracture, eye for eye, tooth for tooth; just as he has injured a man, so it shall be inflicted on him. Thus the one who kills an animal shall make it good, but the one who kills a man shall be put to death. There shall be one standard for you; it shall be for the stranger as well as the native, for I am the LORD your God."

After Moses lead the Israelites out of slavery in Egypt they came to Mt. Sinai, which is between Egypt and Saudi Arabia. There God came and spoke to the children of Israel and gave them their laws, much like the US constitution. You see the children of Israel were not yet a nation. They were just a REALLY big family who were all the descendents of Jacob, whom God had called Israel. At that time they had no set laws. They basically just followed the laws that they were taught in Egypt. However, God wanted this new nation to be set apart, different from the rest of the world. In Exodus 19:5-6 God said,

"Now then, if you will indeed obey My voice and keep My covenant, then you shall be My own possession among all the peoples, for all the earth is Mine; and you shall be to Me a kingdom of priests and a holy nation."

God's plan for the children of Israel was to set them apart from the rest of the world. They were to be a holy nation, a kingdom of priests. With this in mind God laid out a different set of Laws for them to go by, which is what the books of Leviticus, Numbers and Deuteronomy is all about. The Laws that God laid down for them, as a new holy nation.

Now during that time various nations on earth had their own set of laws to keep order. One of those laws was known as the Law of Retaliation. This law enabled the family to be punished for the crimes of the offender. So if someone breaks your arm, under the Law of Retaliation you could destroy his whole family. God, however, had a different set of rules for the children of Israel. He limited the Law of Retaliation by saying, "An eye for an eye, a tooth for a tooth". In essence make the punishment fit the crime. God was trying to teach the children of Israel restraint and mercy, but unfortunately they didn't get it. Instead of seeing what God was saying, they turned it into a rule and reason for punishment. "You killed my cow, now I GET to kill your cow". "You broke my arm, now I GET to brake your arm". You see, just like many of us today, they loved justice and desired to see justice done. And they used this law to get it. However, as I said before, that is NOT what God wanted His holy nation to focus on.

Micah 6:8 says:

"He has told you, O man, what is good; And what does the Lord require of you But to do justly, To love mercy, And to walk humbly with your God?"

and in Zecahriah 7:9-10

"Thus says the Lord of hosts: Execute true justice, Show mercy and compassion Everyone to his brother. Do not oppress the widow or the fatherless, The alien or the poor. Let none of you plan evil in his heart Against his brother.

And Hosea 6:6:

"For I desire mercy and not sacrifice, and the knowledge of God more then burnt offerings."

I think this last verse spells it out quite clearly. "I desire mercy and not sacrifice." God wanted the children of Isreal to focus on mercy. On kindness. On love. Not on getting someone back for doing wrong to them. God would do that, He didn't need the nation of Israel focusing on that.

God says in Deuteronomy 32:35,

"Vengeance is Mine, and retribution, In due time their foot will slip;"

and in Deuteronomy 32:41

"If I sharpen My flashing sword, And My hand takes hold on justice, I will render vengeance on My adversaries, And I will repay those who hate Me"

And in Proverbs 20:22:

"Do not say, "I will repay evil"; wait for the Lord and He will save you"

But again, Israel did not listen to any of these things and continued in their ways. Centuries passed and finally Jesus Christ walked amoung them . He was God's son, Israel's Savior.

After Christ was baptised by John and then tempted in the wilderness by the devil, He then started going around healing people and teching people, telling them that the "Kingdom of God was at hand". One of Christ's first big recorded teachings that is listed in the gospels is called the Olivet Discourse. This discourse took place on the Mount of Olives which is near Jerusalem. It begins in Matthew chapter 5 with this:

"And when He saw the multitudes, He went up on the mountain; and after He sat down, His disciples came to Him. And opening His mouth He began to teach them..."

Now starting in verse 21 Christ begins taking the laws that God gave the Israelites and interpreting it for them. He does this by saying , 'You have heard it said.... law, but I say to you..interpretation'. Now, let's read what Christ says about the "eye for an eye" verse that we read in Deuteronomy.

Matthew 5:38-42 says,

"You have heard that it was said, "An eye for an eye, and a tooth for a tooth". But I say to you do not resist him who is evil; but whoever slaps you on your right cheek, turn to him the other also. And if anyone wants to sue you, and take your shirt, let him have your coat also. And whoever shall force you to go one mile, go with him two. Give to him who asks of you, and do not turn away from him who wants to borrow from you."

Christ was not changing this law around when He said these things, but trying to get the Israelites to understand the law. It's like what we do with the speed limit signs. The reason they were put there was to have people drive at safe speeds. If it's snowing or icy outside I wouldn't be driving at at 65mph, I'd be driving at a lessor safe speed. But that's not what the Israelites where doing. They were out there driving at 65mph in a snow storm and would say, "I'm following the law! I am doing no wrong!". They took something that was meant for good and changed it into something reckless and bad. Incidentally, I believe America is being corrupted in the same way today. Lawyers are defending or persecuting people based on the letter of the law rather than the idea or reason behind the law. Innocent people are getting hurt and guilty people are being set free.

When Christ said "But I say to you", He was moving the Israelites back to the concept of this law. Have mercy and compassion on people. You see, God doesn't like executing justice. Don't get me wrong, He loves things that are just and right and true, but executing justice is not what He desires or looks forward too. Look at John 3:16:

"For God so loved the world that He gave His only begotten Son that whoever believes in Him will not perish but have everlasting life".

Does that sound like a God who is looking forward to executing justice? No, He's giving people a way out. God is earnestly desiring to forgive, to show His mercy. Ephesians 2:4-7 says:

"But God, being rich in mercy, because of His great love with which He loved us, even when we were dead in our transgressions, made us alive together with Christ, by grace you have been saved, and raised us up with Him and seated us with Him in the heavenly places in Christ Jesus, so that in the ages to come He might show the surpassing riches of His grace in kindness toward us in Christ Jesus."

Did you catch that? Even when WE were dead in our transgressions, when We had broken the universal laws of God, when God was the victim and WE were the offender and, according to Romans 6:23, justice could only be served by OUR death, God, whom WE had offended showed us mercy and saved US through Christ Jesus. And now that God has shown this GREAT mercy upon us, don't you think that He wants US to do the same to others?

Look at how Jesus answers Peter in Matthew 18:21-22:

"Then Peter came and said to Him, 'Lord, how often shall my brother sin against me and I forgive him? Up to seven times?' Jesus said to him, 'I do not say to you, up to seven times, but up to seventy times seven'"

I can go on and on and on with examples. We could talk about the parable of the king and the servant in Matthew 18. We could look at the TONS of examples in the Old Testiment of God forgiving Israel and giving them another chance. We could talk about Luke 23 when Christ is being cruicified and asks God to "Forgive them; for they do not know what they are doing". There are examples all over scripture of God showing us mercy and forgiveness.

Justice is not a pretty thing. When God comes to judge the earth, I believe it will be a terrifying time. A time when punishment must be dealt out. God will judge the earth and execute justice because He is just, not because it is something He enjoys doing. Let us make sure it is not something that we enjoy doing as well. Let us earnestly seek after, earnestly desire, forgivness and mercy. Let us RUSH to forgive others, to show others mercy. For in doing this, we are truely ambassadors for Christ, son's of the Almighty God.
