Spiritual Gifts

posted by: Mark @ 12:44 pm

Sometimes things just click. I’ll have read something over and over and over again and think I’ve understood it, then one day God taps one part of my brain and *BAM* fresh water floods in giving new insights into things. This is what happened with me during my lunchtime with God today. It’s probably something that seems simple to everyone else, but to me it finally clicked, and that is good.

So what was it? I was reading through 1 Corinthians 13-14. In the previous chapter Paul had been talking about Spiritual gifts and how God has gifted everyone in the Church with different gifts. After talking about them Paul says that not everyone receives these gifts, but should “eagerly desire the greater gifts"(1 Corinth 12:31, NIV). The greater gifts being healing, prophecy, speaking in tongues, etc. Then he says, “And now I will show you the most excellent way"(1 Corinth 13:1, NIV). Paul goes on to say that these “greater gifts” are good and you should seek after them, however, if you do not have love, these gifts are useless. In 1 Corinth 13:4-8 he defines love for us:

“Love is patient, love is kind. It does not envy, it does not boast, it is not proud. It is not rude, it is not self-seeking. It is not easily angered, it keeps no record of wrongs. Love does not delight in evil but rejoices with the truth. It always protects, always trusts, always hopes, always perservers. Love never fails.” (1 Corinth. 13:4-8, NIV)

He then starts to compare love to the “greater gifts” and basically says that one day the “greater gifts” will not be needed. There will be a time when healing is no longer needed because we will be healthy. Tongues will not be needed because we will all be speaking with God face to face. etc…etc…etc. Then Paul concludes by saying, “And now these three remain: faith, hope and love. But the greatest is love."(1 Corinth. 13:13, NIV).

Now Paul has put the “greater gifts” in context. They are good and we should desire them, but they are merely a tool to be used while we are in the world. Once out of the world(ie. in heaven), they will no longer be needed. However, faith, hope, and love will continue passed the death of our bodies.

Since Paul has now put things in perspective he goes back to talking about the “greater gifts” again. “Follow the way of love and eagerly desire spiritual gifts, especially the gift of prophecy."(1 Corinth. 14:1, NIV). Paul continues on by comparing prophecy to tongues. Tongues is good, he says, but it only edifies the speaker and are more of sign to unbelievers that God is with them, then it is to believers. Prophecy, however, is what we are to desire most out of all the “greater gifts” because “everyone who prophesies speaks to men for their strengthening, encouragement, and comfort"(1 Corinth 14:3, NIV). Prophecy edifies the Church and it seems to me that anything that edifies the Church is an act of, or done out of, love.

I’d like to continue with this, but I need to get back to work. Just thought I’d write this while it’s fresh in my mind.

Active Ministering

posted by: Mark @ 11:28 pm

Today’s church seem to push active ministering. By this I mean going to your workplace or to strangers or to friends and actively share Christ with them. “Make friends with unbelievers so that you can share Christ with them", the church is urging. We are asked to do this or do that so that we can “create” this opportunity. This seems very ungenuine to me. Maybe I’m not really hearing what is being said, which could be the case. Don’t go and try to create a fictitious relationship with someone because you think “God would want me to do this". Reach out to people because of your love for them. If you do it for any other reason you will be, as Paul said, “a noisy gong".

“If I speak with the tongues of men and of angels, but do not have love, I have become a noisy gong or a clanging cymbal. ” (1 Corinthians 13:1, NASB95)

What’s on my mind

posted by: Mark @ 11:44 pm

I had inital started writing something here, but since I’m a bit tired I think I started to ramble on. So I just decided to make it into a quick outline form instead. So……what’s on my mind.

1. I question why conversation plays such a vital role in a social structure. For example…those who tell good jokes and have good stories seem to draw a lot of people around them. Yet those who are quiet or shy seem to repel people away. The deeper thought on this would ask why the church seems to show favortism to the well spoken person rather than treating everyone as equals.

2. I question why I, and other Christian singles, have the desire to have a companion when Christ says:

“For in the resurrection they neither marry nor are given in marriage, but are like angels in heaven. ” (Matthew 22:30, NASB95)

and Paul says:

“But I want you to be free from concern. One who is unmarried is concerned about the things of the Lord, how he may please the Lord; but one who is married is concerned about the things of the world, how he may please his wife, ” (1 Corinthians 7:32-33, NASB95)

Is this desire something placed on us by Western Culture or is it a legitimate desire that God has placed on our hearts. After all the American dream, which seems to have been adopted by the church, is to:

a) get a degree and a job
b) get married and have kids
c) buy a house and work until you can retire and die.

I don’t think this is necessarily God’s dream.

Yosemite Weekend

posted by: Mark @ 11:25 pm

I just got back from a great weekend of hiking at Yosemite. I wasn’t quite sure what to expect as I really haven’t been exposed much to the area nor the people that I went with. Much to my delight, it ended up being a great time. We spent Saturday driving around and taking leisurely hikes, which mostly seemed to be a time just to get to know each other and hang out. It was enjoyable and by the end of the day I felt comfortable with everyone there.

On Sunday we woke up early and made the long trek to Half Dome (or “Half Doom” as a friend calls it). I had no idea what this hike was going to be like, but was amply warned that it was a hard hike. For some reason “hard hike” never equaled “up hill the whole way” in my mind, which is exactly what it turned out to be. Being someone who doesn’t play sports or do any other strenuous activity, besides typing on this keyboard, part of me thinks I did rather well. I was huffing and puffing most the way, which is to be expected, but ended up making it to the bottom of half dome, alive. About 2 miles before I hit that point though, I hit a wall I’ve never really hit that hard before…exhaustion. I just crashed. Up to this point I knew I was pushing myself beyond my limits, but I had never expected to hit the point where I literally could not go on. The people I was with must have been “super-human", because they were going up this hill like it was nothing. Some didn’t even seem to be breaking a sweat. So I waved them along and told them to continue their ascent without me, I had resigned myself to the fact that my body was physically incapable of going any further. It took a bit of persuading but they finally continued their journey up the hill.

Let me take a second to mention something that I had never noticed before. During my hike up this trail I found that having people around me at certain times actually hindered me. When people were around me I just could not focus. It was the weirdest thing. When I was hanging back or was alone up ahead(yes, this really did happen ) I found that I had the strength to continue. I would think about God or His angel that He has assigned to watch over me, and for some reason this strengthed me and I could go on. It also got my mind off the hike, which was nice. Yet when people were around me I started comparing myself to them and getting down that I wasn’t in better shape or wasn’t as smart as they are, or good looking as they are, or one of the million other things you can use to compare yourself with. At any rate, it was interesting to discover that I could push myself harder by myself than I could with other people around.

So back to the hill…I had my friends continue up without me and I just sat there as my mind and my body waged war with each other. My mind trying with all it’s might to force my sore body to continue past a pain it had never felt before(not to mention the blisters all over my feet). As I sat there, up walks another member of our group that we had thought turned back with some others. He was another one of those “Super-humans” who didn’t break a sweat climbing this monsterous mountain. Again, I tried to persuade him to “move along", but he would have none of that. If I had to stop every 3 feet, so be it. “There’s no rush getting to the top", he said. So with every ounce I could muster we continued up the mountain. Though I didn’t stop every 3 feet, it sure seemed like it…and I felt horrible for it. “It just gives me more time to rest", he’d tell me with a smile. I’m sure this was all true, but I still felt bad for having to stop so often, not to mention the snails pace I was going.

That final 2 miles seemed to be the longest in my life. I’m not sure how long it took, but it felt like hours. Upon reaching the bottom of Half Dome I collapsed, feeling proud that I had made it that far. As I sat there twitching , I looked up and saw a set of stairs leading up to the final leg of the trip. I’m not quite sure how far up it went, because, I regret to inform you, I did not continue. After resting for a bit I started up the stairs but ended up heading back down due to my fear of heights. You see, the stairs were nothing but stones stacked on each other on the side of this rock mountain. One false step and there was an easy tumble down to the bottom. So I sat down on one of the rocks considering if I should continue up or not. Around that time this huge gust of wind thunders past me. That was too much. So I headed back down to the base and awaited the rest of the group. I had hiked up hill for over 8 miles only to stop short of reaching the goal by less than a quarter of a mile. To be honest I’m starting to regret that decision, but I’m sure it’s easier to regret when I’m sitting back at home safe and sound…and breathing normally again.

Like I said, all in all, it was a great trip. Though, as usual, I ended the trip feeling down on myself for various reasons. The first thing, as I mentioned previously, was my physical condition. Though I’m sure I did great for someone who isn’t active, that’s not quite good enough for me. I was weak and my weakness affected other people. Nothing serious of course, I just slowed them down, but I hated that weakness. I would have had no problems waiting for someone who was physically weaker, but me being that weak person..I have a big problem with that.

There is more to add, but it’s late and I need rest. Maybe tomorrow I’ll get back to some of the other reasons…we’ll see.

The Old Testament

posted by: Mark @ 1:36 pm

A majority of my lunch times are spent with God. When I first started reading the Bible during lunch I just figured it would be a nice thing to do. Little did I know that it would ultimately become one of my most treasured times in the day. I usually choose a table in the corner of the restaurant, open my Bible to any old place, and just start reading while I eat. Not long afterwards, everything fades away and God joins me. I wish I could describe what it’s like to be with God, but I suppose it’s just as difficult to tell someone what it’s like to spend time with a friend or family member.

The past several years I’ve been drawn to the Old Testament(O.T). I find God to be a lot more revealing in the O.T. than the New Testament, which is why most of my time has been spent in the O.T. I’ll give you some examples of from what I’ve read today(and in the past).

“Therefore, O house of Israel, I will judge you, each one according to his ways, declares the Sovereign LORD. Repent! Turn away from all your offenses; then sin will not be your downfall. Rid yourselves of all the offenses you have commited, and get a new heart and a new spirit. Why will you die, O house of Israel? For I take no pleasure in the death of anyone, declares the Sovereign LORD. Repent and live!” (Ezekiel 18:30-32, NIV)

Can you hear God pleading with Israel? Can you hear His emotion in that? He’s crying out to the Israelites, almost begging them to turn from evil so He doesn’t have to execute judgement on them. God’s desire is again seen in Jeremiah,

“‘The days are coming,’ declares the LORD, ‘when I will raise up to David a righteous Branch, a King who will reign wisely and do what is just and right in the land.’” (Jeremiah 23:5, NIV)

That “righteous Branch” God speaks of is Jesus Christ, but what He expects out of “the King” is also what He expects out of all men; To act wisely and to do what is just and right in the land. You can hear this desire of God echo’d in Micah.

“He has showed you, O man, what is good. And what does the LORD require of you? To act justly and to love mercy and to walk humbly with your God.” (Micah 6:8, NIV)

Do you hear the messanger of God pleading with the people of Israel? “Act Justly!” “Love Mercy!” “Walk humbly with God!". These are the things God desires and seeks after. I think it’s with disappointment and sadness when God says the following,

“The righteous perish, and no one ponders it in his heart;” (Isaiah 57:1, NIV)

God’s passions and desires seem to shout out to me in Isaiah 58.

“For day after day they[Israel] seek me out; they seem eager to know my ways, as if they were a nation that does what is right and has not forsaken the commands of its God. They ask me for just decisions and seem eager for God to come near them. ‘Why have we fasted,’ they say, ‘and you have not seen it? Why have we humbled ourselves and you have not noticed?’ Yet on the day of your fasting, you do as you please and exploit all your workders. Your fasting end in quarreling and strife, and in striking each other with wicked fists. You cannot fast as you do today and expect your voice to be heard on high. Is this the kind of fast I have chosen, only a day for a man to humble himself? Is it only for bowing one’s head like a reed and for lying on sackcloth and ashes? Is that what you call a fast, a day acceptable to the LORD?” (Isaiah 58:2-5, NIV)

Israel accused God of not listening to them. “Hey, we are doing what you commanded, why aren’t you answering us?!” they shout at God. I picture God shaking His head and sadly saying, “You just don’t get it do you?” So God tells Israel what He sees them doing and then says what they should be doing….

“Is not this the kind of fasting I have chosen: to loose the chains of injustice and untie the cords of the yoke, to set the oppressed free and break every yoke? Is it not to share your food with the hungry and to provide the poor wanderer with shelter–when you see the naked, to clothe him, and not to turn away from your own flesh and blood?” (Isaiah 58:6-7, NIV)

Again, God’s passion comes out. “Love one another!", He shouts. “Be kind to others!", He begs. “Do what is good and right!", he pleads.

The Old Testament is full with God’s emotions, desires and passions. It’s why I love spending so much time reading it.
