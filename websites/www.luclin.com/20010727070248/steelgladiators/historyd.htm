Dakhanavar's story

Dakhanavar was born in Rivervale, Antonica, to whimsical, funny parents, the third in a batch of four young halfing boys. Oftentimes considered to be the best looking of the bunch, Dakhanavar developed a saucy, amusingly sarcastic persona, and as he grew into a strapling, he was best know as the city's secret practical joker. Some feats he accomplished were gluing the feet of a Kaladim guard to his camp-post, and carving a happy face into the back-head of the lunatic jack-o'-lantern as he wandered the graveyards. Rarely was he punished, however, since his parents laughed along with Dakhanavar's exploits and pranks, and even participated in them in a red moon. 

With his brothers and cousins, the Ragnaroks ran amok for years, taking in official training at various temples and organizations. Dakhanavar, believing that with the powers of a Druid, he could play jokes on friendly animals, joined the Druid guild in Rivervale, and was greatly pleased by the spells presented to him. 

 The Game

One eve, an old paramour approached him with smirking eyes as he tipped back Dwarven ale and stared at the Norrathian moon. Keta was a flighty girl halfling who was undergoing the Warrior training, and while Dakhanavar and her had been amorous once or twice, her pride and showmanship turned him away, considering the fact that he himself believed that he was the one meant for show, not her. Beyond that fact, he had seen other sorts of races come and go from Rivervale, and was fascinated by these tall, piercingly beautiful Elves who ducked into huts for rations and roots. A fellow halfing woman didn't tickle his fancy any longer, and Keta was fully aware of that. 

She challenged him to a duel "for old time's sake", she chirped out as she drew out her fine steel scimitar and tossed it from hand to hand. "Until one cannot go on any longer, then shall we stop our duel." 

Dakhanavar agreed, and brandishing his Morning Star, they circled the other and began their duel. Fine steel clanged against the other underneath the moon, and Dakhanavar laughed out loud as a blast of fire from his hand caused Keta to stumble and miss him completely. 

Keta angered red, and swung her scimitar violently, slashing Dakhanavar deeply along the arm. "'Tis why I resent you so, Dakhanavar Ragnarok! Your careless laughter in place of real emotion!" 

"And what have I for "real emotion"? Dakhanavar sneered playfully, sneaking another spell to intertwine and root Keta's feet to the grassy ground with green rings of mana. "You were a phase, a way to pass the time until you became so serious. Like all warriors, nothing between the ears but wax and grits of sand." 

"If I have sand betwixt my ears, then so do you!" Keta shouted, struggling to free her feet. "I am a Halfing as yourself, and we are no different! Tis' our destiny to end up together in some form, Dakhanavar, or do you still dream of Elven women?" 

Dakhanavar felt himself go red, since that was a personal sort of thing to him, and to hear the suddenly furious Keta bellowing those words for all in Rivervale to hear made his blood boil. 

"Aye, and what makes you believe that a snobbery Elf would ever fancy you, Dakhanavar? You are short and ugly, with feet larger and hairier than your own head! You might be preferrin' the outsiders to your own kind, but an Elven woman will never prefer you. And nor will any other female in Rivervale, af'er I tell them what you are truly like." 

Dakhanavar felt his face grow red with anger and embarrassment, and without thinking clearly, he drew up his mana into his hands and fired his most draining, violent spell at Keta who ricocheted away and landed hard on the grassy knoll, barely able to breathe. 

The news spread of Keta's defeat at the hands of Dakhanavar, but he cared not for their whispering words of shock and distrust, shunning their looks by using his wolf form illusion to pass among them secretly. And although he still laughed with his brothers, his blood was starting to run cold. A dark angel's sort of blood, he mused to himself in private, part good, part evil, but never more than the other. 

It came to pass that after seasons of Druid experience that Dakhanavar Ragnarok was known as the most dangerous druid in Antonica. The guild of the Steel Gladiators, and Darkreaper in particular, grew curious of this druid who remained in the form of a wolf almost constantly, and was of a higher season than all of them. 

And although he agreed with all the others to propose and invite the druid into the guild's fold, secretly Darkreaper felt the need for a challenge, new blood to wet his sword with. The others were either do-gooders or friends, neither ripe for deadly dueling; in fact, most of them turned away from it. 

When Darkreaper mockily bumped into Dakhanavar from behind, the druid's first instinct was to fry him with a lightning bolt. And after some short, but meaningful conversation, including Darkreaper yanking on the tail of Dakhanavar, it was decided that Darkreaper and Dakhanavar would duel to initiate him into the guild. 

Galathor objected, as did Raventod, insisting that if Dakhanavar wished, he was free to join without initiation, but it was already a pact. Bastar found it amusing, but Raventod even went so close as to stand between the two, prompting Dakhanavar to angrily call him an ass and to step aside. 

The bellows and barks of pain echoed throughout the West Commons. Spells were flung, swords flashed and caught the sunlight, teeth were bared. Blood spilt onto nearby trees, some poisoned, some diseased. In the end, however, Darkreaper was grinning like an idiot. He was sprawled on the grass, bleeding to death, and thoroughly defeated by Dakhanavar. 

Galathor stuck out his hand, reluctant to see his brother wounded, but convinced. "Welcome to the Steel Gladiators." 

Dakhanavar stared at him and silently licked his wounds. 

It should be noted at this point that within the guild, Dakhanavar was one of the most recognized and respected. There was silent respect for Galthor, a name-calling competition between him and Raventod, favors and amiabilities with Celandra and Perro, and a not-so-friendly rivalry with Bastar Odahola. His wry jokes returned to him, but his wolf form remained a constant thing. 

It's difficult to say exactly when Dakhanavar became smitten with Risa, and she with him, but I believe it's safe to say that he always thought her Wood Elven self beautiful. It made for much joking and flirting throughout the guild, and when the time came for their wedding, they both took on the form of wolves for the official ceremonies. 

And nowadays, he has softened a touch, but not enough to make it safe to make jokes about him.
