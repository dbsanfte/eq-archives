Galathor, Darkreaper, Raventod and 
the creation of the Steel Gladiators

Many moons ago, on a farm in the plains of Karanas, a young couple tended their crops with care. They assisted traveling adventurers by offering their wares at fair and equitable prices. They lived happily for many years, thriving on the fertile soil and enjoying the warm sun of Norrath.

The husband, a former member of the Freeport Militia called Heimdall, was a strong-backed man. When asked why he left the military life, he would pause, smile, and wink. Nothing more. The wife, a talented seamstress from Queynos named Alvare, loved her husband deeply and had only one true desire, a child of her own.

As their love grew, so did their family. First came their son Galathor. His eyes bright with hope and promise of a future of nobility and courage, the boy was the apple of his father’s eye. For many years, he grew strong under the tutelage of Heimdall, learning self defense as well as honor from a very young age.  

When Galathor reached his 6th year, a dark and stormy night kept him awake.

ame


Galathor watched as his father herded their livestock into the barn to keep them from the dangerous winds and rain.His father vanished into the barn and did not come out. Galathor waited for an hour, but did not see his father exit the barn. Concerned, the boy turned to investigate and ran into his father…or what he thought was his father.

The man whom Galathor had trusted his whole life glared down at him with a sinister look. Galathor knew instantly that this was an imposter and backed away in fear and anger. The doppelganger swung violently with his arm and sent the young boy crashing to the floor, unconscious. For an instant, Galathor saw the image of the man shimmer and saw him for what he truly was, an evil necromancer stepping into his mother’s room.

The next morning, Heimdall came staggering out of the barn, the victim of a powerful sleep spell. His memory unclear, he found his wife alone in bed calling to him.

The details of what transpired next are unclear to Galathor. It is obvious that his parents knew what had happened, but when it became evident that his mother was with child again, nothing of the incident was ever spoke of. They had chosen to raise the child as their very own.

Their second son, Darkreaper was born on a night as stormy as the one on which he was conceived. It was an omen of things to come. As the two boys grew together, their differences were obvious. Galathor had gotten the best of his honorable parents, but Darkreaper was polluted with the hate of his devilish father. It seemed that it was only the pure blood and love of his mother that kept the young man stable.

The boys learned the ways of battle from their powerful father. Galathor was always anxious for the next lesson, learning his father’s ways quickly. Darkreaper seemed less privy to the schooling on honor than on killing methods.

Regardless, both young men grew strong, quickly surpassing their father in hand-to-hand and weapon combat. Galathor fought with all of the honor in his heart, Darkreaper with the hate deep within his soul. Little did they know that one day, they would be forced to put their skills to the ultimate test.

Meanwhile, in Felwithe, a young High Elven warrior princess named Kalana set out on a quest to deliver a message to the Warrior’s Guild in Queynos. Her father, a famous Elven General who held court over a small part of Antonica, set up the journey to strengthen his daughter and introduce her to the world. She traveled with the grizzled old Dwarven soldier Horgoth who was to make sure her majesty returned to her father in once piece.

Their journey was fraught with peril from Orcs, Goblins, and vicious animals. Their first safe harbor was Freeport, where they were forced to stay at a small inn for the evening. While Horgoth fell into a well-deserved Dwarven slumber, the ever-curious Kalana snuck to the city gate to watch the sun rise above the Antonican hills. A handsome young human thief named Ranthal slinked next to the attractive young elf. A bit of fast-talking and strong mead later, the two found themselves sharing a bearskin blanket for the evening.

The next morning Kalana was surprisingly satisfied and met up with Horgoth outside the inn, using the excuse that she awoke early in anticipation of their trip. The two set out once again and headed towards the Plains of Karanas.

Two months later, they arrived in Queynos. Kalana had come down ill, heaving her breakfast each morning onto the grasses of Queynos Hills. A quick trip to a local shaman confirmed what neither was really expecting. The princess was with child.

Forced to confess her midnight tryst, Kalana found herself with an enraged Dwarf. How could Horgoth return the princess to her father so deflowered? His mind was made up; the child was to be left with its father, if he could be found.

Heartbroken, Kalana resigned herself to the truth. Her father would not accept a half-breed child and thus, she must agree to Horgoth’s plan. They decided to stay in Queynos until the half-elf was born. Just five months later, Raventod came into the world in secrecy. His mother bundled him tightly two days after his birth and then set out for Freeport.

When they arrived back in the eastern port city, Kalana searched all around for Ranthal, even going as far as visiting a Thieves’ Guild beneath the city.  No luck.

One day, a courier arrived with a message from an unknown source. It was a single rolled up scroll that read:

            Ranthal is dead. Seek him no further.

Desperate, Horgoth decided that the saga had to end and he ordered Kalana to leave the baby outside the Hall of Truth one evening in hopes that a noble paladin would take the child in. Kalana, teary-eyed kissed her son goodbye for the last time and shuffled off into the darkness.

Raventod would stay there for several hours until his screams would alert a local Bard mistress named Dorleen. She took the young boy in and raised him as one of her own.

Raventod grew quickly, as most Half-Elvens do, and always held onto his sense of humor. His striking good looks and charisma made him an instant favorite at Marsheart’s Chords. His teen years were ripe with wine, women, and song. What he did not know was that the best was yet to come.

Back in the Plains of Karana, Galathor and Darkreaper were coming of age and the time had arrived for them to choose their path. Heimdall had hoped that both his sons would seek military careers, serving Antonica for the greater good in the war against Orcs and Dervish Cutthroats. However, both young men had other ideas.

Galathor had read of the exploits of the holy warriors known as paladins. He longed to fight on the side of the just with his God on his sleeve. Darkreaper, on the other hand, had a burning desire to embrace the hatred in his heart and learn the ways of  Shadow Knights. A strong fist of evil would always prevail, or so he believed.

On the night that the brothers were to tell their father what they each had decided, disaster struck. A war between some soldiers and local bandits had spilled onto their farm. Heimdall told his sons to stay behind and protect their mother and ran into the thick of the swordplay. He tried to push the battle from his land, but found himself in the midst of the fray. The bandits had forced the soldiers to retreat and soon Heimdall was alone against five of them. He managed to slay two before himself being stabbed in the back.

As they watched their father drop to his knees in defeat, the brothers had distinctly different thoughts. Galathor’s heart filled with the desire for vengeance while Darkreaper boggled at the thought of bloodshed. Both had the same thought about their mother, though. They had to protect her at all costs.

They watched as the bandits pushed the soldiers even further back. Alvare pleaded with her sons to stay by her side. Though both wanted to taste the blood of true battle, they obeyed. They would get their chance in short order, though, as the two last remaining soldiers retreated from the bandits into their house.

Galathor and Darkreaper grabbed their weapons and stood beside the soldiers as the bandits fought their way into the house. The fight came to them quickly and they did their father proud. Unfortunately, the bandits had greater numbers, and magic, and the last two soldiers did not remain on their feet very long.

It came down to four bandits against the two brothers. As the men advanced, they noticed a fifth outside casting a spell. They looked to their mother, who stood bravely, but her eyes betrayed her true fear for her sons. She charged forward, and in a last act of bravery, she threw herself in front of a bolt of fire that had been aimed at her boys.

Both Galathor and Darkreaper felt the rage well inside them at the sight of their mother’s sacrifice. In mere seconds, they had slain four bandits and were after the magic user outside. Unable to complete his incantation, the last bandit fell beneath the vengeful steel of the two men.

A month later, Galathor and Darkreaper had sold their farm and decided to seek out their destinies in the city of Freeport. Galathor enlisted in the Hall of Truth, Darkreaper at the Temple of Marr. In short order, they had their initial training and set out into the commons to seek out quests.

One afternoon, while the brothers shared a meal under a tree in East Commons, the young Bard Raventod met up with them. His Mandolin in hand he asked the brothers if they minded his company as they battled the lesser creatures of the land. While skeptical, both agreed that his song made them feel more confident in combat. In addition, Galathor enjoyed the Half-Elf’s sense of humor and optimism while Darkreaper knew that the magic of song would make him a more effective killing machine.

On that day, an alliance was forged. Those three men would come together and unite in steel, vowing to spend their days battling in the world of Norrath. They became the Steel Gladiators.

Galathor, the eldest, took the reigns as leader and General. Darkreaper would settle for second-in-command, and Raventod, although frowning upon the responsibility of leadership, took the post of Bard-at-arms, third in command.

Each day, Galathor and Darkreaper struggle over the direction of the Steel Gladiators. Do they fight for honor and nobility and seek to bring peace to the land of Norrath, or are they simply out to destroy any creature, good or bad, they stands in their way? Raventod finds himself trying to mediate between the two brothers.

One by one, more members began to appear. First came the mighty wizard Bastar, whose hot temper and questionable motives were lapped up by Darkreaper. The cleric Thunderblade also found his way to the Steel Gladiators, and his healing and resurrection abilities were a welcome addition to the guild.

But those are other stories...
