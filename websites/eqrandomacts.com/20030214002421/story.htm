Chapter Ten by Meriel Silvertree

 

The Wood Elf crumples up the wrapping from the sandwich she just finished eating and tosses it in her backpack dusting off the seat of her pants as she gets up from her place under the tree. Stretching out the kinks from sitting so long, she surveys the valley spread out below noticing the Dwarves have finished the temple to her friend Neao and that the flowers that had been planted are growing very nicely. She sees that the vegetables are just starting to pop up from the well tended rows, and wonders what delicacies Hama, Gwion and Aganitte had been able to forage and if any of them will take root here.

 

As she slowly makes her way down to the temple she notices for the first time the six mounded buildings surrounding the temple on three sides. Smoke is lazily twirling from an oven beside one of the mounds and as she heads in that direction she sees Grimmfaith closing the door with a large wooden ladle filled with heavenly scented elven bread.

 

As she approaches, Grimmfaith swings around with a big grin telling her that he's just finished the baking and seven little ones are on their way to take up residence along with a half-elf teacher and a halfling husband and wife who will help him oversee everything. "Come see the sleeping quarters" he leers wiggling his eyebrows, but wise to his ways she laughs and says "I'll just sit on the bench here in the sunshine waiting until the little ones arrive."

 

"A lot of care has gone into erecting the temple for our brother Neao and I never thought I'd see so many orc heads gleaming in the light" she said as she shaded her eyes with a hand over her brow. "How proud he would be to see all of this and know his brothers and sisters will carry on helping others and bringing peace to at least a small portion of Norrath in his honor".

 

Grimmfaith nods and asks if she's brought the oak seedlings. "Oh yes", she said pulling plantings from her backpack, "we'll get the bigger children to help in planting them down by the stream and around behind the mounded living quarters. Leldara has drawn me a plan designed to be pleasing to the eye and to bring the most shade in the hot summer days and allow the sun to shine warmly on cold wintry afternoons. I was fortunate to run into Dolumar who donated all the foraged plants he's been collecting, which include some very nice Norrathian ivy that Leldara said would work wonders planted down by the temple to weave in and out of all those skulls."

 

Before she has to take off and meet Varigon at the Lake of Ill Omen along with their friends Maitlin and Gorte, Meriel rests her head back against the mound enjoying the feel of the warm sunshine. The breeze floating through the valley softly cools her cheeks with the scent of freshly baked bread and she faintly hears the songs and giggling laughter of children wending their way through the woods to their new home, safe here in the valley of Neao.

Chapter Twelve by Azariel Baalzebub

 

Uriel had whispered of Neao's dark death with remorse and respect near Kithicor Woods. "He was one of my traveling companions..he was my friend". Azariel heard Uriel's voice waver in emotion. Not prone to such emotions himself, being a bone caster, Az was not used to such things. But, yet he grieved for his half-brother.

 

Now, standing on a small hill over looking the vast plains of the Overthere, Azariel witnessed something amazing. Neao's identical twin brother, Neaoh kiting a Sarnkak Constrict...a devilish creature who looked to be quite a gamble for the young druid. He watched as Neaoh raced in and with twitching fingers cast Snare on the mob. As the Sarnak slowly ran toward Neaoh, he was engulfed in a swarm. Nuke after nuke, Neaoh tore into the Constrict until it decided it was best to turn the other direction and run. The last spell did the trick and the Sarnak collapsed to the ground.

 

Impressive Azariel thought. He had heard infamous stories of the older twin Neao. A druid by trade, but a fighter at heart..often getting himself into marvelous trouble. Azariel's mouth touched on the hint of a smile. "Probably what got the bugger killed", he thought to himself.

 

But this twin was different. Already at 20 seasons, he appeared to have quite the handle on his methods of attack. He had barely even raised his Screaming Mace for attack. The necro new the answer...this brother was different, better and much wiser. It would not be his destiny to have the same fate as the late Neao Spellwielder.

 

"I will keep an eye out for this one", Azariel thought to himself, "who knows...there have been stranger things than Necromancers grouping with druids"

 

Azariel chuckled to himself as he ordered his pet to follow. "One day Neaoh the druid, we will meet and group and conquer a great many things!"

 

Thoughts of Giants entered his mind as Azariel walked into a Kunark sunset prepared to raise the dead.

Chapter Fourteen by Grimmbaldis Stormwolfe

 

Grimmbaldis huffed up the path to Neao's College. His time in Velius had weathered him. Gray now sprinkled his light brown hair and more wrinkles were visible around his eyes. His battles had left a permanent mark upon him, the scars on his face telling stories of those who were good...just not quite good enough.

 

As he stepped into the valley, he saw all that had been done in his abscence. Neao's tomb still stood in a place of honor overlooking the busy college below. Children sat in the grass listening to instructors. The neat, well groomed landscape with its new buildings was breathtaking. It was as close to a home as he had ever envisioned.

 

When Grimmbaldis neared Neao's tomb, he saw an addition had been made. A fresh grave was next to Neao's. With heavy steps, Grimmbaldis approached the grave, almost unwilling to know who of his comrades had fallen. Grimmfaith met Grimmbaldis at the gravesite. The short, chubby halfling cleric's smile faded as he watched Grimmbaldis approach.

 

"Tis Beretta's" he said. "Her stone has not yet been placed. Tunare has called her home far too soon for our liking. But such is the will of the Gods."

 

Grimmbaldis knelt in the still damp earth, a tear coursing down his cheek from his one eye. His thoughts replaying the many adventures he had shared with the bright young druidess. She had been like a kid sister to him. Always venturing a little further than was safe. Always eager to explore a little further.

 

As his tears fell to the rich soil, a tendril pushed its way up. Before his eyes a rose bush grew. It blossomed full of small pink wild roses.

 

"She is well." a voice whispered in his ear. "She is in a good place and well-loved. Mourn her not for as long as you remember her, she is with you."

 

Grimmbaldis knew that the voice was that of Tunare. He had never worshipped the Mother of All, preferring instead to worship and find strength in the raging storms of Karana. Yet he never doubted the Goddess had spoken to him.

 

With a weary sigh, Grimmbaldis heaved himself to his feet. "We will need someone to watch over the plants here. Perhaps a young druid from the school would accept the job." was all the middle-aged warrior said. His armor rattling, he hefted the pack full of gifts for the children and started his way down the hill.

Standing now at the graves of his brother and Beretta, Neaoh prayed that this would be the last time he would have to make a trip here to say good bye to a friend but in his heart he knew that more would pass on and he would be here again saying good bye.

 

 

Chapter Eighteen by Grimmbaldis Stormwolfe

Grimmbaldis had spent several months at the Tribute School, teaching and thawing out from Velious. The school was progressing well with many children from all over Norrath attending. Grimmfaith had informed him earlier that he was getting inquiries from parents as well. It seemed that word of the school had spread. Perhaps some of the orphans could be adopted into the local familes. It would be something to check into later.

 

Drinking his ale, Grimmbaldis looked around the main hall where he sat. Hanging from the rafters and on the walls were momentos of past battles and adventures. Tattered banners hung next to broken weapons, each with it's own tale to tell. The main hall had turned into a sort of museum for the Guild. Any usable gear was given to the younglings, but here the unrepairable had found a home. Rather than being a dark and dusty place, the hall had more of an air of comfortable clutter. It was a favorite spot of the older Guild members as well as the

younglings. An ale or three could usually get 'Aunty' Leldara to telling tales, some of which were even true.

 

All in all, Grimmbaldis was impressed with the way things had turned out. The bloody memorial to his fallen friend had turned into center of peace and education. Nowhere else could the offspring of Dark Elves be seen playing with Humans and Dwarves. Here, racial prejudice was not allowed. Those who could not overcome their hatred of another were found sponsors amongst their own race.

 

Grimmbaldis had already seen several truly mighty combat teams beginning to form at the school. The students mixed and matched Race and Class in amazing ways, their young minds unfettered by the prejudices of their elders. Grimmbaldis would never have thought of combining an Ogre Warrior with an Erudite Enchanter, a Wood Elf Druid, and two Dark Elf Necromancers. The Gnome Wizard who had joined them had made an almost unstoppable combat team. They were capable of taking on monsters much more powerful than themselves. It would be interesting to see what other surprises sprang from the school...after his trip to

Velious.

 

Hamadryad had stepped down from her position as Guild Leader and found Grimmbaldis dozing in the sun outside Qeynos. The two old friends had decided to journey to the frozen land of Velious, picking up friends along the way. They had stopped at the school so Hamadryad could clear her mind of the stresses of leadership and oversee the transition of power to the Council. They were almost ready to begin their journey, as soon as some more of their party managed to arrive at the school. But until then, Grimmbaldis planned on relaxing and gathering more items from the nearby dungeons to help finance a new project. One designed to teach Trade Skills to the younglings. Adventuring was not always profitable, and everyone needed a skill to fall back on in the lean times.

 

 

Chapter Nineteen by Grimmfaith

 

Grimmfaith wiped sweat from his brow and stepped back to survey his handiwork. Dwarves from Kaladim had heard of the valley and sent a huge polished marble block to the school. What they expected to be done with it was a mystery but Grimmfaith had decided to put it to good use.

The stout little cleric new first hand how fleeting paper was as a medium so he had commissioned a set of stone working tools and gone to work. The gleaming block now sat in the middle of the courtyard leading to the temple. Incsribed onto all 4 sides were the words of Hamadryad, their founder. They were carved deep into the marble in every written language spoken in Norrath.

 

"WE BELIEVE THAT EVERYTHING WE DO HAS AN IMPACT ON

EVERYONE ELSE.WHEN WE HAVE LOVE IN OUR HEARTS WE HEAL

THE WORLD. WE DON'T EVEN HAVE TO STEP OUTSIDE OUR DOORS

TO DO IT. BECAUSE LOVE IS REAL. AND KINDNESS IS LOVE.

- HAMADRYAD LONG"

 

Grimmfaith took a long swig from the chilled bota next to his toolbox and surveyed his finsihed product. His mind began to wander back all the members of Random Acts he had known growing up as Grimmbaldis' adopted nephew. A more eclectic bunch he had never seen. Human, Elven, Gnome, Troll, Dark Elf...all banded together under the banner inspired by the words of Hamadryad. Each striving to do their best to live up to the conduct expected of them, not by society but

by their peers and themselves. For some it was no great task, for

others a near insurmountable obstacle. Yet each tried. Some succeeded. Some failed. A few original members were still active in the guild while others had wandered down different paths. Each had contributed a part of themselves to Random Acts and the guild was stronger for it.

 

Grimmfaith gazed about the bustling courtyard. Everywhere were children and instructors. Lush gardens bloomed in the springtime weather and the buildings shown with fresh paint. He wondered if the original few ever contemplated their dreams would materialize like this. He himself had often marveled at the tenacity shown by the members of Random Acts. Once, while desperately trying to heal

the broken and bleeding body of his uncle Grimmbaldis, Grimmfaith had seen a tattoo over the middle aged Warrior's heart. It was faded by time and disfigured by scars, but he could still make out the RA symbol and the words "Where Ever I Go". Later, while the grizzled Warrior was recovering the young Cleric had asked his uncle what the tattoo meant. Uncle Grimm had only smiled and told him that one day the meaning would come to him.

 

Grimmfaith had been confused at first, but as time passed the meaning began to dawn on him. Other guilds wore gaudy tunics and trumpeted their prowess to the heavens. Random Acts quietly went about it's business and watched many other guilds fall to internal strife and bickering. Through all the seasons, Random Acts stood firm. And everywhere a member went, they carried the words of Hamadryad in their hearts.

 

"I think I am beginning to understand, Uncle Grimm" mused Grimmfaith. The young Cleric gathered his tools and gave the marble block a fond pat. "I think, maybe, I am starting to get it."

 

 

Chapter Twenty by Grimmbaldis Stormwolfe

 

Grimmbaldis stopped in mid stride. Something was not right in Neao's Valley. Snatching up his sword, the scarred warrior expertly buckled on his armor as he ran up the hill towards Neao's Tomb. If someone had dared to disturb the resting place of his friend, then blood would flow in rivers!

 

Cresting the hill, Grimmbaldis saw the small form of a gnome kneeling in front to the tomb. The gnome's peaceful attitude was all the kept his neck attached to his shoulders. Coming closer, Grimmbaldis recognized one of his oldest friends, Jellybean.

 

The small gnome did not say a word. He smiled and bowed to Grimmbaldis. Yet, when Grimmbaldis went to sweep his friend up an a hug of greeting, his arms passed right through him. Then he knew that his friend no longer walked the land of the living. The realization that Jelly no longer lived struck his heart like a mallet. Tears coursed down his leathery cheek as Grimmbaldis sank to the ground in front of the apparition.

 

Jelly's ghost took on an concerned expression. "Nooo" whispered the spectre. "Weep not, old friend. I am in a good and safe place. It is a brand new adventure I embark on. Weep not for meee."

 

Before Grimmbaldis' startled eyes, the ghost of Jelly began to shine brightly. A rift appeared next to the tomb of Neao and from it a small statue materialized. "Here. I have provided my own monument." said Jellybean. "I will always look down on the valley and watch over it. My shade shall visit from time to time. Be at peace, Warrior. We will be rejoined some day."

 

With the glowing good cheer that earned him the name Goodheart, Jellybean smiled at Grimmbaldis. "Adventure calls. I will scout ahead and see what lies beyond." With a final wave, Jellybean's ghost faded away, leaving only the sound of the wind in the spring leaves and the tears of a man seeing yet one more friend pass from this world falling to the ground.

 

Below him, Grimmbaldis could see the torches of his friends weaving through the valley. Obviously he was not the only one to feel the passing of one of the founders of their guild. Something was gone from them all that night. The passing of a Great One always left the remaining ones feeling a little bit empty inside. A little saddened that the world of Norrath would never again know the kindness, generousity and caring of Jellybean Goodheart.

 

 

Chapter Twenty-One by Gwion Llewellyn

 

Standing at the druid ring, Gwion saw a bright flash of light. Quickly, she turned herself into a wolf and ran toward the graveyard. When she got there, she found only Grimmbaldis, kneeling near a tiny statue.

 

"Old friend," she said, "Was it --" she said, but stopped when she saw a lone tear trickling from the warrior's remaining eye. She looked at the little stone gnome, and nodded. Jellybean Goodheart and his sister Ravenmyst Peaceheart had left Norrath for the unknown world beyond.

 

Gwion had finished her tears on the way to Misty Thicket. Hours before, her heart had told her that her friends were gone. Changing back into a wood elf, she brought a long, curving mammoth tusk from her pack.

 

"That's for you, Jelly," she said, laying it on the ground near the statue. "Dear wee Jelly. It's much bigger than you are! But I know how much you loved them. And this" -- she produced a garland of ivy -- "Is for your sister." She draped the ivy around the statue's neck. "It's enchanted, you know, so it will live always."

 

"Ye've seen." Gwion hadn't heard Grimmfaith coming.

 

"Yes." Gwion turned to face the little cleric. She pointed to Grimmbaldis. "Will he be all right?"

 

"Seems he's seen the shade."

 

"Oh, that was the light I saw."

 

Grimmfaith nodded, then began to sweep up some stray dirt. "A bit upsetting for a warrior. Just leave him sit. Go on into the main hall now, I'll bring him later if he doesn't come himself." He looked toward the steadily approaching line of torches, much closer now. "The others will be here soon and I want this to look nice when they come."

 

Shouldering her pack, Gwion tried to picture the place Jellybean and Ravenmyst were now. A nice forest, she thought, like this one. In her mind the brother and sister walked together, approaching a cozy home full of firelight that beckoned through the mist. She heard the singing, then the welcoming laughter of new friends as she saw them open the door.

 

Smiling through her own sadness, Gwion turned toward the main hall.

 

 

Chapter Twenty-two by Uriel Trueshot

 

Uriel saw the wolf streak past him, heading in the same direction he was going...Neao's grave. He visited on a regular basis, seeing the improvements, smiling of practical jokes.

 

Gwion had not seen Uriel as she ran to the shrine of friends. He hid in the shadows, always visiting the grave alone. He saw Gwion and Grimfaith, the nephew of Grimmbaldis he had never met, speaking quietly. With his elven sight he could swear he saw tears in both of their eyes. Why, he wondered?

 

Eventually they two wandered off, speaking in hushed tones, lost in saddness.

 

From out of the shadows Uriel appeared and wandered up to a fresh grave. He saw the ivy and the tusk, then understood.

 

"Ohh, my little friend" he whispered, "where have you gone now?" Only the wind answered. Uriel had met Jellybean on several occasions and held many conversations with him. Sharp as a tack and even so more kind and cheerful.

 

"This land will miss you, as will your friends. I hope you can make it back to us one day"...his voice trailed off trembling.

 

Reaching over his shoulder, Uriel unstraped his Trueshot Bow. Placing a bone arrow he aimed for the sky."This arrow is you, ever soaring, never falling." He released the arrow and it rocketed to the blue sky.

 

In seasons to come rumors would travel of an arrow shot in Jellybean's name that never returned to Norrath.

Chapter Twenty-four by Grimmbaldis Stormwolfe

 

The wake quieted as Grimmbaldis walked into the hall. His face haggard, the Warrior placed the large bundle in his arms on the floor. Without a word, he unrolled the waxed cloth and started to remove a strange assortment of items.Last to come out was a bundle of daggers. With blows so forceful that even the Ogres in the room flinched, Grimmbaldis drove the daggers into the wall. From each he hung an item that once belonged to the fallen members of the guild he loved so dearly.

 

"This is our Wall of Fame." declared Grimmbaldis. "I have kept a momento of each of my friends that have passed to the next world. I feel shamed that I have kept them to myself for so long. As more fall...and they will in time...I will continue to add to this wall so that future generations of Random Acts will know of those who gave all to secure their legacy. I believe that without our history, we will

have no future. Thus endeth my mourning for Jellybean Goodheart."

 

With that, the scarred Warrior filled his horn and looked about the suddenly silent hall. A slow smile spread across his face and his one eye took on a merry twinkle. "Now, let me tell ye about the time me and Jelly decided he needed a new robe and took on Ol' Najena herself! A glorious battle it was..."

 

His audience smiled and others who had taken part in the raid began to fill in the gaps in Grimmbaldis' tale. Laughter rang out as the stories grew more and more outlandish long into the night.
