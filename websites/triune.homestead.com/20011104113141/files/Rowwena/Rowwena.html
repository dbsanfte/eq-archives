Biography

Born an orphan in the city of Halas, Rowwena Morrigan was raised by her adopted parents, the MacGroves, and grew up in their small shop along Halas's main road.  Although she loved her adoptive parents, Rowwena knew from a very young age that she could never be happy in the life of a shopkeeper. Her spirit was just too adventurous to live out her days behind the counter of the Golden Torc, and she longed to leave Halas and see the rest of Norrath.

When she was still very young, Rowwena spent much of her free time around the field of blood watching her idol, the warrior champion Lysbith McNarth. Armed with a rusty two-hander taken from her parents shop, Rowwena went to train with McNarith who was always anxious to take promising young women under her wing to teach them the art of battle.  Rowwena quickly proved herself to be a remarkable student and through her studies she soon became convinced that her real mother had been a warrior as well, and that it was thus her destiny to take up the sword. Although her adoptive parents objected to Rowwena's plans, they knew in their hearts that they could not hold her back. And so it was that on one especially cold morning, equipped only with the meager supply of armor and weapons that her parents could afford to provide for her, Rowwena set out across Norrath to begin her career as a warrior.

Seeking to put as much distance as possible between herself and her native Halas, Rowwena's travels led her to Greater Faydark. It was here, in the land of the elves, where she found the patronage of the great wizard Sadira, whose kindness and support of the young warrior would never be forgotten.  Making her new home in the city of Felwithe, Rowwena found a family in the Triune Alliance, a guild started by the wise cleric Ciara, who became an older sister to the young barbarian warrior. Along with a new friend, the wizard Inerite, Rowwena began to make a name for herself in Faydark, quickly gaining the respect of the local guards and merchants.

Everything seemed to be going well for Rowwena until one sad day when her dear friend, Inerite, announced that she was going away. Feeling too much sadness to remain in Faydark, Rowwena set out for the Hills of Qeynos, to begin once again. As was the case in Faydark, Rowwena quickly gained the trust and admiration of the locals as she began to master her skills while fighting in the foul caverns of Blackburrow. Yet all of the achievements she accomplished in Qeynos could not take away the pain and loneliness that she felt in the loss of her friend. Matters were made even worse when Ciara’s sudden decision to leave the guild began a process of reorganization that Rowwena felt was too much for her to handle. After a short visit with her parents in Halas, Rowwena moved to the busy city of Freeport and there she seemed to happily vanish into the crowd.

Then one day, just as mysteriously as she had vanished, Rowwena appeared again along the beaches of the Oasis of Marr. It was here that she became friends with a Dark Elven Cleric named Ecclavdra. Although very much different, this unlikely pair quickly became a team, traveling between Freeport and the Karanas in search of adventure and riches. While Rowwena believed that the two of them made the best team possible, their partnership was ended when Ecclavdra made a sudden decision to return to private practice in Neriak. Although Rowwena felt another huge loss in Ecclavdra’s departure, this change would not be as devastating, as Ecclavdra had introduced Rowwena to a young necromancer named Iggwilv. The two became friends almost immediately and began to travel Norrath together making their names known.  Feeling renewed with her new friendship, Rowwena also made the decision to finally become active in her guild once again. For too long she had sat quietly and watched events unfold that she might have prevented. She knew that keeping the guild together was much like keeping a family together, that everyone needed to play an active role, and that she was going to start doing her part.

What followed were the happiest days that Rowwena had known since her youth in Faydark. She had a trusted friend in Iggwilv, and a growing circle of friends that included a smart druid named Evalyn and a wise cleric named Windalyn,  who in so many ways reminded her of her long lost sister Ciara. Finally, this circle of friends was completed by Rowwena’s reunion with Dyanna, a student of Sadira who had befriended her in her youth and was now returned after a long absence. Among this close circle of friends, and other members of the Triune family, Rowwena moved quickly to master her skills in weaponry, and to build her reputation as a warrior. Rowwena had finally found her place.

To Rowwena, this great fortune seemed all too good to last, and in many ways it was. A freak teleportation accident left Rowwena stranded far from Norrath, desperately seeking to find her way back home. In her absence much that she had known was no longer the same. Her friend Iggwilv departed and many of her other friends went their own ways or  just went missing altogether. Yet, despite this, Rowwena was able to overcome the changes once again. With her sister Dyanna, and friend Deelyt, Rowwena has made a new home among the Dwarves in Velious, and currently spends her time seeking out the vile Kromrif giants. She is greatful for her family in the Triune, and for the friends she has found in Winsom, Kalkiba, Ianu, Iinya and many others she has had the fortune to know. As in the past, Rowwena has once again found a place where she is happy.

What's in a Name?

The name Rowwena is a variation of Rowena, spelled to reflect a Welsh tendency to use double letters in names. According to one source its origin is from hrod wynn, from ancient Welsh meaning either "Fair and Slender" or "Fame and happiness."

The Name Morrigan is taken from Irish Mythology. According to legend The Morrigan was the Goddess of War and Death. She could take many forms, most of which the folks of the time hoped they would never see, as her appearance often preceded the death of either themselves or someone close to them.
