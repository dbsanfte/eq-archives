The Beginning

Many people might have thought it strange that I often visited him in his private chambers. But his own rooms put him at ease. He talked more freely with me there then anywhere else. We had met countless times before to talk. And since my father had been his best friend for a long time, and I'd become a very good friend of his as well, I thought nothing odd about meeting him in his private chambers when he invited me to visit.

We often had intellectual conversations that lasted for many hours. He claimed I was wiser then himself. I know he just flattered me. He simply loved my company. He had no one else to share his personal time with. His wife had been brutally stolen from him a long before. He'd never really gotten over it I suppose. It made me sad to think that such a horrible thing could happen to such a wonderful man as he, to someone so honourable and giving. But I guess it was that terrible time that moulded him into the man he is.

I stepped out onto the balcony quietly. Sir Arawan sat already in his favourite chair, looking out into the wonderful night below. The warm summer breeze felt refreshing as it playfully swept by me, tugging gently at my long golden hair. I stopped to take in deepened breaths for a few moments, smelling the scents of flowers. The warmth lingered in the summer night air. It was a beautiful night for a wonderful conversation on his balcony.

I slipped silently to my favourite chair and carefully gathered my gown up to sit down. Sir Arawan did not turn to look at me. His attention remained fixed on the city below us. We often didn't say anything to each other for the first couple of minutes of our meetings. Sort of a game we played I guess. I didn't mind. It gave me some time to get comfortable.

I looked down over the balcony to the city below. Many beautiful horse-drawn carriages rolled smoothly down the torch-lit roads. A few people rode upon the backs of decorated horses without magnificent coaches. And the sidewalks carried many Glaznorians on foot. Even though the shadows had deepened with the setting sun, the people moved about with little concern. I loved to see it. We Glaznorians shared a wonderful peace, free of darkness, free of worries and concerns. Of course it hadn't always been like this. Sir Arawan had given us this way of life.

My gaze came across a young couple outside the Grand Theatre. They separated themselves from the rest of the crowd that stood around waiting to go in. Alone beneath a weeping willow tree, they embraced. They kissed one another over and over. Each kiss grew longer and longer, each one more soft and sensual then the last. The two were very much in love. I could see the glow radiating around them. The air seemed to grow hotter around me as I watched them secretly. My heart began to flutter, my emotions stirred.

"Good evening Lady Serane," Sir Arawan finally spoke, startling me from my secret finding.

I turned to face him with a devilish smile spreading across my face that I could not control. I thought I'd been caught. Annoyingly, I could feel my cheeks warming as well, the tell-tale blush growing there. I hated that I blushed so easily. But he didn't seem to notice it, at least not right away. His brown-eyed gaze drifted up my body from my feet to my head, drinking in the sight of me in a gown. Like a gentleman, his gaze flowed steadily and quickly, not lingering anywhere in particular. He simply admired my beauty. I said nothing about it, knowing that he rarely saw such a sight.

I'd grown up a warrior in the House of Guards, led by Sir Arawan and my father, Sir Pwyll. Both men saw me in armour most of the time. The House of Arcane made me a beautiful suit of lightweight chain mail and presented it to me on the day the High Council declared me as the First Herald of Glaznor. The tight-fitting suit of mail became my regular wardrobe. On this beautiful summer night however, I felt like wearing a light, flowing gown like many of the other girls my age.

When Arawan's eyes reached my blue-eyed gaze, he smiled sweetly. But his eyes betrayed a hint of sadness in him.

"What is the matter M'Lord?" I asked.

"You are a very beautiful Lady Serane, you should not be up here tonight, counselling an old man on issues of the High Council. You should be down there with the other Glaznorians, having fun and finding the love you are capable of. Some poor fellow awaits you somewhere, yet I keep drawing you up here to me instead."

"Silly nonsense," I said, the smile on my face strong. "You are not old. And you know that I love these talks we have, Arawan. I take my responsibilities to the High Council, the Houses, and to all Glaznorians very seriously. Through it all I enjoy all the time we spend together talking. I wouldn't give it up for anything else. Love can wait for now, there will be time tomorrow and beyond."

"There may not be as much time as we once thought, Lady Serane." His eyes lost the happy sparkle I'd come to know. His voice dropped low as his expression turned serious. It worried me.

"What is it you know, Arawan?" My own smile had gone now, stolen from me by this sudden change in him.

"I'm afraid I did not invite you to talk long with me tonight M'Lady. Though I would love your company, there is much that I must think on alone. I have some dreadful news. And an important task I must ask of you."

"Anything," I assured him without a second thought.

"Master Havgan came to see me last night, just after sunset."

That alone seemed very odd. Seldom did Master Havgan venture out of his home anymore. Sir Arawan stopped talking as though to gauge whether I may be ready to hear the rest or not. I gave a small nod, urging him to go on.

"He witnessed a vision through his divinations, one that he showed to me."

"A vision?" I asked, my curiosity plain.

"Yes. A disturbing vision."

"Please tell me," I said.

His eyes grew a little pained. The tempo of my heart increased within my chest. I'm sure my face displayed my concern. Sir Arawan remained silent for several long seconds as he read deeply into my blue eyes. I could hardly bear the silence or his intense look. Finally he seemed to summon his courage before he spoke. His words were slow and careful.

"We saw the Land of Glaznor broken and shattered," he said. "The streets were blackened, the houses set aflame. The city burned. The buildings collapsed. The fire rained down from the sky. It would not stop. The Glaznorians…" He paused. I could hear the lump rising in his throat. "They lay dead all around, their bodies…"

He stopped there. He did not go on. He turned his eyes away from me and looked back out into the city. A look of fear slowly crept across his face as he stared blankly below. It was as though he saw it all happening before him, the city burning, the people dying. The fear in his eyes frightened me. I'd not seen Sir Arawan afraid before in all my years. Suddenly I felt very vulnerable. He had always stood up to protect Glaznor in the past. If he was afraid…

I couldn't bear it. I quickly sat forward and took his hands in mine. His skin had broken out in a cold sweat. I held onto his hands tightly and felt his own grasp slowly tightening on mine as well.

"Come back to me Arawan, please," I pleaded. I could hear the fear in my own shaky voice. "Please come back to me."

He slowly turned to look back at me as I held his hands. Shadows clung to his eyes as the vision played out. I knew he could not yet see me for the vision.

"Stop it," I commanded.

After a few long seconds, he quickly blinked a couple of times before his eyes focused on mine. The shadows retreated. I tried to force a smile to my lips but I don't think I succeeded much. My weak smile seemed to have the desired effect though. Or perhaps he saw the fear in my own eyes and tried to comfort me a little, for he began to smile. I could barely see it but it was there. He was returning to me. Sweat had broken out on his brow and his expression still remained a little blank.

"There is more," he said, his voice raspy and dry, "but I think it is best that I don't tell you the details. I don't want your dreams showing you the visions as I see them now."

With that he let go of my hands but his gaze remained on me. He reached to the little table that sat between our two chairs and grasped the bottle of shaladae there. He poured us both a small amount into short glasses and handed mine to me. I took a small gulp of it into my mouth and washed it down, pleased to wet my throat with the smooth liquid. He emptied his glass and set it back onto the table before sitting back in his chair.

I rested my glass back on the table and sat back in my chair as well. I could already feel the warmth of the alcohol spreading through my blood.

"There is something I want to talk to you about, Lady Serane, something I want to ask you."

He had come fully back to himself now. The shaladae had wet his throat once again so that his voice was his own.

"Go on," I said softly, "anything."

A true smile touched the corners of his lips as he leaned forward towards me once again.

"I need you to record the History of Glaznor. I want you, the First Herald, to set it down on paper for all time. When the darkness comes, I want the world to know about the land of Glaznor. I want to let the people know that we Glaznorians are a strong people, a peaceful people, and that we accomplished something great here."

I nervously brushed my golden hair back over my left ear as I shyly looked away from his eyes. The words rolled over and over in my head as I peered out into the town. Record our History. I stared blankly at the willow tree where the lovers had been kissing. They no longer stood beneath the tree. The crowds had moved into the Grand Theatre a while ago.

"Will you do it Lady Serane?" He asked me, drawing me back to him. "Will you record what has come and gone on paper for all time? Will you record it so that the people of tomorrow, whoever they may be, shall know that the land they walk upon was once our peaceful Glaznor? That such a peace is possible?"

"Of course I will," I said at last as I looked deeply into his eyes. "Glaznor will not die Arawan. No matter what happens, the world shall know. Glaznor shall live on. You have my vow M'Lord."

A smile came over his face, a handsome smile that made my heart flutter. I couldn't help but smile beautifully too. He sat back in his chair, completely content with my answer.

"The time draws near, Lady Serane. We are not sure when it will happen but we know that it will. A quarter century ago, the Dark Lords banished themselves from our lands. We always knew they would return, their hatred multiplied a hundred-fold what it was before they left."

"You speak of the battle of Teshenix, the war of the Army of Light against the Dark Army," I offered. "I know it well. I have read many accounts of what happened that day, written by many people. The Dark Lords vanished, or they may not have even been there at all."

"Yes," he said, "you are well-educated Lady Serane. You are your father's pride. He loves you dearly."

"I know," I said with a smile. Always a daddy's girl I guess.

We talked longer that night then Sir Arawan had intended but time had a way of slipping away from us when we let our conversations wander. Still, it turned out to be the shortest night I'd spent with him. As his manservant escorted me from his chambers, I felt sad that I was leaving him alone in that big empty house. The visions could return but no one would bring him back from them. A house that size should have housed a large family by now, a family who could help and protect him as he did them. Again the thought came to me of how something so terrible could happen to such a good man.

I lost that thought as I walked slowly down the road away from his house. The streets had quietened down by this time of night. Normally I would have felt no worry as I walked. I was in Glaznor, peaceful Glaznor. But if the visions were true, that could change at any time. It worried me.

I began to think on the responsibility that had been laid before me. I was the median through which our History would travel. I had to tell others exactly who the Glaznorians were. I had to relate the lives of our people and their struggles, and the meaning of their existence, to those who would listen. They needed to know what it meant to be a Glaznorian. I knew it would not be a simple task. I understood the trust and the responsibility that Sir Arawan had placed in me. And I was prepared for the challenge.

Introduction

Other Glaznorians may have a different interpretation of our History. That is their right. Not all of us remember it the same. I ask only that you remember that we are all reborn. Our memories are not entirely accurate. I wouldn't expect them to be after so long a time. But I feel that I am well suited to the task of laying this History out before you. Perhaps it is my strong sense of emotions for that which occurred in our past that keeps my mind so clear. Being too that I have let myself refocus on that time in order to write this history, I have been remembering details far beyond what I thought I could. I can almost see it all as though it was yesterday. It hurts, very much. It brings to me many of the old emotions of that time. Pain, love, loss…

It is likely wrong that I mix my memories of old with that of the new. Perhaps that is why I feel somewhat distance and shattered here in this modern day world of Norrath. I all too clearly remember the High Council of old. And I remember losing them. But now, here, the new High Council is reborn. I strive to become to them what I was in the past to the Council of old. As of yet I'm not entirely as successful as I'd like to be. Maybe after this, they will see me for what I am. Perhaps only after then, after I tell them my story, shall they understand and look upon me as the High Council of old did.

Enough of this, for now… I have a history to write.

The History of Old Glaznor

I was not yet born when I feel the history of Glaznor truly starts. What I write at this time may not be entirely accurate. I believe it to be accurate in fact but I had to use my own storytelling to link the facts together in order to add the depth required to make our History truly felt by all. To simply write dates and facts alone does not add the character that Sir Arawan wanted. He wanted everyone who read this to know the people of Glaznor. He wanted their hearts and spirits to live on through this writing. This is what I intend to accomplish. Here it begins.

Sir Arawan sat in the rain by the ocean shore, watching the tattered remains of the funeral flowers wash down the banks and out to sea. The black spring rain started shortly after they had set his deceased wife adrift on her burning funeral bed. The guests, family, and friends all left one by one as the cold downpour grew heavier. Before long he sat there by himself. His cloak no longer repelled the rain. Now it soaked right through his clothes. He had grown cold long ago. But nothing could match the coldness in his heart, nor the emptiness. He felt more alone then he had ever felt in his entire life.

He'd watched the rain drown out the torches that had lined the shore. Many of them now tipped over in the softening earth they'd been driven into. The huge beds of beautiful flowers had been totally destroyed within minutes of the rain beginning to fall. He sort of admired that the heavens wept too, though it did not ease his own burden. He could not have withstood the wounds if the sun had been cutting down from the sky. He would never have forgiven the sun for dishonouring his Lady in that way. This was not a day for the flowers to be growing. The blackened day was appropriate.

"Come Arawan," a voice boomed behind him. Startled, he nearly leapt to his feet as he spun around to face the speaker. The wet ground nearly threw him back down again.

"We should get into the safety of our homes and out of this terrible rain," Pwyll said.

Arawan tried to figure out quickly how he'd not even heard his best friend approach, especially on horseback since he sat upon his white war steed. But almost as soon as he questioned it, he realized he could only hear the rain beating on the ground and pelting into the ocean waters behind him.

Sir Pwyll glanced quickly over his friend from head to foot. He looked terrible, drenched right through by the cold spring downpour and shivering. He raised his gaze to settle on the pained eyes of his fiend. It felt to Arawan like they just stared at each other for a long time, their gazes locked, nothing communicated between them. In truth only a couple of seconds passed. But Sir Arawan turned away to look at the ocean without saying a word. Thousands of raindrops continued to pelt its surface. Each one stabbed at the water like a knife stabbing at his heart, driving home the loneliness that he felt.

"Come. She would not want to see you like this. You know that," Pwyll said loudly over the rain. "Nor do I want to. Get on my horse; I shall take you home. I'll not leave without you."

Arawan turned back to look at his stubborn friend. The rain had drenched his clothes as well. His poor horse, Gilio, looked dreadful.

"I want so much to find the creature who did this to her," Arawan forced out slowly, his words loud enough to be heard over the rain. "I want this evil to pay."

"You know you cannot find it, whatever terrible creature it was," Pwyll said.

Sir Arawan could see the masked pain in Pwyll's own eyes as he said the words. Pwyll could not hide it from him, even through the falling rain. Arawan knew that Lady Liban and he had been close, nearly like brother and sister. And he knew that Pwyll too wanted to believe it was possible to track down her killer.

Sir Arawan moved carefully to cross the distance that lay between them. He had to watch his balance on the slippery grass beneath him. When at last he stood beside the great steed, he looked up into Pwyll's eyes.

"No, I cannot," he said and his expression grew harder, "that I know. Instead I will find all creatures out there that would do such terrible things. Evil shall not be able to hide from me from this day forth."

A small smile touched the corners of Pwyll's lips as he extended his arm down to Arawan. He liked the spirit he'd glimpsed in Arawan's brown eyes as he said it. For a moment, a determination replaced the blank expression of his loss. Life had returned there, even if it had been just for a second. The two men grasped each other's elbows and Pwyll aided Arawan with a pull up onto his horse.

Pwyll set Gilio into motion and the two men rode off, leaving the ocean behind them.

"You mean shall not hide from us?" Pwyll asked after a few minutes had passed.

Arawan had to smile slightly now. "Yes. Shall not hide from us."
