Dogedme's Return
by Dogedme Kingson, Druid of The Seekers of Knowledge and Cassia Spellsinger, Officer of Allegiances, The Silver Daggers

        Cassia had been quiet most of their quick breakfast. The rumor that Samadani had heard about a woodelf being held in the Nektulos Forest had her very worried. She had not realized the danger that he might be in. She had thought his absence had been one of choice. As they reached the edge of the forest she checked her spells one last time, then turned to hug Sam.

“Ready?”

Samadani looked at the forbidding forest, wondering if it really could be Dogedme in there. Surely he would never go in there willingly. She shuddered, took a deep breath.

“Yes I'm ready.”

“We should be able to take anything in here, but stay close, we can't afford to get separated.” She hugged her one last time, trying to ease the worry they both were feeling. “I'm sure he's fine...if it even is him.”
       
        “I’m sure he's all right.” She said softly, trying to convince them both of something they knew in their hearts was not true.

        They stepped through the pass, and into the thick woods. The forest was dark, and alive with creatures. Rain was falling through the trees, unclean and oily, and thunder rolled ominously over head. Cassia paused almost immediately, realizing she had no idea where to go.

“Can you track him?” Her voice was a whisper.

“I’ll try.” Samadani said, her voice just as quiet. She bent down to look at the ground and after a thorough search found a faint trail. Once she’d found it, it was easy enough to follow. “This way.”

        Cassia followed quickly, her eyes scanning the forest for signs of danger. She watched protectively over Sam as the Druid followed the faint traces of Dogedme. Her heart sank as Sam picked up his trail. He was here.

        Suddenly, Samadani stopped short. She tilted her head and listened. “Shhh! I think they are close!”

        Cassia stopped behind her, craning her head to hear what Sam was hearing. She heard the dim sound of voices. Her breath caught in her chest, and she checked over her spells once again.

        “How many, can you tell?”

“Eight, maybe ten. Stay close behind me!” Sam readied her staff, preparing for battle.

“I'm not leaving your side.” Cassia frowned, 8 - 10 was a large number, and could be difficult to control. She did stay close, but also kept her line of sight open so she could mez or stun before the enemy could attack.

Samadani stopped all of a sudden and pointed to a group of people on the forest floor. They were all standing together in a circle, and there did not appear to be any sort of lookout or sentry.

Cassia immediately began to study the enemy and it's relative strength, looking for primary targets...and more importantly trying to pick Dog out.

“Is he there?” They both were silent was they watched, trying to make sure it really was Dogedme they had come upon.

“This lamb,” one of the drow held up a young lamb above the crowd as he spoke. “Represents the blind innocence which all Tunare’s followers possess. That weak insistence that love can protect and serve life.” He lowered the lamb. “Show us, Dogedme Kingson, what you think of the goddess Tunare!”

The crowd shifted, vying for a better angle, and when it did, they could clearly see Dogedme. He was armed, with sword and shield, and his sword was above his head. He spat on the lamb, and put his foot on its back, pinning it to the ground.

        “Tunare is weak and leads her followers to their deaths. The sword will always be stronger than love.” The sword came down, severing the lamb’s head. He sheathed his sword, and knelt before the circle of drow. “I hope that pleases my Mistress.” He bowed his head.

“She will be pleased, when she hears.” The drow said.

Sam shook with rage, her hand clenching her staff. She moved towards the group. Cassia tried to stop her, reaching for Sam's arm, shock overwhelming her. This couldn’t be, how could he be armed, and still be among them without a fight?

        Samadani pulled away from her, striding purposefully into the group of drow. She would saved her beloved Doged, she had to get him away from these people. She was upon them before they ever saw her, and her staff came down hard on the nearest drow’s head, crushing it with one fierce blow.

Cassia was slower to react. She was still stunned, unable to comprehend fully what she had seen. The spray of blood from the inky’s skull woke her up. There was a battle to fight, and she needed to be at Sam’s side. Moving her hands in patterns she’d learned long ago, she cast her area effect mez, and to her surprise stunned everyone except Sam. The thunder-like crackle of Anarchy filled the air, and the inky nearest to Dog screamed in agony and dropped as his mind exploded.

The crackle of Anarchy filled the air again, and Samadani’s staff had claimed another two lives, her blows vicious and true. One of the drow, unbeknownst to Cassia, had been out of range of her mez. He backed into the forest, his eyes flashing widely. He looked to Dog.

“Kill them! Slay them for your Mistress! She will be very displeased.” He hissed, then began to cast gate.

Cassia could do nothing, she was in the middle of casting, and could not switch targets. Anarchy was a slow, but powerful spell. Samadani was too far, and the drow was gone before they could touch him.

        Samadani stepped over the limp body of the inky she’d just dropped, and looked to Dogedme. Emotions flashed wildly across her face, the blood ritual of moments before, blood that was still on his blade finally sunk in. He wasn’t captive, he was a willing guest. One who had defiled Tunare’s name, and who might very well turn on her.

        “Finish them off.” Cassia said urgently, her mental destruction wreaking havok and dropping yet another inky. “We can't leave them alive.” She was starting to feel that edge of panic that she always felt just before a large group mez wore off.

        Samadani’s staff was working again, and nothing could stand before her. She was near Dogedme now, cutting down the drow who stood near him. She continued to hit the drow nearest Dog long after he was dead, her pain and rage consuming her.

        This time, the mez didn’t hit Dog, only stunning the 3 remaining drow. Cassia grit her teeth and cast again. She couldn’t have Dogedme at her back while trying to deal with the inkies. He resisted again, his eyes finally finding Samadani. He didn’t recognize her, and his hand tightened on the hilt of his scimitar, the words of the drow echoing through his head. Again Cassia cast, this time Enthrall. Samadani cut down the drow nearest her, her staff connecting sharply with his ribs, and then as he doubled over, caving in the back of his skull. She stopped before Dog, breathing hard, covered in blood.

        Dogedme was frozen, his hand on his sword, staring incomprehensibly at Sam. He didn’t recognize her, the love that had always shown in his eyes for her was gone, and in it’s place just mindlessness. It was too much for Samadani.

        Cassia’s gaze flitted from the two mezzed drow to Sam and Dog. She didn’t have time to deal with them. She cast charm as quickly as she could, issued the command to attack, and turned her back on the drow. Samadani was trembling in rage and her feelings of betrayal were written clearly on her face.

        “How could you?” She looked from the lamb to Dog. But he couldn’t answer, and wouldn’t have been able too even had he not been enthralled. She turned her back on him, and walked away, into the woods.

        “Sam, wait! Where are you going?” She started after her, but stopped a few paces away. She couldn’t leave Dog, not with the two drow. No matter what he’d done, he was still her friend. Samadani didn’t even turn around, she just stopped, and cast gate, vanishing in the blink of an eye.

Cassia swore, and turned back to those who remained. Her inky had been victorious, and had come to stand at her side. She cued up strip enchantment, and cast it on her drow. When he blinked, finally free to act as he wanted, she cast Anarchy one last time. She watched as he ran, taking her rage and anger out on him. His mind exploded, and he dropped to the ground in a lifeless heap. She moved to Dog's side, watching him closely. She sat, trying to rest her tired mind, hoping she won't need her energy just yet. She offered him some water from her pack when the mez broke.

“You look thirsty.” Her voice was quiet and soft.

He sat heavily beside Cassia, not taking the water. He just stared at her, still uncomprehending, like a man who hasn’t managed to wake from a nightmare.

“C...Cassia?” His voice was unsure, his eyes dead.

She pressed the water to his lips, letting it flow from the flask into his slack mouth. The water was the first he had had for a very long time, and it choked him, but refreshed him at the same time. His hand moved to take the canteen, but he couldn’t grip it, noticing for the first time his mangled hands.

She gasped as she saw the ruin that his hands had become. Her gaze passed quickly over him, and she took in the numerous other wounds, and scars that she had never seen before. She bit her lip, and waited until the choking had passed, and pressed the canteen to his lips again.

“Can you heal yourself?”

Instead of healing himself, which was well within his capabilities, he simply stared at his hands.

“Sam....Sam was here....where...where is Sam?”

She touched his cheek lightly, her frown deepening. She put the water down for a moment, and rose, casting clarity on him in the vain hope it would help.

She sat, and picked up the flask again.

“Drink,” she said, pressing the water against his lips again, her forehead furrowing with a deep frown.

He drank deeply this time, like he'd had nothing to drink but bitter wine for some time now. She gave him as much water as he would drink, then set the canteen down. She touched his hands gingerly.

“Can you heal yourself?”

He looked at his hands, than nodded to her.

“Where is Sam, Cassia? She was here.....I saw her.”

“We need to move to a safer place, but you should heal yourself first.” She couldn’t take her eyes from the wounds and scars which marked him now. She couldn’t meet his gaze. “I don't know where she is.”

“Ok.” He stood and, directing his energy toward his mangled hands, used his powers to heal and soothe them. They were completely healed within minutes. He ignored the rest of the wounds all together. “Let's go,” his mumbled, forcing her to strain to understand him.

She grabbed his pack, and her own as well, her gaze passing over the bodies which littered the ground. She moved back to his side.

“I thought we could go to an Inn, get you some good food, a bath...some rest..”

He wasn’t really listening or comprehending her.

“Yea..that's fine...as you wish.” He just stood there, his clothing and armor looking too big for him now.

“Dog, please, let's get out of here.” The rain had soaked her to the bone, and she kept thinking of the drow that got away, and what he might bring back to them. She took him by the elbow, trying to lead him out. He allowed her to lead him.

“Ok....I'm coming.” He made no move to step out into the lead.

Her gaze darted back and forth, watching the forest which seemed to be alive around them. She guided them back along the path she and Sam had taken in, her mind working fast. When they reached the edge of the forest, her relief was palapable. He was not helping her at all, not alert to his surroundings. When he finally spoke again, his voice was too loud for safety.

“Cassia.....Cassia wait.”

“Shh!” They weren’t out yet. She looked around to see if anyone had heard them, and tried to move him out of the forest. She was trying to quell the emotions that were broiling in her, and just deal with the problem at hand. “Talk while we walk”

        “Where is Sam? She was here, right? Didn't I see her here? Where is she? How did you find me, Cassia? Who knew where I was being held? Did someone tell you?” He was shouting.

She sighed, stopped trying to pull him and turned to face him.

“Dog, can we please get to the Inn before I answer your questions? You are still hurt.”

He looked at himself, then at her, registering some surprise. He didn’t feel the pain of his injuries anymore, or even really know they were there.

“OK” He stepped past her, headed for the nearest Inn. He didn’t look back, but moved ahead of her at a brisk pace.

Cassia hadn’t been ready for his sudden movement and it took her a moment to catch up. She was very worried about Sam now, and very uncertain how to deal with Dog. She caught him, but still had to struggle to keep up. He heard her behind him and suddenly didn’t want to give her a chance to ask questions, so he kept moving fast. He didn’t even pause as they reached the Inn, heading straight to the pub room.

“Ale,” he reached for his money pouch and realized he didn’t have his pack, “give me Ale, Barkeep.....LOTS of ale...Cassia! Cassia where are you!”

Her frown grew deeper as he summoned her, and she moved past him to the Barkeep.

“We want a room, with food, water, and a hot bath.”

“Yea...yea what she said. And ale.” His voice was more controlled now. “Will you pay him please Cass?”

“Of course.” She took a deep breath, trying to calm herself and paid the keep, tipping generously as he handed her the key. She looked to Dog. “Shall we go to the room?”

He collected an armful of filled bottles off the bar and turned to her.

“Yes, of course.” He waited for her to lead again.

She opened the door for him and set the packs down just inside.

“Here, let me help you with those.” She moved towards him, reaching for the bottles. He stood still, allowing her to empty his arms. He watched her, a look of hopelessness on his face. Her expression softened, and she guided him to the table in the room. She helped him sit, then opened one of the ales for him. “Drink as much water as you do ale.” She set the water next to him as well. A knock sounded on the door, and she let one of the barmaids in. The two of them arranged the food on the table, and then the maid went to draw the bath.

        Cassia dipped some bread in the gravy and handed it to Dog. He took the food from her, eating slowly at first, his eyes not meeting hers. After he finished a few bites, he began gorging, washing down huge mouthfulls with half bottles of Ale. She reached over, and put her hand on his, stopping him for a moment.

“Eat slower...it's not going anywhere, I promise.”

At her words, he stopped, dropping the Ale from his mouth, and finally meeting her eyes. His expression was one of utter sorrow.

“You saw, didn't you...Sam saw too. She left, didn't she, Cass? She's left me, and so will you. She may need help, you know. She may need you....” His voice trailed off for a moment, and he looked away. “Not me though...no...not me...she doesn’t need me....not like this.”

She set down her own food, and pulled her legs up under her.

“She's fine, I'll check on her when I'm sure you're okay. She does need you. She just needs some time alone.”


“No..” He was growing agitated, obviously unstable. “No...she's not fine...She saw, didn't she. You saw. Tell me, Cass. Tell me what you saw. By....tell me what you saw, Cassia!” He was shouting now.

She moved the food away, and shut the door to the room, glad that the bath was filled and the maid wouldn’t be returning for a bit. She pulled her chair close to his and tried to take his hands in hers.

“Dog, please, try to be calm. You're still hurt.”

He stood, grabbing another bottle of Ale and smashing the neck off the bottle. He drank straight from the broken top, cutting his lip. He drained the bottle, then turned to her.

“You won't say, will you...You saw that” he waved his arm “back there...you...saw it.” He sneered at her. “You just won't tell me how disgusting I am...I don't need your pity, ok....I don't need...I don't...I..” He tapered off, his energy spent, and just stood there.

She grabbed the ale from him and slammed it down on the table.

“Stop it!” She was yelling now too, her body trembling. “Yes, we both saw what was happening. I'm not pitying you, I'm trying to help you. I care about you. I want to help. I know you don't need my pity, I’m not offering it to you.”

He looked away, embarrassed.

“Leave me here Cassia. Just leave me here and go. They've destroyed me you know...look at me. You and Sam don't deserve this. Go find Samadani. Please.”

“They haven't destroyed you.” She moved closer to him, wanting to comfort him. “I don't know what they did to you, but I'm not done being your friend. I can't go to her, I don't know where she is. You, I can help. Let me try, please.”

“No Cassia...no...” He didn’t move away from her. “I know where she went...she’s back in the Outpost, I'd bet on it. You can find her. You need to get away from me. I'm poison. You need to just go find her and leave me here.” An odd look crossed his face, and he moved to his packs, searching methodically at first, then frantically.

“It’ll take me days to get to the Outpost, and if she decides to leave before I get there, she'll be gone in the blink of an eye. If she wanted me around, she would have told me where she was going. Don't tell me what I need and what I don't need. I am not ready for you to be out of my life.” She tried to calm her frustration and anger, trying not to be overwhelmed. “What are you looking for?”

He began pulling everything out of his pack, throwing it haphazardly, becoming more frantic with each passing moment. He stopped finally, frozen, a small vial in his hand, and stood looking at it.

“Dog?” She asked softly, watching him, concerned. “What is that?” She moved close so that she could grab the vial if she needed too.

He turned and with a quick, viscious fling, smashed the glass vial against the stone hearth, then became still again. She took half a step back, surprised by the violence of his motion, but recovered quickly and moved back next to him.

“You haven't finished eating.” She said softly.

“That was a poison, Cassia. A very quick acting, deadly poison. I told them that you and Belce were hunting and business partners with myself and Sam. They made the poison for you. For you and Belce. I was supposed to kill you. I think I might have actually done it, too,” he trailed off. “I was going to kill you for them...” His voice was quiet. “I was going to do it. You don't need that...you need to go find Samadani. You need to go now. Please.”

        “You were not. I doubt you even entertained the thought. Don't make me go.”

        “I need time Cassia. I need time to....figure out what happened.” He sighed heavily. “I just need some time, that's all. And I'm worried about Sam.”

        She stood there, not having had the time herself to adjust or digest what she had seen, and what had happened. Finally, she touched his shoulder lightly.

“Dog, can you make me a promise?”

He turned to her.

“Of course....you need only ask.” His gaze met hers.

She couldn’t quite suppress the sinking feeling that she was not doing everything she needed to do to help him, yet at the same time, she didn’t know what else to do. She met his gaze, resigning herself.

“Promise me that you won't take your own life. That when you've had time, that you will come find us.”

He looked a bit startled, then his eyes flashed, showing life for the first time.

“Cassia...nothing could be further from my mind than taking my own life. I will wait here, and heal, and regain my senses, and then I will search you and Sam out. I'll not lose you..not like this. No...not like this I won't. I just need a little time, and it's time that Sam may not have. I will find you though. I swear it.”

A strained smile came to her lips as life returned to his eyes.

“We'll be waiting. I'll go find her, I promise.” She knew it was time to go but she couldn’t quite bring herself to do so. “Need anything before I go?”

He shook his head.

“No...I need time and Sam needs you. Except...” He paused.

He sat, thinking, trying to remember.

“A warning...yes. I need to warn...someone...”

“A warning?” She could feel the hackles on the back of her neck rise.

        “You have to warn Haemish and the others. They questioned me mostly about the Silver Daggers. They wanted to know your organization, your tactics, your weaknesses and habits…everything about you. I tried to resist them. I don’t know for sure how much I told them, but you must send out a warning. I believe they intend to destroy the Daggers, and the Seekers and the Dark Oracles, too. I don’t know why. You have to send out warnings though, lest anyone else be caught unawares. They questioned me at length about Haemish, Hazerr and Eleysian specifically. At least warn those three!”

Her eyes widened.

“They tortured you about us?” She tried to digest that, then nodded. “I'll warn everyone, I promise.”

“Warn Sam too, Cass....Katrya...she'll go after Sam. I know she will. She'll be infuriated that I did not kill you and Belce, like she wanted. She'll go after Sam now. You must hurry, please. I'll follow when I can, I swear.”

“Yes..” He was still struggling to remember. “Yes...Katrya...and Sama..Samavar. Those were the two who...they were the ones that tortured me....mostly Katrya, though.”

“I will tell them. Take care of yourself, Dog.” She squeezed his hand, still reluctant to leave, but turned to head for the door.

“I will find you. Tell Sam I love her, more than anything. Please. Tell her I will explain, try to make her understand.”

She stopped to turn around and offering him a weak smile.

“It’ll be okay, Dog. I’ll make her understand. I know she loves you too...don't take too long to find us.”

“Be safe, Cassia Spellsinger. Watch for me, I'll be there.”

“I will, Dogedme Kingson, and I'll take care of Sam, I promise.” She turned away, shutting the door carefully behind her.
