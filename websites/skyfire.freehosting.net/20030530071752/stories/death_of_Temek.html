A Rogue's Exit
by Haemish MacLennan, Clan Chieftan of the Silver Daggers

From the journals of Haemish MacLennan:

"Temek has been in the custody of my enemies for too long. It has been unbearable knowing the torture that the Tier'dal sorceress has put him through in the hopes he will give over with the secrets of the Daggers, but my well-placed bribes and squeezing the right unsavory characters has paid off. I finally have a lead on the location of the Antithesis of Silver.

I have sent messengers out to a few of my Dagger brothers, asking them to meet me at the entrance to that vile forest, Nektulous. I go now to meet Caladil and Kinorf, and hope that the Light sees them through to rescue our wayward rogue."

 

The forest was dark and damp, a place of evil through and through, corruption weaving its way through the trees, burrowed into the ground and even in the air. The Tier'dal gathered around Temek seemed to enjoy it, relishing the fetid stench the night brought with it. He was strapped between two trees, the ropes burning into his worn and malnourished arms, already caked with blood from weeks of being tied tightly. He paid little attention to them, as they tittered around him, excited at the possibility of drawing blood from a fighting opponent. Sabanim, the human monk, looked nervous, though it was hard to tell from his stony stare. Temek was unsure of the monk. He'd spent weeks talking to the monk in the intervals between hours of torture. Stolen moments here and there, telling Sabanim what the half-elf rogue had seen under the amulet's sway. Under the gaze of Ash.... even now he couldn't say the name.

At first Sabanim had beat him, cursing the "lies" spoken, about his family, about his hated enemy, the Silver Dagger Fudil. But the blows were half-hearted, and soon they stopped coming at all. Sullen, Sabanim had begun to listen, listen to how the Dark Elf comrades he had allied himself with had killed his parents, his siblings, and made it look as if his step-brother Fudil had been responsible. And then, just two days before, Sabanim had brought water, a small amount, but so refreshing to Temek's parched lips. It was then that the monk offered Temek the escape of death or a chance for life. In two days time, an ambush of the Daggers was to happen, with Temek as bait. If Temek agreed to help Sabanim reunite with his brother Fudil and seek redemption from the Daggers, Sabanim would help him escape in the confusion. Temek had agreed, and now looked at the monk, who shifted nervously, going through the motions of fighting forms to keep himself limber, every now and again eyeing the rogue.

Soon he heard footsteps, the pad of someone not used to the soggy ground of the forest.... but he saw no one. A rogue? No, even the youngest cutpurse learns to mask his footfalls in the worst conditions. A caster then, with an invisibility enchantment, or a warrior with the same. Randall, the arrogant dark elven mage, heard it to, his pointed ears perking up and his grin stretching malicously. He began to cast some minor enchantments on himself in preparation of the coming magical blows. Temek heard the inrush of air being drawn toward the caster, and the hairs on his arm begin to rise with the coming use of the magical powers of the universe. Randall convulsed with the sudden lack of air around his body, but maintained his concentration and began to cast back, mentally willing his pet to attack his assailant. An enchanter by the looks of him, blue robed with a silver circlet on his head, a high elf, young in the ways of his art. Another appeared, a barbarian Shaman by his bearded looks, one that Temek recognized vaguely as Kinorf. A jolly, though quiet sort of fellow. Temek looked quickly at the monk, gauging his coming actions by his stance.

"ATTACK THE SHAMAN, SABANIM!" shouted Randall, straining with the effort of maintaining his spells under the onslaught. Katrya made her presence felt then, stabbing the shaman quickly before drawing mana to herself. "ATTACK YOU SLUG OF A HUMAN! NOW!"

Sabanim looked once at Randall, almost moving to attack, then thinking better, drew back his throwing dagger and let it fly. With a quick thunk thunk, two daggers found their way into the trees holding Temk, severing the ropes. Not sparing a moment to rub his aching wrists, he began to run, moving as quick as his emaciated legs would move him. Sabanim was off like an arrow, straight and true, diving gracefully into the raging river. Temek dove half-heartedly into the freezing waters, swimming desperately, hoping the sawtoothed fish that infested the river were otherwise engaged. Finally he made it across, Sabanim hastily jerking him from the water and urging him onward. Behind them, the sounds of battle continued, drowning out the curses of his Tier'Dal captors.

When the sounds of battle dimmed to silence, he slowed, gasping for air, his limbs on fire. Sabanim urged him on, as they had gotten close to the cleft in the mountains that would bring them to the East Commonlands, a relatively safe area. From there, to the underground in Freeport, where Temek had spent his youth, and where Haemish had saved him from the rigours of forced "employment" in the rogue's guild. He could send a message to Haemish, could beg for forgiveness, could....

"Halt, rogue... you have something I need."

The voice was chilled granite, the voice of a man who has spent too much time among dead things. Almost soulless.... almost... dead. Walking from shadows, the tall Erudite stranger wore robes that seemed to be made of the night, and bore a scythe that stood taller even than he. The air smelled of decay, of graves disturbed and things defiled. Sabanim readied himself in a fighting stance, one foot behind, perfectly balanced on the balls of his feet. Temek reached for the dagger Sabanim had given him, brandishing it weakly.

"The amulet, rogue... where is the amulet?" He had the eyes of one who brooked no opposition.

"You will not have him, stranger... he is under my protection," Sabanim said flatly, his foot arching out with his words. It never landed, as he was thrown back by the inrush of mana. His skin began to wither and blister, his face wracked with pain. Undeterred and silent, he attempted to spin an elbow to the Erudite's solar plexus, who easily dodged, casting another spell, flinging the monk back and rendering him unconcious. He whirled on Temek, eyes aglow with foul powers, and repeated his command.

"Athalzar has the amulet!"

"And where is this.... Athalzar?" Temek felt the tendrils of magic power begin to gather around him, and a presence entered his mind. Again.... his mind was filled with another's words, another's questions.... and something broke. He began to scream.

 

The fight was not going well. Spell after spell had landed with no appreciable effect on the shaman, or had bounced off harmlessly. His blows with the crooked staff he carried were delicious jolts of white-hot pain followed by the cold grip of his spells. Back she was pushed, while over her shoulder Randall was frantically trying to save his pet from Caladil's calm casting. They would lose this battle, it was apparent now. She began to speak the incantations of the gate spell when the last spell hit her, and then her mind exploded.

"ATHALZAR HAS THE AMULET!"

Randall and Katrya both spasmed with the mind quake, sent by the dying rogue, his mind locked with another. She had very rarely heard of this phenomenon, and usually only from casters of immense power. Or those whose souls had been tainted or controlled by something... else. As she slumped into unconciousness, her mind raced, gathering in all the possibilities, formulating thoughts. Randall, seeing her fall, ran to her, barely sure why he was bothering. He opened a gate, barely dragging her through. They would fight again, and he would win next time.

 

Sabanim struggled to conciousness, pain wracking every fiber of his being. Looking up, he saw Temek fall, and felt the vaguest stirrings of something in his mind, a scream, terror, and the words, "Athalzar has the amulet!" The Erudite caster leaned over and lightly touched the forehead of Temek, smiled, then vanished in shadows. Sabanim half crawled to the fallen half-elf, turning him over, barely able to hold his gorge as the stench of decay from the necromantic magic hit him. Temek was still alive, but wouldn't be for long. Sabanim began to struggle in his pack for bandages, but Temek's withered hand stopped him.

"I have lived long enough, friend monk... thank you ..... thank you for trying."

"But you must survive... you are my only hope for redemption... for... revenge."

"You will have your revenge, friend.... but first you must do something for me. You must find Haemish. Find him and tell him what I will tell you. Promise me!" His wizened hand clamped desperately on Sabanim's wrist so hard it drew blood.

"I will, Temek. I promise on my mother's grave."

"Tell him that a great evil is coming for him. Tell him that he is the one. Tell him that he is the Skyfire Born, and he must save us all! He must seek the gypsy in the Heart of Tears, she will know what he is to do."

"And beware, Sabanim." His eyes, pained and bloody, pierced Sabanim and looked beyond him at something else. "Beware of Ash.... Asherok." A rattle escaped Temek's throat, and his last breath seemed to whisper out of him like wind through dry leaves. Sabanim called after him, but the tiny rogue was gone.

 

Caladil and Kinorf trod slowly and carefully back through Nektulos Forest towards the East Commonlands, searching carefully for any hint of ambush, or of Temek's location. They had almost given up hope when they heard the cries of the monk who had run with Temek. "Back you creatures of the night! YOU cannot have him!"

Running forward, they saw the monk fighting off a skeleton, blows landing as fast as lightning. Anger gripped his face, and behind him lay the twisted body of Temek, still now, no breath or signs of life apparent. As they attempted to approach him, Sabanim stopped them. "You shall not have him.... not unless you bring Haemish to me. I must speak with Haemish, and then you may have him, but not before." Seeing the weight of guilt in his eyes, Caladil agreed, meditating right there, sending a mental message to the Clan Chieftan of the Daggers. The thoughts that returned from Haemish were cold and hard, like steel, a brusque "I will be there soon," the only words in a novel's worth of emotion that was sent with it.

Soon, they saw the blue hued armoured form of their leader, running swiftly and silently towards them. Seeing the corpse of his friend guarded by his enemy, Haemish pushed aside the monk and shouldered the burden of the rogue's lifeless body. "You wish tae speak with me?" Sabanim nodded. "Ye will help me bury muh friend, and then we will speak." Without another word, he turned on his heels, then ran towards the East Commons, his guildmates in tow. Sabanim ran after them, his heart cold.

The burial took over two hours, of hard back-breaking labor, and by the end of it, Haemish, Kinorf and Sabanim stood shirtless, while Caladil sat to the side, sending commands to his pet to help with the digging. Haemish uncorked a flask of hard northman ale, offering it to Kinorf and Sabanim, then to Caladil, who politely refused with a look of disgust on his face. Caladil had tried the ale once, and spent many hours reliving his past week's meals.... he had no desire to repeat the experience. He fished out a bottle of fine elven wine instead, sipping it lightly.

"Ye wished tae speak, monk? What have ye tae say tae convince me not tae take yer head off and post it on a pike right here?" His sword rested on his shoulder, but looked no less deadly for it. Sabanim eyed him levelly, and repeated what Temek had told him. Haemish mulled it quietly for a moment, then looked down and asked, "What is yer purpose fer tellin' me this? Is this another trap from yer mistress?" He spat on the ground at the thought of Katrya; he'd felt her magical attentions before, and found it quite unpleasant.

"I have sinned.... against the memory of my family, against my step-brother Fudil... and against myself. I have watched the pain and death of others and aided in such. All for revenge... revenge against the wrong person. I now seek redemption. I seek forgiveness... and the chance to put right the things I have wronged." His eyes never wavered from the barbarian's.

"Talk tae a priest, monk. What has this tae do with me and the Daggers?"

"You seek to do good... you seek justice. And you can help me avenge my family, give their memories justice. And help me to gain forgiveness from my brother."

"Ye wish tae join our cause?" He laughed, a loud raucous laughter that boomed with strength. "And what assurance do I have that ye will not stab me in the back first chance ye have?"

"None."

Haemish considered this a moment, and laughed again. "All right, monk! All men deserve a chance at redemption, even those such as ye! But ye do it my way, ye follow my words and ye don't stray once. If ye do, it will be yer only opportunity."

"They will want ye .... ye know too much tae let live. I will send ye tae a safe place, tae learn, tae train, and tae think. Ye will be redeemed or ye will be dead. Ye may not know the difference sometimes, but ye will find out." He smiled, preparing to give directions and listing the supplies they would need as they traveled there.

In the shadows, a tiny giggle was quickly stifled, and a smile like daggers in the night flashed, then was gone.
