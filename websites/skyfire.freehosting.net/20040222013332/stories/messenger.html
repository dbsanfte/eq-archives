The Messenger

The smell of the stale ale combined with the salt air sickened Auldiwen’s stomach. Although he was no stranger to taverns. The Seafarer's Roost was a poorly maintained tavern and riddled with small insects moving between the boards that acted as walls for the building. The high winds coming across the waters of the Ocean of Tears did nothing to clear the stench.

“Perhaps the lack of windows in this place prevents it. Although the cracks between the boards in the walls certainly could suffice”, Auldiwen snickered to himself.

The patrons of the tavern were few. Those few individuals that were sitting at tables or standing at the bar appeared to be sailors or staff of the tavern. Little activity was taking place and the usual clamor of conversation was absent at this time of the day.

“That should change by nightfall”, Auldiwen guessed.

Generally, of a jovial and talkative nature, Auldiwen’s mood was more subdued this afternoon. He had been summoned to meet his guild chieftain, Haemish. He was to be given a task to help the guild against their newly discovered enemies, the Guardians of Innoruuk. He was nervous, and he could tell it showed. He constantly fidgeted with his instruments. He knew his lute did not need tuning, he had done so earlier that morning. Auldiwen chastised himself for acting like a young child. He laughed at himself. He was a young child to many!

All sounds ceased in the tavern, only the waves hitting the shore and the echo of the oak door shutting closed could now be heard. Auldiwen looked up from his instrument to see who had silenced the room. As the large barbarian entered the great hall, you could see the uncertainty on the patrons faces and the fear in their eyes. They each turned away as the warrior that stood before them looked upon every one of them for potential threats.

Auldiwen noticed with interest the caution with which Haemish showed to all those in the room. But then, he had reason to be cautious. It had only been a few weeks since the Guardians of Innoruuk had ambushed him in Butchersblock. That thought scared him. Butchersblock was close to home. The Guardians of Innoruuk certainly posed as large a threat to his community of Kelethin as the orcs of Crushbone. Maybe more of a threat, as an orc is a known and easily detected enemy. The same could not be said of the insidious members of Innoruuk.

As Haemish scanned the room he noticed Auldiwen sitting in the corner. Once he was satisfied with the apparent safety of the area, the warrior walked towards the table where the woodland elf sat.

As his guild leader approached, Auldiwen stood and curtly bowed. Haemish acknowledged the courtesy with a similar bow.

Although young in age, Haemish’s face showed scars and lines that indicated a harsh existence in his few years of life. His skin was tan from constant exposure to the sun, and his face bore the familiar woad tattoo’s that were a common trait of his people.

As both sat down, Haemish continued to scan the room, attempting to detect any indication of someone trying to hear the conversation that was about to take place.

It was Auldiwen who whispered first. “My chieftain, what task is it that you ask of me?”

Staring plainly at Auldiwen, Haemish replied softly, “I, the guild, requires you to find and deliver a message to one known as Bullick. I believe my every move is being scryed, and I do not wish to lead our enemies to this “friend”. It could jeopardize his success”.

“Who is this person you speak of and where can he be found?”, responded Auldiwen.

“Let us just say, he represents the “darker” side of society. He is expecting my representative to bring him this bottle of mead, and should be awaiting your arrival somewhere close the arena”, whispered Haemish.

“What message am I to give to this man?”, questioned Auldiwen.

“Tell him this. The ‘silent hand’ must learn all it can of the servant of Innoruuk”. “, replied Haemish.

As the elven bard arose to deliver the message, Haemish spoke again. “One last thing Auldiwen. The message is important, but not more important than your life. If you should face trouble, do not hesitate to run”.

The statement showed why Haemish deserved his loyalty. He cared. The only emotion that his leader usually showed was anger and battle lust. Auldiwen knew that this man truly cared for the welfare of the Silver Dagger members. The weight of concern could easily be seen written on his face now.

Auldiwen gave Haemish a large smile and quickly turned and left the tavern.

Normally, Auldiwen walked or ran quickly to any destination whistling or singing a tune as he traveled. Today, however, he noticed that the paranoia that Haemish displayed in the tavern had spread to him now. As he walked through the alleys and roads of East Freeport and into West Freeport, he caught himself watching all strangers for hidden weapons, or quick unexpected movements. Even the rats moving around the sewers quickly caught his attention.

As he approached the arena, the hairs on his arms began to stand. “Why is this fear inside me? I have been through many encounters, but something strange stirs my mind”, Auldiwen thought to himself.

As he reached the top of the stairs leading into the arena, Auldiwen noticed two things. A man fitting the description Haemish had mentioned of the man he was to meet. And another figure sitting in meditation a little further away. As he approached the stranger he thought to be Bullick, he noticed the second figure to be a dark elf. Auldiwen could feel his anger begin to rise. Obviously, the dark elf must not be evil, or the guards would have killed him when he tried to enter West Freeport…Nonetheless, Auldiwen’s movement forward stopped while he tried to discern the threat the dark elf represented.

”What if the dark elf had bribed the Freeport militia. It was commonly known, although not openly expressed, that the militia was corrupt from the highest officers to the lowest militiaman. Perhaps he was even working for them.”, the thought raced through Auldiwen’s mind.

The rapid movement of the human attacking the dark elf surprised Auldiwen. “What in the name of Kelethin is going on!?”, releasing the statement openly before he realized he had spoken it out loud.

The deadly dark elf response came quickly. He spoke something in an arcane tongue and quickly there appeared a greenish cloud. When the cloud dissolved, a large skeleton stood in it’s place. This apparition quickly joined the dark elf in the attack on the human.

Auldiwen reached for his weapons and began running towards the dark elf. Before he could aid the human, Bullick fell to a crippling blow from the dark elf’s summoned ally.

Auldiwen continued his charge forward. He struck the dark elf solidly. He felt the blade cut into flesh and heard the scream from his opponent.

As the surprised dark elf turned to his new tormentor, his skeletal friend attacked Auldiwen. Auldiwen never relinquished his assault on the caster. For he knew this was the key. By killing the caster, the summoned creature would die. However, the dark elf was nimble and moved quickly to evade further blows.

As Auldiwen attempted to pursue the running caster, the skeletal creature beat upon his back. In addition, the spells that the dark elf had successfully cast, were now burning his skin. The pain was unbearable. Unlike most spells, which hurt for an instant, these increased in intensity over time. He could feel the pain of their burning with each passing second. He tried to maintain concentration on the running assassin. He could see the dark elf was much quicker than he.

Quickly Auldiwen realized he had failed. He could not deliver a message to a corpse. Bullick had been killed by the very dark elf that was attempting to strike him down now. His wounds were beginning to bleed heavily now and his pursuit of the dark elf was still fruitless. He could see his opponent bleeding heavily from a number of wounds. Yet, he was still able to duck away safely from the attempted blows. The words Haemish had spoken then came into his mind. Do not throw away your life needlessly. Auldiwen made a quick decision, he would flee in order to pass the bad news to Haemish.

As he outran his pursuers through the streets of West Freeport, and side-stepped into an alley, he watched as the dark elf sped past him.

As he stood panting in the shadowed alley, the thought of his failure weighed heavily on his mind. And one other thing began to bother him now, “How had his enemies known he would be there?”
