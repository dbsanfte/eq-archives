Two Old Enemies Meet Again

The group of six waited impatiently outside the tunnel entrance, as Athalzar shielded his eyes against the Northern Ro Sun. He wondered about the ability of this unlikely conclave to even stop a novice paladin, much less the leader of an established guild. “Nonetheless, we shall exact revenge for past crimes” he thought to himself. “The leader of the Silver Daggers will know that he has a worthy enemy, and one that is capable of dismantling his life’s work!”.

Looking around at the other members of the party, he realized each had a separate reason for hating the Silver Daggers. There was Katrya, the fiery necromancer from Neriak. Although young, he had already heard rumors of her ability to inflict pain in the shadowed streets of the dark elf city.

A movement to his left caught his attention. It was Ranndall moving around. “Doesn’t he know that such movement may warn Haemish of the ambush?” Athalzar whispered. Athalzar knew Ranndall, although not well. But both had been initiates together in the magicians guild in Neriak. Each had different masters, and therefore they only saw each other during the menial cleaning tasks required by the guild for initiates. “I shall get to know him better. He may be able to teach me things in our craft”, Athalzar mused.

Looking down at the entrance, Athalzar could see Novant. A tall swarthy Erudite. In fact, the first he had ever seen. It was rumored their race was highly intelligent, although no other race could equal the capacity of the dark elves to master the realm of death. He was quite sure of that. Nonetheless, Novant was their designated leader and required their fealty. “Haa!”. He scoffed. “As if a group such as this could ever be loyal to a single individual.” Nonetheless, he would have to trust each one of them if he was to survive the day. He realized that.

Across the entrance, he could barely make out Sabanim and Samavan. This was his first meeting with Samavan and didn’t know what to expect from her. She would bear watching. Sabanim intrigued him. He used a style of fighting Athalzar had seen mentioned in tomes, but had never actually witnessed in Neriak. His body moved in a fluid motion as he walked around. It was almost as if he had the agility of the elven race, but he was human. “I will watch him closely, he may be of use to me later”, Athalzar thought.

Through the endless waiting Athalzar’s mind raced, “Perhaps Novak was incorrect. Maybe Haemish had divined the ambush. A hundred things could go wrong! Surely Haemish had friends that would warn or notify of our attempt to assassinate him…”. Athalzar cursed himself. “I must concentrate! The plan is good and the bones have been cast! Calm and control the mind! I must discipline the mind”.

Instantly, the large warrior walking through the entrance caught his attention. Athalzar could see Haemish clearly now. He was walking cautiously as only experience could teach. He was covered in bright metal and carried a large two handed sword. His sheer size and presence surprised Athalzar.

“So, you are indeed alone…your death shall make a name for myself Haemish”. Althalzar whispered.

The first spells rocked the giant barbarian. The smell of singed flesh and the intense heat of the released magic filled the air.

“Attack Haemish Gavar!” shouted Athalzar to his water servant. The water elemental sped towards Haemish with the blind obediance of a loyal slave, intent on carrying out his masters wishes. Athalzar released his most damaging spells at the same time.

“By the god of hate! He is tough!” thought Athalzar. “My mind is fatigued from the loss of mana, and yet he still fights like a caged shadow wolf!”.

“Look out Novant!”, Athalzar screams.

It is too late, Novant falls to the swinging blade of the deadly warrior. The confused fight continues. The pets from each caster continue to attack diligently, as Sabanim strikes with cat-like quickness from behind with the strange fighting techniques that he has learned.
“I must do something! I can’t just sit here! Ahhhh!. Athalzar screams as he charges into combat. He is not alone as Sabanim continues to strike quickly against Haemish’s exposed rear.

As Athalzar repeatedly strikes Haemish with his staff, he feels the heat of other spells impacting upon Haemish. “I am sorry master” rings in Athalzar’s ears as his pet is banished back to the elemental plane.

The sounds of battle continue. The falling of bones signifies the death of Katrya’s walking skeleton. “Can we not kill this man?!” questions Athalzar. “He is certainly wounded. Yet he still fights with a savage battle lust!”.

Haemish turns his frenzied eyes upon Athalzar. “By the eye’s of Innoruuk, I will not stand in front of this madman!”.

As Athalzar quickly turns and runs, he can see the giant warrior turn on others. Katrya is next to fall to the barbarians blade. She did not have a chance. Her slim build was not capable of standing up the quick and massive blows that were rained upon her. “Another crime to pay for devil spawn!” shouted Athalzar.

Athalzar was weak now, he knew his contribution to the combat was over. The spell casting and physical exertion had sapped all his energy. He needed water and had none. “Why didn’t I plan better! I was too hasty to take part in the kill!”, he chastised himself. He could not recover his concentration. The thirst and hunger prevented his meditation to harness the magical energies needed to cast spells.

Suddenly, there began an eerie silence. The fight was over. Then a lone shout pierced the silence. “The Silver Daggers are strong! We will survive!”. It came from the warrior he knew now as Haemish. He was not alone now. Another stood beside him. A gnarled giant of a man, dressed in full battle gear. “Curses upon the Silver Daggers!”, hissed Athalzar. “We can not kill two such warriors with our gathered strength.”.

From the top of the hill, hidden, Athalzar watched in silent anguish, as the two warriors clasped each others hands in friendship. He couldn’t take his eyes off them as they walked boldly back into the tunnel. “Your time will come Haemish when you clasp the hands of death instead”.

Slowly, the survivors gathered back at the tunnel entrance, and tended to the wounds of the fallen.
