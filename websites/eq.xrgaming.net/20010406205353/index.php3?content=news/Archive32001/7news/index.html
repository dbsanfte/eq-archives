Good News for Paladins!
Source Everquest General Forums
Absor, etc. I just read the long post about the possible upgrades to Hybrids, mostly including talks about the paladins and shadow knights.

I am all for changes to the paladin class, but I feel that a very, very large problem was left out of the thread. This problem involves the first part of the Paladin Epic quest, the Fiery Avenger.

As of now, the quest involves getting a book from vox and nagafen (and of course, the 'new dragons'. The only problem is, while almost every other class has a reasonable easy time getting their epics, Verant has made it very, very difficult for paladins. Problem being, since you implemented the level limit on Vox/Nagafen, you have hence punished us paladins who have levelled up. So, basically, if we can manage to get the levels together to kill Gorenaire, etc, then MAYBE we might be lucky to get a book drop. This is ridiculous.

I think you should strongly consider implementing an NPC or a few NPCs which, like any other epic mob encounter, require a few groups of high 50's, etc, hell, make it a full out dragon encounter, BUT not as difficult as say, gorenaire. And, make these NPCs drop the paladin epic books with a MUCH higher drop rate than say, Vox/Naggy. Also, don't implement a complete Vox/naggy loot table, that would most likely be a risk vs. reward issue. But, the ability for paladins to get epic quest components really needs to be evaluated. Thanks for listening

One of the designers pulled me to the side and explained something to me about this whole situation.

Let's look at the whole picture here.

Most people seem to agree that the way it was before the caps on Naggy and Vox was just fine. The quests were hard, but not too hard. Drops were not too infrequent.

For characters of the right level, that challenge hasn't changed, so that should still be fine.

Now, consider that it takes about the same number of people to kill the tougher dragons as it does to kill the old ones (35 or so at the low-end and as few as 18 at the high end). Also realize that the books drop at about the same rate off the old dragons as they do off the new ones.

So, basically, Paladins have twice as many opportunities to get these books as they did in the past. I would like to know how this is grossly more difficult than any other epic quest?

- Alan

Roleplaying Server: Brad Speaks
Recently Brad replied to a question and comment about Roleplaying servers on the main EQ Forums. This thread is a must read for anyone who supports the idea of a strict roleplaying server.
"1. Implement Trivial Combat code everywhere"

"What the heck does this have to do with Roleplay? Yes, if you re-configured the loot tables for the whole game it *might* work, but it has absolutely nothing to do with roleplaying. No role-playing game ever had the loot "disapear" if the person who killed it was too poweful."

See, people disagree on what RP is, and isn't.

In any case, I'll explain why I think removing bottom feeding has a LOT to do with RP. When I'm in a dungeon adventuring and I encounter farmers and other characters too high level, it abruptly and forcefully removes me from suspension of disbelief -- it severely damagers the immersiveness of the game. And I think that's directly tied to RP, just as some guy using d00d speak hurts an RP environment, or seeing a Troll ally with a Paladin.

In D&D, it just wouldn't be allowed. No DM worth his salt would allow a group of level 8 players to slaughter through a level 2 module... and even worse, to allow another low level group in the module simultaneously.

Or how about from fantasy literature... how 'RP' would it have been if, as the hobbits were fighting against goblins and wolves, they rounded a bend in the forest to find a group of Valar slaugtering the evil denziens with blinks of their eyes?

"4. Disallow Paladins grouping with Shadow Knights, etc."

"Well there are some RP +S and -s to this. For one thing, clerics are both good and evil, why not paladins? The 'sks are anti-paladins' are ok I guess, but the point is, if the god allows clerics to heal - why wouldn't he have his own "paladin-type" of healing warriors? Also, some gods seem to be neutral (they have both good and evil followers), I'm not sure its paladins wouldn't be "ok" with grouping with SKs. Overall its an ok idea though."

The paladin/sk bit was just an example... I'd also apply it to clerics who follow opposing gods, etc.


"Your opinion Mr.Programer is yours, no doubt about it. If they are/were well thought out and prepared for I would like those changes, but several of them have nothing to do with "role-playing". Don't kid yourself."

I disagree, and I think that even though we disagree, both of our opinions are valid, and neither of us are 'kidding' ourselves.

By the way, I'll hereby slightly adjust our opinion as it relates to an RP server...

Now that we have so many servers, perhaps it wouldn't be so dangerous to set up a server with a slightly different ruleset and call it an RP server... even though many, many would disagree with the ruleset (whether it was fair, or truly RP, or whatever), there are perhaps enough people now who play this game that we could fill the server...

Anyway, just some thoughts... We'll think on it.

-Brad

Archery Damage Reduced
There have been a number of complaints that archery damage isnt maxing out where it used to. Verant replies:
Yes, bow damage was unintentionally capped recently. We'll be changing it back when we patch next.

The reasoning behind it this mistake is pretty complicated. Basically, there was a damage cap already in place, but it wasn't being used. When we were setting up stuff for the new discipline we activated that cap (that's how it was intended to work when first designed, I guess).

Basically, we'll be putting it back as it was soon. I'm sorry about the mistake.

Are Necro's That Bad?
The tension on the boards continued with issues revolving around Necros being underpowered. There was a time I can remember when Necros were THE class to be. They were often the target of players saying they were TOO powerful. This is quite a switch as players complain about things like necros role in groups being limited, and other classes having more important roles. Verant replies:
There are other things that a necromancer can do. You all eschew the use of pets on raids, but they work just fine against any creature that doesn't use fear. And there is Dead Man Floating, summoning corpses… I'm not going to pretend to know everything about how a necromancer works. My concern here is that some of you are calling necromancers useless in any big raid, when it's not true. You may not be able to do a ton of damage to the boss monster, but that is by no means all there is to doing a raid. If you see massive damage dealing as the only way that your necromancer is useful, then that is where we disagree.

And a necromancer can return the corpses so that the cleric can work… No, necromancers have as much to do post and pre raid as any other class, and more than some. And I don't hear most of you concerned with that. The major concern here seems to be that at the 'big' fight you are relegated to a support role, and you don't like that (and that you're not exceedingly efficient at it). In other parts of a raid, clearing or recovering, a necromancer is as useful as any other class (yes, even clerics). I hear that druids are in a similar position. On a raid all they do is a bit of healing and other support stuff…

Oh, and you mentioned DoTs making corpses poof. They don't. As far as we can tell, it hasn't happened in months. We haven't been able to make it happen. At least not on anything like a raid-level mob. If anyone has seen a high level NPC corpse poof from a DoT in the last three months, please let me know and tell me exactly how and where and when and what. High level mobs shouldn't poof anymore. We've coded it so that it just shouldn't happen. It will happen on rare occasions for lower level stuff, and that's probably not going to change (though we are looking at this whole thing again right now). But the high-level-raid-boss-monster-type-NPC corpses shouldn't poof at all due to a fix we made months ago. Don't let that stop you from using DoTs.

Tension On The Boards...
The past couple days have been quite heated on the Verant boards. In one such exchange a player said " What's more, I know you (Verant) aren't going to correct any of the "core" problems with EQ. Everything is subject to change, it seems, except the things that most need changing." His issues seemed to center around Enchanters Pets and Enchanting items and other related things. A response was forthcoming from 2 souces in Verant:
What are the core problems? I'm curious about your take on that. Certainly the fact that enchanters don't make magic items isn't a core problem. It might be the core of your personal dissatisfaction with your favorite class, one that I can't deny. I wish it was in there too. But enchanters work, they work well. They are unique, they fit into the game, they… they work. Do they work as they were initially intended? Maybe, but not entirely. Do they work the way you want them to? It seems not. That's unfortunate. But enchanters are still a rockin' class, and they have some of the coolest abilities of any class in game (and I'm not talking about Clarity, I'm talking about illusions).

But, at the end of all of this, I have to ask the personal question, since this is clearly a personal issue between you and me. How have _I_ earned your disrespect?

[Brad Weighs In]

I do recall replying to AuntieShaynta publicly quite some time ago, stating that it was still our intent to implement Enchanters enchanting items, but that such plans were still very much in the planning stages and considered long term.

And I stand by it -- I still think we'll see that one day (and given our desire to pretty much freeze class balancing, it will probably be one of the last significant class enchancements). But hey, never say never :) Consider traps :)

Also, if I recall correctly, item enchantment was also talked about in earlier pre-release (probably pre-beta, but I'm not certain) FAQs, but I later had it removed (along with guildhouses, lycanthropy, vampirism, etc.) It simply was one of the few features we'd planned on but didn't have time to deliver on.

It does trouble me a little that even though I try to be clear about whether something is short term, or long, or something we're only thinking about, etc. that it often turns into a 'promise' over time... but I guess that's unavoidable. It's a form of the 'telephone game' quite often, where one person hears that we said one thing, and then they tell somebody else, and the message sort of evolves from there :)

Just to let you all know, there are still a TON of things we want to add to EQ, and we feel EQ has a very long life ahead of it. For example, although it's too early to say much, I will say that we've got some VERY big plans post-Velious.

And Tera, wow... not sure what to say :( I certainly do my best to live up to my word. Again, though, some times things I talk about as something planned evolve somehow into 'promises'... And then some things I hopefully characterized as 'long term' are still just that: long term plans that haven't happened yet.

All things considered I'm very proud of our track record and just how much of the game we talked about pre-release game into fruition. And I'm also very proud of where the live and expansion teams have taken the game post-release. IMHO, EQ is almost everything we'd planned it to be and a whole lot more.

From: the St. Petersburg Times, Tampa Florida USA

As his 9-month-old son cried last July, Tony Lamont Bragg Sr.'s mind was on something else: a computer game called Everquest.

Authorities believe Bragg squeezed Tony Jr. to keep him quiet, then left him unattended in a utility closet in their Temple Terrace apartment for more than 24 hours. The boy died.

On Tuesday, Bragg, 24, pleaded guilty to aggravated manslaughter and was sentenced to 15 years in prison.

Bragg had been playing the online fantasy role-playing computer game for hours and appeared to get annoyed by his son's crying, said prosecutor Suzanne Rossomondo.

Velious Gets Reviewed AGAIN!
Gamepower released its review of Scars scoring it 3 out of 4 stars. Here are some highlights:
The expansion pack opens up a heretofore-undiscovered continent that includes 16 new zones and expands the world of EverQuest by about 20%. There's a whole host of new creatures -- including frost giants in some impressive new armor; cave bears; snowfang gnolls; dire wolves; ottermen, who look kind of cute until you see their teeth; and lots of dragons. Velious also features the enhanced 3-D engine used for The Ruins Of Kunark expansion pack, so the graphics are a little bit nicer than in the original game. It's not quite as robust an expansion as Kunark, though, because there's no new playable race. Still, it's a solid expansion, but some players may wish it had a little more to it.

There's a lot to like in Velious, but it's not perfect. The basic gameplay is unchanged, so if you were tired of EverQuest before, this expansion won't rekindle your interest. The biggest complaint I have about the game is that it's tuned for level 35+ players. If you don't have a character that high, you simply can't play in the new lands unless you enjoy fleeing for your life. The box does a poor job of making that clear. The only hint on the box that Velious is for advanced players is the single sentence: "Dozens of quests for characters level 35+." It fails to make it clear that there are no areas suitable for characters who are not level 35+. The Kunark expansion contained all types of areas -- zones for beginning characters and zones for advanced characters.

Still, the expansion is inexpensive at $20. So, if you have a character that's level 35 or higher and you still enjoy playing EverQuest, the Velious expansion is a slam-dunk. If you see a level 25 halfling druid running as fast as he can away from trouble in Velious, that's me. Thank goodness for that Spirit Of Wolf spell!

Velious Gets Reviewed!
CGO released a review of the new Scars expansion pack scoring it 4.5 out of 5 stars. Here are some highlights from it:
Velious concentrates more on the stuff. Character levels are still capped at 60. There are no new races. It has less surface area than Kunark (but more than Faydwer). And you can only reach the new continent, which lies southwest of Antonica, via the exceptionally photogenic vessel Icebreaker from North Ro or by hopping along on some wizard or druid's coat tails. It's also a pretty rough neighborhood for anyone below level 40, but considering the rate at which players now seem able to whiz through their apprenticeship (Internet survival guides for every class are pretty thorough), this won't be much of a backbreaker. Once you do arrive, there's quite a bit to see and kill. Including, at long last, little white bunnies.

What detracts from this beguiling immersion? Things that the absorbed faithful probably no longer even notice—the long-standing spelling errors that still permeate the text ("Jobarn's eyes gleam with maddness!"), the glassy stares of the NPCs eternally gazing past you outside their guilds and shops, the crew-less boats, the banal zone-chatter, and the rather incongruous total absence of blood. One need travel no further than the nearest theater playing Dungeons & Dragons: The Movie to feel the outraged shudders of Tolkien and Robert E. Howard from their graves, but EverQuest treats the much-maligned underpinnings of heroic fantasy with such reverence, it's easy to see why the sanest of adult folk get so wrapped up in their character's needs and persona.

Killing the enemies and performing the quests of the race you choose to align with can make a huge difference in your faction standing with all three and govern the degree of difficulty you'll face traveling through their domains. Traversing Kael Drakkan is especially difficult if you haven't built up your faction with the Giants, but at some point you'll have to. Beyond it lies the Wakening Land, the only zone to sport green foliage and the launching point for most of the western continent. From there you can also access the druid-happy residence of Tunare, the Plane of Growth, and the wyvern-infested dungeon of Skyshrine (with a tricky maze of ramps to navigate en route). There's more, a lot more, including the Labyrinth of Velrator, the toothy frost spikes that swarm the corridors of Icewell Keep, and the Plane of Mischief where Bristlebane, the King of Thieves himself, resides. Evil characters will have the biggest field day in both Planes since they can hunt without much fear of faction reprisal elsewhere.
