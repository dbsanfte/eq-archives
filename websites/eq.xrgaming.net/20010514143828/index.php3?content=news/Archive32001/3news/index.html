Scars Gets Reviewed
Gamespy has released their review of Scars scoring it an 88. Here are some highlights from it:
Once again, Verant raises another landmass from out of the oceans of Norrath. The Scars of Velious expansion pack is upon us, and it brings to light the newly discovered island of Velious, which was scarred by the great wurm queen herself, Veeshan, back when the planet was uninhabited. Velious offers players new zones to explore as well as a slew of new items, spells and quests. Verant also increased the polygon count for the new models that inhabit the island, and also increased the level of detail used for the new armor, weapons and textures.

To get to the new land, you can simply be teleported over by a friendly wizard or druid. However, for your very first visit, it's recommended you go by way of boat, so that you can check out the awesome glacier-mashing vessel, the Icebreaker. To go by sea, make your way to the Northern Desert of Ro and locate the new dock and the lone gnome pirate. From this dock, a skiff will arrive and take you to a small island located in the Iceclad Ocean, inhabited by more gnome pirates. On the other side of the sandy island, you'll find an extravagant-looking dock; this is where you will have to wait for the Icebreaker. Once the Icebreaker arrives, you will be taken on a lengthy boat ride to the main island, Velious.

Velious, however, isn't a recommend romping ground for anyone below level 30. The Snow Cougars alone that you will notice prowling around upon your arrival are of low level but have a very nasty bite. They also travel in packs, so they are quite capable of taking out unprepared, low-level adventurers; the invisibility spell is highly recommended in this land.

Paladins and Horses Go Hand In Hand
The classic concept of a Paladin includes the image of the mounted knight. So why cant Paladins in EQ have horses or at least a spell to make it look like they were riding?
First, as we all know, making a horse that could be ridden as a separate item is not going to happen. It's not something that we're going to try (as far as I know) with the engine. Just imagine player summoned boats in every zone…

But the idea of, essentially, an illusion for paladins that makes them look like a horse and rider and gives them some buff (like faster movement and maybe some stats) has flaws too.

All paladins with that spell up would look exactly the same. The illusion would have to completely rewrite the look of the character, and isn't going to take into account what armor the paladin is wearing. He'll look exactly like every other paladin on the same horse. They might be distinguishable by their weapons…

And, well, frankly, the next on the list (in my mind) for some new illusions (and use of the oh-so-precious video memory) should be enchanters.

Can enchanters get more illusions?
Its been a hot topic recently on the Verant boards and things like changes in gender, copying other players and copy creatures has been asked for. Here is Alan's take on that:
Let me guess on these now, and I'll try to get more detailed answers for you later. Illusion gender, I don't know why that couldn't be done. I do know why illusion copycat wouldn't be done. Keep in mind that your race is one of the things that determines how NPCs react to you. If an enchanter could mimic any creature he or she might very easily be able to travel anywhere with no risk. This isn't something that we want. I'd never heard the idea of Illusion Pet. It's interesting, though I wonder how odd you'd look… :)

Everqust Ruining Lives?
Gamers.com put up an article that I thought was interesting about peope who's lives have been "hurt" by EQ who plan to show up in Baltimore for the Fan Fare to educate people with their views. The article reads:
Members of EverQuest-Wids (EQW), an email support group for people whose relationships are suffering thanks to a loved one's addiction to EverQuest, are planning to attend the Baltimore EQ Fan Faire in February.

The convention, hosted by Verant, is a fine arena for making the addiction problem visible, an EQW member suggested, and letting the company see the results of its game. Another said that Verant should explain why a class-action lawsuit should not be brought against it for loss of affection and income.

The devastating effects of addiction to an online game have gotten more attention recently as reports of divorce, job loss and depression surfaced in the wake of a player's suicide hoax. Beyond EQ, an online support group, formed as a result. However, BE emphasizes that Verant should not be blamed nor held responsible for EQ addiction.

LD's caused by People? Framerate?
An interesting discussion on the Verant boards about what causes alot of LD's. One player felt sure it was too many people in a zone but Alan replied:
Not really. It's a black hole for framerate, not bandwidth. And we've added the ability to turn off the dynamic lighting in those situations, and that helps a lot. This is all a problem on the client side, where there is too much stuff for you video card to render quickly. This is a framerate problem, not a lag problem. That's not server-side nor Internet lag. Too many people in a zone could cause server-side lag, but that requires in the neighborhood of a few hundred people, not forty.
