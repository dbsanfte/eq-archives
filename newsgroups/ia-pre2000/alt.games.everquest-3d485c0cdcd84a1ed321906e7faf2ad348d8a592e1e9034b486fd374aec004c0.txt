state a few things for the record...

1.  Numerous attempts have been made at similar legislation in the
realms of other consumer products, from automobiles to recorded music to
yes, software.  BUT--

2.  One of the best things about the US Government is that such stupid
laws written by halfwit legislators usually either wither on the vine of
the legislative process (as in, the guys playing with full decks in
state or federal legislatures invariably get involved at some point and
manage to save taxpayer dollars by killing the stupid bill before it
leaves committee) or, the judicial branch gets involved, tells the
oxygen thieves in the legislature that passed an unconstitutional law to
get a clue, and hopefully the taxpayer dollars squandered on the whole
mess gets the woodheads who wrote it voted out of office.

it, some of which would scare you far more than this relatively inocuous
little violation of the First Amendment.  *Usually*, the stupidity of
the whole exercise in these situations never sees much light of day. 
Occasionally they do, and the courts have to step in and clean up the
mess.  I'd bet some software company put some hefty bucks into someone's
pocket to get this bill written, and as soon as someone bright enough to
read gets hold of it, it'll fall by the wayside.
