The Internet is perfectly ready to handle massively multiplayer games. 
What is lacking is properly thought out design and networking arrangements
for popular graphical MUDs (so far there are only four Meridian59, The
Realm, UO and Everquest).

Now Meridian59 went for good design and a low player/server ratio.  This
has worked brilliantly since the game's inception over two years ago.  It
is truly a well engineered game.  The Realm - which I hated - I can't
comment on other than what I've heard from others and they indicate that
the network side is fine.

Now UO was badly designed to begin with - they didn't understand how to
cope with latency in the game.  They failed to learn from the way, for
example, M59 coped with any network difficulties and programmed the client
to "lock" your character when the connection was throttled (the old one
step, wait, one step, wait thingy).

Everquest's design is again a return to the Meridian model.  The client and
server interpolate to overcome all but the worst network problems. 
However, so far, their networking side is atrocious.  They didn't purchase
enough bandwidth to their servers and their log-in server coding is poor.

They've already patched the log-in server and the client (so it is more
informative while dealing with the log-in process - no more 5 minute waits
while it does nothing) but they still have packet loss which they will need
to obtain more bandwidth to fix.

When they do, it will be a it was in Beta - thousands of people on a server
without any packet loss, latency or other problems.

The internet is ready - the games companies are learning.
