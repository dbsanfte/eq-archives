Well here is the general strategy as I see it (of course Bill Gates is on
the board so to speak of the unified developers group who moved to get this

process to write bills into laws (most likely big money promising more tech
jobs and other perks to these states as well). They also select the state
where they think they can get it done first with the best odds. Then they
choose a time to do this when the focus of the country is on other issues
and any blurbs about this would most likely be at the low interest catagory
therefore never make air press time if things had been quiet from a news
stand point (If you have spent the money, time, and conferred to get these
bills written, you of course will also employ strategies to get them
implemented which would include some or all of the above tactics). Once 1
state has this law passed, it becomes easier to pressure other states to
roll over and adopt them. Sort of a domino effect. What sucks is if these
bills actually do become law, they will be struck down "eventually", but
from the attorney I talked to who has experience in this area, they are as
hard as hell to get struck down, and guess who spends the money to do that?

all can do is make sure everyone we know and talk with is AWARE of this
situation. Then email your government web sites. Call/fax/write your state
representatives ( I know alot of people do not go that far but this is one

like 500+ voters in thier formulas. I know this from my involvement in clean
water action where I donate and help with letter submissions. You can do one
for every member of your family). Other than that there is not much we can
do other than maybe meet it with a consumer software bill of rights and try
to beat them to the punch which unless is already drawn up in some form
somewhare, probably a bit too late for the states considering this now.
