Personally, I thought it rocked. This time there were /actors/. (Mark Hamil
does NOT count, and, frankly, Carrie Fisher never did it for me.) The only
ones in the originals who could act were Sir Alec and Harrison Ford. IMHO,
of course.

Plus, the movie had great effects and the costuming, etc, was fantastic.
Plot? I thought it rocked, too... with the exception of the over-indulgence
of JarJar and Annikin. JarJar's entire excuse, however, is that he was a
replacement for C3P0 who couldn't be in the movie (I guess Lucas wanted
something different this time). JarJar's ENTIRE purpose was to be the comic
relief, just like C3P0 was in the original set.

Annikin? Well, I wasn't impressed with the little tyke, myself. His acting
sucked, but then the only child actor I've ever seen who COULD act was
Natalie Portman when she was younger. And let me tell you, that's exactly
why Lucas picked her. Natalie rocked. Besides, she's nice to look at without
being just a pretty pair of tits on the screen. Women generally like
actresses with depth, and I'm no exception.

Liam? Looked a little too scruffy for me, but he did a good job, IMHO.
Nothing I can say about Ewan except "More!" ;>

And I applaud Lucas for writing a script which is both overt (for the
kiddies) and subtle (for the adults) at the same time. Underneath the JarJar
antics and Annikin shoot-em-ups there are some extremely mature themes going
on in that movie. You just have to be slightly more observant to enjoy them.
And I can really appreciate how his craft has progressed. Lucas was playing
with oh... let's see... I count five or six different story threads off of
the top of my head. For him to be able to juggle that many storylines and
tie them all neatly together with one climax is just freakin' impressive to
me. But then, I'm an English major, so I'm totally into the structure thing.
Go fig.

I just think you have to look beyond the pretty light sabers and JarJar's
stupid friggin' tongue. But I'll admit, JarJar made me laugh.

Incidentally, have you ever noticed the way *all* of the principle
characters are young people? They're children or young adults coming into
their own (ibid. Luke's transformation over the course of three movies from
farm boy to accomplished Jedi Knight... and don't forget his twin sister, of
course, who goes from being a brat to.. well... being a brat with a lot of
power. Annikin? Kid. Padme? Kid. Obi in Episode I? Brand spankin' new Jedi
flung out on his own before officially being released.) It IS for kids, in a
way. Even kids as old as me.
