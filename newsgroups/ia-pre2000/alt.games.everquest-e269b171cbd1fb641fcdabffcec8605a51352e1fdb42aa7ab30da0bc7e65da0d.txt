Well, you can just go back to UO then!  No, wait, thats not right.  I
agree with you.  This game is very addicting, but ultimately, its not
much fun.  Run around, kill things, kill more things, kill even more
things, die to either an instant death monster or a bug ("design
decision") and lose all the time you spent killing things.  Die a
couple of more times recovering your corpse, fight some more stuff,
get tricked into going bankrupt on worthless trade skills, fight some
more, die to a hill giant that spawned over your head, kill some
stuff, argue with a high level mage that keeps following you around
stealing kills, kill some other stuff, repeat forever.  

The game is addictive because you always feel like you're _about_ to
do something fun and exciting, but it never seems to happen.  Part of
that is inherent in the nature of an online game ("Yes!  Yes!  I
killed the super evil person!  I saved the world!  I... Oh, wait,
there he is over there again.") , but most of it is bad design.
Although they can't REALLY let you do something unique and important
that changes the world, they need to at least make you feel as though
you made a little bit of difference.  Just a little.  

Personally, the backstory that I came up with for EQ is that we're in
hell.  Its the only explaination.  I mean, you have to fight and kill
and die continuously, and yet nothing you do matters in the least.
The best thing that you can do is save another player who's about to
die, but really, all you did was save them a little annoyance.  "Oh
darn, have to go find my corpse again."  

The solution?  Something like the following  (Note, this is a cut and
paste from one of my previous posts.  Don't see why I would need to
type it again.)


>I disagree, it would be very easy to make changes to EQ so that it at
>least _sort of_ feels like what you do matters.  Currently, in EQ, you
>often have the following...
>Player:  Hail, RandomNPC.
>RandomNPC:  Oh, I'm in so much [trouble], I wish someone would help me
>Player:  What trouble?
>RandomNPC:  I need (item) from RandomNPC2.  Will you [go get the item]?
>Player:  I will go get the item.
>RandomNPC:  Thank you, please return when you have it.

>Then, you go off, find the item, and give it to RandomNPC.

>RandomNPC:  Thank you!  I will remember you always!  Here, take this!

>You then get a couple of gold, or an almost worthless item, and a
>(meaninglessly small) bit of experience.  So, you talk to them again.

>Player:  Hail, RandomNPC.
>RandomNPC:  Oh, I'm in so much [trouble], I wish someone would help me
>Player:  What trouble?
>RandomNPC:  I need (item) from RandomNPC2.  Will you [go get the item]?
>Player:  I will go get the item.
>RandomNPC:  Thank you, please return when you have it.

>Why, gee, its the exact same quest all over again!  Now, I'm not saying
>that no quest should ever be reused.  What I'm saying is that it should
>store the fact that your character completed the quest, and for the next
>couple of days, when you talk to them, they say something like, "Hello,
>Player!   Thank you again for helping me!"  Then, after a few days, you
>can do the quest again, but when you talk to them, they say something
>like, "Hello, Player.  Could you please help me again?  I need more of
>(item) from RandomNPC2.  Will you [go get the item]?"

>I don't think that this is in any way an impossible thing for them to
>implement.  It would require more space to store the character information,
>yes, but it would add a _lot_ of depth to the game.  It would make you
>feel like your actions matteres at least a little.  As it is now,
>everything that you do is a waste of time because it will immediately
>need done again.
