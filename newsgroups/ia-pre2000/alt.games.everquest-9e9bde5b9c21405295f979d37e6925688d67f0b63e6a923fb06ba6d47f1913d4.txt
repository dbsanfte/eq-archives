Ooooo...  I like these ideas.

>I think there is the problem with most online RPG games. At least UO
>was somewhat playable but still nothing great.
>
>I am so glad you guys are posting about this "dying and loose XP"
>thing. I was almost tempted to try out EverQuest, until I read about
>this. This is a REAL pet-peeve of mine, and I can't stand games that
>make you loose XP for death. Ok, drop my CON, that would make sense.
>Ok, loose my stuff, that makes sense. But LOSE EXPERIENCE??!  Hell,
>DEATH IS AN EXPERIENCE!! It should GIVE you XP when you die.
>
>My "death in a massively multiplayer RPG" ideas:
>
>1. If you die, you are a corpse. You do not respawn. You do not
>pass go. You do not collect $200.
>
>2. If you have friends who can ressurect you, they can carry around
>your body to a temple and pay to have you ressurected. There could
>be a chance for total loss of spirit, and the longer the wait, the
>less likely of a successful ressurection.
>
>3. If you fail ressurection, you are reincarnated as a new being.
>Basically, you get to re-roll the character, with the same XP you
>had when you died. Then you get to choose where you will be placed
>on earth. This could be handled with some really neat "afterlife"
>cutscenes.
>
>3.5 - sneak this one in here. I forgot to make mention, that death
>should be pretty rare in this RPG for this to work. Not something
>that happens every 1/2 hour. It should be like, 3 or 4 times in
>the career of a successful adventurer. To make this possible,
>there needs to be an "incapacitated" status, which basically means
>you are knocked out of the fight due to severe wounds, but if
>the other memebers of your group manage to finish the fight in
>you absense, after the fight you can be healed back up. The only
>time you should be completely dead is if a) the whole group is
>slain, b) the rest of the party has to run away and the monster
>decides to kill you (may or may not, depending on what it is),
>or c) certain cases where you die, like when you fall into a
>pit of boiling acid and no one pulls you out of it before you
>are dissolved. Basically, I would want to design the combat
>system and the game so that characters do not always die every
>time they loose a fight. This could be because many of the
>fights are non-lethal, or it could be for other reasons. Death
>should be a meaningful event, not an everyday occurance.
>
>4. Maybe as an option, sometimes you could turn into Undead of
>some kind after death. Especially depending on how you died.
>Like, if you are killed by a lycantrope, you become one. Or if
>you are killed by zombies, you become one. Vampires? This could
>be really kind of fun to roleplay. There might even be some kind
>of special quest your Undead character has to go on to purify
>their souls so that their body can find rest and their soul can
>be freed to reincarnate. This should be special events, not
>something that happens all the time. And the player should have
>the option to STAY an Undead for as long as that "unlife" can
>last. There should be ways to die the "final death" which will
>ultimately make the player have to reincarnate into a new char,
>possibly with some loss of status in some way.
>
>5. When you die, your physical possessions can be taken by whichever
>character is around when you died. This is most realistic. If you
>adventure with friends, they can get your stuff for you. If you're
>alone, well, tough noogie. You can always go kill whoever takes it
>and get it back yourself.  Taking someone's stuff should be a
>"blackmark" on the "reputation" or "alignment" of the character
>that takes it, unless they were in your "group" when you were killed.
>This will mean players who want to keep a good reputation and
>alignment will not steal from the dead. And the others, well, I
>have some ideas about how to handle the miscreants and evil-doers.
>
>Ok, lets say you die when you're out adventuring alone. You're
>pretty much screwed at this point, but the server should be smart
>enough to know you were not in a party (it should have some way of
>keeping track of who's "sided" into groups), and so at this point
>it will ask you if you want to go ahead and reincarnate, or perhaps
>give you an option to run around as a ghost like in UO so you could
>find someone to come get your body to resurect you. As a ghost, you
>should have the option to grant the right for another character to
>take your stuff for you and have it not affect their reputation
>adversely. Perhaps it puts their rep in a temporary stasis, and
>once they give you your stuff back, it goes up a point (being nice
>means you get better rep), and if they backstab you and keep the
>stuff (which you can decide if they did by choosing some option,
>like an "I got screwed" button in one of the menus), then it hurts
>their rep twice as much as someone who just steals your stuff that
>was lying out there all by itself.
>
>Ok, so what is the deal with this reputation thing anyway? I mean
>what good is it? Well, I would make Rep related to some kind of
>"wanted" status for the character. The worse the rep, the higher
>the bounty on that character. That way "good" (or neutral) bounty
>hunters can actually make money by going out and ridding the
>world of these miscreants. And if you have a bad rep, what happens
>when you die? Bad Karma, dude. Worse stats on reincarnate, maybe
>take an experience hit, whatever. The goal here is to discourage
>PK and otherwise anti-social behavior while still allowing it
>for someone who really likes the challenge. We just want to make
>it tough on them.
>
>Another note on Rep. There should be something that lets other
>players "Vote" for the rep of other chars. In other words, there
>needs to be a way for the actual reputation (from observation
>by other players) to affect the game stat reputation. That way
>if you have a "Kewl D00Dz" character that runs around town
>completely out of character, the other characters can adversely
>affect that character's reputation. Play nice and with good
>RPGing, and others can positively affect your reputation. This
>makes it really advantageous to be a nice guy or girl and
>make lots of friends. This will actively encourage positive
>role-playing. Of course, it can be abused, but abusers can
>be booted. Also, this rep adjustment should be minor compared
>to actual actions like PK or theft.
>
>Well, that's my $0.02 for the MM RPG thing.
