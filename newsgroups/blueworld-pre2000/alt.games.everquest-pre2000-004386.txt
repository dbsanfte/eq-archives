> target, the mass of the ball, air density and wind speed? Or do you

Yes, this is a perfect example of why we need the numbers.

In real life, you can just eyeball the distance and throw the ball
because you have an intuition about the physics of the situation.
As long as a computer game simulates physics, there is no need to
have it tell you the mechanics, because you already know them.
If you had to throw a ball in a computer simulation, and all you 
could do was tune a few force parameters in your virtual arm, you
would need to know things about the ball, like how heavy it is
and how big around it is.  Is there wind resistance?

Now suppose you want to throw the ball as far as you can.  You are 
in an online competition for long distance virtual ball throwing.  
Pick your favorite: the red ball, the green ball or the blue ball.
Bleah.  Wouldn't you at least want to know the diameter and mass
of each ball?
