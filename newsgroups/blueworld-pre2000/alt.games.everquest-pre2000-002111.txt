Let me restate that a bit.  I don't want any skill to neccessarily take
longer just for the sake of extending the advancement ladder.  What I do
want is the elimination of ways to jump up ladder i.e. macros, exploits,
cheat programs.  The type of PK's that are problematic seem to be the
ones who max'ed out their characters these ways.  They essentially
bypassed the advancement ladders altogether, elminating any challenge
these ladders might have posed them.  Once so maxed, there really is no
other ladder of advancement for them to pursue OTHER than PK'ing.

If there was no way to become a GM archer or swordsman or mage without
actualling going through the process of fighting monsters who could
actually hurt you, would high level PK's be as much of a problem as they
were?  I tend to doubt it. Most of the PK horror stories you hear follow
the same themes (that of a high level players or group killing some
random player who had no real chance against them).  The types of
players who jumped the advancement ladders generally are not looking for
a true challenge, thus having to actually work up their combat skills
"legitamately" would probably not hold their interest for long.

The way I see it, the PvP ladder of advancement should be the last and
most difficult ladder of them all, thus it should also be the one most
rigidly maintained to eliminated shortcuts and exploits (macro'ing off
murder kills, using mules to go to town, etc etc).  It should have
serious but reasonable in game repercussions for players who choose to
kill other players without their consent.  So long as there are no true
consequences in game, PvP can easily degenerate mindless PK'ing.

Mining, on the other hand, requires a great mind :-)
