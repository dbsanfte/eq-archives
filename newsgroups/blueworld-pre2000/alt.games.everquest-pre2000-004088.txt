> Lessee.  Removal of basic required features

Not required- just your idea of important...

> (secure trade, banking),

A good example. I admit, I miss both, but can't really say gameplay is 
lacking. They are nice conveniences, but don't significantly add nor 
remove from gameplay. imho.

> tacit support of exploits ("trapping" is permitted),

Heh, trapping isnt even necessary- the AI is so bad, the mobs generally 
trap themselves. This is my biggest gripe with AC- it has the worst AI 
I've seen in years. If they tried to ban people for exploiting it, that 
would just be stupid- the AI is the flaw, not the fact that people 
exploit it.

> lack of basic support of roleplay (no naming policy - every little helps),

I find it much easier to roleplay in AC. I agree that the naming policy 
was a step forward, and its absence a step back, but the world in general 
is much more conducive to creating a personality in than EQ is. Maybe 
some people prefer the cliche cookie cutter characters of EQ, but I find 
it stifling. In AC, I create who I want to be more fully, and have a 
reason for being in the world. Can't quite explain why I feel compelled 
to spend all my time slaughtering almost everything in sight, but I've 
yet to see a CRPG that gets around that problem.

> lack of diversity amongst player races (they have *one*, of which there 
> are three casts),

This is just preference- its part of the world design, it wouldnt make 
much sense if players could create drudge or tumorok characters. Dereth 
is not a cliche fantasy realm, so multiple playable races doesn't have to 
be part of the world. The PC races of Dereth are cultures, not races in 
the sense of dwarves, elves, etc. If one were smarter, and another 
stronger, etc, it would be blatantly racist, as Ispar is pretty 
synonamous with earth -which gives us the oppurtunity to draw from 
cultures we're already familiar with.

> skill based system (everyone will be a tank/mage - well archer mage - 

How much have you played the game? Tank mages are not an issue. And the 
skill system is also just a matter of preference- I hate the class system 
of EQ, I like feeling like I created my own character, unlike in EQ, 
where I pretty much just named a predefined character and called it my 
own.

With a little creativity, the skill system can prevent super hybrids, ala 
UO's tank mages, and AC has gone most of the way in the right direction 
there.

> This genre is new, it needs each new product to propel it forward and add to
> the previous.  Ultima was a kind of side-ways step.  It added to the
> Massiveness that Meridian59 could never manage but it's playability was a
> huge leap backwards, Everquest was a great progression.  Asherons, has no
> redeeming features, except, possibly, for the dynamic world events.

No, all four games are just differennt games in the same genre- that 
doesnt mean they have to be evolutionary steps, they are different enough 
to be like apples and oranges, some people like one some people like 
another. I think EQ's main focus of gameplay (sit in the same spot in a 
dungeon for hours on end) is a huge step behind, compared to AC. But then 
again, I'm not really inclined to compare, they are just different games.
