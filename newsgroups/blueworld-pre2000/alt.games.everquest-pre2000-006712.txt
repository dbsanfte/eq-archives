describing above is basically a bug and not a design mistake.  No one
has yet been banned because they took advantage of the design mistake
in EQ--said mistake have often been "fixed" resulting in various
outcries of "nerfs".

Example of a design mistake:

Feathered Cloak - It was a design mistake to give it an unlimited no
recycle time levitate casting ability.  This wasn't a bug in the code,
this was a concious effort by the coder to make this item behave this
way (ie., to spec or design if you will).  The resulting design
allowed people to chain cast levitate to go up higher then they were
suppose to.  Again, so far no code bug and no one using this has been
warned/banned from the game as far as I know (did anyone get
warned/banned for this?).

Example of a bug:

Corpse duping - By dying twice and standing equidistant from each
corpse and summoning it, it's possible to duplicate your corpse (at
least this was reported in Dr. Twister).  This is obviously a bug in
the code--there was no design specification that allowed anyone to
ever duplicate their corpse.  Any one utilitizing this bug will, I
suspect, be warned/banned immediately.

However, the statement incorrectly uses the term "design mistake".
There is no such thing as a "no-no" where design mistakes are
concerned--at least no one has given a specific example as yet.

Allowing a DoT to take full effect when things are chasing you, that
was a design mistake and it wasn't a "no-no".  Having a critter chase
you into a spot where it can't reach you is a bug (pathing bug) and is
a "no-no".  

What's the difference between these two?  The design specification
prior to the DoT nerf specifically stated that DoTs will do the same
damage regardless of whether the critter is chasing you, standing
still or running from you.  The code was written to spec.  The spec
itself is however, flawed since no one really thought the process
through.

The design specification at no time stated that a mob should get stuck
somewhere and not be able to reach you--this was clearly a coding
flaw.

Taking advantage of the latter is warnable/bannable.  Taking advantage
of the former merely means you get to have it nice until the next
nerf.
