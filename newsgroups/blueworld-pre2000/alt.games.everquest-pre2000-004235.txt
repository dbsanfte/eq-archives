OK, min/maxers will always reduce *any* game system to the most advantageous
combination. That's the whole point of min/maxing...

That being said, it appears you guys are looking for a system which has been
on the shelves since 1980, and has even had an online massively multiplayer
role-playing game made from it.

Rolemaster :)
(the MMORPG made from it was the old Gemstone 3 before they lost their
Rolemaster license.)

It's a level based class system with skills...

You pick a class just like in EQ, Fighter, Rogue, Thief, Ranger, Animist,
Conjurer, Warrior mage, etc. However, you may still learn any skill you wish
it simply costs some classes more than others (somewhat similar to AC, but
not quite) For example it is extremely cost efficient for warriors to put
their development points ( a stat based sum of points gained each level to
be spent on skills) into weapons use, or shield bashing, or body development
(hit points). If he wishes though he can put points into lock picking or
stalking and hiding, it won't be as efficient as his core skills but he can
choose to do so, even less efficient would be a warrior trying to learn
magic, although he could by sacrificing much of his martial ability (because
he would have to use many points to gain a relatively weak spellcasting
ability, when the same points could be use to substantially increase his
fighting ability)

A warlock can swing a sword, a druid could wear plate, just remember,
because of the increased costs neither of these two would be as good as one
of their type that would have specialized in being a Warlock or Druid  (and
in the case of the plate wearing druid, it would hurt his druid ability
severely due to the dramatic cost for them to wear plate, none the less, the
determined could have their way)

Does this mean there wouldn't be one class, and one set of skills that would
become the best? Of course not, the min/maxers will always find it. :)
