On Tue, 25 Jan 2000 09:51:18 -0600, "Olaf" <olaf@houston.rr.com> wrote:

>No, there DOES need to be server caps.  Not a cap on the number of people
>that can play on a server at one time, but an ACCOUNT cap.

The problem with this approach is that no one knows just how many people
will be online at any given time. Historic usage stats might give you a
clue, but we have all (us players and Verant themselves) been blindsided
by the upsurge in players since Christmas, so you can't rely on
historical data that much.

If you put a cap of say...10,000 accounts per server (just a figure
pulled out of thin air to illustrate the point only), making the
assumption that at any given time 20% of the accounts will be online,
that translates to an average of 2,000 players per server. What if
everyone hears that there will be a cool GM event and plans to attend?
Now you may have 5,000 people online. Granted, this can happen right now
with no caps, but I'm pointing out that with even a modest 10,000
account cap, you may have overcrowding.

Okay, then...drop the account cap to 5,000 per server. An overcrowded
50% presence now equates to 2,500 players online. But a typical 20%
presence is now only 1,000 players. You might say "1,000 players in a
server is very cool; no lag!" and you might be right on that account,
but that is a really low population which might underrepresent PC's in
some key areas, such as Highpass, where a larger population means more
campers for orcs and gnolls. "Less campers means more for me!" you say?
Well, that may be true for those of a high enough level, but all those
low teens trying to run across a confusing place like Highpass will die
without their camper protectors. It's happened to me and many people I
know a bunch of times.

Also keep the following in mind: Form Verant's perspective, it's
probably not economical to put up a server for every "X" thousand
accounts. I don't know what the "best" solution for overcrowding is, but
I know that putting server caps is not it. I tend to think that Verant's
current method, however kludgy, is probably the way to go.
