> >     If you design your game such that if the players lose "something
> >truly awful happens," you have to prepare for the eventuality that
> >"something truly awful" will happen.  
> 
> It doesn't have to be something truly awful.  I'd be happy if
> something happened, period.

     Either way, although the smaller the impact the more you're going to
feel restrained.  The key point is that game players just don't play nice
with the game designer's system, and if you give them the ability to fail
you darn well better be prepared to handle it if they do.  And the players
need to be able to handle it as well.

     A lot of people are going to approach it like a thrill ride.  They
want the illusion of risk, but they don't want to ever have the reality of
losing.

> >town" and have an opportunity to retake the town.  Of course, if the
> >monsters keep respawning how do you do that?
> 
> Yah, I want some way to change things in the world.  Think Covetous is
> ever going to run out of rats or the gnolls will ever be driven out of
> Blackburrow?  I don't.

     No, and I would love to see a game where that sort of thing could
happen.  What happens, though, when you run the gnolls out?  Does the area
become civilized, with nothing to fight?

     I think it would be cool to be able to eradicate the gnolls, maybe
even permanently with enough effort.  And if the players do there aren't
any more gnolls.  Seems like if that were difficult enough you could give
the players something to work hard for, but still make it rare enough that
you can keep up with introducing new monsters and threats.
