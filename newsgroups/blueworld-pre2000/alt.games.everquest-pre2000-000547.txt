This is the problem with your logic. The amount of C14 in the atmosphere has
been stable for thousands of years, until 1945 when the above ground nuclear
detonations changed the concentrations of C14 in the atmosphere for the next
several thousand years. So C14 dating will work on anything that a)
breathed, b) died before 1945, and c) died less than about 8000 years ago.
Since C14 decays fairly rapidly, it is only useful for dating objects back
about 8k years, anything that died before that has so little C14 remaining
in it that it is difficult to acurately measure it by radioactive decay,
although careful laboratory analysis may in fact allow dating to a less
acurate degree (give or take 1000 years) back to about 12 thousand years.
