Kethos wrote:
> 
> Reposted from EQ's General Board:
> ------------------------------------------------------
> 
> Q: What if say you had a group of a level 40, 38, 38, 35, 33,
> and a 32? Does the exp given come from the level 40 (meaning
> the exp changes dependings on their level) or is it based on a
> level 36 person (Average level of group) or is the amount of
> exp given the same no matter what level the party is but that
> higher levels need more exp and thus their bar raises more
> slowly than the level 32? I mean how exactly does this work?
> 
> Gordon: We choose not to release any formulae in regard to how
> experience is divided up. That is probably why you haven't seen
> explained "exactly". What I will say is that it is divided up
> in such a way that a group of friends who always play together
> will level at a similar rate.

Why are those formulas not released?  All you are doing is
causing frustration.

I am fairly certain that experience is divided up weighted by
total earned experience for each player.  This would preserve the
ratio of total experience between players in a group.  (If you
have half as much experience as another member in your group, you
will still have half as much experience after fighting all day.)
Assuming that the experience curve is roughly exponential, this
means that your level difference will also be preserved.  We know
that the curve is not strictly exponential because of hell
levels, so you will see people drawing closer together at hell
levels and drifting further apart between hell levels.

> Q: Why exactly were hell levels placed in the game? When I
> first hit the hell level (I was second on my server to do so) I
> thought it a bug and reported it. GM Solist said he couldnt
> understand the hell level and said he'd look into it as well as
> several senior guides but after a while it was considered a
> game feature. Why though?
> 
> Gordon: It's the way the experience curves work. Unfortunatly,
> going in and fixing it at this point isn't an option. Anyone
> who had gone through a "hell level" would instantly get another
> level or two. Additionally, people who had already fought there
> way through several of them would be cheated out of the work
> they did.

It could be fixed, but you would have to change people's
experience totals to match the new curve.  The curve could easily
be smoothed out without changing the total experience required to
reach 50.  You would probably want to adjust each character's
experience total to display the same level and fraction of a
level.

Of course, it might not be worth the effort and the step curve
might even be desirable.  The effect of the step curve is to give
people faster gain for four levels and then painfully slow gain
for one.  Smoothing out the curve would give people a constant
rate of gain, slower than the current "normal" rate, but much
faster than hell levels.

> There is one upside to them. It helps groups stay together.

That is not true in the long run.  A group that spans a hell
level will seem to be closer in level, but by the time they have
passed it they will be spread out again.  The only way to get a
group closer together in levels is to let the lower level
characters get experience without the higher level ones or to
have the higher level characters die a lot.  It would be nice if
the group experience formula were tweaked a bit to gradually
bring groups closer together in levels.
