The Shortest Barbarian On the Block wrote:
> 
> Can someone more technically minded than m'self explain what mapping is,
> what the advantages/disadvantages are, and what other cards can use it?

I'm long on theory, but short on practical knowledge, so I can answer
the first part of that. ;)

Texture maps are images that are essentially painted onto polygons
in the scene.  When you are rendering a polygon, each pixel on the 
screen gets a color from a pixel in your texture map.  If the polygon 
is far away, one pixel on the screen might correspond to rather a lot
of pixels in the texture map.  If you only pick one, the texture will
look very jittery.  If the polygon moves relative to the camera, 
different pixels in the texture will be chosen, essentially at random.
This is called aliasing, and it looks really nasty, rather like bugs
crawling over the surfaces of all your textured polygons.  

Mipmapping is a technique for avoiding this kind of aliasing.  Instead
of using a single texture for an object, you use the big detailed 
texture when the polygon is close to you, and you use smaller filtered
textures when the polygon is further away.  One simple algorithm is 
to create a texture map half the size in each dimension as the original,
with each pixel being the average of four pixels in the original.

Advantages of mipmapping: 
	Reduces aliasing
	May allow the video card to store less texture data

Disadvantages of mipmapping: 
	Someone has to make the mipmaps
	Can result in blurry images if the mipmaps are chosen poorly

If this is too techie, ask about the unclear parts and I will try 
again.  I'm really supposed to be able to explain this kind of stuff. ;)
