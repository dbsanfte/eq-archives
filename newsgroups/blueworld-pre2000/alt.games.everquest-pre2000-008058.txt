They are the same list.  The list just holds two values for each person.
This makes sense from a programming standpoint since everyone who is 
hurting the monster is going to be hated by the monster.  It fails when
the monster has too many potential hate targets and forgets who was 
supposed to get credit for killing it. ;)

You are also mixing up different factors in your list.  These factors add
to the hate value:

  Attacking a monster in any way adds hate.  (Resists add hate for the 
  full spell up front.)

  Casting any beneficial spell on anyone that the monster hates 
  (including yourself) adds hate.

  Anything that angers a monster angers its nearby friends.

The monster has a list of people it remembers.  It keeps a hate value and 
a kill credit value for each person.  It also has a current target for its
aggression.  As the monster makes its action, it chooses a target.  Factors
that make it more likely to choose you are:

  How much it hates you.

  Being the current target.  They don't like to switch.

  Being close by.

  Being seated (looting counts as seated).  I think this is to prevent 
  people from meditating during fights.

  Having a small number of hit points (whether by being wounded or by being
  a caster).

The combination of small hits, close by and current target produce the
phenomenon we call frenzy.

Sitting down makes green things that would normally leave you alone brave 
enough to attack you.  Some green creatures will enter a fight to help 
their friends.  Everything will fight to defend itself.
