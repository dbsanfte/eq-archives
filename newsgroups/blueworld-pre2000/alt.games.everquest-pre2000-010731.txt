In any case, if we look at this from a Christian perspective - a lot of
Christians don't seem to give God any credit for intelligence.  (Primarily
why I abandoned the Christian faith - too many Christians have taken the
Living God, and suffocated Him under a huge mountain of inflexible dogma.)
They assume that, if God has said "don't worship anyone else", then simply
playing the part of one who worships someone else is automatically "sinful".
What about players in films or theatre?  Are these things sinful because
people are required to play a part who might not be Christian?

Think about it.  Any God who's created a world as complex as ours has to be
quite a bright critter, when all's said and done.  And yet so many
Christians don't judge Him intelligent enough to know the difference between
real life and play.

My character was a follower of Innoruuk.  But that doesn't mean I have to
worship Innoruuk in real life, now does it?  And all you Christians out
there are just going to have to trust me - God won't mind if you subscribe
your character to Bristlebane Fizzlethorpe, Innoruuk, or Karana the
Rainkeeper - or WHOEVER - because your God knows it's *just a game*.

If you're STILL uncomfortable, then go for Agnostic.  At least then you're
not committing yourself, and you're not claiming to follow any other gods.
Finally, I think I ought to point out that most practical Christians know
that there's a certain interpretation to be placed on the concept of
"spreading the word", and there are some places and situations where
evangelising just isn't necessary.  I'd say that a fantasy role-playing game
is one of those situations - surely it's best to save it for real life?
