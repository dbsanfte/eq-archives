|Exactly my point.  Why act unethically, thus becomming an outcast, if there
|is nothing to gain by it?

	Uh, because there IS something to gain for it.

|If you allow me to perish to the dragon and then
|kill it yourself, what have you gained?  

	The same thing you were fighting the dragon for; experience and its pHaT
l00t.

|A pixel of experience maybe - maybe
|I lost a bit of experience.  But was any real loss to be had?

	Uh, yes; you lose experience, you lose the opportunity to acquire that
mob's assests, you lose the time required to retrieve your corpse (and all the
assets that you could have acquired in that time) and the time required to
recoup your lost experience (which would have been spent improving your
position had you not died).

|In Ultima Online the temptation to behave unethically is there because the
|reward to be had from performing this act is right in front of your face -
|you get to wear that guy's armor.  You get his reagents.  Those things are
|expensive, and they tempt a person every time he walks by a fallen hero.

	No, they're not expensive.  Nothing in UO is expensive except housing, and
that only because all or nearly all possible lots have been built on.  UO
allows the players to simply create money as needed, so if it's available to
be bought you can have it.  Gold is fundamentally worthless.

	Furthermore, your point just lies down and dies here.  If UO's environment
does reward unethical behavior (and it does, I'd say) then it is not
surprising that it is populated almost entirely by unethical people (which it
is), because the ethical ones will *LEAVE* when they realise the system is
selecting against them.  You seem to have come rather late in life to the
realisation that rotting meat attracts maggots.

|Again, my fascination is not in the beauty of the game play (anything that
|makes you hold down the right mouse button to mouselook has problems, and
|anything that makes you hold down the right mouse button to move has
|problems - that's the problem with RPG nerds making MUDs that look like
|FPS's, and those are one example of a long chain of problems with EQ and
|UO)

	I have no problem with either.

|, but in how the people respond to various temptations that are offered
|by UO.  It's not that these temptations result in quick, instant profit, but
|the path is quicker, if you're willing to take the risk.

	What risk?  UO has no death penalty save loss of equipment, and PKs don't
use top-of-the-line stuff, as the game conveniently provides them with
disposable gear like bone armor that's just as good as the expensive stuff.
They also win much more often than they lose.
