> I still don't get the bad for roleplaying bit.  I could definitely ask your
> class/level in character (and have given such examples previously).  Moreover,
> it's an increased chance for RP interaction.. if you truly want RP, I just
> don't understand the argument that being unable to INSTANTLY pin down your
> capabilities somehow *enhances* roleplaying.

It enhances role playing in a couple ways.

First of all, it adds detail to your character.  Currently, you have
your name, your clothes, your speech and actions, your character profile,
and your class to distinguish your character from those around you.  Many
of these factors are irrelevant much of the time.  People speak in group
mode, so you can't hear them.  That stupid message prevents people from
seeing your character profile.  Most of the time, the only things people
can see are your name, your clothes and your class.  Removing the tag
"Shadowknight", "Ranger" or "Paladin" from the tiny set of interesting 
things that you can show others about your character makes you more bland
and weakens your role.  

If the /con system did anything remotely reasonable, seeing someone's exact 
level would be unnecessary.  However, there will be ranges of 30 levels 
that you can't distinguish.  An accomplished and powerful Druid should 
look different from a fresh faced youngster.  Currently, you can display
this difference in only one way.  Turn off anonymity.

Would you want your name to be hidden as well because it is unreasonable 
that you are always wearing a name tag?  Somehow I don't see that enhancing
role play, even though anyone who wanted to know your name would have to 
ask you.

Also, anonymity is so often used as a "Do not disturb" flag, that people
tend not to talk or interact with anonymous characters.

Perhaps the problem is that we have different ideas about what role playing 
is.  Personally, I think that role playing requires that you somehow convey 
your persona to others.  Some people seem to think that you can role play a 
character that nobody else ever sees.

> "You would seem to be a communer of nature.  Might I inquire as to whether
> you've mastered the ability to travel between druidic circles?" is
> significantly MORE in character than "Since you're a 38 druid, could you
> please teleport me to North Karana so that I can do a corpse run?"

Why do you think that a person who would ask for a port in an OOC 
fashion wouldn't ask if you can do a port in an OOC fashion? 

If someone is roleplaying, then they will take the /who information 
as what one would get from sizing you up and they will approach you 
accordingly.  

> Both seem equally polite, but only one seems in character in a roleplaying
> since, given that levels are arbitrary OOC things.

The people who are going to role play are going to do it whether or
not your name is purple.  Similarly, people demand things of me all 
the time that I can't do at all.  They ask an Enchanter, who is never
anonymous, to heal them, summon food/water/bandages, or to teleport 
them around the world.

If you are going anon to fend off requests for teleports or buffs, 
then go ahead and do that.  Just don't try to pretend that it enhances 
role playing.
