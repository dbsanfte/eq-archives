Urm, you're vastly oversimplifying. It's not just licentiousness. It's
just literally, greater freedom of action.

It's pretty clear that a fundamental design difference between UO and
EQ is that EQ is a very very channeled experience, and UO is not. A
large part of why EQ works so well is because of restrictions that are
on players at every turn. The flip side is that there are those
players who are looking for an experience not explicitly allowed by by
the designers of the game. UO is more conducive towards using the game
in ways not predicted by the designers, I think.

And I don't mean only negative ways, though I think you will probably
pounce on that. ;)

Leaving the silly conspiracy theories aside for a moment, what I mean
is ladders of advancement as in alternate gameplay modes. For example,
you can play EQ viably as a hack n slasher, with success measured in
levels and experience--basic mechanic & ladder, kill for XP. You can
play it as a socializer, with a (pretty typical of online games)
complete lack of in-game measures of advancement or status. You can to
some degree play it as a quester, but there's no separate ladder of
advancement there and it is therefore not really supported as an
alternate means of play--basic mechanic, run errands for XP.
Ultimately, there's only one ladder of achievement visibly supported
by the game--XP. The chief complaint lodged against EQ's trade skills
is that they are not a ladder of advancement, but are instead required
to use the hack n slash ladder, which is in the end the same
playstyle, not a different game.

In UO, we strive to provide many ladders of advancement, and not be
tied too closely to one measure of achievement. Yes, I'll be the first
to admit that as a result, no single one of our ladders is as deep as
EQ's XP ladder.
