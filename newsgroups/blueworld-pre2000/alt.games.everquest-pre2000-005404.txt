   Well I am certanly not pro lawyer in alot of ways. I imagine there are
alot of lawyers that despise the legal system and wish it could have stayed
atleast pure in intent along the way. Things that dawn on people as they
grow older and others help them see things from a new perspective include
some pretty interesting conclusions. In America, we were founded and nursed
into a nation by lawyers, with a constitution developed and passed by
lawysers, a system of laws formed by lawyers, and many people in politics
are lawyers. This has been in front of us all our life but many of us do in
fact ignore this or fail to see it in it's basis and meaning to the USA.
It's by no coincidence then that the U.S.A. is considered lawsuit happy by
others not growing up here. After all, it was a system developed by lawyers
that obviously in thier own self preservation perpetuated ways to perpetuate
themselves, and in America, freedom, in the form of lawsuits... Now, without
casting a blaming or judgemental eye on this, one has to look at it in a way
that, when in rome, do as the romans. So, in America, people born and raised
here, have little choice about filing lawsuits when they need to to fight
back or try and defend themselves, or help make change in thier country
without lawsuit, or revolution. Considering the former is more peaceful with
alot of paper tiger warfare, it seems the most peaceful manner in which to
proceed. I can only imagine in other countrys how it may seem alien or
radical. But to us it is our only peaceful option outside lobbying (not real
good at addressing iminent issues that weasle past cracks in the system),
appealing to our governments (which is regulated and established by more
lawyers), or seeking our own lawyer. Now as humorous and admitedly worthy of
major satire, it is fortunately and unfortunately our way of life. It is in
this light, understanding "how it all works here" is much more understood.
You don't have to like it, or even be party to it, but none the less, that
system is firmly in place, and I don't see it leaving anytime soon. With
that understanding, people in America either sit back and take it, or file a
lawsuit. In a way, this represents:

Opression - the sit back and take it route.
Freedom - the fight for your rights, mostly through lawsuits route.

I think too much detachment occurs when buying into anti-lawsuit speeches
and trying to reduce the number of law suits talk is aired. I think people
connect with the ugly, unsavory aspects of the lawsuit image and forget
that, it's about our only way to defend/fight back in this country. I think
people also need to realize that every time something representing freedom
is opressed, freedom is opressed. And I directly connect trying to opress
lawsuits as a form of directly opressing freedom, atleast in America. I am
not a lawyer.  Thought about being one at one time, and in a way it wouldn't
hurt for everyone in America to be one. The sad truth is that Shakespear was
right about "kill all the lawyers", but to do that you would have to "Kill
America". And considering weighing the goods and evils of that prospect, I
will choose to live with America, atleast for now. All it's bad things, all
it's pure evil things, all of it's completely ugly things, as long as we
have freedom. The day we don't, I will be behind the sentiment of "kill all
the lawyers!" in every application of it's form. But right now we seem to be
still struggling, even wallowing in things that maybe will work out, maybe
won't. In the mean time, I will do what I can to see that it does, sue when
I need to, support others who do if I believe in them, and forget about the
lawyer bashing, after all, they are our greatest evil, and our greatest
champion. And we have little choice being born into the lawyer arena outside
of "tearing it all down".
