In article <89p0j7$r9j$1@nnrp1.deja.com>,  <plan32@my-deja.com> wrote:
>I've seen the reports running around the net that some classes have
>horribly difficult xp tables. Stuff like a halfling warrior can get to
>50 in just about half the experience points it takes a 50th troll
>shadowknight.
>
>Those points are real - I'm not disputing that.
>
>However, whats of major interest is HOW experience is divided in a
>group. It is NOT divided equally. (Most of this post is just a
>rehash of whats posted on the showeq boards- those guys have seen
>the xp they get).
>
>For example, lets say for argument, a 30th halfling warrior has 4
>million experience points. And a 30th Troll shadowknight has 6 million
>experience points. Now, people always try to group with those of
>relatively equal level. So if those two group together they will split
>xp but not equally.
>
>Again, keeping things simply, lets say the kill a mob worth 10000xp. The
>troll will get 6000xp and the halfling will get 4000xp. The experience
>is divided equally according to the amount of xp they already have.
>
>There were age old posts about how grouping with a paladin or ranger
>would suck away your xp - well if they are of equal level, its probably
>true.
>
>If that halfling warrior were around 34th level, he may then have 6
>million xp. THEN if he grouped with the 30th troll shadowknight they'd
>get experience equally.
>
>SO - if these two characters grouped from level 1 to level 50 strictly
>together, they would perpetually ratio the experience among them. That
>is, they would level at exactly the same rate! Sure the halfling has an
>easier table - but the troll is stealing the lion's share of the xp!
>
>Anyway, this is how I understand the results of what the showeq guys
>found. Check it out yourself.
