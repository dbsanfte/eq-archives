I don't forget them, rather, I'm unaware of them.  This is exactly the
sort of feedback I was hoping for in posing this topic; it tells me
more about what different classes can do and how they work.  By
eliminating the interactions between the wide number of possible
groupings, concentrating on what one class can do, but taking it
out of Solo mode, where group support, which to me is at the
heart of a multiplayer game like EQ, is still highlighted, I get a look
at what the class is really capable of from a fresh direction.

I rarely see charm used in normal groups, although it has
been quite effective in some situations; trouble is getting a
group reliable enough and communicative enough that
the mob being targetted by charm is left alone, it seems.
Taking one of the opponents out of the fight and adding
it instead to your team seems like a great tactical move,
and I'm not entirely clear on why it seems to be a
special play rather than an abiding standard...

In a one class group, I find that everyone is much more
clear on what their teammates are capable of and
what they are implying when they say things in shorthand
in mid battle.  Also, strategic talk before battle is much
easier since everyone is familiar with the basic abilities
of everyone else, and tends to think in relatively similar
frameworks.

I know you've almost certainly tried this, but what
happens if you issue a /pet no taunt command to your
charmed mob?  Perhaps the default condition for a
mob is no taunt, and you have to flag it back on?
Try particularly with charmed warriors or something
that clearly should have the taunt capability...

I'm also curious what happens when you charm
a caster mob.  Say an Orc Priest; does he sit
there and proc mighty no mana heals on your
party while things are beating on him?  I've
long enjoyed the luxury of elementals mysterious
endless pool of mana and incredibly low
chance of being interupted... and far too often
been on the recieving end of enemy mobs similar
(but more powerful) deep mana well.
