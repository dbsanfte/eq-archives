Comparing online games (like EverQuest) to single-player games (like
Half-Life) is an apples vs. oranges comparision.  People that enjoy
these games play them for years, not months.  Online games do not have
the transient popularity of typical computer games.

Let's look at Ultima Online.  Ultima Online is a "pay for play" online
computer RPG, similar to EverQuest.  It was released to the public in
September of 1997, over two years ago.  According to Deja.com,
rec.games.computer.ultima.online received 1289 new postings for the
week of Oct 11, 1999 thru Oct 17, 1999.  That works out to an average
of 184 postings per day, for a two year-old online computer game.

My personal opinion is that UO is possibly the single worst online
game ever created.  But 184 daily posts is not insignificant.
Particularly two years after release.  And with not one, but two
similar products competing for the same customer base.  Regardless of
my opinion of the product, the demand for a Big-8 group is there.

To put it another way, this is quoted directly from the proposed
rationale:

"The creation of a big-eight newsgroup dedicated to a game seems to
have questionable value.  Games, by their very nature, tend to enjoy
only transient popularity; computer games even moreso.  Popularity for
a given computer game title typically will peak just after release,
maintain itself for several months, then quickly taper off.

"Online multiplayer game forums tend not to follow this pattern.

"Usenet, by its very nature, is a creature of the Internet.  One must
be a user of the internet to access online multiplayer games.  Thus
online gamers tend to utilize online resources such as usenet more
readily than a player of a typical computer game might.

"Online gamers tend to be more fervent in their support of the games
which they play.  Online multiplayer games are open-ended, so players
do not "finish" the game and shelve it.  They continue to play for
extended periods of time.  As an example of this, there were over 300
new messages posted in the group alt.games.everquest on October 3,
1999.  EverQuest was released to the public on March 16, 1999."
