The next quesiton is not what out hardware can do for software... it is what
the software can with those massive systems. We are limited now by trivial
things like "memory" and "bandwidth". Psch. While we may not need, in
general, fast system do do daily things (e-mail, office stuff - businesses
should certainly NEVER need more than 500 mhz for general offices - such
systems will one day cost relative pennies, and be smaller), with those kind
of specs, you could easily start doing real voice recognition, motion
recognition, video streaming/processing, photo-quality 3d processing, etc
etc. It is about changing the interface we use with computers... though it
will take quite a revolution to replace keyboard/mouse (something along the
line of eye movement tracking, for the mouse). Either we are too comfortable
with what we have, or it is just good (no matter how bulky moving a mouse on
a 2d surface perpendicular to a 2d monitor rendering a 3d image may seem, it
works). Link it to a wireless finger button system or some junk and voice
recognition, and you can have a fairly integrated system. Things like VR
shit will never work, because people don't want to exert the effort to wave
their arms around like idiots (same thign with touch screen monitors). And
it takes too much damn effort. ;-) Anyway, that is the end result of faster
computers. The games are a midfield, really, in making stepping stones.

And the limit is miniaturization. Eventually, it will come down to how small
we can make batteries and objects in general before they become useless and,
frankly, too easily lost. I think that my Rio 500 is the perfect size in
between too small and too big (counting its weight). Pocket Friendly should
be a technical term... but technical "marvels" like PDAs are limited because
they are just too f*ing small for what they do. When you have to squint to
read something or poke a $400 object with a stick, it sucks (in my book,
anyway).
