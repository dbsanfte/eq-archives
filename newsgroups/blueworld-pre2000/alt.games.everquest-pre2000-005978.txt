EverQuest advocates are living in glass houses, so they really
shouldn't throw stones.

EverQuest has such a rigid set of rules that it feels like you're
playing a board game most of the time. All of the classes, regardless
of race or statistics, are carbon-copies of each other -- an ogre
warrior, is a human warrior, is a halfling warrior. The statistical
and racial systems are a joke -- neither has a significant amount of
bearing on how effective your character is. There's no thought
involved during character creation. All you need to do is make sure
your melee stats are at 75 and, if you're a caster, that you dump as
many points into intelligence/wisdom as possible. Even Diablo has a
better statistical system.

As for the exploration-factor, there is none. EverQuest is a series of
levels, not one coherent world. One level has creatures of a certain
type and level, while the next might have creatures of an entirely
different type and level. It's like Doom, only instead of keys you
collect experience and loot until you outgrow the level. There's
nothing epic about EverQuest's world -- at least not enough to make it
somehow better for exploration than Asheron's Call, which you hinted
at.

For the record, I have just as many complaints about Asheron's Call,
but I won't discuss them in this newsgroup. Both games are too narrow
in scope for my liking. I don't play either game anymore, but I'll
discuss them forever since I know a fair bit about how they play.
