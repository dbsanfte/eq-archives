I quit EQ for a while after getting frustrated with the overcrowding and all
the problems it causes (almost all of EQ's worst problems).  A few weeks
later, I had the urge to play another online game, and on a whim I picked up
Asheron's Call.  I was in the beta, and hadn't been particularly impressed,
but figured I'd try it with a more open mind.

I enjoyed it in the beginning, for the most part, and the longer I played,
the more I began to enjoy it.  For a short while, I was having more fun
playing AC than EQ, although I still considered EQ the better game, because
of the far more developed world, quests, NPC's that you can interact with,
etc.  AC was just relatively simple fun.  You had a very large world with no
real boundaries, if you developed your Run skill a bit, you could outrun
nearly anything in the game, making exploring relatively safe, and there was
lots to do.  I spent a great deal of time exploring and enjoying the
scenery, as it looked very nice running at high resolution (1152x864 on my
TNT2).  Eventually it occured to me why it was such good clean fun; more
than anything, it felt like Diablo outside of a dungeon in a large world.
But eventually, its own problems just caused me to lose interest and return
to EQ.  The big ones, as I see them, are:

1)  The world feels sterile.  One of the features of AC that's touted the
most is Turbine's ability to very easily and quickly change the world
however they want .  Unfortunately, the toolset that makes this possible
also keeps any of the areas from developing their own personalities.  Make a
wireframe for a section of land, slap some textures on it, throw in a few
trees or some water, and voila, a whole new area.  If you go to the desert
in the Gharun'dim lands, it looks virtually the same as every other section
of the desert.  The cities of any given race all look near indistinguishable
except for differences in size.  They can drop a city smack in the middle of
nowhere and populate it, but the NPC's are just static mannequins that stand
there waiting for you to deal with them, rather than EQ where you can feel
there's actually a world going on around you, that doesn't depend solely on
your existence.  The faction system in EQ is one of the best examples, as
you can discover rivalries between various races, as well as factions within
a given race.

2)  Game balance.  No matter how much people complain about balance in EQ,
it's still far better than in AC.  Although the powers of mages (especially
the way overpowered Life Drain and Harm spells of the Life magic), were
seriously nerfed, they are still considerably more powerful than the melee
classes.  Even more so than in EQ, because levels in AC are almost
completely irrelevant.  At 9th level, I was out exploring and on a whim
decided to attack a Lugian which was around 18th level.  I threw one of my
DD spells at it, and it wasn't resisted.  Threw another as it plodded its
way toward me, then ran when it got close enough to swing.  I could easily
outrun it, so I just repeated until the Lugian was dead, standard practice
for mages and archers in AC.  At 18th, I was killing 30th level Lugians with
very little risk.  Once I hit around 26th level, I decided to explore the
Direlands.  Which, interestingly enough, looked exactly like everywhere
else.  Exact same type of monsters, only now they had different suffixes to
their names.  Instead of 5th level Drudge Skulkers, I was now fighting 47th
level Drudge Stalkers and 55th level Drudge Reavers.  And I was dropping
them, easily, using the same old tactics.  Fire off a steady stream of DD
spells until the Drudge gets in melee range, then run away until it gives up
chase.  Come back and start again.  Rinse, repeat, and I just killed a
monster that was roughly 30 levels higher than me.  At the same time,
keeping a paranoid eye out for those evil 8th level mountain rats that would
fry me in 3 seconds, or the extremely deadly 12th level Silver Rat, which
could drop me before I even knew I was being attacked.

3)  Allegiance system.  While on the surface it seems like a great idea,
from my experience it just creates more headaches.  Imagine people begging
for SoW, then replace the words "SOW ME PLZ!!!"  with "PLEDGE 2 ME, I NEED
VASSAL!!!!" and you have a good idea of the situation.  Only instead of the
half-hour or so of speed you get from a SoW, the person to whom you just
pledged gets a steady stream of experience from each of his or her vassals,
all the way down the line, so you can imagine the persistence of some of
these people.  It's not deducted from the vassal's exp, so there's no reason
for most people not to pledge to the first person who offers them a shiny
toy, then break allegiance and join the next person who offers a different
shiny thing.

It has its fair share of good points, but even so it just couldn't maintain
my interest for more than a few weeks.

Anyway, just a few thoughts ...

Steve
