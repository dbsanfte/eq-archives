> Well, in virtually everything I've ever read, elves (with the
> exception of dark elves) are generally considered to be the embodiment
> of good.  Considering how much EQ has stolen from D&D, I'd bet that's
> supposed to be true here, too.

Well, High Elves might be hypothetically "good", I suppose. I'm
not aware of any EQ material that states that, though. Sure,
EQ borrows heavily from AD&D (more like it borrows from DikuMuds,
which borrowed from AD&D), but elves are allowed to be evil
in AD&D. Most of the _NPCs_ elves are 'good' in vanilla AD&D,
but there are no player-character alignment restrictions.

As for wood elves -- they worship the God of Puttin' the Smack
Down on Anyone Who Hits a Wolf or Bear, which, while not evil,
certainly isn't good. :) They should probably express some
angst at killing animals, but killing guards? All this assumes
a non-agnostic character, of course. Agnostic characters fall
very much into the "I know my race generally thinks X, but
I don't" category. 

> Either way, though, even neutral races should look _very_
> unfavorably on individuals that murder other races they are
> not currently at war with.

Why? Ostensably "good" people have a LONG history of not caring
about what goes on in foreign lands. The various EQ races
don't seem to get along terribly well; you'll generally
start out apprehensive or dubious to them. This tends to
indicate a "we don't like those damned dwarves/elves/humans/
whatever" attitude.

And, to be blunt, the lack of a faction adjustment pretty
much ends the argument. If you don't get a hit with X
when you kill something, X doesn't care that you're 
killing it.
