Dean Siren wrote:
> 
> Could the speed of light limit how we play computer games against players on the
> other side of the world?  

Ultimately... yes!  
But there a hell of lot of problems before you get to worry about all
that.

On the positive side there are schemes that can be used to
"mask" this latency so end users aren't aware of it (or... less
aware of it).  Highly dependent on what the game needs to 
communicate and how the servers operate.


> For example, for someone in Paris to play against
> someone in New Dheli, the controller/keyboard/mouse signal would take 1/14 of a
> second to get to the other player and another 1/14th to get back, 

Ohhhh... it's MUCH worse than that :)
1)  If you want speed of light connection
    a) you have to bounce off a satellite which adds a lot of latency
    b) you run through optical cable = fiber optics
       (which in reality is well beneath the speed of light in a vacuum)
    = your figure just can't be done
2)  Most connections are through electrical wires which are well below
    speed of light.
3)  The you have to consider processing time by routers (even a
    satellite would have to do it), which will take your speed
    of light signal and *really* cut it down to size.
    At best, the optical signal has to be converted to an electrical
    one, a set of instructions are exectued to interpret the packet
    on a processor, the electrical signal has to be reconverted to
    optical and blah... blah... blah...  Lot of shit going on there!

> thus updating the game state only 7 times per second, 

Errr... no, not true at all.  All signals can be pipelined.  
That would be only true if for every signal sent (I press a key)
my computer had to wait for a response before the next signal
could be sent.  Which, luckily, is not the case.


In other words, I wouldn't go worrying about speed of light 
as a bottleneck.  There are many contenders for that crown
and the speed of light is at the end of the list.
