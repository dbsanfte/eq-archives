I don't know. I woundn't call the place where you can travel from
one end of the continent to the other in 20 minutes (okay, 5
minutes if you're a druid, which I was) a big place. EQ isn't
big, but it requires you to travel cross continent all the time.
Your favorite dungeon is too crowded. One of your buddies want
to do quest in a god forsaken place (believe me, the quest in
EQ has you go all over the place -- did anybody ever do the bard
mail quest?).

The fact that AC is a *huge* place might be a boon. Since, by
nature, population is localized. More realistic anyway. Remember
the standard conversation in EQ.

player A: where are you B?
player B: Ak'Anon, you?
player A: Highpass, we are headed toward Najena.
player B: Okay, will be right there in a few minutes after I
          got my Staff of Observer
player A: See you there

3 minutes later...

player A: Where are you now? Don't bother with Najena, all the
          named ones are camped solid. We're heading to Cazic.
player B: I'm in Lavastorm, I'll just gate directly to Feerrot.

Sound familiar. Nothing unusual on the surface until you stop
to consider that all the named places are supposedly scattered
throughout the world. Ak'Anon is on another continent. Nektulos,
Najena, and Lavastorm are in the NE region of the main continent.
While Feerrot and Cazic Thule are in the SW region of the main
continent. No wonder people complain about travel time. The world
can't be that big, can it?, if people are discussing travelling
to 3 corners of the world casually as if they're discussing where
they'd go for lunch.

The question is, can Atomic make all "zones" relatively
interesting? If not, people will tend to gravitate toward
wherever that offers the most lucrative adventure, leaving
much of the rest of huge world barren.
