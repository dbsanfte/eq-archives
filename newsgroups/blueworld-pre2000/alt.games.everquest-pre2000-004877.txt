No, it has to die in 7 ticks for the shaman's single DOT to be more
worthwhile than any combination the necromancer throws.  Or for the
Shaman to be doing the most damage overall.

Even at 7 ticks and less, the Necromancer can still outdamage the
shaman in the DoT arena if he wants.  The necromancer is no
longer "mana efficient" but so what?  He has more mana to play around
with over the long haul.  Although if the fight is that short, its not
clear to me why anyone is using so much of their precious mana anyway.
The shaman should be casting Togor's and the necro should be using
Venom and that's about it.

This is not really an argument with a right or wrong answer.  I concede
that shamans are competitive with necromancers as DOTers under the
right circumstances, and that they always bring a lot to the table.
But there are a lot of circumstances where necromancers so clearly
outclass shaman as damage dealers it isn't funny.  Our pets STILL dish
out considerably more damage than the wolf spirit, before either is
buffed.  And over the long haul, consisting of multiple fights, we will
cast a LOT more times than the shaman, especially if the shaman is
meleeing.  My pet, the L39, got smacked the hardest of any pet by the
nerf.  I lost the full 33% of my pet's damage output.  But I'm sure I
still shell out more damage in my core group than anybody else.  Which
class is "Better" ultimately boils down to matters of taste, playstyle
and experience.

Personally, I give shaman a strong edge in large groups, and
necromancers a large edge soloing and a small edge in small (4-) groups.

So necromancers and shaman are now comparable in terms of overall
strength.  I don't see that as particularly unfair.  Ideally, all
classes should be comparable in strength.  They are not.  Wizards,
clerics and hybrids all have serious problems pulling their weight in a
competent group compared to the shaman, the druid and the pet class
pure casters.  My ideal group is still a necromancer, enchanter,
shaman, magician, druid and "pure" tank.

And I think its debateable how well the pure tank would pull his
weight.  One tank of some sort is a near requirement in close
quarters.  But that's about it.  A group can get by without two
indoors, and with none outdoors.
