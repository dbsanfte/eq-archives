>How often does this *simple* point need to be covered.
>
>The distribution of hits in combat is *non-normal*.  The modal number is
>((2*weaponbasedamage)+((level-50)/3)).
>
>In an analysis of damage, most hits are on this number.  You're level 29,
>I'll assume you have a BIBS, its base damage is 20.  Ever wondered why most
>of the time you hit you hit for 41?  In two levels most of your hits will be
>for 42.  If you have the same weapon at 50, most of the time you will hit
>for 48.
>
>Thats called the "Magic Number".
>
>Your average damage relates to the Magic Number *not* your strength (at
>least not to an important degree).  Strength *will* increase your maximum
>hit.  but as the distribution of hits os *non-normal* and concentrated on
>this Magic Number it is not worth worrying about.
>
>If you analyse a large distribution, a Warrior with 75 Str will have an
>average damage of around 79% of their Magic Number.  A Warrior with 255
>Strength will have an average of aroun 81% of their Magic Number.
>
>So what?  You say "thats a good 2ppts".
>
>Yeah, it is, but to get that 2ppts of average damage you will have
>sacrificed 50 to 100 points in Armour Class.  That will mean you will take
>perhaps 20% more damage in battle.
>
>That is not a good trade off.

I did have one question about strength and damage.

I know that changing strength values changes the attack number
that appears on the persona screen.

I have been working under the assumption that the crucial item
when fighting even con mobs at lower levels was the difference
between my calculated attack value and their defense value.

So that while both my newbie dwarf cleric (95str) and my newbie
High elf cleric (70 str - 80 when yaulping which she does a
lot) regularly hit for 8 or 9 points with their newbie clubs it
appears that the dwarf is hitting more often.

Of course, his attack is in the 80s (i have to check my
numbers, but lets use this) while hers is in the 60s.  That is
a huge ratio difference.

At higher levels, a difference of 25 points of attack might
mean the difference between an attack of 600 and 625 - a much
smaller proportional difference.

If my assumptions are correct (I do not have a log parser and
do not care to spend the time testing) then a higher strength
value will be useful in getting new characters from newbie-dom
to mid-levels.  At higher levels stats no longer make a
meaninful difference.
