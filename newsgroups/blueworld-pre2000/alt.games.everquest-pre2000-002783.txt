That's one of the best things I've heard so far, and it's true - each game
tends to appeal to different people.

Personally, I happen to like both games.  On the one hand, I've played the
Ultimas since the beginning of the series, and I really enjoy playing in a
world that has as much history as Britannia.  I also love the feeling of
playing in a world that I've, in a sense, been a part of since the
beginning.

On the other hand, I've been playing MUDs for several years (EQ basically
being a graphical MUD) and, while there are some differences, I'm having a
great time with EQ.  I enjoy the graphics, the world is very richly
detailed, and the interface is very familiar and easy to use.

Each game has its downsides - with UO, there's massive ammounts of player
killing and untamed urban sprawl.  I often got very dissappointed when I'd
go hunting in the woods - which were already cleared out by the hundreds of
people before me - or, when I'd go into a dungeon, and there tended to be a
person in every room and hall - with no monsters nor treasure to be found.
Also, once your dead, you're essentially sent back to the beginning - your
corpse is usually instantly looted, your abilities decay at an atrocious
rate, and you have to make sure you keep a decent amount of stuff in the
bank - you'll need it.

Then again, UO's interactivity can't be beat - the trade skills are
wonderful, even though it's often hard to sell what you've made - you can
make and dye your own clothes, and - if you don't mind the urban sprawl -
you can actually own "real estate".

On the EQ side of things, you don't have to put of with the housing - and
the player killing only happens if you want it to.  You're able to keep the
equipment that you fought for if you die (and make it back to your corpse in
time), and I've found *many* more players on EQ that are willing to roleplay
than I have found on UO.  It's not as interactive and dynamic as I'd like -
but then again, you don't have to worry about another player completing a
quest before you and possibly ruining it, and since the monsters respawn
you're not consistently entering woods that are monster-free.

All in all, I like both games - and I tend to play the one that suits my
mood at the time.  Yeah, they both have their share of problems,  but each
game is excellent in its own right.  It just depends on which style of play
you like best.
