It's pretty simple, really...

It's a game, and they made it known. If you have issues with them 
scanning for programs interfering with the function of EQ, it's no 
longer a privacy issue- You can cancel your subscription, in which case 
absolutely nothing on your drive is 'compromised', or you can continue 
playing because you don't care what they consider a program which 
interferes with the function of EQ.

It would be different, in my eyes, if the folks at Verant hadn't said 
word one about the scan, quietly slipped it into their license 
agreement, and then proceeded to do it, time and time again.

They didn't though.

A lot of people are screaming about their privacy rights using the 
slippery slope argument, except for the fact that as a commercial 
product, you have the choice whether or not to use it. There's a store 
chain here in Canada that asks for all kinds of personal information for 
their customer database when you buy something as small as a battery. I 
choose not to shop there because of it. If Verant's policies so deeply 
offend your sensibilities, don't use their products.

Of course, people will then scream about how they are paying customers 
and have rights, so on and so forth. Yes, you're paying customers. You 
don't like a companies policies, stop giving them money and being paying 
customers.

Addicts, of course, never seem to recognize that they are addicted.
