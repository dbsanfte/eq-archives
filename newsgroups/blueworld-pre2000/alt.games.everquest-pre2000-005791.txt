 Hmmm - the customer service of banks must suck by this definition. If I
go into a bank, and say "I deposited $100,000 in here last week. I have no
proof, and I know you don't have records. But, I do have 10 friends here
who will all attest that I did deposit the money!", they will usher me
out. They might investigate it first. 

 Come to think if it, if I walked up to you on the street, and told you
that you owed me $20, I highly doubt that you'd assume, given the lack of
any previous data about me, that I was totally honest, and that you must
have forgotten meeting me and borrowing $20 from me. 

 Now, does this mean in either the above cases, it wouldn't be highly
aggravating to the people if it did, honestly, happen to them? Of course
not. However, as a customer of the bank, I would hope the bank would
exercise caution before fulfilling such a request, by way of demanding
proof. If they didn't, soon my money would be gone, given away to any Jack
or Jill who could rustle up ten friends. 

 Of course the preferable solution is that good records are kept. Of
course, enormously complex software, such as EQ, is bound to have many,
many bugs, no matter how much testing has been done to it. 

 It would be nice if perfect records were kept. However, if the choice was
- perfect records can be kept, but the game will run 10% slower, in order
to fix a bug that happens in one in 1000 transfers - I'd rather have the
bug. If they could fix it, but had to up the price $1/month, in order to
pay for the cost of development, testing, and patching, I'd rather have
the bug. 

 The fact that they do, in fact, provide bug fixes on a weekly (or nearly
so) basis, indicates an amazing level of customer service - at least from
my view, compared to my experiences in software development. 

 Does that mean I won't complain about various bugs? Heck no - that's a
way of reporting them, and giving feedback on what you think is important
to fix. They can't fix what they don't know about, and the number of users
reporting it, I'm sure, is used to weigh the importance of the bug.

 I am worried that the transfer bug might happen to me, one day, with some
important item, so I do hope they fix it. I'd be more annoyed, however, if
they just gave items away to anyone who claimed they had lost them,
without proof - then why bother to even go attempt to collect the item in
the first place?
