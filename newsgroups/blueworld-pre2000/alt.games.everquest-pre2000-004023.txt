On Thu, 20 Jan 2000 15:54:41 GMT, "Alasdair Allan"
<posthamster@x-static.demon.co.uk> wrote:

>Lessee.  Removal of basic required features (secure trade, banking),

Although I would say that secure trading is something that's rather
necessary for PvP- games, banking really doesn't seem like all that
much of a problem in AC.  Trade notes pretty much take care of the
problems of having money after death, and banking items really does
nothing except encourage hoarding stuff that you don't need anyway.

>tacit
>support of exploits ("trapping" is permitted),

As opposed to "We're going to punish you because we just don't feel
like fixing the bug."  Its one thing to punish people for finding a
new exploit, using it, and keeping it a secret.  Its a completely
different thing to know about a bug for months on end and do nothing
about it except punish people for using it.  My opinion is, if you
aren't fixing it, it isn't a bug - its a feature.  You don't punish
others for your own incompetence.

>lack of basic support of
>roleplay (no naming policy - every little helps),

Actually, the lack of a naming policy can be a help to roleplaying, if
you look at it in the right light.  If everyone had roleplaying-style
names, you can't tell who the immature assholes are just by looking.
It makes it easier to avoid the kewldoodz when they kindly label
themselves as such.

>lack of diversity amongst
>player races (they have *one*, of which there are three casts),

For those who may not have noticed, AC is a different game taking
place in a different universe.  Its idiotic to argue that because 1
game has fantasy races, every game should.  Hey, the DragonLance
novels had lots of different races in it.  What's up with these other
crappy authors (fantasy and otherwise) who write books that only have
humans?!  Everyone knows you can't have a good novel without elves.

My point is that you may prefer games with lots of different fantasy
races.  Other people may prefer more a "realistic" fantasy world.  To
argue that you're right about this and others are wrong is, quite
simply, stupid.

>skill based
>system (everyone will be a tank/mage - well archer mage - in 6 months if not
>sooner).

Class based systems make each character generic.  You have the generic
warrior, the generic ranger, the generic wizard...  You wind up
_exactly_ the same as the other 10,000 people playing your class with
no way to differentiate yourself.  Skill based systems give characters
more variety and personality.  Yes, with poorly designed skill based
systems you wind up with overpowered combinations, but with proper
planing its very possible to create character archetypes while at the
same time providing leeway for individuality.  AC has screwed this up
to some degree, but they still managed to create a far better system
than UO.

>I've not even mentioned the lack of challenge and poor creature AI
>(although this sort of leads from some of these problems).

Supposedly the creatures in AC got an AI upgrade.  I don't play AC so
I really don't know how well they did with this (Actually, the main
thing I was put off with in AC in beta _was_ the bad AI.  I may need
to try it out again.)

>This genre is new, it needs each new product to propel it forward and add to
>the previous.  Ultima was a kind of side-ways step.  It added to the
>Massiveness that Meridian59 could never manage but it's playability was a
>huge leap backwards, Everquest was a great progression.  Asherons, has no
>redeeming features, except, possibly, for the dynamic world events.

Ultima Online was an attempt to build a virtual world online.  Its
purpose was to give the players that inhabited that world the freedom
to be whatever they wanted to be - tradesman, soldiers against evil,
thieves, and murderers.  Its main problem is that it was made by some
of the most incompetant screwups in the known universe.  

Everquest is a massively multiplayer hack-n-slash.  It can be fun, in
a mindless sort of way, but it never really tries to be anything more.
Trade skills were added so that they could advertise them in a blurb,
but they don't add anything more to the game than that.

My point here is that to compare EQ and UO is pointless - they're not
in the same genre.  Its like comparing Planescape:Torment to Diablo:
They both get called CRPGs, but they have virtually nothing at all in
common.

AC tries to be something in between, but it leans more towards the UO
theory of a virtual world.  In a way, I do think it kind of fails at
being what it wants to be, mostly because of the parts of EQ that it
tries to incorporate and the parts of UO that it failed to
incorporate.  But to try to make a 'timeline' of advances like you do
above is completely inappropriate.  They are, quite simply, not in the
same category of games.  You may not like virtual world aspects and
want only online hack-n-slashes.  That's OK.  Hack-n-slashes can be
fun.  But to say that others are wrong because they -do- want online
virtual world aspects in their games only makes you look stupid.
