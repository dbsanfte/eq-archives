But it doesn't happen that way now, so your point is moot.  What happens
now is you can kill mobs over and over without taking a scratch using
safe areas (unless a mob happens to blunder up the one narrow path they
can use to reach you; rare).  You get just as much exp for "shooting
fish in a barrel" as you do for going toe to toe with them.  I really
don't think that is a good idea.  It allows archers/mages to kill things
way above their level and thus gain a lot of experience quickly, or to
put it another way, it is extremely unbalancing.

> But to simply call it an exploit, and make it impossible seems like a

>
> same manner- How EQish...

It is a poor solution to an untenable problem.  Face it, with any game
with a lot of players, a lot ot them will look for the "best" way to
beat/take advantage of the game system they are given.  This leads to a
lot of behaviours the designs never really intended to be in game.
Leaving it up to the players to use potentially unbalancing tactics
"reasonably" is naive at best.  Often the only thing they CAN do is
modify the game system to reduce/eliminate the problem play behaviour
(EQ's mods to DoT spells to make kiting less effective is an example of
this).

When the changes have to be made after the system has been in place for
a while, they often are seen as artificial, and aggravate players who
now have to change their playstyle.  Elegant solutions are much rarer.

AC could take steps to eliminate the potential for abuse by changing the
monster AI to allow better pathfinding, increase mob missle/magic
defense ratings, giving more mobs ranged attacks, changing exp point
distibution methods (as you suggested), etc etc.  To date, they have
done none of the above.

So, the question is, why not?

To me, the answer is that the designers didn't pay attention to what
happened with UO and EQ when it comes to leaving quick roads to power in
game.  I think the decision to intentionally leave secure trades out to
foster "in game trust between players" further support my opinion.
