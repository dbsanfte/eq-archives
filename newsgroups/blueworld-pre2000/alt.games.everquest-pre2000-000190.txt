I guess I passed the atheist test since I still know there is no God.
Take a look at the test:

1.  The design for the Coke can was thought of by a human being, using
his or her own creativity to come up with the can.  There is no
debating at all that the can was designed, and manufactured, by humans
and their tools - a human that you can see, touch, and converse with.
As such, it is a human whose existence that we absolutely can not
deny.

2.  It does indeed seem to be a 'statistical monstrosity' that the
human body could happen on it's own through Nature's course.  Really,
one could reasonably suppose that it is not just extremely unlikely,
but downright impossible that such a thing could happen.  But consider
for a moment that there are billions of stars in the universe, and
that there are billions more planets.  Each planet is a chance,
however slight, that something like our Earth could happen.  It is
easy to look at the wonders around us and think that what we have can
not possibly be a random occurence.  But things that seem impossible
just haven't had a solution found yet.  Nature did find this one
solution.  It is called Earth.

3.  The same as question two.  When a 'statistical monstrosity' of a
random chance is carried out a billion times, the impossible may very
well happen, and it may happen more than once.

Imagine rolling 2 six-sided dice.  If you rolled them 50 times, the
odds of getting four 12's in a row are pretty slim.  What if you
rolled them one billion times?  Maybe you'll get fifty in a row.
Maybe you'll get none.  Either one is completely within the realm of
possibility.  But the universe isn't a pair of dice.  It is something
much more complex, with more chances for things like humans to come
along.

Could 50 oranges be dropped and land in a five by ten grid?  Sure, if
you do it couple of billion times - about the same number of chances
Nature had to come up with Earth and humans.

4.  Question 4 is nonsense.  To state that there is no gold in all of
China is something that can be proven - either the gold is there or it
isn't.  Whether it is a Montana-size chunk or a simple wedding ring,
gold is gold and it's existence can be found to be there or not be
there.

The argument is made that no person can possibly know of the existence
of every single particle within our universe.  Therefore, since no
person can disprove the existence of anything, God must exist since
his existence can not be disproven.  It is asked that if a person knew
even 1% of everything there is to know, then isn't there a larger
chance that the concrete evidence of proof of God simply hasn't been
found yet?  If we follow that logic, then it must also apply to the
Big-Bang theory and evolution.  Except for one small thing - we ARE
finding scientific proof of the creation of the universe and the
evolution of man.

5.  The first five Commandments mean nothing to an atheist since he or
she knows that the God that is referred to does not exist.

The other five Commandments are simply good rules of living that just
about any civilized society would come up with.  When a society
decides on what is right and wrong, laws are made to enforce those
ideals.  We can live well by simply following the Golden Rule.  In the
end, it is the treatment of each other that truly matters. 

Man should not have to be coerced into believing in God for fear of
eternal damnation and pain and suffering.  Man should do what is right
with his fellow man, and make amends with those he has wronged.  That
is true penance, not simply asking a spiritual being for forgiveness.

Take a moment, please, to take the God Test:

Imagine for a moment that you were raised in today's society without
ever being told of the existence of a divine being.  Imagine that
every day of your life, you see the wonders of Nature and mankind
without the teachings of the Bible, without anybody ever mentioning
the ideas of creationism.  Imagine that your parents, and their
parents, and all of the generations before had not been taught from a
book that has come through the ages with many interpretations and
translations of convenience.

Would you, on your own, have come up with the same divine being?
Would your Ten Commandments be the same?  Would your ideas of the
beginning of life and the universe and the end of it all be the same?
Would you, not being taught through most of your life of these things,
create a god in the image that has been described to you?

The human mind, like Nature, is a varied and wonderful thing.
Different societies have come up with their own answers to the
question of higher powers.  Can they all be wrong?  Would you feel
that Hinduism was wrong if you were raised in India?  Would you feel
that nature deities were wrong if you were born an Indian on the
plains of America?  You would probably believe exactly what you were
taught to believe.

I say probably because some people have been taught the religion of
their culture and not believed.  Are they wrong?  Who is to say which
one is the right one?  Or IS there a right one?  What if there isn't a
God at all?  I guess that would mean that people have just been
blindly following along, believing what they have been told without
question.  That's not possible, is it?  Ask 6 million dead Jewish
believers if it is.  All in the name of God and religion.

Atheists are not necessarily wrong.  They are not necessarily bad or
evil.  They just believe differently.  

By the way, the word "man" was not meant to imply anything sexist.  We
atheists aren't like that.

Dan Hudzinski
