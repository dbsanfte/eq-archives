Gordon Rankin wrote:

> Actually i would put wizard at the top of a single class group and there is
> a very good reason for this.  It seems to be little know but i shall
> explain.  A wizzie duo needs no tank whatsoever all they have to do is stand
> on opposite sides of a mob and keep away from it.  They then ping pong the
> nukes.  Each time one of the wizzies nukes it aggroes the mob, then the next
> nukes and aggroes again.  Therefore the mob is just running backwards and
> forward never actually managing to get close enough to hit one if the
> wizzies.  This obviously is not as effective in a larger group but in a duo
> it works a treat me and my partner have taken down reds (at the lower
> levels) in a matter of seconds with this method.  it takes a bit of practice
> but the gains are worth it.

This is part of the reason I wrote this all up; I'm interested in some
of the tactics that emerge when you _don't_ have a traditionally
balanced group set up.

I was thinking in terms of larger groups than duos, by and large,
however, and too, larger groups of mobs than one.  Isolated
and outnumbered mobs rarely fare well against any EQ group...
including a magician and his pet.  The real test is when your
group is outnumbered by stuff that cons at least partially yellow
or red.

If wizzards have the crowd control capabilities to force only
one mob at a time to fight them, then this is still a workable
tactic.

What prevents a larger group of wizzards from playing team
tennis; standing in two groups of equal number and taking
turns back and forth across the "table" A, 1, B, 2, A , 1, ...
etc?  Odd numbered groups could play three cornered in the same
way even numbered could play across the table... but this would
require more room to work in.

It seems to me that despite not needing healer, this is going to
have you sitting on your can medding an awful lot, too.  Nice
thing about tanks and healers and nukers all in a group is that
things can even up so that there is very little down time.
