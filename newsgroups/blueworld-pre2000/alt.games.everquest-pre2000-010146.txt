For the most part, yes.  For sense heading, specifically, I found this to be
inadequate to "jumpstart" the skill at low levels.  Luckily for my chars, I
tend to travel on the boat a bit, which provides good downtime for practicing.
If I just stayed around where I started, though, at some point I'd have to
take my "game" time to sit there and push a stupid button because of the 
braindead implementation.  I STILL think this is flawed design.  Compare it,
for instance, to combat skills, which are constantly tested against so long
as you engage in combat.

I agree with the idea that people shouldn't be able to go off and "play" all
day while their physically removed from their computers.  But, in all honesty,
I can't see where this is the tremendous draw that it was in UO.  There's 
simply NOT that many skills that have a use to the characters that can be
practiced this way.  Essentially, there's very little "gain" to be had in 
this behavior.  It is currently limited by THIS, rather than an aboveboard
ban against macroing.  Macroing was banned in UO and still took place to
tremendous degrees, because there was something to be gained there.  There's
no equivalent payoff in EQ.  The closest example I can come up with is 
macroing things like the Turnip quest.  There are other fixes for such 
problems besides banning macroing.

> I do think the advancement of some skills needs to be looked at. Pick Locks

> downtimes, like when healing from a fight. Isn't that how most of us would
> practise such skills in real life rather than hopeing to find a handy door
> to play with? That would reduce the draw of macroing to advance these skills
> and also make a nice money sink. More advanced locks could cost more and
> even be from quests or drops.

All of the above being said, I agree 100%.  Fix the source of the problems, 
not the symptoms.  I'd also like to see sense heading as an auto-increasing
skill that is checked against when you're travelling.  (In all honesty, there
is NO reason for there to be button pushes involved in training it.  It's
arbitrary, and tedious, and as such doesn't belong in a "game".)  Again, I
think Verant has set out, for the most part, a decent skill implementation, 
they just need to fix the glaring offenders.
