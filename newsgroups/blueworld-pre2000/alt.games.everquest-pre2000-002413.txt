Okay, well, I don't disagree with that outcome, given that scenario, but you
seemed to be using the example of Vox as an acid test to the idea that no
matter what limits you put on players, they will eventually find a way to do
the "impossible" and defeat "unbeatable" enemies (i.e- the old paper rpg
adage that "if you give it stats, someone will kill it".)

So the only true acid test is whether players using characters within the
CURRENT allowed range (lvl 50) with magic items in the current range, can
beat the current Vox solo or in a small group.  Of course, I'll concede that
none of these things are likely to remain static in this particular case,
but I think that if they DID, and assuming that an open-ended kill method
(like Poison or provoking in UO) isn't discovered, you would be quite
incorrect, and this is the principle I'm trying to make you see.


>>  Sure, people are going to figure out more and more effective ways to
fight
>>them over time, but there reaches a certain point where the math just
can't
>>be stretched any further, without adding more bodies.
>
>That depends on the capabilities given to players as part of the
>ongoing addition of higher level mobs, items, and skills (which
>trickle down).

But as long as you don't let those abilities get open ended, you can always
simply tweak the Mobs to keep the correct balance as players get smarter and
better.  As soon as you leave the kill mechanism open-ended (like anything
that does damage as a percentage instead of a flat number, or is clearly far
out of proportion to other abilities of comparable level), you guarantee
that no mob can possibly be a lasting challenge.

Sorry to pick on the mechanics of UO so much (I play about 20 hours a week,
so it isn't inspired by hate) but I really do think some of them are
extremely broken, and have been for quite some time, plus it is the most
likely set of mechanics to be widely recognized by most of those frequenting
these NG's.
