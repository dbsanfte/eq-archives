Morgan <morgana@misleading.com> wrote:
>Robert Link wrote:
>> 
>> Didn't you ever play D&D or some other over-the-table rpg?  If so,
>> didn't any of your characters ever aspire to rule their own kingdoms?
>> Empire building is one of many valid goals in fantasy gaming, and it
>> certainly has its precedents in fantasy literature.  I think the
>> biggest limitation of the current crop of multiplayer rpgs is that the
>> support for goals other than monster bashing is weak. In that sense
>> they haven't advanced much beyond the multiplayer rpgs popular on
>> timesharing systems in the early to mid '80s.  The phenomenon of
>> tinpot guild dictators could just be players trying to kluge the
>> system to allow them to break out of the "kill monsters to get
>> exp. and items so you can kill bigger monsters to get more exp and
>> items..." cycle.  The next logical step would be to allow a band of
>> heroes to "liberate" a region that has come under the sway of some
>> such miscreant.
>
>Sure, but I always wanted to rule over NPCs!  I would expect that 
>the other PCs in a game like that would be too busy ruling their
>own kingdoms to want to come play serf for me. ;)
>

That's reasonable enough in a game with 6 or 8 PCs, but I'm not sure
it's practicable for every PC to have a kingdom in a game with
thousands of PCs.  I'm not sure that's a bad thing, though.  First,
not all subjects are serfs, so there are still interesting roles for
PCs not at the top of the hierarchy to play (didn't you ever aspire to
become a knight in service of an NPC ruler?).  Second, if the world is
big enough there is always room for recluses who aren't part of any
kingdom to go off and do their own thing.  And third, PCs who are not
happy with the current ruler tend to make more interesting malcontents
than NPCs.

>Let me hire a band of mercenaries or attract followers.  

Absolutely a must for the next generation of multiplayer rpgs.  The
last time my old gaming buddies and I got together we kicked around a
few ideas for how this could work in practice.

>That is
>fine as long as thinking human beings are not put into a role
>where they have to follow my orders.  

There are always choices besides just going along with the orders.
You can find a kingdom where the laws and customs are more to your
liking, or you can find an area that isn't under the sway of any
particular ruler, or you can join up with other malcontents and rebel
against the ruler that you don't like.  One solution would be to have
one kingdom set aside to be "ruled" by the GMs, where the government
is basically one of benign neglect (which would pretty much match the
current state of affairs in multiplayer rpgs).

>The reason large scale 
>tactical combat games don't go anywhere is that most of the roles
>are boring!
>

That's true of the rank and file soldiers, but daring raids behind
enemy lines, desparate quests to obtain potent artifacts that will
turn the tide of battle, and other such are excellent seeds for PC
adventures.
