> No, it doesn't fail.  Unless you specifically get the "Your target has
> resisted the Harmony spell" message, it took.

> What you most likely saw was a misunderstanding of what harmony
> actually does.  It doesn't make mobs stupid or "sleep".  What Harmony
> does is reduces the aggro range of the mob.  The amount by which it is
> reduced is related to the level of the caster and the target.  The
> greater the difference in favor of the caster, the smaller the aggro
> radius.

Okay.. so it doesn't "bounce".  But the above behavior is sufficiently 
non-useful so as to constitute failure by my book.  If I cast harmony and it
takes, but it reduces the aggro radius by 1%, and it still overlaps the aggro
radius of the other monsters nearby, then it has "failed" at it's purpose.
The upshot is that this makes it likely that harmony is most useful when it's
least needed.  I'm MUCH more likely to need to harmonize a spawn consisting of
low reds and yellows than I am of high greens and blues.  (Well, given the
same number.. I definitely can think of places I wouldn't want every blue in
the game on me.)

So I accept that harmony never fails, then.  The discussion becomes one of
semantics:  there are certain situations where it "fails" to do it's job more
often than others. :)
