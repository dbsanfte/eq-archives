I think the legal standard that SHOULD be applied here (not that it
will be, although it probably would were this a criminal case) is
"willful blindness," NOT "negligence."  Willful blindness requires
showing that the defendant "deliberately turned a blind eye" to the
facts in question, as well as that the facts would have been obvious
to any reasonable person.  That is, Yahoo would be responsible for an
ad stating (to use a more significant example) "Heroin Variety Pack:
includes 7 top-quality brands from China, Vietnam, and Thailand. One
gram of each is included.  Starting Bid:  $1500." (the Soldier of
Fortune magazine-murder case is another example of this principle).
However, Yahoo would NOT be responsible for an ad for a boxless game
that turned out to be pirated, unlessthe ad contained information to
make the piracy "obvious."

Even with the standard of negligence (where the plantiff in a case
like this merely needs to show a failure to take proper action), Yahoo
should be in good shape.  One point about negligence is BALANCE.  An
ad that may or may not be for plastic explosives or a SAM would be
MUCH more demanding of action than the sale of merchandise which may
or may not be a copyright infringement.  I expect most judges to agree
that, in cases where the possibility for damage is small, proper
action only extends as far as eliminating ads for OBVIOUSLY pirated
merchandise.
