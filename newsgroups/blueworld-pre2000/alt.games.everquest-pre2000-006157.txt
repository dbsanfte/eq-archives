> In presenting a contract to you to sign the company (or whoever) is
> making the reasonable expectation that you are intending to accept it
> and, if not, you will reject it.  If it is your intent to change it you
> should inform them and failing to do so might render the contract
> invalid.  This would be particularly true if it did not require
> subsequent signing by the company, since there would be no evidence of
> their acceptance of your changes.
>
> If they sign the document after you change it then I can see that the
> onus is upon them to check it before they sign it - no question about
> that.
>
> Furthermore, if there is an alteration to a contract that has not
> clearly been acknowledged and accepted by both parties, then it could be
> argued that the amendment was made after the execution of the contract
> (in original form) and therefore the amendments are not valid and may
> even be considered a fraud.
>
> Lastly, changing the contract with the intent of "tricking" the other
> party because you don't expect them to notice the change may also be
> conidered an attempt to obtain benefit by deception.
>
> I would not recommend anyone trying such a thing.
