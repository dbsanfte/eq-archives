Rudolf Potucek <potucek@acs1.acs.ucalgary.ca> wrote:
>I have a bit of an issue with items being valued at the 100s of plats let 
>alone thousands.

Why?  Do you have an issue in the real world with some things costing
millions of dollars while others cost just a few cents?

If something is rare+useful, it will cost a lot to buy from a player.  If
something is intended by Verant for a high-level player it will cost a lot
from a vendor.

Economy in this world is just not right.

This I agree with.  However, there's no way to fix it, as the fundamental
problem is that the power/loot curve is very very steep as people level,
and that every time a mid-level monster spawns, a bunch of platinum is
created.

>(A) Prices don't change when you trave across the world: I can sell a polar 
>    bear pelt for the same ca. 1 gold whether I am in Halas where they are 
>    common or in Ak'Anon where they must be a legend only.

Travel is very easy, especially for wizards and druids past L20.  Prices
SHOULD be similar across the world, as it's only a 10-minute job to transfer
a whole lot of stuff from Odus to Faydwer.  

This can be seen by player-trade prices.  Low-level items (mino axes)
may change price a tad between Faydwer (4pp almost always) and Halas (up to
6pp sometimes, but usually 4-5pp).  High-level items are more mobile, and
cost the same everywhere.

>(B) The markup is simply ludicrous. I go and sell Rusty Spiked shoulders
>    (Magic shoulderpads 3AC -10 SV magic, found in BB) for about 2 plat 
>    but the shopkeepers will sell them for about 70. So what is the item 
>    worth? 

Again, it's nonsensical, but there is no fix possible.  Verant wants to
encourage player trading, and the only way to do it is to have a wide gap
between bid and ask at a vendor.  

>    To a high level player? Nothing ... I can go to BB and pick up 
>    another in less than 30 minutes real time. (not to mention the -10 SV 
>    magic ouch).

Agreed.  Common and useless = very low value.

>    To a low level player? Nothing ... because I can't afford 70 plat.

But you CAN afford 2 plat, offering more than a store would.  Thus the
value is between 2 and 70 plat.  Well, really it's between 0 and 70 plat,
as there's a transaction cost in getting there or carrying the thing to
the store such that you can perhaps get it for free by asking nicely.

>Plus the Verant economy is not based on supply and demand either (price 
>fixing) whereas the player economy is based on supply and demand.

Supply and demand requires intelligent self-interested parties.  Players can
do this, NPCs cannot without much difficult program design.  Such code
would very likely result in 3/4 of the merchants closing shop and the other
1/4 deciding to sell only meat pies instead of muffins and rations.

>That is bound to eliminate one or the oter economic system.

Why?  Think of it as a chemical buffer - the fixed price NPC vendors act as
a limit on the player "economy".  Both have their place.  NPC vendors are
used for the vast majority of common/low-value transactions (random loot,
food).  PC transactions account for rare/high-value transactions (good
armor, magic weapons).

>Which one dies mainly depends on the amount of $$ found off kills. It
>seems the Verant system is the one doomed though: generally you cannot
>buy what you want and when you can it is too expensive by far ...

How is this different from the real world?  I cannot buy everything I want.
I can't even buy many things that my friends have.  This would be different
if my parents twinked me more, and will improve a bit as I get more xp and
increase my job level.

>I mean buying a full set of banded mail will cost me ... what 200-500pp?

Yup.  A warrior on a young server with no high-level friends doesn't 
get banded until his mid-teens and doesn't complete it until the low 20s.
That's normal.  A warrior on a mature server can afford it much earlier.

>Learning blacksmithing to the level 
>where I can make one myself will cost me less.

Hmm.  I doubt it, but even so that's just fine.  Verant's merchants put an
upper limit on what the PC merchants can charge.  Successful system.  Also,
if you do so AND banded is going for that much over cost, you'll be able to
undercut other PC vendors and still make a profit.  Successful system.

More likely on any but the newest servers, Banded is already going for just
a tad over (Verant's enforced) cost of materials and failures so PC vendors
have already matched the limit set by NPC vendors.

>Forgive the rant but the system doesn't work as it is.

I disagree.  The fact that you can't get banded as early as you want is by
design.  The fact that my mage didn't complete his L8 spells until L14 is
by design (and by the fact that a first character is much less efficient
and gaining and spending money).  

It could be argued that it's a failing that most high-level items are
trade-only, but I suspect people just aren't offering enough platinum.
When someone turns down 50,000 platinum for an item but accepts another
item in trade, then I'll believe that plat is useless.  Until then, it's
simply the case that decent items drop more often than their platinum
equivalent, and there's actually a platinum SHORTAGE at high levels.

>I believe that making more items available at a more reasonable cost would
>deflate the trade economy a bit.

Of course it would.  And that would be a bad thing, as tradeskills are
already marginally profitable at best.
