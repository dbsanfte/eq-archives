What I think they're shooting for by not showing the numbers is that fun 
that the person got when they first decided to play baseball.  They don't 
study the velocity and wind resistance of a baseball, and then go "Gee, I 
love this... maybe I will play baseball".  They play the game, enjoy it, 
then possibley play professionally or just start competeing.  Eventually, 
they might start studying the wind resistance and physics of how the ball 
travels to gain an edge to compete better and become more efficient.  But 
they're doing that now because it's a job now and they have to strive to 
become better at their job, not because it's a past time.  By hiding the 
numbers from us, I think they're trying to keep that feeling we have when 
we just start a new hobby we enjoy.  Rather than making it become a job, 
where we have to find new ways to become more effecient over and over like 
there's a boss watching us.  They want us to play like that kid who got a 
sparkle in his eye from his first baseball game, because he truly enjoyed 
playing the game more than the absolute "must"-of-winning because it's 
having become career.  They're trying to stress fun over research.

competition.  And yes, it would be nice to see numbers for those
