Poor phrasing on my part.  Obviously, melee is a subset of combat.  The 
original poster's implication, however, was that melee characters should have
no expectations of any type of balance outside of their strict physical combat
ability.

If that's the case (melee is combat and ONLY combat) then the only melee 
characters are monks, rogues, and warriors.  Obviously, the melee
definition verant uses doesn't jibe with this.  His statement is false: all
extra combat abilities do NOT have no parallel.  (Excuse the double negative,
please.)  My ranger, a melee character by _binding_ definition, has parallel
abilities for a subset of the extra-combat abilities.  So too do bards, 
paladins, and shadowknights.

I'm really having a bit of trouble determining whose hair you're trying to 
unsplit.  The previous claim would seem to be along the lines of "Hey, deal 
with it.  You play a melee character, so all you should expect to be able to
do is combat."  If that was the intention, I take issue with that viewpoint.

I also (still) contend that binding in the manner in which most of us melee-ers
are discussing it is a game artifact, not a play component.  I couldn't care
less if casters can bind places we can't.  I care greatly that casters can
bind at _times_ we can't.  (Namely, when they're one of the few around for many
zones.)  The former is no big problem, and can easily be justified as a play
design issue.  The latter is a heavy-handed attempt by Verant to force their 
game to work the way they think it ought.  With that, I bow out, since I really
_ought_ to have better things to do, even if it's only gaining levels.
