Bad analogy.  When you order a steak with mushrooms, do you have 
the right to demand that the chef explain why he used button 
mushrooms when you would really prefer porcini?  Do you start a
petition among the customers to force the restaurant to offer a
porcini option for those who want it?

Verant has created a game in which one can role play.  They can't
force people to role play.  They can't even define what role 
playing is!  If you ask ten different people, you'll get ten
different definitions.

As for creating large scale ongoing player-driven plot lines,
that's an unattainable ideal.  If you've ever run a game, you 
know how hard it is to create a compelling story line, even with
a nice cooperative group of players who are all on the same side
and not trying to catch you in inconsistencies.

If Verant started the same story on every server, it would take a
different twist and they would end up having to write a unique
story line for each server.  There are hundreds of NPCs out there
who would have to change their conversations based on the ongoing 
plot.  Your $10 a month just does not buy that kind of service.

Verant has chosen rich detail over flexibility in most situations.
Character portraits are a good example.  You choose from a small
of very nice faces.  Quests are another.  The NPCs have lots of
interesting stories to tell, but those stories don't change very
often.  AC went the other way.  They kept things flexible at the
expense of having nice details.  I prefer EQ's route.
