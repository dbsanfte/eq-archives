Actually, one of the things that I think that detracted from UOs appeal,
in my eyes, is that -- in spite of all claims to the contrary -- the
designers did seem to want to make Sim-Britannia, and that the effort to
do so was simply (and understandably) too complex for them to effectively
manage.  

Be that as it may, even if we do grant the "Virtual World" hypothesis, one
thing to consider is that, in the real world, outlaws risk suffering
serious and long-term (if not outright fatal) consequences. UO, after much
complaint, finally instituted a system of long-term consequence, but it's
equally clear that PKs have found numerous ways to work around them.  In
this sense, I believe that the Virtual World aspects of UO have been an
unmitigated failure.  As for the "mere" gaming aspects, I would tend to
agree with the points that you expressed already.

Any society that suffers such extremes of murder and theivery is, in real
world terms, a society that is in a state of collapse.  When I visit UO, I
often get the feeling that I'm at the fall of Rome and that the Vandals
are running rampant.
