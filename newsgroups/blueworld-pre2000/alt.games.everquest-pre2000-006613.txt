On Sat, 11 Mar 2000 18:02:27 GMT, marka@cdmnet.com (Mark Asher) wrote:

>You know, they probably have never sat on their asses to begin with.
>We've had numerous patches and tweaks, new zones, and in about a month
>we are getting a large new expansion to the gameworld. How exactly is
>that "sitting on their asses"?

The expansion is a seperate product.  What Verant (and to a smaller
degree OSI and Turbine) is missing is that these sort of games are
different from traditional games.  Online games with monthly charges
are _not_ about shipping products - they're about creating a service.
They money is not in selling more boxes, its in the $10/month, and if
you want that money to keep rolling in, you need to provide content
for that.

The expansion shows that Verant is still very much in the mindset of
shipping boxes.  Everquest is finished.  Sure they make minor patches
to keep things running, but they _have_ to do that.  Every 2-3 months
they add a new zone, but they do it in the same way that Poptop has
added scenerios to Railroad Tycoon 2, or Stardock with Entrepreneur,
or even Id Software adding things to Quake games.  Its done as a
'gift' to customers, not as part of an ongoing service to constantly
upgrade the game.  After EQ shipped, they started right to work on the
expansion.  When the expansion ships, they'll either get to work on
another expansion or start on EQ2.

There's going to be a major paradigm shift in this business in a
couple of years, when the competition arrives.  For the companies that
survive the shakeup, the emphasis is not going to be on creating
products, its going to be on providing service.  The average game
costs about $50.  That means that for $10/month, as a basic part of
the service, you should be providing the equivalent of an entire new
game every 6-12 months if you want to keep your subscribers
interested.  A new zone every 2-3 months isn't even coming close to
what's going to be necessary to compete in this marketplace in 2
years.
