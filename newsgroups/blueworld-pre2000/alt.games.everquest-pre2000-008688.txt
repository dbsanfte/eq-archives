Lokari <lokari@enteract.com> wrote:

> It is no more unfair than a pawn in chess being unable to move the
> same way as the queen.

Hmmm, so you contest then, that the overall effectiveness of a pawn is 
equivalent to a queen?  The pressing advantage of pawns in chess is that you
have _eight_ of them to your one queen.  The analogy breaks down horribly for
EQ in that you're not comparing multiples to one for balance.. the 
correspondance is one to one, so the abilities should be balanced predicated on
that correspondance.

> Maybe if you'd read what I was responding to, you wouldn't make such

> options - that if the lack of binding ability for tanks was not
> unfair, then it must be an advantage. *That* was the claim I denied -
> that lack of bind ability is an advantage.

Err, no.  I was making the point that lack of binding for tanks in CITY zones
_is_ unfair, as it's a game mechanic completely removed from the rest of the
game.  The fact that casters can bind anywhere is their advantage for being
a caster, and I'm fine with that.  Three zone runs for a corpse are annoying,
but they're DOABLE when you're a tank.  At least much more so than for a 
caster.  Multi-continent runs aren't doable for anyone.  (Of course, some 
casters get ports/gates which makes it much easier for them, so they don't 
run into this problem.)

> Obviously a caster has an advantage over a tank where binding is
> concerned. Just as a tank has an advantage over a caster where taking
> hits in battle is concerned. Each class has strengths and weaknesses.
> That some people refuse to accept this basic fact more than a year
> after the game was released is an endless source of amazement and
> amusement for me.

It's not the fact that casters have a binding advantage.. in combat, tanks
have a melee advantage, which is offset by the caster's spell advantages.  
The interdependance that Verant wished to produce is there.  There is _NO_
equivalent advantage to mages being able to offer binding.  It's an 
extra-combat ability, which has no parallel for melee classes.  Moreover, it's
an absolutely necessary function.  What if there were no summon food/water
spells, and you had to track down a warrior to remain fed?  It's the same type
of imperative, fundamentally NECESSARY function in game.  It's not a balancing
tool, or a socialization tool, it's a necessary game function which can be 
denied through the vagaries of current surver population distribution.  And 
evidence points to the fact that the casters _don't_ need the warriors enough
to go out of their way towards making sure this function is always provided.
That reeks of poor game design.
