Morgan <morgana@misleading.com> wrote:
> Richard Melvin wrote:
>> 
>> There's two theories that could explain this.
>> 
>> The simple one is that people are very good at spotting patterns in
>> random numbers, even when they are not there.

I buy this one, for the reason listed below...


>> The other one is that Verant uses a performance hack to prevent having
>> to generate 10s of thousands of random numbers per second.
>> 
>> Instead of generating each number as it is needed, they could generate a
>> table of a thousand or so every few minutes. These just get used in some
>> (hopefully non-repeating) sequence until it's time to make a new table.

While this is a performance hack, there's simply NO way that you could exhaust 
the table of such a hack in a reasonable amount of time.  Generating, say,
10^9 high quality random numbers every 5 minutes would be trivial, to say the
least.  As such, unless they apportioned them out to each player exclusively 
for random events, the table would not likely be exhausted before it was 
regenerated.  Even if they did do this, the insertion points for a player's
random number checks would be essentially random.


>> In the long run, all numbers are still equally likely, so everything
>> balances out. But in the short term, you might get a table with lots of
>> high or low numbers, and so get lots of successes or none.

The table idea isn't likely to contain a statistically significant favor for 
good/bad numbers, assuming their generator is good quality to begin with.  The
only way this could happen the way you state is if a character happened to get
a string of good numbers at their specific point, and that's simply as likely 
as getting that string of random good numbers if they're generated on the 
spot.

>> If the second theory is right, you can beat the odds doing jewel-craft
>> by not just sitting down and doing 200 attempts straight out, but
>> instead doing a sample run of say 10. If you get more successes than
>> expected, carry on, if fewer go and do something else for 10 minutes
>> before trying another sample run.

Nope.  By definition, there's no way to beat the odds.  You can get runs of 
good "luck" but it's just that.  And there's no a priori correlation between
getting 10 "good" results and then continuing to get "good" results.  Unless,
of course, Verant is using a braindead random number generator/algorithm.  
More likely than anything else is the explanation that the human brain tries to
find patterns in _anything_, even if there isn't one to be found.

Sorry to ramble on about random numbers, but there's no reason for people to
spend much money on the belief that there's some type of implied correlation
between doing well in anything now and later (except inasmuch as it may raise 
your skills, which actually changes your probabilities).
