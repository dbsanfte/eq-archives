Well, actually, I suppose I'll go in blatant defiance of the UP force and post a
response totally off topic.  Also, Im not trying to step on anyone's toes here (
I feel enough like Im stepping into the ring with mental titans here, and Im a
Graduate student soon to be a Doctor ), so please don't take offense to anything
I say, for no insult is intended.
But how would you say Evolution ( a theory, still, granted, but remember a
theory is a hypothesis that is backed up by a boatload of proven scientific ( I
know some of you guys hate that word, sorry ) fact ) contradicts the existence
of God?  Perhaps it may conflict with the account of the Creation in the Bible,
but it by no means whatsoever ( at least as far as my understanding, which is
not perfect, Im willing to admit ) immutably denies the existence of a God.
Evolution merely attempts to explain the effects of the environment and breeding
on the genetic course of a species through time.  Take for instance the dog.
The dog is possibly the direct result of human influence on the natural course
of evolution of a species ( like the various toy variety of dogs, which are
really small tiny versions of a dog - - I always felt really bad for those dogs
for some reason ).  Does this mean man came from monkeys, or possibly an ape?
Maybe.  Maybe not.  Hell, its only a theory.  And as anyone knows who has read a
scientific document, no scientific fact can ever be 100% proven.  They can only
be believed through a preponderance of evidenced arrived at under controlled
conditions.  A lot of scientists must put a lot of ( oddly enough ) faith in the
scientific process to support their conclusions.  Just as Christians must put a
lot of faith in the belief of God.  Neither can be absolutely, infallibly
proven.  But no where at all does the theory of evolution say " There is no God
".  No where in The Origin of the Species did Charles Darwin discount the
existence of God  ( This is an old argument, "Evolution vs. Religion", that I
think, frankly, has gone on long enough.  The two have about as much to do
together as Bob Marley and Tyne Daily - - maybe not the best of analogies, but
for some reason, its the only one I can come up with at the present ).  I also
must take issue with the statement "every 100% proven scientific fact has never
contraindicated the Bible" - - as I said before, to the best of my
understanding, there is no such thing as a 100% proven scientific fact.  Also,
there's a lot of scientific facts out there, and you'd be hard pressed to
examine each and every one of them of them in the light of religious
contradictions.  And I can't say anything on the statement you made that the
"Bible has a 100% track record so far" - - I don't know to what it is you are
making reference to.  But back to the point, evolution is the single best theory
our scientific community has come up with to describe the course of human
development through the millennia.  And I have to admit, I for one have a lot of
faith in the theory of evolution, it just makes good sense, and if anyone
understands the basics of genetics, they would be inclined to agree.  I realize
that these are purely scientific beliefs, but they are backed up by VOLUMES of
human experience . . . visit a medical library to truly experience just how much
good work has come from the sciences of genetics, which is a cornerstone of
evolutionary theory.  As I said before, its only a theory, though, and it has
its holes.  These holes, however, aren't big enough to sink the whole ship,
though, so to speak, and while I know these holes must exist, I doubt I have the
background to expound upon them . . . maybe we could get a geneticist in here
for more information :).
However, let me state that I do believe in God.  And my belief, though fairly
new, is arrived at as the end result of my own education in the fields of human
anatomy and function.  You'd be amazed at the clockwork precision, the minute
precision of the human body.  Its so damn complex that most of its working are
largely unknown.  I have to believe in a higher power that created the human
body, based upon my own observations of its workings and its delicate
interactions with the environment.  I must admit, I have never placed much faith
in the Bible, and it is not the cornerstone of my faith, my own belief is.  "I
believe in God, not in the Bible" - would be an adequate summation of my own
faith, and I belief that the two are not mutually exclusive ( or is it
inclusive?  . . . . where is my Webster's . . . . ).  perhaps understanding
that, you may understand my views a little more, or at least why I think the way
I do.
One last thing, and this is not directed to anyone in particular, but to the
entire discussion:  It seems that this entire thread ( and I admit, it should
probably not even be on this group ) has been poisoned by ego, and the debate
has decayed into less of an intellectual discussion, but more of an ego
contest.  Faith in God is personal thing, it does not demand of others, but
demands of you, the believer.  It does not require the uniform belief of those
around you, nor does it denigrate them when their faith is different or
nonexistent  ( I don't see anyone in here willing to start a debate with the
Jewish community . . . ).  The scientific method, as well, demands the observer
( i.e., you ) remove all outside interferences, including your own personal bias
of belief.  Personal bias ( the preexistent belief that the result of an
experiment will yield result X instead of results X or Y . . . or any other
possible results for that matter ) poisons the pure rational process and
invalidates the result of the experiment, rendering the outcome subject to
debate.  Both science and faith in God demand a lot of the individual, but
demand nothing of everyone in general.  To argue over what each other should
believe in is pointless, and will inevitably decay into an argument based on
conjecture and insults ( though name calling is kinda fun . . . ), so why
bother?
Finally, someone once said, I don't remember who, and I don't know if I got the
quote exactly right:
" Not only is the Universe queerer than we imagine, it's queerer than we can
imagine. "  We are human, we are finite, and ultimately, we are limited.  We
cannot understand everything, and ultimately, in our physical lifetimes, I don't
think we ever will.  So saying that something is or it is not can never be
accurately ascertained, and therefore, trying to force your views onto someone
else, or ridiculing someone else for your own beliefs, is pointless.  That said,
I will shut up.  Im sorry for the length of this post, and I would like to say,
barring the emotional content of some of this thread, I've learned a lot.
Please take no offense to anything I've said here, for none was intended.

-Hoots
