I think you missed what he meant by archetypes with a skill system instead 
of a class system.

In such a system, you have "kits" or a "pattern" that loosely defines what 
a certain archetype is.  For instance, a thief would have the following 
general definitions that would make him a theif:

High manual dexterity and/or agility.
Sneaking and hiding skills.
Lock picking skills.
Pick-pocket skills.
Street smarts.
Light, easy to use weapon skills.

Now, if a player wanted to play such a theif, but with a twist in that his 
thief has lightly dabbled in magic, he could "purchase" some minor magical 
skills with extra points.  But, because he has most of his skills and 
points put into his thief skills to begin with, he will not be a strong 
spellcaster.

Later, however, as he progresses, if he finds that he likes his magical 
skills more than his thievig abilities, he can concentrate on those.

Basically, it means that the archetype is a starting stone for your 
character, but with freedom to customize later.  We, as humans, do have 
strong archetypes we use in our societies.  Think about it, picture your 
average police officer.  Now picture your average doctor.  Your average 
garbage collector.  It's not hard to see that, for better or worse, we use 
stereotypes and archetypes to define people and things around us.

Such a game system would be more realistic, because we mostly are 
archetypes of some sort.  I'd fit the Computer Geek archetype, as when I 
was growing up my skills grew in computers.  Likewise, I do not fit 
Football Player archetype...heh.  As well, in such a game system, you 
*could* build a character from scratch...the archetypes are merely 
"suggested" skills and what-not for that kind of character.

I hope this all makes sense. :o)
