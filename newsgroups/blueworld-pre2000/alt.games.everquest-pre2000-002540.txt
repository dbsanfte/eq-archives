Yes, it is possible. However, given time and increased player
expertise, the monsters simply get proportionally weaker. It's a
really, really, really common thing to see on muds. Muds where it
doesn't happen are extremely rare. See Shaky's reply for examples from
Legends of Kesmai.

>>If a year from now there are level 150 players with magic items 5
>>times more powerful than what we have now, then why would it even be
>>surprising to you if Lady Vox is taken down by a group of three?

>Okay, well, I don't disagree with that outcome, given that scenario, but you
>seemed to be using the example of Vox as an acid test to the idea that no
>matter what limits you put on players, they will eventually find a way to do
>the "impossible" and defeat "unbeatable" enemies (i.e- the old paper rpg
>adage that "if you give it stats, someone will kill it".)

Generally speaking, players will because the designer underestimates
their ability to circumvent the barriers he puts in place. As I noted
elsewhere, there's a lot more player brainpower applied to the problem
than designer brainpower. :)

>So the only true acid test is whether players using characters within the
>CURRENT allowed range (lvl 50) with magic items in the current range, can
>beat the current Vox solo or in a small group.  Of course, I'll concede that
>none of these things are likely to remain static in this particular case,
>but I think that if they DID, and assuming that an open-ended kill method
>(like Poison or provoking in UO) isn't discovered, you would be quite
>incorrect, and this is the principle I'm trying to make you see.

I don't know Vox's capabilities nor the player capabilities up that
that level well enough to assess that scenario. Nor, for that matter,
am I the type of player who is good at figuring out said tactics. :)
But I bet that say, Rick Delashmit (former lead programmer for UO, now
over at Wombat Games) was let at it for a while, he could probably
figure out a way to drastically reduce the apparent difficulty of Lady
Vox. I say this because I have seen him take mobs of that type on
other muds and make them seem easy through the application of clever
tactics and unexpected combinations of useful items, spells, etc.
There are people like that out there, and they will make your tough
monsters seem lots easier than you intended. And once they crack the
puzzle, everyone will be able to do it.

>Sorry to pick on the mechanics of UO so much (I play about 20 hours a week,
>so it isn't inspired by hate) but I really do think some of them are
>extremely broken, and have been for quite some time, plus it is the most
>likely set of mechanics to be widely recognized by most of those frequenting
>these NG's.

Well, actually, a lot of the mechanics we're talking about sort of
imply the existence of a level system. The deflation of monster
difficulty does of course occur in UO too, but it's somewhat less
visible only because there aren't hard level numbers you can attach to
the monsters to measure the deflation with. :)
