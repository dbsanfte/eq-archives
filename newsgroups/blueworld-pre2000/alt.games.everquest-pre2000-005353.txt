That's the problem with the current software license racket.  A person
lays out their hard-earned cash, and  receives essentially nothing...
the "right" to use someone else's property.  This worked in the early
days of consumer software; it helped to protect those that wrote the
code.  Times have changed.

Today people rely on software for their businesses, home fincances,
communication, and entertainment.  The current laws don't offer much
protection to the consumer because he hasn't really bought anything...
the property is still owned by the publisher.  What is truely needed
is some sort of "ownership" when you give a publisher your money.  We
need warranties applied to this ownership.  We need to know that the
publisher will support this product 2, 3, or 5 years down the road.
When you buy a car, if it is defective, the manufacturer has to fix
it... not so with software.  In some industries, if you sell a
product, you must manufacture parts and support that product for 7
years... not so with software. (how many times have you called tech
support and were told "we don't support that version anymore, you must
upgrade"?)  If a defective "real" product causes bodily harm or
monetary damages, the manufacturer is financially resposible.... not
so with software.

Software is no longer a toy for geeks.  It is every bit as important
as tangible goods to the economy and personal lives.  It is time for
the legal system to recognize that, and to start holding software
publishers responsible for the goods they sell.  Oh... that's right,
they don't sell anything... they simply loan you their "lawnmower" for
a few hundred dollars.
