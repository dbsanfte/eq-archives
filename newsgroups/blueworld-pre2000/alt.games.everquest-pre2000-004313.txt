NBarnes wrote:
> 
> Morgan wrote:
> >
> > NBarnes wrote:

[snip]

> > EQ tried to let people differentiate themselves at higher levels by
> > allowing magic specializations.  Unfortunately, there is really only
> > one good choice in specialization for each class.

Specialization reduces mana cost and fizzles.  Since fizzles
are really not a problem at high levels, I would only worry
about the mana cost.  Each class, other than Druids, has one
clear best choice for specialization and generally one or two
other choices that would not be horrible.

>  Eh.  Druids have a legitimate choice to make between alteration
> and evocation.  

I was going to list Druids as an exception.  The Evocation/
Alteration choice is essentially a solo/group distinction and is 
significant.

> Enchanters have to choose between alteration
> (almost everything but mezzes) and conjuration (mezzes).  

Alteration is where your mana is going at critical situations
and non-critical situations.  I very rarely run out of mana
doing mezzes, and if I am going to run out it's because we are
overmatched.  Specialization Conjuration would let us flail 
about for another minute before being forced to retreat.

> Necros
> between alteration (lifetaps), evocation (nukes), and conjuration
> (most DoTs).  

Necros want Alteration.  They get exactly one nuke that affects
creatures other than undead (and it is a Conjuration).  Although
their disease and poison DoTs are Conjuration, their heat blood
and life tap spells are Alteration.

> Magicians between conjuration (their second-best level
> nuke, magic based and thus harder to resist) and evocation (their
> best nuke, fire-based and much easier to resist).  

Magicians generally want Evocation.  Most of their conjuring is
done offline, with an opportunity to med, while their evoking is 
done at critical moments.

> Clerics, shamen,
> and wizards are much more cut and dried, though.

Evocation for Clerics or Conjuration for Shamans are about as
useful as Conjuration for Necromancers.  There is a choice to
make, but I think it is pretty cut and dried for most classes.

>  In any case, while specilization choices are a good example of the
> sort of thing that EQ needs more of, well, EQ needs more of them.  :)
> Specilization does not sufficently differentiate the classes in
> EQ, even at higher levels when you would think you could tell
> the difference.  I _cannot_ tell the difference between a alt-spec'd
> druid and an evo-spec'd druid (well, not without extrapolating from
> their playstyle) I'm grouping with at 45th, and that's about the most
> dramatic shift of any of the choices.

I would like to see more choices up front.  For example, it 
seems as though stats do very little.  If stats were significant
and people knew what they actually did, then they could be used 
to tune the character at creation time.

Of course, you always have your name and your role playing to 
distinguish your character from the masses.  That's probably the
best way to make a unique and interesting character at low levels
and high levels. ;)
