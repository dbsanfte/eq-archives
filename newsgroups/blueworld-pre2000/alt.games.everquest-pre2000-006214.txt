My comment here is not directed at your post, just at the realization of
what the problem with Verant is....

Basically these guys are a bunch of Fat White Guys that don't plan on doing
anything but play games all day long.  This is why you end up with rare
spawns every few "days" and even rarer "drops" - these guys seem to have
lost the ability to understand how the average player lives in real life.
Once you figure that out, you realize why they made it so the game is
affectionately called "EverCamp" rather than EverQuest.  The only thing you
are ever "questing" for in the game is your dead body.

It's really too bad - I look back on my MUD days, and remember what quests
were - granted they weren't all perfect, but most of them were fairly
straightforward, and many had some interesting puzzle solving involved.  The
main puzzle you have to solve with the "quests" on EverCamp is trying to
figure out what some of the NPCs are trying to tell you to do, raising your
faction so you even speak with them in the first place, and then how long
you will have to sit on your rump "camping" in X number of locations
(beating off other would be campers) for that "special" opportunity to loot
the items you need.  To top it off, you can't even rest without fear of
being attacked by GREEN creatures that you get no xp for in the first place,
whose only job it seems is to make SURE you sit at the screen so you won't
die while camping.  It's no wonder that people have gotten spawn rates down
to an exact science so they just bop in, blast and leave.

Graithe

> I have no problems with the speed of leveling in EQ, but just sitting

> know.
>
> I think that to make EQ a more enjoyable game, I would love to see some
> of the following implemented:
>
> 1) The mana and hitpoint regeneration should be doubled.
>
> 2) The spawn rate should be doubled.
>
> 3) The experience gained from each MOB should be halved.
>
> 4) Item drops halved.
>
> 5) Ability for the "weak solo" classes to solo greens at a much reduced
> experience gain perhaps as something to do while waiting for a group.
>
> 6) *TRY* as hard as possible to come up with game balance solutions that
> do not *TAKE AWAY* from something that someone already has. There is
> absolutely NOTHING Verant could do which would discourage players more,
> than losing an ability that they worked very hard to get. For instance,
> if I create a druid because I KNOW that at level 14 I'll get SoW and
> they were to remove that ability, I would delete the druid. I know that
> many people look ahead to what they will have at level 40-50, when they
> get the more defining skills/spells of a class. To have that skill or
> spell removed or "nerfed" is absolutely devastating to someone who has
> spent a month or more working on a character.
>
> EQ is a great game, I love it and am quite happily addicted. I hope
> Verant will keep up the good work, and if they were to implement these
> changes I would bounce myself right out of my computer chair. This would
> allow the player more "play time" versus "sit time". This would not
> unbalance the game since, yes you will be killing more Mobs, but you
> will be getting less experience and loot for each mob killed.
>
> I would also like more information on exactly what some things do. For
> instance I would feel sorry for someone that put all their stats into
> INT because they thought that, as a warrior, it would make them "fight
> smarter" or some such thing. It would be very bad if someone got to
> level 10 or so before learning of this mistake and had to start over,
> you get the idea.
>
> There is a difference between "discovering the world" and making a
> terrible mistake due to lack of information that will only cause
> frustration. Does DEX actually affect spell casting? Knowing this would
> not allow "super casters" to be made because you still would only have
> so many skill points to allocate, but the caster should know that maybe
> INT is not the only thing he should concentrate on, and what the
> consequences of each stat are.
>
> It would also help "pet classes" in the grouping situation if it was
> public knowledge exactly how much experience a pet will take and under
> what circumstances. This is not something that can be found out in the
> game very easily and this lack of information simply forces players to
> rely on 3rd party software such as ShowEQ.
>
> Exactly how much mana do I have? You list exact amounts that each spell
> requires, yet you do not tell us our total mana available. You show
> exactly how many hitpoints, but not mana, why? Again we are forced to
> use 3rd party software to find this out. I hope that a number is also
> added to the status screen where the hitpoints are displayed for mana.
>
> I've read in one post by Brad that they will not tell us things like
> this, just like a DM would not in AD&D. In AD&D all of this information
> is made available to the player before they even create their character.
> Of course the DM will not say, "the solution to this quest is..." or
> "when you walk into the next room your going to come face-to-face with a
> dragon", as this would spoil the surprise. Things such as Dexterity and
> Strength are made well known, in detail, as to how they affect the
> player's character and his abilities. We do not ask for you to release
> the algorithms used by the game engine, but something along the lines of
> listing exactly WHAT is affected by each stat would be nice, such as the
> DEX and spell casting, or AGI and speed of running, or AGI and armor
> class. I know that this is a lot of information, so perhaps pick a stat
> to describe in a weekly or monthly article on your web page on the
> effects of that stat. This would be a good distribution method, and
> another reason for people to go to the web page.

> the Illusion line of spells, because they were FUN, not because they did
> anything useful. I wish ALL the classes had more spells/skills like
> these even if they did absolutely NOTHING that would help you to level,
> or affect game balance. They just add more fun to the game. I'm sure
> every enchanter out there has probably spent hours just playing with
> Minor Illusion. All classes should have more fun spells or skills like
> these (this was pre-patch before the illusion spells did much of
> anything). I don't even play my enchanter much except when I want to
> fool around and have a good time.
>
> Twinking:
> I think EQ should have level restrictions on items (Finally! A use for
> the Identify spell!). This would help curb the eBay fiasco, would
> drastically reduce farming, and allow others a chance to get at the
> spawns. I don't care if you give your second character money and items,
> since the low levels are just plain tedious. I do think that a level 1
> character in planes armor is absurd though...
>
> Camping:
> The above would help a lot, but just plain good usage of currently
> available zone space would be much better at solving this issue. You
> could plop down about 20 more camps of various levels in the
> Butcherblock Mountains with hardly a dent in the available space. After
> all, what good is all that space with just giant bats and rats? Keep the
> newbie areas of course, but what newbie is going to hunt out in the
> middle of the zone away from the guards and merchants? There are MANY
> zones that could use this treatment, after all there should not be
> 50-100 people in Crushbone and 10-20 in Butcherblock to name only 2
> zones. This would allow a MUCH more diverse hunting area, without having
> to add more zones. Just even out the available hunting in the ones we
> have.
>
> Class Specialties:
> I think that if I start a wizard to be able to dish out massive damage,
> then I should not be out-damaged by a mage, and would not expect to be.
> Each class is supposed to be the be-all-end-all of it's defining field
> and not outdone in that field by any other class. I do not see this in
> the game. A wizard, to continue this example, gives up many abilities to
> be able to do this, and yet is barely able to outdo other classes in
> this regard, and only at certain levels are they more powerful than
> other spellcasters and this INCLUDES Ice Comet. A mage lives and dies by
> his pet, and should have no competition in pet power compared to other
> classes. These classes should not "squeak by" the others in these
> fields, but completely outstrip them. I understand that game balance is
> very important, and I play many different classes, so I do not feel that
> I'm biased toward any one of them. There is nothing wrong with a player
> wanting to be a "god class" and they should have the opportunity to do
> so in their field of specialty. If I'm a rogue I should be able to more
> damage with a dagger than a warrior could with a dagger, it's my
> specialty. The warrior can use heavy armor and any weapon he chooses,
> and be good with that weapon. He is a jack-of-all-trades in combat.
>
> Economy and Trades:
> The "player run economy" is in poor shape. Only certain trades are
> profitable. I think one good way to make the trades worth learning is to
> remove items that they create from the merchants. If we have to buy a
> bow and arrows from someone with a good fletching skill, then that skill
> will be worth learning. It is true that the equipment made by players is
> better than the one bought from a merchant. But to increase dependence
> on the Trades, the players should have to go to other players to get
> their bows, swords, armor, jewelry, potions, etc, and NOT have to spend
> 20-100pp for ONE potion, this is just ridiculous.  I have yet to buy one
> of these potions (sorry shaman's, but I needed a very good example)

> free, or pay them a COUPLE of pp at most! I'm very happy that the shaman

> potential. Whenever a price is carved in stone for semi-mundane items

> get this piece of armor that another player farmed that gives me 15ac
> and perhaps some stat increases for 100pp? If the cost to produce player
> made items is so high that nobody will buy it, there will never be
> demand for it. If the costs of supplies to make a long sword force the
> price of the long sword up higher than that of a farmed Ykesha, then
