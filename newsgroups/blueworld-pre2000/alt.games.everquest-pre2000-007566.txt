Well, I got tired of the texture flashing problems (turning agp off fixes
this but one of the games I have recently been testing does not play with
agp 16). Asus has been telling me the wrong information and Guillemot has
been telling me wrong information, so I decided to fry my card and get a
VooDoo3 until I get a new system and go back to GeForce. Well the steps I
took to fry my card are as follows...

First I took off the crappy fan that are stock material for GeForce (these
things are the cheapest of cheap, and they go out alot from all the posts I
read). Then I remove the plastic pins holding on the semi heat sink housing.
Under that I find out Guillemot has been using lard (conductive grease) to
contact the sink to the chip. This is probably the least efficient method
possible. After all of this, I clean the chip up and head out to my local
electronics parts store. I pick up a p3 massive heat sink, and a couple of
486 heat sinks (never can find tiny sinks), also pick up silvered conductive
epoxy. This stuff is pretty slick, bonds like the best epoxy and also works
like a solder joint, as well as conducting heat very well. Anyways to make a
long story short, I slap this mushroom cap of a heat sink on this chip, let
it set for 4 hours, come back and crank up my cpu and memory settings,
almost all of my texture flashing problems are gone (3 games I have this
problem, which is 3 more than I want). So I decide to take this a step
further, and start sawing the 486 heat sinks into quarters. I paste those
(VERY CAREFULLY) to the memory, repeat the wait and bake of 4 hours, and
crank the memory up even more. This card sings. I have it at 145mghz cpu and
350mghz memory and I don't feel like I am anywhere near the ceiling. All of
this also included those scary moments where you get a weird output on
screen, some crazy lines, boot to safe mode, as well as weird unexplained
phenomena while you scratch pins and reaffirm the integrity of each pin and
area you suspect may have some epoxy causing problems (hair lines). But in
the end after blowing it all out with compressed air and saying a few
prayers, it's quite an achievement. To have something faster than you can
buy, as well as fixing some problems caused by stock settings. I am not a
big overclocking fiend, but I must admit, the problems I had drove me to
find solutions while improving performance so I hoped to win in both, and I
achieved those goals. It has been fun in it's own way, and I have a feeling
I will never look at a video card the same way again. ;-) If you are
anywhere half way tech savy hardware wise, and can afford to blow up a card
if you are a clutz, then I suggest trying out the overclocking rig. It looks
alien, it is like some waffle from outer space, but it purrs like a kitten
while striking like lightning.
