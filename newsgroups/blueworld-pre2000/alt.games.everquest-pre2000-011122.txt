> A friend of mine has tried to persuade me that there are optimal groups
> for the high level Kunark dungeons. He says it is  a warrior, a cleric,
> an Enchanter, and two wizards. You have each base covered (crowd
> control, taunt, healing and DD).  I say poppycock to that for the
> simple reason that one often doesnt have a real choice as to who can
> join or when those that do have to leave. Well coordinated "pick-up"
> groups will earn more XP in the long run by being more flexible and
> therefore more adaptable to changing circumstances, and thus give the
> group more time in game adventuring.
> 
> The key difference in the two approaches is that coordinated "pick up"
> groups have the flexibility of having characters with multiple roles in
> the party, and they can ehtn adjust to the changing circumstances. A
> druid can do respectable DD or heal. A Paladin can tank and do some
> healing or res the cleric after a nearly total wipe out, so that the
> cleric can get back in and res everyone else. The Ranger can still
> track, forage food and water, and effectively taunt every MOB off the
> casters that might ever get on them. But the versatility that comes
> from being able to adjust to early departures, unexpected deaths and
> misfortune is the necessity that reality instills, leaving theories of
> optimal groups in the realm of the hypothetical where they belong, if
> anywhere.
> 
> But the worst part of such attempts at "optimal groups" is the rudeness
> displayed by those three and four man parties that let the ranger,
> druid or paladin go away begging for a group, instead of grouping with
> them and letting them have some fun alongside of you. It is simply
> greedy narcisism to do such. And besides, you will enjoy the game much
> more if you dont have one eye on that gold bar ;)

I agree with you.  Also, your friend is missing a few points.

1) The person behind the character is usually more important than
   the particular class they play.  As long as you have the basic
   roles filled (i.e. some healer, some tank, and some crowd
   control) you are almost always better off picking the better 
   player over the optimal class.

2) Warrior, Cleric, Enchanter, Wizard, Wizard is not an optimal
   group for most situations.  I could list my own recipes for
   making good groups here, but the given one is pretty bad.  
   There are only a very small number of situations where you 
   would choose a Wizard over a Druid, Shaman, Magician or
   Necromancer.  Also, more fighters is always better.  Any of
   the six fighting classes would make a great addition to that
   group or replacement for the second Wizard.
