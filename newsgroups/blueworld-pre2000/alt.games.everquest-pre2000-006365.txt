That's not a fair analysis of the situation.  The game mechanics of
EQ are a relatively complex system.  Because all characters are
progressing in levels and fighting different monsters at different
levels, it is nearly impossible to judge the worth of any particular
piece of gear or change in stats through normal play.  Too much will
change between fights.  You can't tell whether, say, higher charisma
makes stun spells work better, because in the same time you have 
raised your charisma you have also gone up two levels and switched
from fighting lizards to fighting undead frogloks.  

Also, it is very hard to perceive the difference between a 60% success 
rate and a 70% success rate without counting over a long period of time.  
The only way to determine the difference that a particular stat makes 
is to run experiments where nothing changes except for that stat.
Most tests that people have done to try to determine how strongly stats
effect combat have found that the effects are within statistical noise.
This brings us back to the question of what effects are the stats 
supposed to have?

We don't require complete knowledge of game mechanics.  All we want
is the players handbook version, a truthful account of what stats affect
what aspects of the game.  Leave it to us to determine how strong the 
effect is and how much we care about it.  We would like to believe that
every stat has some use for every character, but more importantly, that
each stat that is listed as a primary stat for a class does contribute 
meaningfully to the effectiveness of the class.

You make me angry when you say that you are withholding information
from me because you think I might somehow be damaged by it.
