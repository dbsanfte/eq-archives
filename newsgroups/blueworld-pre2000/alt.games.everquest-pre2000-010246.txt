> Why work with unenchanted metal?  As I understand it,
> the enchanting process doesn't destroy the metal if it
> fails.    But a successful jewelry making attempt will
> produce much more valuable items if the metal is enchanted,
> while the difficulty will not be influenced at all.
> 
> It seems to me that only a power twinker trying to get
> skill to skyrocket in the very least amount of time
> possible would work unenchanted given the choice.

It depends on your financial state and your jewelcraft skill.

Silver and electrum stuff fails about 50% of the time when
it is near to trivial, so you are going to lose tons of money
no matter what if you are selling to NPCs.  If you take up
jewelcraft at level 20 instead of level 8, you might be willing
to write off a bit more money in exchange for *much* faster
practice.

By the time you get to high end gold stuff, you are succeeding
almost 95% of the time on the near-trivial items.  The difference
between enchanted and unenchanted items just about makes up for
the failure rate, so you can practice for a long time losing only
a small amount of money on those items.  It would cost thousands 
of plat to get those last few points if you worked on unenchanted
gold.

I actually practiced with unenchanted metal up to silver jade 
because nothing below jade had any magical properties when I was 
doing it. ;)

> As a non-enchanter, I was happy to work enenchanted,

> but if it was my own spell, I'd be using it to get the underlying
> magic skill improved!  Cast it a bunch till oom, then meditate
> and make jewelry while you get it back.
> 
> Am I confused?

No, but it takes a long time to get the mana back.  I would always 
cast until OOM enchanting metal bars then turn all those metal bars
into jewelry while I meditated.  I would normally be back to about
one bubble of mana when I finished making the jewelry.  You definitely
need to read a book or chat with friends when enchanting metals. ;)
