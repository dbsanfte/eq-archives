 Excellent question!

 Hmm, this devolves into two issues for me. 

 Firstly, in a single player, isolated sense, maps, descriptions and 
such are very much like walkthoughs. These are sort of a "cheat". 
However, in a single player sense, the real issue is your own enjoyment. 

 I know I'd much rather have the maps than attempt to make them myself. 
Perhaps if I had much, much more free time than I currently have, I'd 
have a different view. For me, the online info is generally helpful and 
appreciated. Plus, I enjoy the colaborative nature of these resources. 

 This, of course, will vary from person to person. Some folks like the 
challenge of doing it all themselves, including maps and developing 
tatics. Others don't want to hassle with any challenge, and would rather 
just enjoy "seeing the sights". I'm in the middle - I like it to be 
challenging enough to be interesting, but don't mind help. 


 Secondly, in a multiple player sense, maps and other online resources 
are only a "cheat" if all the players don't have equal access to them. 
Since they don't come in the box, the view could be taken it's an unfair 
advantage for those "in the know". 

 Now, while it may seem obvious that most players will be able to get 
access to the online resources, a more extreme example would be things 
like ShowEQ. Most would agree (myself included) that this is a cheat, 
but in some senses, it's another way to gather info, in a way that the 
developers of the game didn't intend. 

 I guess for me, it's really a question of if the potential "cheat" 
gives others a significant advantage, isn't easily available, and how 
much it affect the internal game balance. Maps and most online resources 
are easily available, and while they offer an advantage, I don't mind 
it.
