No... it is the way I said it the first time... until a court decides
that the provision is NOT binding, then it is in fact binding.

If you go to court where the very ideas and provisions of a contract
are in dispute, many times a judge will grant injunctions that hold
the contract to its written intent until time that the contract
provisions are deem invalid.  Therefore, the contract IS binding until
the court decides that it is not.  This is a often used, sly,
underhanded trick pulled by the American legal profession

It works like this: Write a contract that is knowingly invalid, and
hope that the "other" party just accepts the provisions.  If the
"other" party challenges the provisions, use legal stall tactics to
hold the case in court for the term of the contract thereby getting
the "other" party to hold to the contract provisions for a limited
time.  Hey its better than nothing!
