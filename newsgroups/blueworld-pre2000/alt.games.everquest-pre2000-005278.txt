    Actually, jury nullification is one of the few remaining vestiges of the
Constitution drafters' intention that ultimate national power resides in the
people, rather than the federal government.  The doctrine principally
applies to allow juries to acquit an individual if they believe that the law
under which the defendant has been charged is just plain wrong.  It can be
applied in civil cases, as well, but very rarely, if ever, happens.  And in
fact, it very rarely happens in criminal cases.
