On Sat, 12 Jun 1999 20:34:16 -0400, smedley@icubed.com wrote:

>But the very fact that they're in "trouble" is a bit scripted, is it
>not?

Uh, no.  These are PCs who genuinely got in trouble, either they
attacked something that was too powerful for them, or they found
themselves outnumbered.  I can play a healer the way that I never
could in UO (since with potions and easy availability of heal spells,
no one ever needed one).  One of my characters is a follower of the
god of Life, and he has dedicated himself to healing and helping
others.  Even if they don't roleplay back.

I've found that to be very true in EQ, people are genuinely helpful.
You get in a jam, and people assist you.  They heal you, cast buffs on
you, perhaps step in and take a few whacks themselves.  You don't have
to be afraid that the person you just healed up is now going to start
whacking *you*.

>"Part of the plot line"?  Here's what I'm trying to say.  I don't want
>to be "part of the plot line" in the sense that I join or design some
>quest in advance, I want to suddenly find myself in the plot without
>having to look for it.

Hmm, that's a toughie.  But I never got that in UO either, even though
it was around for a couple of years.  It might take EQ a little while,
but they'll get there.  Verant is very interested in keeping a major
plot going in their world.

>As I recall, if you do not eat in EQ, you die.  

Wrong.  Your mana and health regeneration goes down, but you don't
die.  I've existed for long periods of time hungry and thirsty before.
My health doesn't bounce back as well, but I figure I shouldn't really
be fighting while ravenously hungry or thirsty, either.

>And food is pricey
>enough that to stand around and just talk to people would thus be
>strongly discouraged.  Does this not limit spontaneity and player
>interaction in the course of "normal play"?

Food is *cheap*, as is drink.  As low as six copper per, in my home
city of Qeynos.  Killing just a few first level mobs is enough to put
you in food and drink for several hours.  Not to mention that Rangers
and Druids have the ability to forage food and drink, and spellcasting
classes can summon it.  Several creatures even have food and/or drink
as loot on their corpses.  I've never seen a concern about food have
*any* impact on people's decision to stand around and chat.

>I did not say game designer.  Player run events planned and arranged by

Things suddenly "happening", totally and completely spontaneous events
which are supposed to be part of a greater plot and storyline? Pardon
