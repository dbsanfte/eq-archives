Thanks. Very useful errata.

Of course I disagree with many of the posters' conclusions about how these
problems make the book "useless" and "dangerous." It seems like the list on
this thread represents a quite reasonable degree of error for a 400+ page
book on a subject as complex as this one. That doesn't mean we don't take
the problems seriously, we do. On the other hand, many of the "problems"
listed are merely differing interpretations, or sheer cynicism (I was
especially bemused at the poster who criticized the book for including
creature stats).Yes, there are some that are just plain wrong, and some of
those are probably serious (again, I wasn't one of the main writers on this
project, so please don't gage the Everquest knowledge of the actual writing
team from my ignorance).

I'm probably going to get flamed for this, but let's face it ... a big,
complex book is going to have errata, just like a big, complex game will
have bugs. From what I could tell from that thread and this, people aren't
so much sore because the errata in the book is particularly pervasive or
heinous, but because mistakes happened at all. (Actually, it sounds like the
book is taking a certain amount of reflected heat from people who were
already sore at Verant, and who therefore expect an unrealistic degree of
fact-checking from Verant on an official book, but let's not get too
psychological here). There are always mistakes in a book this big, and we do
take them seriously, and we do want to know about them, but I'm not going to
come here and say that a book this big is a failure because of a few errata.

And Prima still puts out the best strategy guides in the business. Not that
there's never been a dog, but by and large the standard remains very high.
Simple fact is, games are more complex than they were a few years ago, and
MMP games are more complex (and more subject to individual interpretation)
than any other kind. That means more potential for error than there was even
a couple years ago.

So thanks, very sincerely, for pointing out the flaws in the book.
Nonetheless, I still stand by everything I've said in this thread ... This
is a damned good book, despite its flaws. I hope at least a few of you will
go back and take a look at the big things we got right, now that you've
already noted all the little things we got wrong.

Finally, you know what would be REALLY useful? If some at least slightly
sympathetic soul would go over the errata from this thread, and note WHY
each one of them is a mistake, and what they think we SHOULD have said,
instead. Not that our writers couldn't figure most of that out themselves (I
couldn't, but others could), but because we need to be sure what the
problems that YOU see are, and what you expect from the book.

Thanks again.
