Mason Barge wrote:
> 
> I disagree with the people who think you should start a halfling warrior or
> should "learn the interface" with an "easy" character or whatever.
> 
> The good thing about a halfling warrior is that he levels up fast.  But if you
> are going to spend enough time playing a character to care about levelling, get
> one you want to play to 50+.

Learning the interface with a warrior is a fine thing to do, and you 
level so fast that you can play them up to four or so in no time.  I
started tons of characters who never made it to level four.  I don't
think my first ogre ever managed to get out of Oggok.

> Since you wanted a wizard  -- magic user, etc. -- I would suggest you try any
> of the other casters, and would suggest a High Elf mage or enchanter, or a
> halfling druid.

If you just want to learn the game, a wizard, magician or necromancer
is fun and easy to play for the first few levels.  Pick any of them and
stomp around your newbie area.  The only problem is that memorizing
spells is extremely slow before level 4, so it is nice to get the basics
of walking, talking and swimming mastered with a character who can just
stand up and keep playing after death.
 
> Warrior is a poor choice to learn the game IMHO.  You learn nothing about
> casting and healing, it is difficult to learn very much about group tactics, it
> is very inconvenient in terms of lack of travel/summoning/binding.  And worst
> of all, people who pick warrior usually do so because they want to be the
> "strongest guy in the game" and then spend their lives whining about other
> players (and pet casters especially) doing more damage than they do.

Travel, binding, and group tactics probably will not come up for a 
learning character.  A warrior is fine for walking around, drowning,
getting killed by your guildmaster, and losing your guild tunic.

> You need to educate yourself about the game while you go.  I suggest that you
> do not play an erudite, an "evil" race (dark elf, troll, ogre)(although troll
> or ogre would be OK if you were dying to be a warrior), a ranger, or a wizard.
> 
> After one or two very short-lived guys, I picked a High Elf mage and played it
> to 51.  It was great.
> 
> I'm now playing a halfling cleric.  He's great too.

My advice would be to start out with whatever seems interesting, but
expect to be a bit frustrated at first.  A warrior will be easier to
play for the first few levels, so it is a good choice for learning.
When your first character gets to level 5 or 6, it will seem like you
have sunk a whole bunch of time and effort into them, but really that
effort will have gone into learning the game.  Characters you start
after that will get to that level much faster, so start a couple more
and see what you like best.
