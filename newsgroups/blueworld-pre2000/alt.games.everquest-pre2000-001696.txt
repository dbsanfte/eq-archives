>The low ar leather / studded leather stuff is great for people with
>high meditation who want a little protection but not a lot. GM tailors

I used to sell quite a bit of it.  It wasn't worth the protection it
afforded.  Shade used to wear it and then figured out that it didn't
really protect her enough to be worth the hassle of making.  We might
have bought it but very few other people were selling it.  Eventually
we quit selling it too, just because it wasn't worth the time required
to make it.  The difference between wearing it and wearing nothing
isn't worth noting.

Overall the combat system was very poorly balanced in terms of
trade-offs between one piece of equipment and the next.  There were a
handful of pieces worth using, and the rest was junk.  And if you
couldn't wear that armor because of meditation, then you were better
off, and no less effective, running around naked.  In PvP you *had* to
have deadly poison, and you *had* to have macroed ressit, or you were
dead meat against a naked mage.

And don't even get me started on the PvM issues...  mobs that warriors
just flat COULD NOT fight, but lone mages (or even archers,
eventually) could harvest risk-free.  And OSI's response to that
being, "Let's make the mobs tougher, and give them even deadlier
poison!"  Which of course meant jack squat to the BS'ers and archers
on ridges but moved warriors even father out of the picture when it
came to killing mobs.

There's an awful lot I like about UO, but the skill system and the
combat system isn't *even* on the list.  And they dictated a lot about
what items players could make that anyone would ever buy.

The NPCs selling finished goods for less than the raw materials cost
killed the markey for all but a handful of remaining items.

And the final nail in the coffin?  You could make more money tailoring
threadspools into gold than you could by making *any* of that 'handful
of remaining items'.
