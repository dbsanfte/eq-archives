The problem is that your graphic is extremely limited.  The 
information that is hidden by anonymity is not the identical
information that you would expect to see by looking at someone in 
real life, but it is still distinguishing detail.

You can't necessarily tell a person's profession and expertise by
looking at them, but you probably could tell a lot more than you
would get from some generic anonymous human in EQ.

Try to put yourself in the game world.  If you were going to hire
a fighter to guard your keep, you would be able to guess something
of their ability by looking at them.  You could see they looked 
strong or fierce or bore the scars of many battles.  What you get
in EQ is "[Warrior 39] Fred (Barbarian)".  Perhaps this number is
too accurate for some people's tastes, but I would rather present 
too much detail about my character than too little.

> And you're not forced to answer/ask about levels or slip out of genre,
> either.  If you're REALLY into the roleplaying aspect, it seems to me
> the whole notion of quantified levels (and stats) is a verboten subject.
> It just won't ever be applicable.   I dont' understand how you figure
> it's both an OOC concept that you nonetheless *need* to know...

You have just put your finger on the reason that anonymity is 
harmful to role playing.  There is no nice way to get the information
that you need without broaching verboten subjects.

Level is definitely an OOC concept, just like AC and hit points are.
It doesn't have a nice real life analogue and all the euphemisms for
it are pretty painful (rank, circle, plateau of enlightenment ...).

You need to know what level someone is if you are going to group with
them.  As you get higher level that number becomes more important and 
as you grow more familiar with the game it becomes more meaningful.  
Various classes gain whole sets of abilities at different levels and 
you need to know that, for example, a particular druid has the 
capability to evacuate your party from a nasty spot.

> You might sus out the approximate level by asking legit roleplaying
> questions about their abilities or their subjective opinion on the real
> danger of a particular mob.

You can do that when you meet someone and you both have the time to
share that information in a natural way.  More often you are in a
situation where there are many people and you need to find the ones
who are around your level to group.  In that case, walking up to 
each person in the zone and asking their class and level through a 
sequence of indirect questions is probably not entertaining role 
playing.
