	But, with the purchaser of the product only being privy to the
terms on the outside of the box, and not the terms on the inside nor the
terms on-line, the degree to which either of the later are binding is
still highly questionable.  That does not even take into account the
ability which Verant has to change that notice without our knowledge.  
	My opinion, and this is not a legal fact, is that the EULA is
presented as the broad policy.  Company's are well aware they are not
enforcable which is why they never enforce them.
	I am not trying to bash Verant... for the most part I like the
company.  They are going to overstate their rights in the EULA like all
companies do.  Then they will only take action against those who they are
SURE they will have the legal backing to handle.  That is largely limited
to Copyright violations.  I may be wrong, but I do not believe any
software company has ever even attempted to enforce usage policies... even
Microsoft's own laughable policy which eliminates the concurrent usage
standards of the industry.  (Meaning if you buy 1 copy of office and use
it from 3 different machines but never concurrently, that is a violation
of Microsoft's licensing policy.)
