This is true too.  The example of McKlusky was used since he is always
the same level so the amount of experience points gained should be
constant.

I've heard that AC tells you exactly how many experience points you
have into the level.  When different people kill the same critters, the
experience
they get is always the same (i.e. 300) regardless of the level of the
person.  So it is definite that in AC the experience gained for a mob
is constant and does not vary based on the level of the person.

In EQ, though, we just have to guess.  It could be that your experience
gained for a red is X percent of the experience you need to level. A
yellow would be Y, even would be Z, etc.  Of course, X > Y > Z.  I
doubt that this is the case since different mobs seem to give different
experience even though they are the same level.  For example, level 19
griffawns seem to be much better exp than level 19 beetles when I was
hunting in NK.  But there again, EQ could have different X,Y,Z values
for each mob.

In conclusion, it might not matter which method EQ uses.  If you get
a fixed number of points and you need more points to level, or if you
get a percentage of the level, the results should be just about the
same.  About 6 reds per bubble, 7 yellows, 8 blacks...etc.

And one last thing about quests related to experience that you
mentioned.
I turned in a *lot* of crushbone belts in my day and it took more
and more belts to get a bubble as I went up in levels.  I think at
level 4 it was like 4 belts per bubble.  But at level 10, it was like
20 belts per bubble.  I can't remember exactly what the numbers were,
but
it definitely took a lot more belts to get a bubble of experience.
