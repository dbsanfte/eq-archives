On Sun, 30 Apr 2000 19:05:53 -0600, "CMY" <ychad@uswest.net> wrote:

>I just read down in the forum that you can use a slocket to upgrade to a
>coppermine?
>Is this true?

Its somewhat true.  You need to make sure that the bios for your
system supports the coppermine.  Chances are it does and even most
motherboards that don't have support in bios for coppermine still
work, though they tend to give odd cpu speeds at boot. 

 Example is a friend had a coppermine 500e in a board that didn't
support coppermine. At boot it showed Pentium II 433. In windows
though running a program like cpuid it showed a 500e running at
500mhz.

>what are the disadvantages?

Disadvantage would be if dell does indeed have some quirky bios
installed that doesn't work at all with coppermine. Doubtful, but I
would check.  Other problem would be lack of voltage support. But if
you use a flip chip P3 and a slotket this is a non issue.

>I thought the dell oem motherboards prevent you from doing this?

About the only way they could prevent you is to block the bios support
or solder the cpu in.  Very doubtful they would bother, especially
with the latter option.

>

Thats up to you.  You have to weigh how your other hardware is. If you
want a bigger hard drive. More memory. Faster cpu. Better video card.
etc then may be better to just sell it and build another. If all its
lacking is cpu power , check with dell on the bios and coppermine
issues and pop in the new cpu.   

If you could identify the motherboard manufacturer and model # you
could defenitely check it for Cu support.
