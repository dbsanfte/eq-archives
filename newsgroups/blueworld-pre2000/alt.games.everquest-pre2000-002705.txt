If it is a connaction problem: There is a green horizontal bar in the
lower right or upper left corner of the world screen (depending which
view you prefer). It shows the packet loss (should be 0%, and will
cause problems that sound as you describe above 10%), and the ping time
(should be around 600 ms max). There is a freeware traceroute program
for UO, unfortunately I forgot the name; you should be able to find it
listed on most major UO sites. It will send out loads of packets, and
will show you which hop on your way to the game server causes the
problem. It comes preconfigured with the UO servers, but you can add
any other server yourself.

> Oh, and PS, what exactly can you do when you get a "server not
> responding"?  So far the only thing that works is hitting the reset
> button, due to the questionable "let's screw up multi-tasking" part of
> the program.

Try this, it works for me in most cases on Win98: Ctrl-Alt-Del should
bring up the task list, now you tell it to kill Everquest. The screen
goes black again. A few seconds later you should get a message box that
says that Everquest is not responding. Tell it you want to kill the
task. Now you should be back to the Windows desktop. Check if you lost
connection to your ISP (e.g. open any web page), happens to me quite a
bit after having been a few hours online. Restart the connection to
your ISP, if necessary, and restart Everqest.
