Hm.  Let me do some rough math.  

Say people play, on average, every other day for about 4 hours (14 hrs/wk).

Without considering peak hours, the average population would be:

	0.5 * (4/24) = 1/12 = 8.33% of all players

Say 80% of all players are in PST-EST (others are European, Asian, Australian)
Say 50% of all players play in peak hours (others are students, swing shift)

That's getting me an average during peak hours of about:

	0.5*0.5(0.8 + 0.2/5) = 21% of all players

with the 20% being the PST-EST peak hours players and the 1% being 
the rest.

and at non-peak hours you would see:
	
	0.5*0.5*(0.8/5 + 0.2/5 + 4*0.2/25) = 5.8% of all players

which is 4% PST-EST off-peak players, 1% being non-PST-EST peak
time players and the last 0.8% being the other non-PST-EST players. 

I am making a couple of questionable assumptions here.  I am
assuming that people who are not in PST-EST are evenly distributed 
in time zones around the world and that people who don't play at
peak hours are evenly distributed in play time around the clock.

The numbers should be a little higher than this because the play
time distribution has a long tail.  There are people who play much 
more than 14 hrs/wk on the high end, but you can't get below 
0 hrs/wk on the low end.  I would guess that my averages should 
both be about 10-20% higher than they are because of this factor.  
(Intuition gleaned from staring at too many probability density 
functions.)

If you play during peak hours, it would probably seem that closer to
95% of all players are in PST-EST.  I play at insane hours.  A lot of
people I meet are on Asian or Australian time.  People who play for
40-60 hrs/wk and people who play at peak hours will also be over 
represented, for obvious reasons.
