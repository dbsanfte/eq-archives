You were being reasonable, he was being a jerk.

It gets tricky though.

Last night I was leading a group of 18-24 (I was the 18 and
chose to invite the 24s) that was getting some surprise
powerleveling help from a husband and wife team.

(we had earlier been doing minos and muddite elders in a
pick-up group of 4 while their lower level characters did
goblins and muddite minors - we actually checked with them
first because we were pulling to the same spot, asked them to
please grab the aggro greens that had tailed along with our
trains, and had our cleric cut back on buffs so that he could
heal them as needed.  We were very pleasantly surprised to see
these two show up with their higher characters and return the
favor - especially since they had a level 50 cleric handy for
experience rezzes! Turns out they have side-by-side computers
and had been playing since the initial release day.)

So a high-level shaman pulling, druid dispelling fire shields,
and we were doing continuous take-down on reds.  I gained 3
bubbles in runnyeye (and got a BIBS!)

But then a couple of other folks wander by, including a
guild-mate.  I was swamped with juggling our loots, planning
pulls with our guardians, and doing /tell conversations with 2
of the party members. 

I ended up telling both that we were full up, and that as folks
left we would gladly invite them in.  And then suggested that
the 3 folks standing around might want to group.  (apologized
to the guild member, but I will not disband a good grouper
just because a guild member wanders by.  as group leader your
first loyalty is to the group.)

If I had had some more time I would have suggested that we make
2 groups and work something out.  As it was the guild member
wandered off, someone else left, and we invited the fellow who
had been waiting to come in and join us.  So it worked out.
Soon after folks got tired and went to Rivervale to bank or
just to camp, and eventually there were two people hunting
minos again.

The point - sometimes it takes the group leader a lot of effort
to keep the group running smoothly and they do not have time to
negociate cleanly with others in the vicinity.

Oh, and all the good things happened because we wandered over
to the low-level cleric and enchanter and discussed how 2
groups of different levels could hunt different critters at the
same location.

Ted K.
