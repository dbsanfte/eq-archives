On Thu, 10 Jun 1999 19:34:33 GMT, rkoster@origin.ea.com (Raph Koster)
wrote:

>EQ will have housing, mark my words, just like they added trade skills
>late in their development cycle. :)

Verant plans to have housing; just not *player-built* housing, like
that which has caused numerous problems in UO.  That's one thing that
UO messed up on; they had all those empty buildings, or buildings
filled with so-and-so the Sage, or whozits the Mystic Llama Herder,
when they could have actually been *using* those buildings for
something.  Instead, they wasted those buildings and let the housing
situation get totally out of hand.

And for adding trade skills so late in development, Verant certainly
has done an amazing job.  Limited availability of raw materials
encourages trade between cities; quite a few PCs make their business
by bringing ore and sharpening stones to Qeynos to sell to the
blacksmiths there.  Their player-run economy, while very simplistic in
its outline, is also incredibly effective.  There's more trade and
interaction between players on an economic level than I ever saw in
UO; and why not? It's not feasible in EQ to have a warrior/archer/mage
who is also an expert tailor and blacksmith/miner, and has no need to
interact with anyone else.
 
>Ultimately, there's only one ladder of achievement visibly supported
>by the game--XP. The chief complaint lodged against EQ's trade skills
>is that they are not a ladder of advancement, but are instead required
>to use the hack n slash ladder, which is in the end the same
>playstyle, not a different game.

Incorrect.  The trade skills are a "ladder of advancement" in and of
themselves; they're not capped by level like magic/melee skills are.
If you had a supply of money, you could be a Level 1 master smith.  If
you don't have a supply of money, then you need to go out and do
something to earn it, which is usually either running errands for PCs
and NPCs, or going out and slaying creatures until the point at which
you can sustain yourself wholly on mastery of your craft.  Kind of
like life.

Likewise, there are people out there who don't care about the "hack n
slash ladder"... There are bards who do nothing more than wander the
zones, holding impromptu concerts or poetry readings.  There are
merchants who learn only what travelling spells are necessary to take
them (in relative safety) from one town to another.  There are people
(like myself) who will abandon that planned hunting trip because of
that mysterious note gotten off of the body of a dead gnoll, and they
want to track down where it leads and what will happen.

>For me, depth is not about providing a basis alone--I want depth in
>the interaction and depth in the plot too, not just in the backstory.

Ironic, considering that I played UO for over a year, and never once
heard or saw *anything* from Lord British or any of the "movers and
shakers" of UO.  In fact, for the longest time the "major plot"
involved advertising the new "second age" lands.  Britannia was
absolutely static; the promised plot between British and Blackthorne
turned out to be nothing more than an excuse to townkill.  The only
plot I ever saw was from the players themselves, like the intrigues
surrounding the town of Kinship, or the Clomp-fests of the Shadowclan
Orcs.  The work those players put into roleplaying was really
admirable.

Luckily, we have such dedicated people starting to plan events in EQ
also.  The Heartstone saga, the Straylight saga, various invasions,
merchant bazaars, and player-run governments... All these are starting
to pop up in EQ.  I even heard that last night was planned a slave
auction by the Teir'Dal (Dark Elves).  Not to say that Verant has been
absent from their Players' lives... The War of the Gods on the final
night of beta was absolutely awesome, and hearing reports of GM/Guide
controlled creatures attacking towns makes me wonder what they have
planned next.

Plot is more than just advertising or massive spawns.  It's a few
rumors here, a strange NPC wandering around there, an item or note
found on the body of a particularly hard spellcasting orc or goblin.
Inklings of something more to come.

As background, I played UO for over a year; I was one of the "Charter
Edition" members, and while I wasn't in beta, I did start playing on
the first day of release.  After a long time I ended up being one of
the first members of the Shadow Clan orcs on Great Lakes.  I moved to
Catskills shortly after the Shadowclan did, and was involved in the
Picts, the Celts, and the player-run village of Kinship.  Finally, the
effort of trying to keep one step ahead of the PKs was just too much,
and I got tired of being chased around by plate-wearing, heavy xbow
wielding guys named PimPMassAFlasH (no joke).  The first day of EQ's
beta phase 4 was the day I cancelled my UO account.
