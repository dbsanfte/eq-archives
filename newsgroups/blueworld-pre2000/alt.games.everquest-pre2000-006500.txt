Yes.  Yes.  And Yes again, dammit.  One essential element of a game
like EQ or AC is exploring.  Seeing the cool new zone just added, or
running across the continent just to see the other side.  Anything
that adds to exploration is a good thing;  anything that detracts from
is is a bad thing.  Simple so far, yes?  Now, getting bound to the new
city you just ran to is not just good - it's absolutely essential.  If
you have to have this explained to you, mail me and I'll try, but I
hope this basic fact is clear to most if not all.  

Now, Brad specifically and verant in general has stated that forcing
non-casters to beg, trade, buy, etc. a bind every time they want to
travel is necessary for class-interdependence and will remain as is.
C'mon.  Give me something a little harder to argue against:

1)  Class interdependence and grouping is enforced through so many
mechanisms in the game that making bind free to anyone, ala lifestones
in AC, would have no affect whatsoever on these elements.  Class
interdependence should be a factor when adventuring in a group - each
class has a required role to play, be it healer, blaster, tank,
buffer/dot'er, whatever.  You go out solo and you'll get nailed.
That's class interdependence.  Suggesting that keeping the ability to
bind in the hands of casters only is necessary to this interplay is
ridiculous.  It enforces a one-way, completely inescapable dependency.
There is no analagous game-mechanic dependence that casters have with
regards to melee chars.  None.  

2)  Suggesting that allowing NPC's to bind is the same or even close
to allowing an NPC to buff is about as straw man as a straw man
argument gets.  A buff is a luxury.  Something nice to have.  A bind
is a necessity, like food.  You want to travel, you have to have a
bind.  You want to adventure, and be able to possibly recover
afterwards, you need food.  Both are essential to life in EQ.  Now,
what if food weren't available at a shop, and could only be bought
from someone with a cooking skill?  Sure, most of the interactions
would probably be positive.  Cooks could spread goodwill by giving
bread to newbies for free.  But wait until the first time you're
sitting in a underpopulated zone with no food, and then tell me if you
like the cooks.

3)  Brad & Verant's refusal to bend on this issue may be the number
one contributor to the perception that Verant places its own desires
for the game first, and the enjoyment of its players second.  As a
principle, this approach truly does bother me.  I offer as a
comparison AC's treatment of this issue, with lifestones and portals
that make travel simple and enjoyable.  Combined with the huge expanse
of monster-and-loot-filled territory to cover, this means that
finding, exploring, and hunting in your own little corner of the game
world, by yourself or with a group, comes easy.  Does this contribute
to class interdependence, the game economy, class balance, or any of
the other holy grails that Verant holds forth?  No.  Does it
contribute to exploration, a more even spread of the populace among
the available territory, and overall enjoyment of the game?
Absolutely.  Now, before you begin the AC-centric flames, let me say
that AC has its own share of problems (see my post on EQ v. AC
elsewhere on this newsgroups), which have me currently bouncing from
EQ to AC and back.  However, this is one area where AC so completely
tops EQ that it really makes you wonder about obsessive-compulsive
stubbornness amongst certain game producers...

I truly don't understand how this can be an area of contention.
