Okay... the developers would certainly tell you that the game is what you
make of it, this is true and not true.  Leveling is important, pretty much
the most important aspect of the game.  If you forever remain level one you
won't be able to travel very much at all.  The game is mainly about hunting
in an area until it is no longer worthwhile, then moving on to the next zone
/ dungeon.  Basically the same zone will always be the same with the same
monsters, npcs, buildings etc. with a few extremely rare exceptions.

I think one big difference from the game you mentioned is, grouping is a
necessity at certain stages!  A few classes can solo well, but most are
dependant on finding a group.  You'll hear many shouts for different classes
needed for fighting xxxxx.  This can be a good point or a bad one depending
on your perspective on grouping.  Necros and Druids can solo very well
though.

When you finally do get to a new area, it can be very exciting.  Different
zones look very different from eachother.  I remember the time I first saw
the swinging axe and lava pits in SolA.  *shivers*

Some would say I'm crazy, but I play on the pvp server and I think it helps
to transcend the simple minded level building.  Guilds mean more, you gain
definate friends and enemies based on your pvp beliefs / style.  (I am a DE,
who is pvp roleplay meaning I wish to exterminate pale skins).  The only
problem is getting past the newbie stage, or should I say slaughtering
grounds.  But this isn't an issue on the teams servers where you can't
attack those on your team.  (for example Dark Elves, Ogres, and Trolls are
allies and can't harm eachother except for duels).  PvP is pretty much zero
on the "blue" or "carebear" servers.

There are also quests, which get mixed reviews. And besides level building
there is also item building which is a whole other task.  Trade skills are
another goal, but they aren't intrinsically profitable.

Despite this speel of a satisfied EQ player.... no one can give you a clear
picture of how YOU will like the game, I suggest sticking to your
try-it-and-see method.
