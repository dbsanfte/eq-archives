	I think a better comparison than Doyle is Lovecraft.  Both are the
definitive authors of their genre.  Neither one concentrated on the
readibility of their work, but focused on the content and the creativity
there-in.  Both wrote a "pulp" genre with a literary bent.  

	Doyle was writing his Holmes stories for cash only... he even grew
to hate those stories as they kept him from any real literary recognition
for his other works and while his stories did go a long way in defining
the detective mystery area, they were never intended as "literature."

	As for why Tolkien isn't taught... that is because ciriculli are
generally very narrow as to what is considered literature... and they tend
to focus on those works with either a Romantic ideal or strong
mechanics.  It is a pity, as I have made a great study of the Horror genre
in getting my degree and it is obvious that creativity is not of interest
in academia.  There are very few works that I have been exposed to through
courses that are much beyond character studies.  Occasionally you find a
good plotter.  Very little outside the conventional boundries of
reality... A little Poe maybe, Beowulf, Jane Eyre (stretch,) Milton, and
Dante... but nothing from within the last century.
