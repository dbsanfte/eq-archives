You get so much more feedback in real life that "doing it a few 
times" actually tells you something.  The real world follows a
consistent set of rules.  Once you figure out things like gravity
and friction, you can use your logic to figure out how other things
work.  You can use it to choose between dragging, carrying or 
throwing an object.  You can apply your logic to novel situations.
The more a virtual world presents a logical self consistent set of
rules, the more the players can use their own reasoning to find
solutions to their predicaments.

If you go pump iron for a bit, you feel stronger.  After you do it, 
you can figure out how much better it made you at throwing balls.
But, you also know before you start pumping iron that you will
probably be better at throwing balls afterwards.  That's the missing 
link.  I don't think I need to know the exact probability for every 
event based on my stats and skills.  (That would certainly be nice, 
but not necessary.)  I just want to know ahead of time that pumping 
iron will help.  Rough percentages would be fine. (e.g. A person 
with 100 dex is 10% more likely to get a critical hit than a person 
with an 80 dex.)  Saying "more likely" is not terribly helpful 
because that could be 0.1% more likely or 50% more likely.

I don't actually think this debate is going to be resolved, since
what I call "making informed choices" you call "min-maxing".
