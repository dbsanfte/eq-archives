(1) If you're going to spam people, either you give a _genuine_
contact email address (so people can use it to buy stuff) or a
fake one (to make it hard to identify the culprit.) Giving a
genuine address, misspelled, doesn't achieve either of these
aims. I find it unlikely that even the most clueless spammer would
misspell his _own_ name, not once but *twice*.

(2) The original spam makes reference to an offer by MVP which,
as far as I can tell from their website, doesn't exist.

Certainly, Mr. Snyder _could_ have posted the first message -
there's not enough information to identify its poster - but it seems
rather unlikely.


> If you are in fact a victim of impersonation I suggest getting PGP.

How would this help? Having a PGP key may prove that you
_did_ send a particular message, but the absence of a PGP
signature is no proof that you _didn't_ send a particular message.
And the majority of readers are unlikely to say "wait, let's go and
look at this guy's website to see if he normally uses PGP" before
flaming/ringing him up for 'spamming'.
