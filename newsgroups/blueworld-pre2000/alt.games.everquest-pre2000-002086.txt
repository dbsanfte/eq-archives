I concur; in many ways their overall game design ambition were beyond
the reality of what they could reasonably accomplish..  The more complex
the interaction between the various factors in the gameworld, the
greater the chance for changes to bring about a negative unforeseen
effect.  Take the virtual ecology thing.

"If players kill off enough local deer, the wolf population may become
more aggressive as their natural prey was eliminated, increase wolf
attacks on humans"  Sounds good on paper doesn't it?  But, the reality
of the situation is there would be players off to make the deer
population extinct just to screw with the system.  Of slaughter all
wolves so deer would run rampant.  Balance is so hard to achieve when
you must rely on the actions of a large group of people, all of whom
play any way that want.
