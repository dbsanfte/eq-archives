> From the looks of it... and I haven't studied the proposed bill... but
> from the looks of it, this bill is simply trying to establish a
> standardized set of contract rules for consumer software that will be
> adopted in all fifty states and thus be uniform.  It seems to be an
> extension of the Uniform Commercal Code or UCC.  The UCC is a
> comprehensive set of rules that govern every sales contract in the
> US... it has been adopted at the State level in all fifty states.  For
> instance did you know that when you buy a car, you have three days to
> cancel the sales contract without reason?  That's right, if on the
> third day you decide that you don't want the car, you can take it back
> to the dealer for a full refund.  This is a provision of the UCC... it
> is a part of every sales contract involving autombiles.  They are
> looking to do the same thing for consumer software.
>
> The problem is that there is alot of consumer protection provisions in
> a UCC contract, but this software legislation looks like it is
> designed to protect developers not consumers.  The "freedom of speech"
> thing will have to be handled carefully.  As is, a software developer
> can already prevent you from talking or writing about the product...
> they simply need to make it a provision of the license agreement.  I'm
> not sure what remedies they could expect to get if this part of a
> license is violated, but it is a perfectly legal contraint.
> Unfortunately if you agree to something in a contract, there is
