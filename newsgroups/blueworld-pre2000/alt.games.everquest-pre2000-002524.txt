Well, let's consider this.  (I will speak in terms of UO, because that is
what I know.)  An adventurer will usually be seeking fame and fortune,
correct?  In these tpes of games, said fame and fortune tend to be in the
company of monsters.  Big, scary monsters.  Typically, the larger the payoff
in terms of what an adventurer wants tends to have a larger beast taking
care of it.  These monsters want to kill adventurers.  Who knows why, but
they do.  Our adventurer wants what the monster has.  Therefore, a
successful adventurer is an adventurer is one who is born and bred for
SURVIVAL.  After all, you can't be a very good adventurer if you're dead.
Am I right? Ok, with that out of the way, let's talk about what survival
entails.  I will detail a fighter, in order to keep this post somewhat
short.  A fighter seeks to kill the beasts.  They have his fortune and fame,
they must die.  The fighter, therefore, must be good at dispatching the
monsters, or he will die.  As was mentioned before, dying is not what a good
adventurer does.  So, combat, and anything RELATED to combat, is what a
fighter will concentrate on.  Anatomy, weapon skills, tactics, these are
things our fighter needs.  Parry is helpful, and healing is a must, for
without a method to heal ones wounds, one might die.  And, as previously
mentioned, this is not a good situation for an adventurer to find himself
in.  Resisting magic is good, for in ones travels, he might be set upon by a
lich.  A lich uses magic, and without the ability to resist said magic, the
adventurer might just end up dead.  And, as previously mentioned, a good
adventurer does not die.  So resisting magic is in.  So, a good fighter
adventurer must have a fighting skill, tactics, anatomy, parry, healing, and
resisting magic.  And logic dictates that the better one is at these skills,
the less one's chance of dying.  And, as previously mentioned, a dead
adventurer is not a good adventurer, and vice-versa.  Now, the powers that
be have limited the amount of knowledge that one person can hold, and thus,
when one fills up one's head with the knowledge of how to be a good fighter
adventurer, any additional knowledge might be detrimental to the fighter's
fighting skills.  This might cause him to die, and as previously mentioned,
dying is....  bad.  Hence, a tailor is not as successful an adventurer as
one might think.  Well, one should not think a tailor WOULD be a good
adventurer, but you seem to, Janey.  You see, it seems that tailoring is
knowledge, and as already covered, only so much knowledge might be acquired
by a single person, before their minds cannot take more.  So, in closing,
one's profession can interfere with their being a good adventurer, and not a
dead one.  Dead ones are not good, and the good ones are not dead.  such is
the way things are.  Oh, and one more thing.  Re-read my previous message,
and tell me what you think of all the issues I brought up, and not just a
select few you think you can explain. :)

have you see where I am coming from.  Forgive my temper?  :)
