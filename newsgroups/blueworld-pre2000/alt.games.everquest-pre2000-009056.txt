> >had my skill raise 1 or 2.  Never been able to figure it out.
> 
> There's two theories that could explain this.
> 
> The simple one is that people are very good at spotting patterns in
> random numbers, even when they are not there.
> 
> The other one is that Verant uses a performance hack to prevent having
> to generate 10s of thousands of random numbers per second.
> 
> Instead of generating each number as it is needed, they could generate a
> table of a thousand or so every few minutes. These just get used in some
> (hopefully non-repeating) sequence until it's time to make a new table.
> 
> In the long run, all numbers are still equally likely, so everything
> balances out. But in the short term, you might get a table with lots of
> high or low numbers, and so get lots of successes or none.
> 
> If the second theory is right, you can beat the odds doing jewel-craft
> by not just sitting down and doing 200 attempts straight out, but
> instead doing a sample run of say 10. If you get more successes than
> expected, carry on, if fewer go and do something else for 10 minutes
> before trying another sample run.

I have done this from time to time.  Even the sample test run is pretty 
expensive since I have to work with platinum to get any kind of real 
failure rate.  Contrary to popular myths about the prosperity of master 
jewelers, I do not have 1000pp sitting around that I can blow on platinum 
bars to see if I am feeling lucky right now. ;)

I haven't tried practicing some less expensive skill (like blacksmithing) 
to feel out the result set, but that might work.  It depends on how the 
probabilities are generated.  If they are generated modulo different bases
then there may be no correspondence at all.
