Yes, Silverlock, they are blowing smoke.  Part of making a good
graphics engine is working within the limitations of the technology
available to you.  Most consumer level graphics cards do texture
really well and don't do much in the way of geometry.  Verant 
cleverly used the strengths of modern computer hardware and hired 
good artists to create very convincing and beautiful effects.

However, EQ does in fact have an unimpressive graphics engine when 
it comes to terrain rendering and visibility.  I have no idea if AC 
does any better.  Some of these problems could be solved in the EQ 
client without redoing the game entirely.

Hierarchical terrain meshes - You can cut down in polygon count 
for big scenes by using large polygons far away and small polygons
close by.  There is no particular reason why EQ couldn't adapt
their rendering engine to use a scheme like this.  It might even
help with the light source issue. ;)

Occlusion culling in buildings.  As far as I can tell, there isn't 
any.  EQ does all of its culling with the z-buffer.  This is why it
is so easy to wake things up through walls and why the house in 
Unrest bogs slower graphics cards down to a crawl.

Zones and zone boundaries.  This is one that EQ is stuck with.  
Even if they reworked monster AI and streamlined their communication 
protocols so that zone boundaries were transparent, they would still 
be stuck with a world that was designed as a connected graph of sand
boxes.
