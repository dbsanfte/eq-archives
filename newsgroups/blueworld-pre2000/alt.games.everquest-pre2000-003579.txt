Catch 22 really...if it were more interesting for people to be there,

As for being completely inaccessible, that's true, and I can't think of
a reason it really shouldn't be...evil is supposed to be a limitation
=). Besides, a lot of places are 'off limits' to good characters unless
they go there with slaughter in mind (Grobb comes to mind). Every zone
should not be accessible to every class and player with equal ease.

> > As for more 35+ levels? Nope...with the release of Ruins of Kunark

Yup. A few actually. My girlfriend quit EQ a while back to concentrate
on Baldur's Gate, but she's thinking of buying Ruins of Kunark and
starting over. I know a few folks in this situation, with long cancelled
accounts who are going to pick up Ruins and play again. I can't imagine
they are unique.

Also, note that EverQuest, unlike Ultima Online and Asheron's Call NEVER
had a media blitz promoting the original game, and most of it's press
has been through standard industry interviews, small print ads and word
of mouth. And it's done better than both. Ruins of Kunark could do the
same thing for a whole new group of people.

Also, keep in mind the European and Japanese markets...currently next to
untapped by Verant, and if they choose to open servers overseas and sell
big time overseas (it took OSI over a year to start doing it with Ultima
Online) you suddenly have the potential of hundreds of thousands of new
accounts without the zones to support them. 

I think it's dangerous for the EQ designers to mess with the original
LEVEL of a zone (i.e. What level the zone is intended for) precisely
because of this possibility. It worked fine in MUDs and MOOs, because
after a certain point you knew your player base and you could always
limit entry to it. However, with something as potentially open ended as
EQ, they should be a lot more careful than they have been with these
decisions.

I'd disagree...Kerra Isle is as prone to trains (maybe even less so) as
Blackburrow, however the layout is a lot different. Kerra Isle,
currently, requires more thought and preparation to hit than
Blackburrow, and that's part of its problem. You can't hit the kobold
prisoner area without running past no less than 5 spawn points with
things more powerful than what you're actually trying to get at.

OK loot is only one of my suggestions, but I think it can work as a
starting point. It certainly couldn't hurt =). Along with revamped loot,
they have to make it more interesting to hunt there. Right now you can't
hit specific MOBs because they are locked away in areas that are hard to
get to without a very big fight you can't really handle. In Blackburrow,
you have 'The Elite Ledge' and the 'Commander Room' and the 'Shaman
Bridge'. Areas which are difficult to get to, yes, but which are
preceded by WEAKER MoBs. In order to get to things like the Kerran
Pridesman (which tend to be grouped together) you have to fight through
five or six male or females who, together, take WAY more out of you than
a single Pridesman could.

I'm thinking more of keeping Odus out of touch, it fits with the idea of
what Norrath is. They are supposed to be aloof, superior, avoiding the
taint of 'common' men. Erudite Clerics and spellcasters are excellent
classes to play and there are enough players playing them that if
dungeons like Kerra Isle catered more to them at starting levels, they'd
form those groups and societies without ever leaving Odus. It would
probably even create a more interesting game dynamic in that you'd have
way more people acting (intentionally or not) the roles of their race
selection: Erudites, isolated on their little isle, start viewing the
mainland as barbaric and not nearly as good as their home, Mainlanders
viewing Erudites as haughty isolationists, and Trolls eating all of them
to shut them up.
