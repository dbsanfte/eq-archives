You are really taking what I said out-of-context, as I was speaking only to
the pet/dual wield issue, and how doing the preferred fix on it would cause
more problems than it solved.  Many of the other issues that you mentioned
here are issues that we *choose* not to change because it doesn't fit into
our vision for the game, not because such a change would be too difficult
and prone to problems.

> random loot and random spawn

This is something we *choose* not to do because it doesn't fit into the
vision for EQ.  We want special items to be obtained by virtue of doing
something special.  Those items should have a context in which they exist
and from which they are acquired. Random loot drops will never happen for
the best items.

> and make alchemy a viable skill

Archery is viable for its intended use, it's just not generally viable as a
primary means of combat.  This is as intended.

> They will not fix stats

That's because they aren't broken.  Just because you don't like the way they
work out doesn't mean that they are broken.  There are many people out there
who believe that stats work fine.

> Verant is just not willing to make radical changes to EverQuest

Why on Earth would we want to make radical sweeping changes to a vastly
successful game?  *Most* people enjoy EQ.  *Most* people have played the
game for a period of time in excess of what we forecasted.  Why would we
risk messing all of that up to make radical changes to the game?  If we made
radical changes, it wouldn't be EQ any longer.

> If the time comes that you just don't seem to enjoy it any longer, move on

Here's a sound piece of advice.  We really don't expect people to play EQ
forever, but we will continue to make improvements and additions to the game
to keep the people that are here playing.  Certainly, if it gets to the
point that playing the game is a chore, perhaps you should put it on the
shelf.  I really can't fathom why people would keep playing if they hate the
game.
