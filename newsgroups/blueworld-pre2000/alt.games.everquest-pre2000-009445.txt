There is so little information in your character's graphic, that 
giving up the few distinguishing characteristics you have remaining
seriously diminishes your character.  Thus I feel it is very bad 
for role playing to be anonymous.  Also the fact that so many people
use the tag merely to make themselves anonymous destroys its purpose
in identifying role players.  All it does is force people to ask
you grossly ooc questions about your class and level.

I hate being asked game mechanics questions in direct speech.  Such
questions should be confined to /ooc or /tell.  However, I do answer
when asked, often chiding the person for not trying /who first.

> Maybe I'm operating under a

I did not say that.  Only if you had a terrible name or were being
very rude would I ignore a direct request, and that has nothing to
do with being anonymous.  I feel that most people who go anonymous 
are merely misguided, not despicable. ;)

> If you're simply saying you won't randomly buff me (assumably with
> decent buffs, as I randomly buff anyone I run across with my weak ranger buffs,
> on the assumption that if they take, they probably will be appreciated), I'm
> fine with that.  If you're saying me being /role makes me a pariah to you
> simply because you've had bad experiences with other /anon characters, then I
> challenge the pigeonholing of all of us who see a decent reason to be /role.

When I am not terribly pressed, I do a /who on every person I see.
If the person is a caster or a hybrid over level 9 and not an 
Enchanter over 29 (i.e. anyone who uses mana and can't cast Clarity), 
I give them Clarity.  If they are a fighter of any stripe, I give 
them Augmentation.  Sometimes I also cast Brilliance, Insight or 
Radiant Visage if I feel those would be useful and I have mana and
time.  I never spontaneously buff anonymous people who I don't know.

If I get a request for Clarity from an anonymous person who appears
to be a Druid or a Wizard, I feel that they are being hypocritical 
and may not cast it.

I disagree with anonymity in principle, not because of bad experiences
with anonymous players.  I feel it is bad for role playing and I think
that its inclusion in the /roleplay tag renders that tag useless for
its intended purpose.
