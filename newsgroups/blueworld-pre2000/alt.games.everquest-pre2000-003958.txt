The sense I have been getting from these discussions is that
the combat algorithm uses, in decreasing order of importance:

attacker's level + attacker's skills + attacker's equipment +
attackers stats 

against

defender's level + defender's skills + defender's equipment +
defender's stats.

I also get the sense from these discussions that stats may give
something closer to an absolute increase and not a relative
increase in your effectiveness.  So an increase of, say, 10
points is big when the combat factor totals to 50, trivial if
the combat factor totals 500.

This ties into one of the other oddities of the game and of the
discussions here, which is why I bring this up.

I get the sense that there are three stages to each character's
life.
at low levels stats matter a lot, clerics are adequate in
melee, in fact, everyone can melee if they have to.

In the middle levels, stats start to mean less, equipment is
still useful, and casters will generally back out of melee
except in controlled situations.  (note that if I ever decide
to level my cleric I WILL be using those controlled situations
to max my combat skills, just in case I need my defense later
on.)  at mid levels the various melee classes will still have
fairly comparable abilities.

at high levels it is all level v. level, plus equipment to
break any ties.  This is where the warriors and monks separate
themselves from the hybrid melee classes.

I make these distinctions because it appears that some of the
excess flamage on the group comes when people make a statement
that is valid for one category and try to apply it accross the
board.

Of course, my highest level character is 9th, so this is all me
trying to make sense of what the folks on the groups are
saying.  There is no personal experience in other words.  

Still, does this make sense?
