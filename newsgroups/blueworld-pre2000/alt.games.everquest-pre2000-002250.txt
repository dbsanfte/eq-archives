Ahh the virtual world.  I have posted on this topic before so I'll give
you a "short take";  the problem with the virtual world concept is that
is assumes human interaction within it will reflect the same general
types of behaviors in the real-world, in about the same proportions.
This is not the case.

People's behavior online does not mirror their real-world behavior: the
anonominity of the interent, the "it's only a game" mentality, the total
focus on self, call it what you will.  What it all stems from, in my
opinion it is lack of consequences.  In the real world, the actions you
perform and the words you say carry consequences, both positive and
negative.  If you speed, you get a ticket.  Commit murder, go to
jail/death penalty.  The fear of negative consequences alone can deter
people from performing undesirable actions.

In an online game, this generally fails b/c there are no true
consequences.  In-game consequences are not high enough to discourage
undesirable, yet performable, actions.  The only way to control such
online behavior is through the game design itself, b/c the normal
societal policing cannot exist.  As such, a realistic virtual world is a
pipe dream b/c it is by it's very nature (that of being virtual),
unrealistic.  That is why I am of the opinion that when it comes to game
design issues, realism should always take a back seat to enjoyable
gameplay.
