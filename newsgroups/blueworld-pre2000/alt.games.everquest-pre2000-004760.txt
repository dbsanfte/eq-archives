Sam Schlansky wrote:

> >Crippling Blow is less reliable in terms of landing than procing
> >Lay on Hands or Harm Touch,

> LOH and HT do not proc. They are skills that renew once every
> everquest day, or 42 minutes, I believe.

By proc I meant getting it off successfully. They can still fail, albeit
extremely rarely.

> >and per level does a lot less damage than Harm Touch.

> A level 50 had a crippling blow of 300; a level 50 shadowknight's HT
> hits for 500, and is only usable once every 42 minutes. It can also
> be resisted.

A good warrior should not be in a Berzerk state more than once or twice
in an EQ day, unless they are soloing. And Crippling Blow may NEVER land
in that 42 minutes, or it may land 4 or 5 times.

The point being, you cannot RELY on it. A Shadowknight and a Paladin can
rely on their special abilities and use them as they see fit. A warrior
simply has to hope that his special abilities come into play when most
needed, and very often they do not.

> >In addition, before it can be used you have to be
> >in a condition which will, under normal conditions, result in your
> >death if the MOB you are facing will be impacted to an appreciable
> >degree by Crippling Blow.

> Right. So?

See above.

A Paladin with 100 hit points left fighting 2 Gnoll Soothsayers at level
20 can win if he successfully uses Lay Hands, and 90% of them time he
will be successful.

A warrior with 100 hit points left fighting 2 Gnoll Soothsayers will
have to land a minimum of 2 Crippling blows (for 100 points of damage,
at that level) in order to survive.

Unlike a Paladin, however, the Warrior has absolutely 0 control at that
point over whether or not they will be able to use Crippling Blow. A
Paladin can rely on their ability and time it for the point where it is
of maximum use. A warrior must rely on luck.
 
> >It's a great addition for Warriors, don't get me wrong, but it
> >still places them at barely on par with Paladins and Shadowknights
> >for overall usefulness (if not less useful in groups),

> You're saying warriors are LESS USEFUL than paladins and
> shadowknights? In GROUPS?

I didn't say less useful in all situations. A warrior in a Group,
depending on the makeup of a group, is incredibly useful, especially
when buffed. They can tank like there is nothing else to be done. In a
magic light group, however, a Paladin and a Shadowknight are more useful
as tanks as they can also provide backup in the form of their magic.
Depends on the group. Warriors are excellent additions, for the most
part, but if you've got a group that requires a little more
buffing/healing/damage dealing potential, a Shadowknight or Paladin
might be a better choice. Shadowknights, in particular, can be
incredibly useful for Harm Touch alone over a Warrior in the damage
dealing department.
