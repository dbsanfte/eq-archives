Ok, there is a distinction here that has escaped every post I've seen on
this newsgroup that mentions "realism" that I want to bring to the fore.
There are two kinds of realism- realism compared to the Real World, which
almost nothing bothers with because, you're right, it's usually sacrificed
for enjoyable gameplay, and always in a fantasy setting. But that is _not_
the kind of realism that is important.
What's important is _internally consistant_ realism. You can create a
world and give it its own rules and then see what it 'realistic' in that
world and what is not. When someone complains "is it realistic for
shopkeepers to wander out of the shop then teleport when spoken to?" and
someone else responds "is it realistic to cast fireball?" they are
comparing the wrong kind of realism. In Britannia, the answers are No and
Yes. In Britannia, when people die they become ghosts, and healers can
Resurrect them. These thngs are, _in Britannia_ realistic. Some other
things are not- mining through walls, for instance. 

I realize that this has little bearing on the current conversation, but
damn I've been waiting a while with that pent up. You'll probably see me
say it again if that distinction is missed. Looking back at the post I
responded to, or at least what I didn't delete of it, it might not even
have misused the word. But it was an opportunity to point out the
difference.
