There are many pitfalls to this. To cite one major one, if you do this
then you have a discontinuity in your availability of mobs for a given
level. A levelk-based game HAS to pay very careful attention to the
quantity (and frequency of death) of mobs of given levels, and their
geographical distribution.

>Newbies are running around in Banded armor now. But what is the
>problem? A L10 still can survive in Unrest alone no matter how tweaked
>he is.

Well, it does mean that the balance of the game as a whole has
shifted. You have compressed levels 1-10 into the equivalent of one
level's worth of difficulty (or more accurately, levels 1-9 fell off
the bottom end of the scale). This reduces the advancement ladder and
therefore increases churn.

>So people on average spent 20% less time to reach L50. So what? It is

Designers can never upgrade as fast as people play thru the upgrades.
This is an issue, believe me. :) Also, 20% faster play to max level
means a significantly faster churn out of players. This affects your
bottom line.

>From where I see it now, the best design choice is a long power curve

Long power curves are good. Evolving play mechanics are better. Both
is ideal. IMHO, EQ does the power curve thing beautifully, and the
evolving thing not so well. And UO is the exact opposite. :)
