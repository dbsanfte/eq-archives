|Nothing you do, in either game, ever, makes a difference in the world.

|I envision UO2, because I want an online world, and not an online
|game, and EQ, entertaining as it is (and it is entertaining, you EQ

	These products will _always_ be games, not worlds.  They will become
worlds when they don't go away when you don't want them to be there.  They are
forms of entertainment, and nothing more.

|The people come here to settle the world - and they do.  They
|colonize.  They build.  They pacify a hostile land.  And it is
|pacified.  They have houses that they built with their own hard work
|and they live there, in peace - a peace that they fought for and won.

	And then next week?
	
	What you are describing is what Mr. Koster wants; a kind of fantasy-themed
SimEarth with the players as the sims.  News Flash:  SIMEARTH AIN'T FUN FOR
THE SIMS.  It's fun for the guy watching it.  If the little guys inside have
feelings then the're not going to be amused when what they've worked for over
weeks goes up in smoke in seconds.  And nobody plays a game when it doesn't
amuse.

|They enjoy that peace and celebrate the heros and mourn the dead that
|caused it to happen.

	But there are no heroes, because there are no dead, because it's all just
a game.

|Eventually they are threatened.  It doesn't matter by what.  NPC Mobs
|or kewlios or "roleplaying evil persons" or whatever.  Their peace and
|happiness and *success* is threatened.  It doesn't matter by what.

	What you are describing is a multiplayer competetive political game, only
from an RPG perspective.  Political games are interesting -- for about five
minutes.  That is because they are all inherently the same game.  You just
have to learn what the strong positions are and who the strong players are,
and then the winning strategies become the same as in every other political
game you've ever played.  There are people who go through their lives without
ever sassing this, and I've met more than a few who deny it entirely even when
confronted with it.  They tend to lose a lot.

|But the war does matter.  If they lose - they lose their peace and
|life sucks.  If they win - they win their peace and life is good
|again.  A break in the perpetual conflict.
|
|Or even if they lose, that horrid evil-infested, ruined world is not
|perpetual either.  They can pull themselves up from the ashes and

	The only thing stupider than a political game is one lacking a victory
condition.  Unless the game can reach a point where you say "Side A wins, Side
B loses, game over, start the next game" you're right back to "Day of the
Dove".

|But never quit.  Never die.  Never give up.  Because they know that a

	*What* good times?  It's all over entries in a database.
