Well truth is you write a vxd that chains in ahead of anything at the user
level (leaving the other kernel drivers intact so the darned things work)
for the Kbd and mouse - it can mask off the message pump for the WM_
messages dealing with focus - meaning EQ will not know it has lost focus.
But this is only good if you figure a way to plop your GUI on top of the
EQ D3D full screen setup.  Which is why I said the first "hacks" will
likely be dual monitor setup required.  

I've messed a bit with just such a thing (vxd's are a pain in the ass - I
cribbed most of my code from an old MSJ article) - and was able to steal
the mouse from a VB progam and spring it over to my app (VB sitting on a
DLL that drilled into the vxd) without blowing the focus message set (it
was chained and not released until I left my "hacker" app).  Of course it
wreaked merry hell with the screen as the mouse was in one spot and the OS
knew it, but the app that the focus was supposed to return to (the VB one)
didn't have the slightest idea where the mouse was - and I couldnt send it
a "got focus" event - eventually I got arround it by recording the mouse
location when it generated the "left the app" message, subtracted 1,1 and
just stuffed it back there and kicked out a "mouse up" to wake the
"fooled" ap up and exited my prog.  Doing this on top of a "live" program
(that changes on its own) as opposed to the basically inert VB program
(all it did was change color when it got and lost focus) may cause
problems if it tracks mouse location and that mouse is nowhere near being
on its coord system (I dunno if they use the mouse directly or go thru the
DirectInput interface to the mouse - meaning it would have relative rather
than absolute coords - im not familiar with Direct Input so Im talking out
my ass on this one).
