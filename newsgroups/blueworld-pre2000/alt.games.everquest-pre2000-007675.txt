> On Fri, 07 Apr 2000 19:43:06 GMT, Nan Wang <nwang@shore.net> wrote:

I've done it for very long stretches at a time and not suffered unduly 
or crippled myself as a result.

I could get by just fine only ever using them at work, for work, and 
where what I do with the computer is monitored far more than it ever 
could be.

If EVERY software company had a License Agreement like UO or EQ's 
proposed one, I'd laugh, because most don't include internet 
connectivity. If the company demanded the right to inspect my computer 
at any time, I'd simply not buy the software.

Would I give up my computer if EVERY SINGLE PIECE OF SOFTWARE were like 
this? Yes. In a second. Nothing, and I mean nothing, in my life REQUIRES 
a computer. It's a wonderful convenience and entertainment device, and 
that's it.

I haven't ever owned a car either, and it hasn't hurt my job prospects 
any, although to be fair, where I live you can get anywhere with mass 
transit in under a half hour, and apart from that the nearest major city 
(other than the one I live in) is 8 hours away.

In fact, not owning a car has INCREASED my living standard by a 
significant degree. My investments are such that I can retire with 
several million in the bank by the time I am 40.

So, in this theoretical world where every piece of software is linked to 
the Internet and its creators have the right to sniff your computer any 
time they want for whatever they want, what would I do? Not own one. Is 
it required to live in that society? Let's assume it is. If my privacy 
is so valuable that this would violate it, I'd move.

Slippery slope arguments like the one you posit work great in theory 
because they so seldom, if ever, come true in reality.
