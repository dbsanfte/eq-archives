My favorite game system is GURPS.  It tries to model the real world.
This means that your intuition works.  You can do whatever you want
and there is some translation into game mechanics.  

If you pick up a shield, it does just about what you would expect a 
shield to do.  The shield can passively defend you by getting in the
way when someone attacks you or you can use it to actively deflect
an attack.  You don't have to know the numbers involved to know that
a shield is a good thing to have.

The big problem with a high fantasy level-based system is that your
intuition doesn't work.  There is no real world equivalent to being
level 20 or to raising your magic resistance.  So, you can build
intuition either by knowing the mechanics or through experimentation.

Unfortunately, EQ's random number generator is so bad that it is 
nearly impossible to determine the effect of a change.  Any experiment
that you might try (like taking a few swings with a new sword) 
degenerates to a test of what the random number generator feels like
doing right now.  Also, acquiring the gear that you want to test is
a non-trivial process in itself.  Few characters have the luxury of
affording a complete set of high AC gear and a complete set of high
stat gear to compare against each other.

I would say that everyone who can be bothered should learn the mechanics.
That is just the intuition part of the game.  You should know that extra 
Dexterity gives you a greater chance to hit with your weapon, dodge an 
attack or aim a bow.  You should know that Willpower will help you 
maintain consciousness when sorely wounded or maintain concentration when 
hit while casting a spell.  It is part of what should be your physical 
sense of how the world works and how your body works.

The rest of the information, such as the exact stats of monsters and
information about parts of the game world that your character hasn't
seen, should remain secret.
