>Q How detailed is the world? I was amazed when I first played EQ. Quests
>were detailed, most NPCs would talk to you. Every single building was
>crafted with much detail and care. (I LOVED Kelethin when I first saw it.)
>Each town/zone was unique and rich. How's it like in AC? As detailed?
>Because detail is kinda important to me.
>
>A Well, let me see.  I can't stress this enough, you truly have to see it to
>believe it, but the AC world is HUUUUGE.  When you walk over the top of this
>mountain and see a VAST lake stretching before you that you can't see the
>other side of, it's truly awesome.  You'll have experiences where you'll be
>climbing this LONG winding road up a gradual slope for several minutes, and
>then you get to the top and see a steep downhill slope into a valley with
>mountains in the distance.  Also another incredible thing is that you can
>compare it to actual scenery.  If you go on top of a high hill on a sunny
>day in real life and look off into the distance, you can see rolling hills
>and tiny points of interest in the distance.  The same holds for AC.  If you
>look in the distance you can actually see that mountain you're going to get
>to if you keep running.  No static backgrounds, and no LACK of backgrounds.
>Also, the world is continuous.  You don't "zone".  Technically you use
>portals, but not to navigate the world.  They're used to access dungeons and
>points of interest.  As for quests, I've done about 6 or 7 and haven't found
>a broken one yet.  I'm sure some broken ones exist, but I haven't found any.
>I really enjoy how all the people that are not assocated with shops actually
>have things to say to you.  However, I'd say that 8 out of 10 NPC's are shop
>owners or vendors.  As for the buildings in the towns, I find them boring on
>some occasions.  One culture, modeled on the far east of the real world, has
>some cool towers, and the desert towns are intrigueing (sp?), but I don't
>really like how they "recycled" real life cultures.  Makes me think they
>took the easy way out in this area.  I didn't buy a copy of the Encyclopeia
>Brittanica ;)  So to summarize this answer, the world impresses me to no
>end, meaning its vastness and beauty (it really is like looking out a window
>from your house -- at times), but buildings and cultures are modeled on real
>life, and may be boring to some.
>
>Q Are the classes balanced? I was very dissapointed in EQ about the balance
>between casters/melee. And how some classes were just ignored and still are.
>(Shadow Knights and Rogues anyone?)
>
>A Hehe..well, if your looking for a better balance between casters and melee
>in AC, look elsewhere.  This is the biggest beef people have with the game
>right now.  I chose a caster because I thought it would be fun to get the
>'power' spells later in life just like in EQ.  Not so.  I was immediately
>extremely powerful.  In EQ the rewards for mages came late in life, but
>those rewards were a godlike ability to deal punishment.  In AC, you can
>deal so much punishment as a mage that you do quadruple the damage as a
>melee fighter of the same level in the same amount of time.  I expect to get
>my mage "balanced" by the dev. team any day now...  I have a level 16
>warrioress friend who I can actually "steal" kills from (meaning I do more
>damage) and I'm only level 7--note that this is only if said creature
>doesn't attack ME first ;)  So balance sucks, and I would say that
>unfortunately the only way to resolve it is to actually nerf the mages,
>which will piss them off, or leave it alone, which will piss off the warrior
>types.  They really should iron these issues out BEFORE release.
>
>Q Which has a better magic system? I loved how diverse the magic was in EQ,

>in EQ MUCH better, but the fact that 2 vastly different spells in EQ looked

>
>Q Which game is more 'complete?' I don't wan't to play another game with
>missing features, broken trade skills, broken quests, ect.
>
>A The game in terms of quests and messages and spell graphics is EXTREMELY
>complete in comparison to EQ.  All of the NPC's talk to you.  I like that.
>I have a key that has 3 uses in AC.  When I use it once, a little tiny bar
>on the left hand side of the icon goes down a third, kind of like the gauge
>on a fuel tank.  Those little additions just give me a warm glowy feeling
>inside.  It's exactly the perfectionist attitude I would have taken if I had
>done the same work.  No half ass attempts for me ;)  As for balance, see #3.
>
>Q One thing in EQ which really bothered me was how I never really 'stood
>out'. I always knew that there were always 300 people online at the time
>that could do exactly the same things I could do, and most likely better.
>I've mostly played a Rogue in EQ becuase there were so few and I stood out
>more. Does AC let you 'stand out'? Be your own character and not some
>'canned' one?
>
>A [[ALSO SEE #7]] Well, AC bragged about having so many facial combinations
>that it would be rare for two people to look exactly alike on the same
>server.  Although this is TECHNICALLY true, it is not ACTUALLY true.  The
>facial graphics are not as detailed as EQ, and the icon you get when you
>examine someone is horribly stretched and washed out.  I am guessing this
>was done this way on purpose because of the way characters are created.
>When you first create your face, it's done like one of those sliding
>puzzles.  The face will be divided into 3 parts.  You choose the 'nose' you
>want and it slides into it's middle 'slot' on the face.  By washing out the
>faces, they were able to make all of the 'noses', 'eyes', and 'mouths' fit
>together in any combination.  The effect is that even though you may have a
>different nose and eyes than your friend, if you are from the same culture,
>the details are too small to be of any significance and you look similar.
>One nice thing I love about AC though is that EVERY piece of armor or
>clothing I've put on in the game is represented on my character.  In EQ, if
>my character had had all of the armor represented, I would have looked like
>a slightly retarded mime with red clothes (the idea of a veil was scary).
>But in AC, this would actually show up on your character.  It makes for some
>cool armor effects.  (Try on a gronmie hide leather breastplate--sky
>blue--with a gold chainmail coif--golden--and green arm pauldrons..it's
>cooler than it sounds, trust me.)
>
>Q Are the Rogues actualy useful in AC? I was always dissapointed at the lack
>of locks/traps in EQ. Making my Rogue little more then a down-graded
>Warrior.
>
>A There aren't actually 'rogues' in AC so to speak.  Instead, lockpicking is
>a skill that can be learned by anyone.  The question then,  is this for
>example:  Do I want to have the lockpicking skill this time, or do I want to
>save it for the item enchantment skill.  I like this system better.  In AC
>nobody is actually denied the chance to have any one skill.  In this way,
>there is no defined class.  Warriors CAN cast spells, and mages CAN use
>heavy armor and weapons.  But they DON'T because if mages for instance
>concentrated on using their hard-earned experience to raise stats in
>strength (to carry all that heavy armor and weaponry), they would suck later
>on in magic.  So you aren't technically forced into going into a path.  It's
>much more real-life based (I write C++ programs for a living, but who says I
>can't also be a body-builder in my spare time eh?) and I like this.  SCORE A
>BIG ONE HERE FOR AC!!
>

>seen trade notes worth 110,000 gold..actually the coin is called a pyreal,
>not gold, but you get the idea.
>
>Q I love to travel/explore. I loved making my first run to Qeynos from

>Q Any other issues I should know about in comparing the two?

>A I guess it really depends on what you like.  I've found playing AC to be
>an extremely calming experience, kind of like taking an extremely short,
>mini-vacation to relax.  Dying is not very stressful, you don't 'lose'.
>Well, you lose a large portion of the money you are carrying and your most
>expensive item (not counting trade notes), but you can recover this, much
>like in EQ.  I have a low run skill in AC, but unless the monster is
>powerful and can kill me quickly, or if there are a lot of them, I can
>usually run for a long enough time that the monster gives up.  I though it
>was stupid and ridiculous how in EQ a monster could track you to any part of
