As GZ explained when druids bitched about the bullshit damage of our
root hitting the mob after the root effect, any damage (melee or not)
has the potential of giving the mob a new "save" against the root.
Furthermore, the "save" gets a bonus as time wears on.  Meaning, if
you hit a mob immediately after it's rooted, it has a less of a chance
to break root than if you hit the mob after it being rooted for about
a minute--each time a root save check is done, the bonus increases.
This is why higher level druid roots are suppose to be better--the
interval at which it's checked is greater.  This was posted in the
druid boards a few weeks back (when they "fixed" the root spell
because of *necros* complaining about their damage + root spell having
instabreaks...)

Now, it's quite possible that like many other things, Verant could be
blowing smoke on this--but as a general rule, GZ at least doesn't
outright lie about spell effects.  He may get annoying trying to hide
some information out of "roleplaying" reasons...

On an interesting note, I found out why I was getting so many instant
breaks on my roots--it seems that when I tank the mob to try and
reroot it, my *damage shield* from my coat line (the gimpy 5hps per
smack damage shield) appeared to be giving it another save.  When I
snare it, move back and then root it, it sticks every time (well,
nearly everytime)--the duration varies widely, but at least I didn't
get the "normal" amount of instabreak last night when I did this.

Need to do more log analysis to confirm this, but if this is the case,
I'll have to snare/root the mob everytime...
