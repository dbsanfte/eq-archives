The ease or difficulty of implementation here is really moot.  How often
must we take steps to save people from their own bad choices?  The freedom
to make bad choices is essentially part of an RPG, and we don't want to go
Confirmation Box Crazy just to stop people from making them.

We've gotten tons of suggestions for various confirmation boxes in game.  If
we implemented all of them just on the basis of them being easy to
implement, you wouldn't be able to take a single action in EverQuest without
having to click YES.  Among those suggested: Box for Attacking. Box for
Dropping items.  Box for handing items (above and beyond that which exists).
Box for Hailing NPCs.  Box for Zoning.  Box for Combining Items in a Kit.
Box for Forgetting Spells.  Box for Disembarking a Ship.  In addition, for
each box that already exists in game, people have also suggested secondary
"Are you *absolutely* sure?" boxes for most of the things listed above.

Confirmation Boxes are a crude and ugly way to try and make a piece of
software foolproof.  That said, there are situations where they are
appropriate, such as for character deletion.  Beyond that, we have to
examine the possible benefit of the  box.

I'm sure everyone has heard the old joke where the guy goes into the Doctor
and says, "Doc, my arm hurts when I do *this*" (Picture arm twisted around
in an unnatural position).  The Doctor says, "Don't do that".  Problem
solved.

If the risk that comes from binding yourself in a bad location is too high
to be palatable... Well, don't do that.
