Morgan wrote:
> NBarnes wrote:

> >  drocket has a point that one of the flaws in EQ is that their
> > characters are _very_ static.  A 10th lvl warrior really is _very_
> > similar to a 30th lvl warrior, which is in turn not all that
> > different, play-wise, from a 50th lvl warrior.  The other classes,
> > to varying degrees, are much the same way.  There are almost no
> > significant decisions and choices to be made in developing one's
> > character within the constraints of your class, and the classes
> > remain fairly static.

> I think your example is biased by the class you chose.  My set of
> abilities and strategies has changed drastically at certain points
> in my career as an Enchanter.  Charm (level 12) and Clarity (level
> 29) both brought many new options.  The ability to meditate without
> staring at the spell book at level 35 has also changed the game
> substantially, since I can now watch the fights rather than hoping
> that my companions let me know what is happening.

 I did say that other classes had the same problem to varying
degrees.  And enchanters are one of the classes that changes more
compared to the rest of EQ.  However, once the enchanter reaches
16th level and gets Mezmerization (AoE mez), you have more or
less finished evolving as an enchanter.  Clarity is very nice, to
be sure, but doesn't change your playstyle all that much.
 This is exactly the problem that every class in EQ has.  Once you
hit a certain, low, level, your class stops getting anything new
and interesting.  Oh, you get _bigger_ versions of things you had
before, but new stuff comes very rarely, if at all.  The only
thing that I would call very major that comes late for any class is
the shaman's pets, which they start getting at 34th level.

> Perhaps what you mean is that all 10th level warriors are similar
> to each other.  That I would certainly agree with, and would agree
> that it is a flaw in the game.  There are many race/class combinations
> to choose from and I enjoy playing the classes as they were designed,
> but one level 30 Troll Warrior is much like any other level 30 Troll
> Warrior.

 There are two aspect to this problem.  One is that the classes
tend to be rather homogenous as they advance in level (depends on
the class, though.  Warriors or wizards at almost any level play
much the same, at once extreme).  The other, more serious problem
is that _all_ of the examples of a given class at a given level are
almost identical.  A 30th level warrior, of any race, with almost
any equipment, is going to be _very_ similar to any other 30th
lvl warrior.

> >  This is a flaw in EQ, not a flaw in class-based systems.  For a
> > different approach, look into what Blizzard is doing with Diablo 2,
> > where the different classes get different skill trees to explore,
> > essentially containing five different skill systems within thier
> > five classes.  In theory (and, yes, I'm fond of the approach and
> > am looking forward to Diablo 2), this keeps the characters distinct
> > without leading to degeneracy.  In theory.

> EQ tried to let people differentiate themselves at higher levels by
> allowing magic specializations.  Unfortunately, there is really only
> one good choice in specialization for each class.

 Eh.  Druids have a legitimate choice to make between alteration
and evocation.  Enchanters have to choose between alteration
(almost everything but mezzes) and conjuration (mezzes).  Necros
between alteration (lifetaps), evocation (nukes), and conjuration
(most DoTs).  Magicians between conjuration (their second-best level
nuke, magic based and thus harder to resist) and evocation (their
best nuke, fire-based and much easier to resist).  Clerics, shamen,
and wizards are much more cut and dried, though.
 In any case, while specilization choices are a good example of the
sort of thing that EQ needs more of, well, EQ needs more of them.  :)
Specilization does not sufficently differentiate the classes in
EQ, even at higher levels when you would think you could tell
the difference.  I _cannot_ tell the difference between a alt-spec'd
druid and an evo-spec'd druid (well, not without extrapolating from
their playstyle) I'm grouping with at 45th, and that's about the most
dramatic shift of any of the choices.
