          Yes, the ballot was designed by a democrat.  Yes, it was passed
          on by both parties.  Yes, it was apparently confusing (unless you
          really believe that all those people intended that there should
          be a 1/2 a vote each for both Gore and Buchanan).  What should
          be done?  Florida has gone both ways in the past.  They have
          reheld elections in some cases, and simply tossed ballots out
          in others.  Its for a Judge to decide.  The Judge who will hear it
          is a Republican, an appointee of Bush, Sr., I have been told.
          (I'm not certain that this is true, but it should be easily

          I really don't much care who gets in the White House.  Both Congress
          and Senate are closely enough split to prevent _most_ instances of
          excessive partizanship by either side.  I actually would like to
          see this election play out as by law it should.  Neither side
          should resort to extra-legal means to win it, But both should
          completely exhaust their legal options with regards to it.  Thats
          why those options are available, and it is neither unstatesmanlike,
          dishonorable or wrong to excersize them. (As it has somehow come
          to be viewed.)

          You can tell who voted. (They sign in the register). You can
          probably allow any recount of the absentee ballots to stand
          (i.e., as soon as you get them counted and agreed on by both
          parties, you accept the counts from those.), since they used
          essentially different ballots, and there did not seem to be a
          problem with them. Then, simply allow the people who voted in
          the polls the opportunity to do so again. One might assume that the
          majority of the people who voted for Buchanan will vote for Bush,
          and the majority of those voting for Nader will vote for Gore, but
          so be it.

          One would assume that neither political party should be able
          to complain about people being able to actually vote for the
          candidate that they wanted to, all rhetoric aside.

          As a friend of mine from Ethiopia put it, "If a country in Africa
          had a election where the candidate who won by popular vote was
          not allowed to serve due to some political trick" (again, his
          words), "the U.S. would be the first to urge NATO to send in
          peacekeepers to remedy the situation."

          I don't view the electoral college vote as trickery, but its part
          of the laws of the land, and needs to be upheld until changed.
