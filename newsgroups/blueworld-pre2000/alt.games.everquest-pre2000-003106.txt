In article <7uh7topn4@tines.hsc.fr>, archer@hsc.fr says...
> I admit it, I've been relaxing out of EQ and playing Asheron's Call beta
> for a while (haven't pushed a character very high yet, will try to do so
> before beta ends). Since AC will hit the stores early november, and thus
> will *NOT* change in any significant manner before release, it is now
> safe to make a meaningful comparison between both.

I played EQ for about 6 months. I played AC for about 3 days. So maybe I 
shouldn't comment on AC, but I can't help it. First of all, the main 
reason I only played for 3 days, is because the game really bored me. But 
maybe I'll buy it anyhow, and give it anoher chance...

> * where AC wins over EQ
> 
> - Outdoors. Now, you really have a sense of a world. If you tought that
>   Norrath was huge, Dereth beats it down. I ran my ass off for 20mn, and
>   found out on a A3 map that I had travelled 2cm on it...

Yeah, it was huge. I ran for a long time... but without seeing *anything* 
-I tried to run from one town to another, and all I saw was a desolate 
landscape, and an endless supply of mobs, all just sitting motionless, 
each "camp" little more than a few steps from the next, nearly identical 
camp.

I finally arrived at the next town, and it was only 3 huts, and 3 
inhabitants.

> - Skill system. It allows for a wide diversity (I picked a Blademaster, and
>   dropped useless skills, and added Missile Defense instead. Other would
>   add ranged attacks, or a minor in creature magic) of player profiles.

I really didnt see the diversity in the character creation- there seemed 
to be far fewer skills than in EQ. It's nice to have the freedom to 
create my own "class" but with so few skills to choose from, there really 
didnt seem to be very many viable skill combinations.

> * when AC loses to EQ
> 
> - Magic system. The concept looks initially interesting, but it's only that.
>   If you're willing to endure the tedium of it, you can figure out all
>   spells out by yourself. There's also only a moderate variety of spells,
>   which only marginally look different.

I was pretty disappointed to realize this. In EQ, it was so exciting to 
get my new spells, when I levelled, wondering what nifty things I'd be 
able to do. If they were all DD or healing spells, there would not have 
been that excitment at all...

> - Monster AI. What AI? Monsters have a range (X% of the radar view). If
>   you are in range, they go straight to you (and get stuck on a wall).
>   I've seen that behaviour on the 2nd floor of a dungeon. Monsters in the
>   1st floor would go straight to me... then figure out they couldn't get
>   to me at all (because I wasn't on the same floor). If there is no straight
>   line from where the monster is to you, you're safe.

In retrospect, I'm really impressed with the NPC AI in EQ. The mobs are 
just NPCs, and the NPCs are just mobs. They respond to the players and 
eachother based on faction, as well as relative strength/level. Fear, 
anger, benevolence, etc...

In AC, there seemed to be none of that. merchants were just vending 
machines. Mobs just attacked any player, and ignored other mobs... 
Very simplistic. 

> - Lack of zone character. The areas look incredibly "same". Not much
>   character to these.

Was hard to stay interested in the game, as I realized that. After seeing 
the same wilderness no matter where I went, I felt like, why bother going 
anywhere... 

> - Lack of group support. The support for groups is the most primitive
>   I have seen. A group is simply a bunch of people that share XP. To someone
>   used to Everquest, AC gives a poor support.

Well, that isn't necessarily a negative, imho. EQ is just so group 
oriented, whereas in AC, it helps, but isnt necassary. EQ went too far, 
in that respect. All that group support was just the other side of that 
coin, I think.

> - No banks. Enough said: you carry all your belongings and spare belongings
>   on you. Not even a local bank. Mules everywhere.

Heh, no wonder I couldnt find one...

> * Overall conclusion.
> 
> I appreciated AC because it offers a refreshing change. But the bad parts
> *far* outweight the good ones - for a casual gaming experience, AC wins. But
> the lack of bank, lack of group support, the awful monster AI prevent it
> from being a good game, let alone a game winner.

Sadly, I have to agree, although for some different reasons. The 
desolate, uninteresting world being the primary reason.

> If asked by someone whether to pick EQ or AC to play, I'd consider the player
> profile. A casual player that plays a few hours a week would probably
> appreciate AC, for its solo-ability and sheer size. A steady, serious player
> that plays an hour a day or so would probably get bored or frustrated by AC's
> shortcomings quickly.

I would recommend EQ, hands down, to anyone. Unless they've already 
played EQ to death. But assuming this potential player hasnt tried either 
one, I can't imagine them preferring AC.
