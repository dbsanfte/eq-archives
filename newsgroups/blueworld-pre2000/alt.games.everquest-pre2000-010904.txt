> The various buffs that increase your HP (either directly or by
> increasing your stamina) actually increase your _maximum_ HP.  They do
> nothing to your current HP, and do not increase your current HP.
> 
> The HP meter represents a percentage of your total health, up to 100%,
> not an actual number of HP.
> 
> If you have a max of 100 health and are fully healed, and a buff gives
> you +100 HP, your maximum HP will go up to 200, and your HP meter will
> be half full, because you still have 100 HP.  You will need to heal up
> to 200 before the meter is full again.
> 
> If you then receive 150 points of damage, so that you have 50 HP left,
> your HP meter will be one quarter full.  If the buff goes away then, you
> will still have 50HP, and your HP meter will then show half full.
> 
> If you have more than 100HP, and the buff goes away, you will be reduced
> to your normal maximum of 100HP and your HP meter will be full (this is
> the only case in which losing the buff makes you lose HP).

Cleric, Druid and Shaman hit point buffs heal as they raise the 
max hits, so your +100 hp buff would leave you at 200 health,
fully healed.  Pure caster shielding spells just raise the capacity
without healing you to fill it, just as you described.

When any of these spells wear off, the max hit point value is 
decreased, but you only lose health if you were above your natural
max.  So if you were at 160/200 hp when your +100hp spell wore off
you would have 100/100.

Items that add hits when worn, such as the fire opal rings made by
jewelers, add their hits when you put on the item and remove them 
when you take it off.  So, if you are at 100/100 when you put on a 
+50hp ring, you will be at 150/150.  If you drop to 120/150 and
take the ring off, you will be at 70/100.  If you dropped to 20/150,
taking the ring off would leave you at -30/100 and would kill you.
