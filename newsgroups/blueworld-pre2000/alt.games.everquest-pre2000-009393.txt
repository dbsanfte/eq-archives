When I see a new enchanter around Felwithe, I will often go buy them the
level 1 pet spell and a few tiny daggers.  It's only a couple gold and
it makes such a difference.  I think a lot of people have a soft spot 
for their own class. ;)  They don't get anything if they ask, but I will
always answer questions.

> 'I need to get to my corpse. SoW/Port me.'
> I like to give the benefit of the doubt on this one. But its becoming more
> and more common for guys to stash their stuff in the bank, get their buff or
> port and then grab the stuff out of the bank again. This is why an offer of
> cash comes across as more sincere. Even if you just died you should be able

You forgot the high level beggar line: "My corpse decayed and I lost 
everything."  This actually happened to a friend of mine last week.
He didn't beg for gear, of course, just set out to rebuild his kit. 

> 'I'll pay XX for NN.'
> I haven't gotten to the point where I need the money first before the
> service but I can see it coming. When you don't pay after the offer you do a
> diservice to everyone in this situation. Often I will end up giving the
> service for free when cash has been offered, but I want to see the
> legitimate offer, don't assume. Same goes to taking the cash for the service
> and then not providing of course.

I've always paid after the buff or port, not before.  Is this considered 
rude?  If the spell needs components, I hand over the component or pay 
first.

I was very irked with one druid who insisted on being paid up front 
before a teleport.  I wanted to get to the commonlands from butcherblock 
quickly, so I offered to pay for a teleport.  A druid at the docks said
she would take me.  First she sat around for about twenty minutes waiting 
to get a full group.  I am not sure why she didn't just port the three of
us and come back for the last two.  I had given her Clarity, so it's not
like mana was a problem.  Then, when we finally had a group, she asked 
for money before porting, so everyone fumbled with cash for a few more 
minutes.  I would much rather have just ported, handed her the cash and 
then run off while everyone else fumbled.  Of course, if I had known how 
slow she was going to be, I would have just taken the boat and saved 
myself some frustration.
