>Exactly.  Many players found the above too difficult to deal with, that
>is to say they found it to be unreasonable.  It is one thing to have to
>resolve such situations in real life; it is quite another to have to
>deal with them in a game setting.  Marian's Dilema attempts to determine

The difficulty is, how badly are you going to cripple the game in
order to please one side or the other? And the question really comes
down to what sort of game you are trying to make.

If you are trying to make a virtual world where as wide a range of
human interaction as you can support is present, then discarding all
that is a huge, huge blow to the integrity of the game.

>My ideal MMORPG would include full PvP, but the players themselves have
>to be mature enough to handle that freedom, and there need to be some
>in-game consequences that mean something.  I have yet to see method
>which allows for such total freedom of action while maintaining enough
>safeguards to prevent abuse.  The ability to use mule characters allows
>PK'er to circumvent what is supposed to be a heavy negative consequence
>of their action; the ability to go to town and engage in commerce
>there.  The abilty to continuously start new characters with no history
>of the players negative actions further undermines good PvP.  And
>neither of those two factor are even gameplay related issues.  Oh well,
>so long as online RPG's continue to be popular, we will see even more
>attempts.  Maybe one will work to my liking.

There's no doubt in my mind that the rep system would work much better
if there were one character per account. Hell, the whole GAME would
work better if there was only one character per account. We're talking
about only allowing one on the new version of the Abyss.
