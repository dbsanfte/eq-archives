>The ease or difficulty of implementation here is really moot.  How
>often must we take steps to save people from their own bad
>choices?  The freedom to make bad choices is essentially part of
>an RPG, and we don't want to go Confirmation Box Crazy just to
>stop people from making them. 

Why not? Add confirmation boxes for EVERYTHING. Add a confirmation box for 
jumping, if it'll make one player happy... what do you care? Just allow us to 
turn them off, thats all.

I mean, really.

>We've gotten tons of suggestions for various confirmation boxes in
>game.  If we implemented all of them just on the basis of them
>being easy to implement, you wouldn't be able to take a single
>action in EverQuest without having to click YES.  

That's a facetious argument, and I'm fairly sure you know it. The confirmation 
boxes should be optional.

>Among those suggested: Box for Attacking. 

Why not? Haven't you accidentally attacked your guildmaster once or twice? 
Wouldn't a confirmation box be helpful when you're level 1? Sure, everybody 
would turn it off afterwards, but why NOT add this?

>Box for Dropping items.  

Why not? How often do you actually want to drop an item? Hell, I pretty much 
NEVER do it... I just use the destroy button personally. This one is kind of a 
big deal, especially for bards.

>Box for handing items (above and beyond that which exists). Box for
>Hailing NPCs.  Box for Zoning.  Box for Combining Items in a Kit. 
>Box for Forgetting Spells.  Box for Disembarking a Ship.  In
>addition, for each box that already exists in game, people have
>also suggested secondary "Are you *absolutely* sure?" boxes for
>most of the things listed above. 

Again, why not? Your customers are asking for something that would be trivially 
easy to implement and that would affect nobody negatively-- seems like a win-
win situation to me.

>Confirmation Boxes are a crude and ugly way to try and make a
>piece of software foolproof.  That said, there are situations
>where they are appropriate, such as for character deletion. 
>Beyond that, we have to examine the possible benefit of the  box.

If it benefits ONE player and doesn't inconvenience ANY, the box is worthwhile 
in of itself. After that, you just have to look at the time taken to code them 
in. The item dropping box should absolutely be added; I know bards that have 
dropped ykeshas.

>I'm sure everyone has heard the old joke where the guy goes into
>the Doctor and says, "Doc, my arm hurts when I do *this*" (Picture
>arm twisted around in an unnatural position).  The Doctor says,
>"Don't do that".  Problem solved.
>
>If the risk that comes from binding yourself in a bad location is
>too high to be palatable... Well, don't do that.

That simply isn't good enough. That answer is no good.
