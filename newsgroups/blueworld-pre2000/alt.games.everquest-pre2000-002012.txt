Raph Koster wrote:

> The pattern I would predict for a a nonPvP world would be huge initial
>
> demand, forcing opening of a bunch of worlds to cater to it (actually,
>
> more likely, conversion of existing worlds, which means that the
> current style of play vanishes), followed by lower retention rates
> overall and an inability to go back to the previous model.

Why do you predict lower retention rates overall?  That is apparently
the crux of the matter as this is what drives your business model.  If
it is true that non-PvP players wouldn't play as long as PvP, it might
make sense to hold off on a dedicated server.  However, I have no idea
why that would be the case.  If this information is based on past
experience with MUD's and the like, I would be very interested to see
it.

I guess that is my main gripe.  The whole business about non-PvP players
not playing as long.  It just seems like an assumption made up out of
whole cloth.

Also, why would it be so hard to add additional servers?  Why would it
be necessary to convert existing worlds?  The idea is to add more
people, not just move the ones you already have.

> Remember, the money is in retention, not in demand.

To a point, yes.  But if you can continuously introduce more customers
into the system than leave, you still have overall net growth in
dollars.  It doesn't matter how long they stay so long as you have more
people entering than leaving.  Granted, the longer they stay the more
benefits accrue to you, but the overall length of time doesn't seem as
important as general increase in population.  Now if the overall
customer base is flat (i.e. the number entering and leaving are the
same) or decreasing, retention is paramount.  Is this what is happening?

> Which is why I think the right answer is to support both within a
> single world (and note that this does NOT mean a switch, as far as I
> am concerned), so that players who tire of the safe gameplay can try
> the current gameplay without starting over.
>
> -Raph Koster
> Lead Designer, Ultima Online

BTW, you certainly could open a single non-PvP shard with a user cap on
it.  Try it out for whatever the average length of retention you
currently have.  The you would have some hard data about your rates.
Now, this would cause massive unrest due to all the players who would
like to be on that server im sure :-).  Maybe you could hold a lottery
for interested people.

My point is simply this: the people who enjoy UO now without
participating in PvP conflict are just as apt to play long term as those
that do participate in it.  Most people (at least from what you read on
newsgroups) seem to be at one end or the other of the PvP spectrum (they
either want it all or none of it).  I would guess there would be no
noticeable difference in the retention rates myself, but then again, I'm
basing this on my opinion.

Roleplaying doesn't have to involve conflict.  What conflict is there
sitting in a tavern swapping tales, or having an in-wedding (now a
divorce may prove different), or practicing your trade in town?
Roleplaying is all about interaction between other players which can
take a myriad of forms.  Allowing all players to attack any other player
is just as limiting to roleplaying as artificially restricting them not
to have that ability.  It forces people into a role they may not want to
play (i.e. that of combatant).

Give the people a choice!  It's a free country! (Insert appropriately
themed stirring patriotic music here)
