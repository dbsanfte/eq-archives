Quatoria,

To start with, I absolutely oppose this law, and I will engage in a
good deal of "civil disobedience" if it ever materializes.  That
said...

You are absolutely correct... the government cannot pass legeslation
that infringes on someone's right to free speech, but they can pass
legislation that ALLOWS a licensor (software publisher) to dictate
that the licensee (the consumer) cannot publically discuss the
product.  It's all contract law, and the constitution does not matter
when two parties are creating a contract.

This type of thing is already being done in software licenses; this
law would just extend these provisions to consumer-based software
licenses automatically.  Have you ever seen Oracle (the database
software) reviewed in a classic head-to-head matchup with its
competitors complete with benchmarks... and you won't!!  Their license
specifically prohibits benchmarking, comparative specs, or technical
reviews.  Violation of the license would be grounds for a very
expensive lawsuit.

The problem with this scenario is that the consumers are being duped
into agreeing to this sort of business arrangement.  Who reads the
license agreement before they click "Yes".  This form of software
distribution is archaic and needs to die.  If my friend buys a
software license (i.e. game) and ascents to the license agreement by
pressing the "Yes" button, can I violate the license... I didn't agree
to the contract?  Can I even legally use the software (i.e. play the
game) because I'm not a licensor... I haven't entered into a license
contract?  Under this new law, can I watch someone else use the
software and then violate it's non-disclosure provision... the company
cannot sue me, because we haven't entered into a contract?

Basically, what I am saying is that the whole software license racket
(and it is a racket) has got to go.  We need laws protecting the
consumer, not protecting the publisher.  I want to know that when I
pay $50, $100, $500 for something that I have something to show for
it... I want more than the "right to use" it.  I want to know that if
I pay for a product, and that product doesn't work, then I can get a
full and TIMELY refund.  

If you think this is bad, take a look at the recording industry!!  At
least they haven't put a "piracy tax" on our equipment or software...
yet...
