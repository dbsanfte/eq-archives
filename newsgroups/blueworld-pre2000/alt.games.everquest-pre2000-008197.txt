I'm with Morgan.  Every time you whack something (or poke it with a
dagger), that's a tick of mana less that you have...  And every bit
makes a differnce.  And don't forget that the tanks are there to deal
damage as *well* as absorb it--and they easily have two times the HP you
do.  It's okay to let them get beat on a bit while you meditate.  (It
took me forever not to feel guilty doing that. :>)

Think of it this way, too.  If you're a healer-type, a few whacks is one
more heal you can get off onto your tank.  If you're a nuker, it's one
finishing nuke you won't have to polish off a mob with.  If you're a
Shaman or Druid, it's one DoT you won't be able to lay on a mob, one
root or snare you can't cast.  And if you're a Wizard or Druid...  It's
an Evac you can't perform.

If you want to read an illustration of this it's below; but be warned,
it's also some misplaced venting. ;)  But trust me...  Mana conservation
is important.  Sure, you can drop all your mana on that Rook to make it
drop a little faster...  but what're you going to have left when that
Avocet jumps on your tank just because he's a Troll?

Letting the fight go along a little longer to conserve mana takes up
much less time than corpse retrieval and experience recovery.  Trust me.
:>

--<illustrative story-slash-rant>--

Tonight, a Paladin and I (Warrior) were tanking for a couple of Druids
and a Cleric, who'd just joined us.  The Druids were snaring and DoTing
as well as putting damage shields on us tanks, and using what little
they had to keep us all alive.

The fights went well, but our Cleric was smiting every chance he got.
Smite, smite, smite.  (And no, we weren't fighting the Undead.)  Then
when we got into a bad fix, both the Paladin and I started to drop HP
*fast.*  Both of the Druids and the Cleric had mobs on them and couldn't
get off heals.  The Paly and I were taunting everything in sight so we
could be healed, and once we got the Cleric clear, we figured, "Great,
now he'll heal us, since we're under 2 bubbles."

But he started to melee.

"I need a heal," I said.

Clericboy said nothing; the Druids were still being pounded.

A minute later, "Heal, please," I said.  (Normally I just rely on my
healers to know when to heal, but I was getting worried.)

Clericboy just kept smacking the things with his big stick, while the
Druids managed to get off a couple of wispy heals before being OOM.  My
HP kept dropping.

And another minute later, "Clericboy, please stop meleeing and heal me
before I die."  (Yes, I really typed that all out, typing 100 WPM has
its advantages.  Besides, I'd seen it coming. ;X)

"Can't," came the reply.  Whack, whack, there goes his staff.  The
Paladin kills waht he'd been whacking and steps back to heal
himself--because no one else can or will.

About then, a bystander steps in and heals me just enough to keep me
alive.  After the whole thing is over, the Cleric stands there waiting
to loot, and after the Paly and I exalt how glad we are to be alive and
thank the Druids for getting their heals off, the Cleric says...

"Sorry.  Was OOM."

Okay, so our Cleric had been Smiting the shit out of things and instead
of meditating to keep his team alive, he just smacked it with his big
stick.  It's bad when the tank has to step back to heal himself or (as I
had to do 10 minutes later) bind wounds--in combat.

Moral of this story?  My reponsibility as a tank is to make sure I have
the weapons and armor I need to keep you safe, and that I use taunt and
keep my attack on so that the mobs don't beat on you.  Your
responsibility is to make sure you have the spells you need--and the
mana to use them.

</illustrative story-slash-vent>

>
> Running out your mana and then fighting melee is rarely the
> best strategy for any pure caster.  Unless you are only fighting
> very occasional reds with no possibility of interference, you
> should keep at least a bubble of mana in reserve for emergencies.
> As a Necromancer, you may need to heal your pet or your team mates,
> immobilize an enemy, steal some life, or escape a hopeless fight.
> If you have no mana, your options are extremely limited.
>
> In general, your group does better if everyone recovers at roughly
> the same time.  So, if you find that the rest of your group has
> mana when you are out, you should spend less so that you don't
> hold them back.  Try to figure out how many of your DoT spells
> you should cast on each type of foe.
>
> Weapon fighting is a luxury for casters.  Any time you spend
> swinging your weapon is time you are not meditating.  If your
> group is pulling too slowly to keep you drained, then you can get
> in some weapon practice.  But if you are out of mana you should be
> meditating, not meleeing.
>
> As a pet class, you may find that you have to stay heads up to
> keep an eye on your skeleton.  In that case you might as well
> swing your weapon while you watch him if it isn't going to get
> you in trouble.  Just remember that proximity is a huge factor
> in getting a monster angry, so you might have to keep a distance
> if the monsters are suffering under the effects of your spells.
