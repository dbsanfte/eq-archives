Sam Schlansky wrote:
> 
> Personally, I'd like to see a skill-based system based on "archetypes"...
> kind of like a class-based system with a lot more flexibility.


I definatly have to disagree.  What makes Ultima a unique experience is
that we are not hindered by someones conception of what a "class" should
be.

I liked the fact that the Great Bob has taken a journey with his skills
and knowledge, from a simple swordsman, to a skilled archer, to a
powerfull mage.  In a class based system I probally would have just
started over with a new character, rather than take the Great Bob
through a process of discovery.

Fixed classes means that people become clones.  Skill systems with a
fixed cap assures that many different avenues will be explored.  The
reason why everyone was a tank mage in UO was that there was a limit to
the number of effective choices.

With meditation, necromancy, anatomy for mele damage and healing, the
healing skill, and a host of other new abilities, we are begining to see
the growth of diversity inside UO.  I now see more "pure" mages (ie.
mages who have trained in mediatation, eval int. and magery instead of
three different combat skills) than I see "tanks"

I would find a class based system to be a limitation in a game where
freedom of action makes the system so interesting.

If I wanted to be forced into certaint roles, and play in a certaint
fashion I would choose EQ or something similar.  Instead I can do what I
wish in UO.

Great Bob
