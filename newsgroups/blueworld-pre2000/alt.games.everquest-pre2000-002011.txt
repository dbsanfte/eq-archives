I don't follow this line of reasoning.  You seem to be of the opinion
that without the "conflict generation" of PvP, lots of people would
quickly lose interest and stop playing bceause they wouldn't feel
"passionate" about the game.  I don't see that at all.  As a matter of
fact, there are plenty of people who feel "passionately" that
unrestricted PvP is not worth their gaming dollar.  If these people feel
strongly enough to either stop spending money on your product, or to not
spent that money at all, how can you say they are uninvolved emotionally
with it?

Further, if you KNOW that a non-PvP server would be your most popular
one, yet still choose not to take advantage of that fact, is that really
wise from a pure business standpoint?  It has been postulated that the
majority of current UO players are not in favor of a PK switch or non PK
shard.  If we accept that as a fact, then opening a non-PvP shard would
only be attracting NEW players who find it desirable, and old players
who have left the game (many for this very issue).  Either way, it
increases your subscriber base.  It seems to me you would want to appeal
to the widest audience possible so the product remains financially
viable.

As to losing interest, so long as the game continues to evolve and grow,
the online community will evolve and grow right along with it.  If you
were to cease development of new feature and simple leave the game as
is, eventually, most people would lose interest and move on to the "next
big thing".  It is the development teams job to ensure that the
gameworld remains engaging and invloving for the long haul.  Listening
to the customer base, adding new features, tweaking existing features.
All of that helps keep the customers long term.  You know that already.
So why the rationalization of this whole "PvP creates conflict, conflict
creates passion, passion breeds loyalty" pyschobabble.  The first two
parts of that could even be true, but the last it obviously a matter of
opinion.  Passion could just as easily mean anger and frustration with
the product to the point of discontinuation.
