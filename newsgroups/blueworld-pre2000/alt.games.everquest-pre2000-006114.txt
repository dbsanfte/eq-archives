Seriously, it works.  So don't just think about it.
"Use the facts, Luke!"  It's a fact that contracts
are mutual agreements, so you're equally entitled
to set forth terms and conditions.  The best part
is that the law is obliged to back you up.

Might be quite useful when obtaining insurance
to _add_ a clause covering you for all fires,
floods, electrical accidents, etc.  Scratch
out any sections you disagree with.

In the eventuality that the agent spots the changes
and declines to sign on his/her dotted line, then
try another company and/or agent, and another,
until one of them does legally agree.

This is exactly the modus operandi of companies
which the law evidently approves of, so it's
perfectly justified both morally and legally
to turn the tables.

In brief:

* The virtue of contract law is that it applies
to both parties to a contract. *

Unfortunately, many consumers are unaware of their rights,
and still bow like serfs to the supposed squires.
