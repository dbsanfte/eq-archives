> >The short hate list theory seems to hold up the best so far.
> >
> >The monster remembers eight people that it hates.  (I don't know
> >the actual number, but I assume it is fixed.)  For each of those
> >people, it knows how much it hates the person and how much damage
> >that person has done.
> >
> >Whenever someone antagonizes the monster (by dealing damage, helping
> >one of its opponents, or merely walking within aggro range), the
> >monster checks its list.  If it already knows about the person, it
> >adds the new damage and hate to the current record.  If it doesn't
> >know about the person, it adds the new person to its list with a
> >starting hate and damage value.
> >
> >When the monster dies, the damage values on the hate list are matched
> >up by group.  The group that did the most damage gets the credit.
> >
> >The problem comes in if the monster was already mad at eight
> >different people when a new person hurts it.  Then one of the eight
> >people on the list will be forgotten to make room for the new person.
> >The person removed might be the one that the monster hates the least,
> >but it might be the person who has done the least damage, the last
> >person added to the list (which is my current guess), the person who
> >has been on the list longest, or completely random.
> >
> >If a group of mages, warriors and pets are all beating on the monster
> >at the same time, they can overflow the list, perpetually knocking
> >each other off the monster's register.  When this happens, damage done
> >later in the fight will be more likely to show up on the hate list and
> >damage done early in the fight will be more likely to be forgotten.
>
