> Never play the waiting game.  Any new hardware products that come out in
the
> next two or three years that you just gotta have will almost certainly be
> available to you regardless of choices made today.  Make sure the
> motherboard speed is 100 or 133Mhtz, and has at least 4 PCI slots and 1
ISA
> slot to allow expansion.
>
> 90% of the time, RAM is the first thing you will upgrade, and it happens
> about 2 years into the life of the PC.  Since RAM prices fluctuate
> unpredictably, there is no reason to assume RAM will be significantly
> cheaper 2-3 years in the future (it could actually be more expensive.)
Most
> folks are surprised to learn that very often the cheapest way to get RAM
is
> with the purchase of a PC, so you're better of buying that extra memory up
> front and having it over the entire 3-4 year lifespan of the PC, rather
than
> paying the same money for it halfway through that lifespan anyway.  (Basic
> dollar cost averaging.)
>
> In general, buying the second or third best processor available presents
the
> best long term investment.  New chips cost more because the manufacturing
> yield is so low:  50% of new chips are flawed and must be destroyed, but
> eventually they get that number up to 90%, and that savings is reflected
in
> the price drop you see in older, slower processors.  If 1000 is the
fastest,
> go with a 733 or 800.
>
> Final thoughts:
>
> Unless you live in downtown silicon alley, what possible incentive would
you
> have to build your own system from scratch?   Contrary to popular belief,
it
> costs MORE money to build a system yourself than to buy one direct.
> Manufacturers get discounts for volume purchases and pass the savings on
to
> you.   Ordering just ONE part from a reseller/vendors means you're paying
> more for it.  Plus you pay for the shipping of each part.
>
> Building a PC can be fun, and you get absolute control over the components
> you choose.  But unless you consider the extra cost to be worth the fun
and
> educational value of building the PC, just save yourself the extra scratch
> and go put together a good one from a direct manufacturer.
