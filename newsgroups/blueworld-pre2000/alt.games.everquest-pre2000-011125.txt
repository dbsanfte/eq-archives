> For example, your computer can send your current position to the
> server 100 times per second, and the server can in turn pass your
> 100/sec position updates to your gameplay opponent at the same rate.
> There's no reason at all to "wait for" a reply after each update
> before sending the next "where I am now" update.

With a 56k modem, you can send over a minute of NES or Game Boy controller
signals every second, but not one instant of that signal will arrive on time.



> The only limitation that the speed of light places on you is how
> UP TO DATE each of your 100/sec position updates are when they
> arrive at your opponent's computer.  Using your numbers, they'll
> be only 1/14th of a second "behind" where you actually "are"
> at the moment, which isn't too terribly bad.

It would be like playing StarFox on the SNES, which played OK, but I'd like to
be able to play more responsive games as well.  If you stay within a 2500 mile
radius, you'll get about 30 updates per second, at best.



> And even that can be adjusted by calculating how much of a delay
> is involved, then "projecting" your position "forward" that far
> in time (using the most recently received several "position
> fixes" as your "trajectory", and then computing where you
> would be 1/14th of a second later if you kept moving along
> that path).

That will work in games where you're not inputting most of the time, like
strategy games, but not in action games where you must constantly press buttons.
Even in Myth II, if you start clicking around a lot in a net game, the game will
start pausing.



> But the point is that while there may be a delay in getting
> position updates from you, they can still arrive at a fast
> enough pace (e.g. 100/second) that your movement as plotted
> on the recipient's computer is just as smooth and fluid
> as your original motion was.
>
> And of course the same situation holds in reverse when
> it comes to his sending his positions to your computer.

But it's delayed, thus making it impossible to synchronize movements between two
players across the network.
