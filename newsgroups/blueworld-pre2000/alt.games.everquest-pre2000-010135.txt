I still think this provides a nasty possibility;  the idea truly seems to be
that Verant thinks I should sit there and watch, with interest, my castings
fizzle or my complete inability to determine which direction I'm facing.

I have trouble with the latter especially, since someone at Verant obviously
found being able to tell which direction you're going reliably important 
enough to make the compass.  Yet, if we don't train the skill up naturally in
some type of "proper" manner, we're evil?  Bah.  Impure design, to say the 
least.

> Now THIS I wholeheatedly agree with, but not because of gamepads or macros.
> I don't like the fact that there's not a clearly publisized set of standards
> and guidelines for GMs.  Further, there should be some sort of "Uber-GM", to
> which you could /report and /petition to when you think you've gotten a raw

I would add to this that there needs to be defined, on stone tablets, a set
of "uber-rules" which are not nearly so fluid as everything else in this 
game seems to be.  OR, the benefit of the doubt should be given to the player
through sufficient warnings.  If Verant has designed their product correctly,
third party programs are a nuisance.  They have the possibility of allowing
more information about the way the game works out of the tight controls of
Verant.  Assuming adequate design (things such as server side dice rolls, 
combat checks, etc), third party programs don't significantly affect others.

If there were ways to remove things from the world through macro use, I would
feel different.  But there aren't.  At worst the macros can increase otherwise
tedious to raise skills, and perhaps give a group a bit better efficiency at
timing spawns.  Even the latter is something that good design (small
randomization across ALL spawn times) could fix.  I guess my main gripe is the
concept of being bludgeoned under the heavy hand of VI for their design 
oversights.
