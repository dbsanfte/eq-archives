Bongy Boy, you got me laughing on that one.  Ive designed many C/S  and 
multi-tier setups in the telecom world over the past decade -- primarily 
used straight TCP/IP or CORBA, with Oracle SQL*Net as a decent setup for 
the backend - and hell, Ive even used slow assed Microsoft MTS as a 
middleware layer.  And these systems are by nature fast, secure, and 
contain configurable security audit trails and tracability logs.  Its not 
that difficult an issue.  Just start streaming a log (be concise and 
accurate with the data), use a log chopper, a few shell scripts, a cron 
job, and compress then spool them to a backup "near time" storage, and 
from there to tape after they age out.  Write a tool for analysis of 
these logs (Perl does a good job, toss in TkPerl for a GUI on a UNix 
box), and a consistent naming strategy and the job is done.   This is how 
we track hundreds of thousands of daily transactions for billing of the 
main switches in the telcom and datacom world.  And you age them off tape 
after a time (pretty fast aging for Verant compare to a telco - no law 
says they have to keep accounting stuff for as long as the telcos do).  
This is common practice on large scale C/S projects if you know what you 
are doing - which Verant evidently doesnt and neither do you if you cant 
grasp such an idea.
