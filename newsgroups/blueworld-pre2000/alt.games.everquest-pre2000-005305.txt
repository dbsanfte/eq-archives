Now you are getting ridiculous.  A contract is invalid if it induces
any party to the contract to do something illegal.  The problem here
is that you, and most Americans for that matter, confuse the Bill of
Rights with laws.  The Bill of Rights is a set of rules that the
GOVERNMENT must adhere to, NOT the citizens.  Freedom of Speech is
something that the government cannot infringe upon, but ordinary
citizens can.  Come over to my house and not only will I infringe on
your right to free speech, but I'll also infringe on your right to
bear arms (I'm the only armed person allowed in my house... and I'm
armed!).  Oh yeah the "unwarranted search and seizure" amendment...
you know the one that everybody assumes means "right to privacy"...
I'll violate that one too... I'll rifle your bags, without a search
warrant.  I can do these things because I'm a private citizen and you
are in my house... you have no rights in this case.

As to "signing away" your constitutional rights, I'll give you an
example that everyone here should be familiar with... the
Non-Disclosure Agreement.  This document that is so pervasive in the
business world today (I can't even keep track of how many NDAs I'm
subject to), essentially requires you to "sign away" your freedom of
speech in a given context and a given amount of time.
