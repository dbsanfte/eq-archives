You know, when I read this, I just shrugged my shoulders.  If you use
Windows, you have no security, you have no privacy.  The only thing that
protects you is the general good nature of most humans and the technical
stupidity of most criminals.  Back your stuff up frequently and don't put
anything you aren't willing to share on a networked PC.

Why do you think the US government was so pissed when they found out that
CIA Director John Deutch had been copying secured data to his home PC?  From
CNN's report on the case: "Former CIA Director John Deutch's alleged use of
a home computer to store classified materials has sparked a security scare
in the U.S. intelligence community and has also pointed out a problem many
Internet users are not aware of.  The minute anyone logs onto the Internet,
financial records and other personal information stored on a home computer
are an open book to any cyber-thief."  Windows PCs are the least secure
devices I can think of.  You'd be much better off with any of the other
available options.

However, even the most secure systems in the world are open doors to
sufficiently knowledgeable individuals.  From a March 21st CNN arcticle:
"The Defense Department has already tested and proven that hackers have the
ability to infiltrate the command and control systems of major weapons,
including Navy warships. According to a training CD-ROM on information
assurance, published by the Defense Information Systems Agency, an Air Force
officer sitting in a hotel room in Boston used a laptop computer to hack
into a Navy ship at sea and implant false navigation data into the ship's
steering system."  Now, that officer had a heck of a lot more knowledge
about the DoD's security systems then the average hacker, but the point is
that nothing that transmits or receives information can be 100% relied upon.

The bottom line is that if you have private information on a Windows PC, and
you have a network cable or phone line coming out of the back of that PC,
you are stupid.  If you have private information on ANYTHING with a network
cable or phone line, you are taking a substantial risk.

P.s don't rely on firewalls either; they don't mean squat if you accept ANY
incoming traffic.  Now that they finally work fairly well, all the Windows
programmers tunneled their services over HTTP.  The documentation for these
products state that the intention for this was to get through firewalls.
Hey developers, here's a clue: if we wanted your product to get through the
firewall, we'd have opened a damn port.  Long and short is that if you have
a single web server open, better assume that you've pretty much opened
everything.
