VWilcox wrote:
> 
> > This is easily explained by the "short hate list churning" hypothesis.
> > The hate list is a short array of [playerId hate damage].  When the list
> > is full, it starts over again at the beginning, overwriting all previous
> > damage and hate.  So, if there are N entries in the list and more than N
> > people and pets attacking, the person who did the most damage out of the
> > last N attacks gets the kill.
> >
> > If there are fewer than N people and pets attacking, then they never get
> > rolled off the list, and everyone gets credit for all the damage they
> > did.
> 
> Very interesting.  I hadn't heard that theory (or you just made it) but it
> is very reasonable.

It's been bouncing around for a while.  I did not come up with it.

> Hate list would likely be a fixed length array.  I use
> storage template almost exclusively and avoid fixed length arrays like the
> plague (speed difference is negligible in almost all cases.)  Of course,
> with today's machines and the doubted existence of more than 1000 mobs in a
> zone -- simply upping the fixed length array dimension to double it's
> current length or more wouldn't likely use too much RAM either.  Programmers
> tend to maintain their old, old habits from their TRS-80 days when memory
> and speed were premiums.

Memory may be a problem, but speed is definitely a problem.  As long
as the lists are short, linear searches and unsorted lists are fine.
As soon as they set the hate list length to something adequate, like
32, those searches are going to get long.  Every time anyone irks a
monster in any way, the hate list for that monster has to be searched
to see if the person is already on the list.

My proposed solution is to make the list 16 entries long, which will
still result in hate list churning in big fights in the planes, but 
maintain a running total of group damage with each group member.  That 
way every member of a group would have to fall off the list for their 
damage to be forgotten.

> > > Another fix would be to suspend hate data that is blurred instead of
> > > clearing it.  Then if the players killing the MoB in the first place
> > > hit it for 4 points of dmg it REMEMBERS the 800 they did previously.
> >
> > There are two pieces of data associated with each person on the hate
> > list: damage done and hate level.  We know that these are separate,
> > because it is possible to get a monster very angry at you without
> > getting any kill credit, such as by healing someone who is fighting.
> 
> Agreed -- but the dmg done may be a parameter of the 'event' item in the
> history list.  It is also possible that they simply keep a sum of dmg done
> and hate level for each player that interacts with the MoB somehow.  

Yes, I think this is the case.  When you damage a monster or cause
it to hate you, the new damage and/or hate is added to your current
values if you are on the hate list.

I think that the hate list is not sorted by damage or hate.  I think
that each new person is added on to the end of the array as they 
enter the list, and if the list is full, they are added to the top,
replacing the person who has been on the list longest.

> If so,
> mem blur could be made to wipe all hate levels to 0 but keep dmg done.  But,
> to avoid the players doing partial damage multiple times to a MoB and then
> leaving (to get credit when someone else does the full kill)... the hate
> list would need to be completely wiped when a MoB healed to full or close
> and the hate levels were 0.

I don't really like the idea of letting the monster run around with
kill credit but no hate.  Something seems wrong with that.
