On 11 Jun 1999 19:54:48 GMT, madman@well.com (Adam Burr) wrote:

>What's important is _internally consistant_ realism. You can create a
>world and give it its own rules and then see what it 'realistic' in that
>world and what is not. When someone complains "is it realistic for
>shopkeepers to wander out of the shop then teleport when spoken to?" and
>someone else responds "is it realistic to cast fireball?" they are
>comparing the wrong kind of realism. 

The most important phrase there was "You can create a world and give
it its own rules."  That is exactly what the game designers do.  They
can make the choice of "lets have everyone able to kill everyone else,
because that's realistic" or they can make the choice of "lets let
only those players who want to kill each other, kill each other,
because other people don't want that stort of thing.  Screw realism,
in this case it interferes with people's enjoyment."  Verant chose the
latter, and I agree with them.  

In both UO and EQ, realism is sacrificed for convenience or gameplay.
When the house/boat breakins using gate were prevalent, UO's designers
argued that vulnerable houses were realistic, but they tried their
best to fix the bugs anyway.  But when it came to PKing, they (IMHO)
pandered to the PKs, even though there were thousands of people who
hated the current system.  They could have just as easily said, "In
this instance, just like with house break-ins, we're sacrificing
realism for a more enjoyable gaming experience."  But they didn't.
