dirk@midgard.cbi.msstate.edu wrote:
> 
> How EQ 2.0 will work.
> 
> The Questing system will receive a complete overhaul. Let me walk you
> through an xample, you will see how things fall into place as it goes.

[snip - example - much like the monk headband quests except that
 the items always drop]

I think this is a good concept for quests, in general, but has 
some drawbacks.

> - Joe Swashbuckler was always busy and having fun. MOST IMPORTANT!
> - All quest items dropped 100% of the time. Every kill, they dropped it.

The only problem with this kind of collecting is that the stuff has
to drop as loot.  If you add up all the quests and put all of the 
items as common drops on common monsters, you will end up with an
awful lot of junk on every single kill.  Perhaps the items could be
gruesome trophies, like tongues, heads and tails.  Rather than 
finding them when you loot, you would have to cut them off the 
corpse.

> - All quest items were LORE, he had to physically get them. At his level
> the monsters were hard, but alone with lots of luck or with a group he
> could get them. Noone else can get the items for him, although they can
> kill the NPC for him. See next.

I think you meant NO DROP, not LORE.  LORE means you can only have
one.  NO DROP means you can't drop, sell or trade the item.

> - A higher-level character cannot twink a lower level char with the
> item, it is NO DROP. He has to go through the WHOLE process with the
> lower level character, and god help them if the item is in LowerGuk and
> the newbie is level 5.
> - The item cannot be sold on eBay. :P

You'll have a hard time convincing Brad that cool items should be 
NO DROP.  He doesn't seem to understand that as long as there is 
a player market for stuff, there will be a real money market for
the same stuff.

> - Skills (bakery, jewelry, even foraging) will become more important as
> quests will require them. Player-driven economy will increase.

I think it's better to make the skills useful on their own merits,
although making items from quested or looted materials is very
appealing.

> - The item gets better as time progresses. And not many people will
> always have the ending best item. For example, PC1 may like the 4/5
> stage item of a sword that is +5 wisdom and procs combust. PC2 may like
> the ending 5/5 (of quests) that changes the sword to +5 wisdom and procs
> greater heal.

This only works if the NPC says what is coming.  It would really 
be a real pain to get to level 20, hand your sword to your guild
master along with hard won quest components and have him hand you
back a weapon you like less.  Reliance on spoiler sites is bad.

> Also if you really wanted it to be strict, you could impose a level
> limit on each stage of the item. For example, Stage 10/15 in the Quest
> for a Ring of the Jackal (+10 str, +5 chr, 5ac) you would have to be
> level 10 to wear it. Next stage, level 11 and so on. But I don't think
> you would need to do this honestly.

Strict upper level limits are a bad idea, because there is no way 
to be certain that Joe Swashbuckler will talk to the right NPC at 
the right time unless he is an avid reader of spoiler sites.  Lower
level limits are fine as long as the NPC tells Joe, "Come back when
you are more experienced."
